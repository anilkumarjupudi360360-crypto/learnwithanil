Sets, Relations & Functions

  1. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (CS & IT) · Q1 · Discrete Mathematics · Sets, Relations & Functions

    Which of the following are partitions of {1,2,......8}?

    1. {{1,3,5},{1,2,6},{4,7,8}}
    2. {{1,3,5},{2,6,7},{4,8}}
    3. {{1,3,5},{2,6},{2,6},{4,7,8}}
    4. {{1,5},{2,6},{4,8}}
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) {{1,3,5},{2,6,7},{4,8}}

    A partition of a set is a collection of non-empty, pairwise disjoint blocks whose union is the whole set. In (2) the blocks {1,3,5}, {2,6,7}, {4,8} share no element and together contain 1 to 8, so it is a partition. (1) has 1 in two blocks; (3) lists the block {2,6} twice, so its listed blocks are not pairwise disjoint (read strictly as a set, the repeated block would collapse into one, but the examiner treats the repetition as overlapping blocks); (4) leaves out 3 and 7.

  2. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (CS & IT) · Q3 · Discrete Mathematics · Sets, Relations & Functions

    Let A = {0,1} × {0,1} and B = {a, b, c}. Suppose A is listed in lexicographic order based on 0 < 1 and B is in alphabetic order. If A × B × A is listed in lexicographic order, then the next element after ((1,0), c, (1,1)) is ________.

    1. ((1,0), a, (0,0))
    2. ((1,1), c, (0,0))
    3. ((1,1) a, (0,0))
    4. ((1,1), a, (1,1))
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) ((1,1) a, (0,0))

    A in lexicographic order is (0,0), (0,1), (1,0), (1,1). In ((1,0), c, (1,1)) the last component (1,1) is the largest element of A and c is the largest element of B, so both "roll over" to their smallest values (0,0) and a, and the first component moves up from (1,0) to (1,1). The next element is ((1,1), a, (0,0)).

  3. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (CS & IT) · Q4 · Discrete Mathematics · Sets, Relations & Functions

    Let P(A) denote the power set of A. If P(A) ⊆ B then

    1. 2^|A| ≤ |B|
    2. 2^|A| ≥ |B|
    3. 2^|A| ≥ 2^|B|
    4. 2|A| < |B|
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) 2^|A| ≤ |B|

    The power set of A has 2^|A| elements. If P(A) is a subset of B, then B has at least as many elements as P(A), so |P(A)| = 2^|A| ≤ |B|.

  4. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (CS & IT) · Q5 · Discrete Mathematics · Sets, Relations & Functions

    The inverse of the function f(x) = x² − 5 is

    1. f⁻¹(y) = √(y + 5)
    2. f⁻¹(y) = √(y − 5)
    3. f⁻¹(y) = √y − 5
    4. f⁻¹(y) = √y + 5
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) f⁻¹(y) = √(y + 5)

    Put y = x² − 5 and solve for x: x² = y + 5, so x = √(y + 5) (taking the non-negative root, i.e. treating f on x ≥ 0, where it is one-one). Check: f(√(y + 5)) = (y + 5) − 5 = y.

Combinatorics & Probability

  1. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (CS & IT) · Q2 · Discrete Mathematics · Combinatorics & Probability

    Let S = {1,2,..., n}. Let S₁ be the set of all subsets of S that contain 1. Let T₁ denote the set of all subsets of S that don't contain 1. Which of the following statement is correct?

    1. |T₁| = |S₁| = 2⁽ⁿ⁻¹⁾
    2. |T₁| ≠ |S₁|
    3. |T₁| + |S₁| = 2⁽ⁿ⁻¹⁾
    4. |T₁| + |S₁| = 2ⁿ
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) |T₁| = |S₁| = 2⁽ⁿ⁻¹⁾ / (4) |T₁| + |S₁| = 2ⁿ

    A subset of S that contains 1 is fixed by choosing any subset of the other n − 1 elements, so |S₁| = 2ⁿ⁻¹; likewise a subset without 1 is any subset of {2,...,n}, so |T₁| = 2ⁿ⁻¹. Hence statement (1) is true, and since every subset of S falls in exactly one of the two classes, |T₁| + |S₁| = 2ⁿ⁻¹ + 2ⁿ⁻¹ = 2ⁿ, so statement (4) is also true. Because both are correct, TSPSC accepted both (1) and (4); (2) and (3) are false.

Lattices & Algebraic Structures

  1. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (CS & IT) · Q6 · Discrete Mathematics · Lattices & Algebraic Structures

    Let D₃₀ = {1,2,3,5,6,10,15,30} and let the relation / (divides) be a partial order relation on D₃₀. Which of the following statements is correct?

    1. All the lower bounds of 10 and 15 are 1,2,3,5,6
    2. All the lower bounds of 10 and 15 are 1,2,3,5
    3. All the lower bounds of 10 and 15 are 1,5
    4. All the lower bounds of 10 and 15 are 2,3,5
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) All the lower bounds of 10 and 15 are 1,5

    Under divisibility, a lower bound of 10 and 15 is an element of D₃₀ that divides both 10 and 15, i.e. a common divisor. The divisors of 10 are 1, 2, 5, 10 and of 15 are 1, 3, 5, 15, so the common ones are 1 and 5 (the greatest lower bound is 5 = gcd(10, 15)). 2, 3 and 6 each fail to divide one of the two numbers.

  2. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (CS & IT) · Q7 · Discrete Mathematics · Lattices & Algebraic Structures

    Let P(S) be the power set of S = {1,2,3}, and then define a lattice L under partial ordering of set inclusion induced on P(S). Which of the following sets are the sub-lattices of L. A = {∅, {1,2}, {2,3}, {1,2,3}} B = {∅, {1},{1,2}, {1,2,3}} C = {∅, {3}, {1,3}, {1,2,3}} D = {{1}, {3}, {1,3}, {1,2,3}} E = {∅,{3}, {1,2}, {1,2,3}}

    1. A and D are sub-lattices of L
    2. B and C are sub-lattices of L
    3. A is a sub-lattice of L
    4. D is a sub-lattice of L
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) B and C are sub-lattices of L

    In L the join is ∪ and the meet is ∩, and a sub-lattice must be closed under both. B and C are chains (∅ ⊆ {1} ⊆ {1,2} ⊆ {1,2,3} and ∅ ⊆ {3} ⊆ {1,3} ⊆ {1,2,3}), and a chain is always closed under ∪ and ∩, so both are sub-lattices. A is not, because {1,2} ∩ {2,3} = {2} ∉ A, and D is not, because {1} ∩ {3} = ∅ ∉ D. (E is also closed under ∪ and ∩, but no option mentions it.)

  3. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (CS & IT) · Q10 · Discrete Mathematics · Lattices & Algebraic Structures

    A group has 11 elements. The number of proper sub-groups it can have is

    1. 0
    2. 4
    3. 5
    4. 8
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) 0

    By Lagrange's theorem the order of a subgroup divides the order of the group. Since 11 is prime, the only subgroups are {e} (order 1) and the whole group (order 11), which are the trivial (improper) subgroups, so the group has no proper (non-trivial) subgroup. (Under the convention that {e} counts as proper there would be exactly one, which is not offered.)
