Fluid Mechanics

  1. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q46 · Fluid Mechanics & Hydraulics · Fluid Mechanics

    The dimensions for Kinematic Viscosity is

    1. FL⁻²T
    2. ML⁻¹T⁻¹
    3. L²T⁻²
    4. L²T⁻¹
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) L²T⁻¹

    Kinematic viscosity ν = μ/ρ, with units m²/s, so its dimensions are L²T⁻¹. FL⁻²T and ML⁻¹T⁻¹ are the dimensions of dynamic viscosity μ.

  2. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q47 · Fluid Mechanics & Hydraulics · Fluid Mechanics

    The stream function for a potential flow field is given by ψ=x²-y², the corresponding potential function (Φ), assuming zero potential at the origin is

    1. x²+y²
    2. 2xy
    3. x²-y²
    4. x-y
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) 2xy

    From ψ = x² − y², u = ∂ψ/∂y = −2y and v = −∂ψ/∂x = −2x. With u = −∂Φ/∂x and v = −∂Φ/∂y this gives Φ = 2xy (zero at the origin); Φ and ψ are harmonic conjugates, and the opposite sign convention only changes the sign to −2xy.

  3. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q48 · Fluid Mechanics & Hydraulics · Fluid Mechanics

    Water flows through a large size pipe. The stagnation pressure and static pressures measured by a pitot tube are 0.3m and 0.24m of water. The velocity of flow in, m/minute is

    1. 1.08
    2. 65.00
    3. 10.8
    4. 0.65
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) 65.00

    Dynamic head = 0.30 − 0.24 = 0.06 m, so V = √(2gh) = √(2 × 9.81 × 0.06) = 1.085 m/s. In m/minute, V = 1.085 × 60 ≈ 65 m/minute.

  4. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q62 · Fluid Mechanics & Hydraulics · Fluid Mechanics

    Poise is the CGS unit of

    1. Kinematic viscosity
    2. Dynamic viscosity
    3. Mass Density
    4. Weight Density
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Dynamic viscosity

    1 poise = 1 dyne-s/cm² = 0.1 N-s/m², a unit of dynamic viscosity; the CGS unit of kinematic viscosity is the stokes (cm²/s).

  5. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q63 · Fluid Mechanics & Hydraulics · Fluid Mechanics

    The velocity gradient is 1000/s. The viscosity is 1.2×10⁻⁴ N-s/m². The shear stress is

    1. 0.12N/m²
    2. 1.2×10⁻⁷ N/m²
    3. 12N/m²
    4. 12×10⁻⁵ N/m²
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) 0.12N/m²

    By Newton's law of viscosity τ = μ(du/dy) = 1.2×10⁻⁴ × 1000 = 0.12 N/m².

  6. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q65 · Fluid Mechanics & Hydraulics · Fluid Mechanics

    If ψ = 3x²y - y³, the values of u and v are

    1. 6xy, 3x² - 3y²
    2. 3x² - 3y², 6xy
    3. (3x² - 3y²), -6xy
    4. 3y² - 3x², 6xy
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) (3x² - 3y²), -6xy

    With the convention u = ∂ψ/∂y and v = −∂ψ/∂x: u = 3x² − 3y² and v = −6xy. Some Indian textbooks use the opposite sign convention (u = −∂ψ/∂y, v = ∂ψ/∂x), which gives option (4), but the official key follows the first.

  7. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q66 · Fluid Mechanics & Hydraulics · Fluid Mechanics

    In a three dimensional motion of a fluid, the component of rotation about the x-axis, ω_x is

    1. (1/2)(∂w/∂y - ∂v/∂z)
    2. (1/2)(∂u/∂z - ∂w/∂y)
    3. (1/2)(∂v/∂x - ∂u/∂y)
    4. (1/2)(∂v/∂x - ∂w/∂y)
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) (1/2)(∂w/∂y - ∂v/∂z)

    Rotation about the x-axis involves only the velocities in the y-z plane: ω_x = ½(∂w/∂y − ∂v/∂z); option (3) is ω_z.

  8. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q67 · Fluid Mechanics & Hydraulics · Fluid Mechanics

    Existence of velocity potential implies that

    1. Fluid is in continuum
    2. Fluid is irrotational
    3. Fluid is ideal
    4. Fluid is compressible
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Fluid is irrotational

    If u = −∂φ/∂x, v = −∂φ/∂y, w = −∂φ/∂z, every rotation component such as ½(∂v/∂x − ∂u/∂y) becomes zero, so a velocity potential can exist only for irrotational flow.

  9. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q68 · Fluid Mechanics & Hydraulics · Fluid Mechanics

    Piezometric head is the summation of

    1. Velocity head and elevation head
    2. Velocity head and pressure head
    3. Pressure head and elevation head
    4. Total head
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Pressure head and elevation head

    Piezometric head = p/γ + z (pressure head + elevation head), the level to which water rises in a piezometer; adding the velocity head gives the total head.

  10. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q69 · Fluid Mechanics & Hydraulics · Fluid Mechanics

    The Bernoulli's equation is written with usual notation as p/γ + v²/2g + z = Constant. In this equation each of the terms represents

    1. Energy in kg.m/kg mass of fluid
    2. Energy in Nm/kg mass of fluid
    3. Energy in Nm/N weight of fluid
    4. Energy in KW/kg mass of fluid
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Energy in Nm/N weight of fluid

    In this form every term has the unit of length (m), i.e. energy per unit weight (N·m/N), which is why the terms are called heads.

  11. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q71 · Fluid Mechanics & Hydraulics · Fluid Mechanics

    Reynolds number of a flow is the ratio of

    1. Gravity forces to viscous forces
    2. Gravity forces to pressure forces
    3. Inertia forces to viscous forces
    4. Viscous forces to pressure forces
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Inertia forces to viscous forces

    Re = ρVD/μ = inertia force / viscous force; the ratio involving gravity (inertia to gravity) is the Froude number.

  12. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q72 · Fluid Mechanics & Hydraulics · Fluid Mechanics

    Ratio of the average velocity to maximum velocity for steady laminar flow in circular pipe is

    1. 1/2
    2. 2/3
    3. 3/2
    4. 2
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) 1/2

    For laminar (Hagen-Poiseuille) flow the velocity profile is parabolic, u = u_max(1 − r²/R²), whose mean over the circular section is u_max/2.

  13. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q73 · Fluid Mechanics & Hydraulics · Fluid Mechanics

    Difference in elevation between TEL and HGL of pipe flow at a point is equal to

    1. Datum head
    2. Velocity head
    3. Pressure head
    4. Piezometric head
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Velocity head

    The HGL is at p/γ + z and the TEL at p/γ + z + V²/2g, so the TEL lies above the HGL by the velocity head V²/2g.

  14. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q74 · Fluid Mechanics & Hydraulics · Fluid Mechanics

    Boundary layer separation takes place when

    1. ∂p/∂x>0
    2. ∂v/∂y≤0
    3. ∂p/∂x>0 & ∂v/∂y≤0
    4. ∂p/∂x<0
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) ∂p/∂x>0 & ∂v/∂y≤0

    Separation needs an adverse pressure gradient (∂p/∂x > 0) that slows the flow near the wall until the velocity gradient at the wall becomes zero or negative; a favourable gradient (∂p/∂x < 0) prevents separation, and an adverse gradient alone does not guarantee it.

Hydraulics & Hydraulic Machines

  1. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q49 · Fluid Mechanics & Hydraulics · Hydraulics & Hydraulic Machines

    A rectangular channel has a width of 1.8m and carries a discharge of 1.8m³/sec at a depth of 0.20m. The specific energy is

    1. 1.03m
    2. 1.47m
    3. 1.87m
    4. 2.0m
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) 1.47m

    Velocity V = Q/(by) = 1.8/(1.8 × 0.20) = 5 m/s, so velocity head = 5²/(2 × 9.81) = 1.274 m. Specific energy E = y + V²/2g = 0.20 + 1.274 = 1.47 m.

  2. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q70 · Fluid Mechanics & Hydraulics · Hydraulics & Hydraulic Machines

    A 50mm diameter jet having a velocity of 25m/s strikes a flat stationary plate, the normal of which is inclined at 60° to the axis of the jet. The normal force exerted on the plate in Newtons is

    1. 460N
    2. 360 N
    3. 640 N
    4. 630 N
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) 460N

    With the plate normal at 60° to the jet, F_n = ρaV² cos 60° = 1000 × (π/4)(0.05)² × 25² × 0.5 ≈ 614 N (reading 60° as the angle of the plate itself gives ρaV² sin 60° ≈ 1063 N instead). The official key gives 460 N, which neither standard working reproduces, so treat this key with caution. (Official final key; some sources differ.)

  3. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q75 · Fluid Mechanics & Hydraulics · Hydraulics & Hydraulic Machines

    For a uniform flow with a depth of 0.6m and Froude number of 2.0 in a rectangular channel, the specific energy will be

    1. 2.4m
    2. 0.8m
    3. 2.6m
    4. 1.8m
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) 1.8m

    Fr² = V²/(gy) = 4, so V²/2g = 2y = 1.2 m and E = y + V²/2g = 0.6 + 1.2 = 1.8 m.

  4. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q76 · Fluid Mechanics & Hydraulics · Hydraulics & Hydraulic Machines

    A water turbine of expected efficiency 85% operates under 36m at a flow rate of 10m³/s. The unit weight of water is 10kN/m³. The nearest shaft power in MW is

    1. 3.06
    2. 3060
    3. 3600
    4. 850
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) 3.06

    Shaft power = ηγQH = 0.85 × 10 kN/m³ × 10 m³/s × 36 m = 3060 kW = 3.06 MW (3060 is the answer in kW, not MW).
