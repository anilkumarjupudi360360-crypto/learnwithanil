Highway Engineering

  1. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q10 · Transportation Engineering · Highway Engineering

    In functional classification of highways, which one of the following highway type have highest mobility and less accessibility

    1. National Highways
    2. State Highways
    3. Major District roads
    4. Street and Village Roads
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) National Highways

    In the functional hierarchy, arterial roads such as National Highways are meant for long-distance through traffic, so they give the highest mobility with restricted access. Mobility falls and accessibility rises down the hierarchy, with village roads and local streets giving the most access.

  2. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q12 · Transportation Engineering · Highway Engineering

    For the calculation of stopping distance, the longitudinal friction co-efficient values of ______ have been recommended by Indian Roads Congress.

    1. 0.35 to 0.40
    2. 0.15 to 0.20
    3. 0.40 to 0.45
    4. 0.45 to 0.50
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) 0.35 to 0.40

    IRC recommends a longitudinal friction coefficient ranging from 0.40 at low speeds down to 0.35 at high speeds for calculating braking distance in SSD. Values of 0.15 to 0.20 are of the order used for lateral friction on curves, not braking.

  3. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q14 · Transportation Engineering · Highway Engineering

    Calculate the value of lag distance in SSD for a highway with a design speed of 65 kmph?

    1. 45.18 m
    2. 36.14 m
    3. 32.50 m
    4. 451.80 m
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) 45.18 m

    Lag distance = 0.278 V t, with the IRC reaction time t = 2.5 s. So lag distance = 0.278 × 65 × 2.5 = 45.18 m.

  4. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q15 · Transportation Engineering · Highway Engineering

    The psychological widening of pavement is calculated using which one of the following formula (take V = speed of vehicle in kmph; R = radius of the curve in m)

    1. V²/(9.5√R)
    2. V/(9.5√R)
    3. V/(9.5R)
    4. V³/(9.5R²)
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) V/(9.5√R)

    IRC gives psychological (extra) widening on horizontal curves as W_ps = V/(9.5√R) m. It is added to the mechanical widening nl²/(2R) to get the total widening.

  5. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q16 · Transportation Engineering · Highway Engineering

    Find out the rate of change of centrifugal acceleration for a design speed of 75 kmph, using IRC recommended formula.

    1. 0.63 m/sec³
    2. 0.53 m/sec³
    3. 0.48 m/sec³
    4. 0.73 m/sec³
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) 0.53 m/sec³

    IRC formula: C = 80/(75 + V) = 80/(75 + 75) = 0.53 m/sec³. This lies within the IRC limits of 0.5 to 0.8 m/sec³, so it is adopted.

  6. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q18 · Transportation Engineering · Highway Engineering

    In a flexible pavement, the different materials with the CBR values are available as follows: 80%, 60%, 15% and 4%. Indicate the order (top to bottom) in which the materials are to be placed for making a good pavement

    1. 4%, 15%, 60%, 80%
    2. 4%, 60%, 80%, 15%
    3. 80%, 60%, 15%, 4%.
    4. 4%, 80%, 15%, 60%
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) 80%, 60%, 15%, 4%.

    In a flexible pavement the wheel stresses are highest at the surface and spread out with depth, so the strongest material (highest CBR) goes on top and weaker layers below. Hence 80% (base), 60%, 15% (sub-base) and 4% (subgrade) from top to bottom.

  7. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q19 · Transportation Engineering · Highway Engineering

    Calculate the Equivalent Axle load factor (EALF) for single axle load of 10 tons using fourth power formula.

    1. 2.26
    2. 22.60
    3. 1.23
    4. 11.23
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) 2.26

    By the fourth power rule, EALF = (axle load/standard axle load)⁴. With the standard single axle of 8.16 t (80 kN), EALF = (10/8.16)⁴ = 1.2255⁴ ≈ 2.26.

  8. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q20 · Transportation Engineering · Highway Engineering

    The hardness and toughness properties of a road aggregate will be obtained from

    1. Aggregate Impact Test
    2. Aggregate crushing test
    3. Los Angeles Abrasion Test
    4. Aggregate Shape test
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Los Angeles Abrasion Test

    In the Los Angeles abrasion test the aggregate is tumbled with steel balls in a rotating drum, so it is subjected to abrasion (testing hardness) and impact (testing toughness) at the same time. The impact test measures toughness only and the crushing test measures strength.

  9. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q21 · Transportation Engineering · Highway Engineering

    In the rigid pavement fatigue analysis, the ratio of flexural stress due to load and the flexural strength due to concrete is less than 0.45 indicates

    1. the allowable number of repetitions of the axle loads is infinity
    2. the allowable number of repetitions of the axle loads is zero
    3. the allowable number of repetitions of the axle loads is 4500
    4. the allowable number of repetitions of the axle loads is 45000
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) the allowable number of repetitions of the axle loads is infinity

    In IRC:58 fatigue analysis, when the stress ratio (flexural stress/modulus of rupture) is below 0.45 the concrete can withstand an unlimited number of load repetitions. Such axle loads therefore consume no fatigue life.

Traffic Engineering

  1. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q11 · Transportation Engineering · Traffic Engineering

    Webster's equation for computing saturation flow rate in signal design (S = saturation flow rate PCU/hour; W = carriage way width in meters.)

    1. S = 225 W PCU/hour
    2. S = 525 W PCU/hour
    3. S = 550 W PCU/hour
    4. S = 250 W PCU/hour
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) S = 525 W PCU/hour

    For an approach width of about 5.5 m to 18 m, Webster's method takes the saturation flow as S = 525 W PCU per hour of green, where W is the approach width in metres.

  2. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q13 · Transportation Engineering · Traffic Engineering

    In total reaction time of the driver, the time required for the sensations received by the eyes/ears to be transmitted to the brain through the nervous system and spinal cord is called ______.

    1. Intellection time
    2. Emotion time
    3. Volition time
    4. Perception time
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) Perception time

    In the PIEV theory, perception time is the time taken for the sensations received by the eyes or ears to be carried to the brain through the nervous system and spinal cord. Intellection is the time to understand the situation, emotion the time spent on fear, anger, etc., and volition the time to decide and act.

  3. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q17 · Transportation Engineering · Traffic Engineering

    Estimate the theoretical capacity of a traffic lane with traffic flow stream speed of 50 kmph (take average centre to centre spacing of vehicles as 10 m)

    1. 500 vehicles /hour/lane
    2. 5000 vehicles /hour/lane
    3. 3000 vehicles /hour/lane
    4. 300 vehicles /hour/lane
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) 5000 vehicles /hour/lane

    Theoretical capacity C = 1000 V/S, with V in kmph and spacing S in m. C = 1000 × 50/10 = 5000 vehicles/hour/lane.

  2. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q23 · Transportation Engineering · Railways & Airports

    For airports serving big aircrafts, ICAO recommends the cross wind component should not exceed ______.

    1. 25 kmph
    2. 35 kmph
    3. 15 kmph
    4. 23 kmph
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) 35 kmph

    ICAO limits the crosswind component to about 37 km/h (20 knots) for large aircraft that need long runways (reference field length of 1500 m or more), which Indian textbooks quote as 35 kmph. Smaller aircraft are allowed lower crosswind limits.

  3. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q24 · Transportation Engineering · Railways & Airports

    The monthly mean of average daily temperature for the hottest month of the year is 30°C and the monthly mean of the maximum daily temperature for the same month of the year is 45°C, Find out the airport reference temperature

    1. 25°C
    2. 35°C
    3. 40°C
    4. 15°C
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) 35°C

    Airport reference temperature = Ta + (Tm − Ta)/3, where Ta is the mean of average daily temperature and Tm the mean of maximum daily temperature of the hottest month. ART = 30 + (45 − 30)/3 = 35°C.

  4. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q25 · Transportation Engineering · Railways & Airports

    The minimum value of Composite Sleeper Index (CSI) prescribed on Indian railways for track sleeper is

    1. 783
    2. 1352
    3. 1455
    4. 873
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) 783

    Indian Railways prescribe a minimum composite sleeper index of 783 for timber track sleepers. The higher values 1352 and 1455 are the minimums for crossing sleepers and bridge sleepers respectively.

  5. Manager 2015 Paper 2 (Civil) · Q26 · Transportation Engineering · Railways & Airports

    The formula used for calculation of superelevation on railways is ______ (take G = gauge in meters, V = Speed in kmph; R = radius of curve in meters)

    1. GV²/(127R) m
    2. GV²/(127R) cm
    3. GV³/(127R) m
    4. GV²/(225R) m
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) GV²/(127R) m

    For equilibrium on a curve e/G = v²/(gR); with v = V/3.6 m/s and g = 9.81 m/s² this gives e = GV²/(127R), since 3.6² × 9.81 ≈ 127. With G and R in metres the superelevation e also comes out in metres, so option (2) has the wrong unit.
