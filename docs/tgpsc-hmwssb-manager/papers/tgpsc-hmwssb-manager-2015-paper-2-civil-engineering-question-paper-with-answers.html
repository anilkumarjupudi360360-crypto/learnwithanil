  1. Q1 · Surveying & GIS · Surveying

    If θ be the angle of slope and the length of chain is 30m, then the correction to be applied per chain length is

    1. 30 (1 - sec θ) m
    2. 30 (1 - cos θ) m
    3. 30 (sec θ - 1) m
    4. 30 (cos θ - 1) m
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) 30 (sec θ - 1) m

    When chaining down a slope, a 30 m chain laid along the ground covers only 30 cos θ horizontally, so the arrow is set forward by the hypotenusal allowance 30(sec θ − 1) m in each chain length to make the horizontal distance a full 30 m. Option (2), 30(1 − cos θ), is instead the slope correction subtracted from a length already measured along the slope.

  2. Q2 · Surveying & GIS · Surveying

    Which one of the following angles can be set out with the help of French cross staff?

    1. 180°
    2. 90°
    3. 45°
    4. any angle
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) 45°

    A French cross staff is an octagonal tube with sighting slits on all eight faces, so unlike the ordinary open cross staff (which gives only right angles) it can also set out 45° angles. It cannot set out any arbitrary angle; that needs an adjustable cross staff.

  3. Q4 · Surveying & GIS · Surveying

    The line Joining points of equal dip is called

    1. Isoclinic lines
    2. Aclinic lines
    3. Isogonic lines
    4. Agonic lines
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) Isoclinic lines

    Isoclinic lines join places of equal magnetic dip, and the aclinic line joins places of zero dip. Isogonic and agonic lines refer to declination (equal and zero declination respectively), not dip.

  4. Q5 · Surveying & GIS · Surveying

    If the forebearing of a line AB is 50° and that of line BC is 20°, then the included angle between the lines is

    1. 70°
    2. 120°
    3. 220°
    4. 150°
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) 150°

    Back bearing of AB = 50° + 180° = 230°, so the included angle at B = FB of BC − BB of AB = 20° − 230° + 360° = 150°. Equivalently, the line deflects 50° − 20° = 30° to the left at B, and 180° − 30° = 150°.

  5. Q6 · Surveying & GIS · Surveying

    When contours of different elevations cross each other, it indicates

    1. Level surface
    2. Overhanging cliff
    3. Saddle
    4. Vertical cliff
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Overhanging cliff

    Contours of different elevations can intersect only where the ground overhangs, as in an overhanging cliff or cave. For a vertical cliff the contours merge into one line without crossing, and a saddle shows as two sets of closed contours between two summits.

  6. Q7 · Surveying & GIS · Surveying

    In theodolite survey, the telescope is said to be inverted, if the

    1. Vertical circle is to the right of the observer and the bubble of the telescope is up
    2. Vertical circle is to the right of the observer and the bubble of the telescope is down
    3. Vertical circle is to the left of the observer and the bubble of the telescope is up
    4. Vertical circle is to the left of the observer and the bubble of the telescope is down
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Vertical circle is to the right of the observer and the bubble of the telescope is down

    In the normal (face left) position the vertical circle is on the observer's left and the telescope bubble is up. After transiting, the telescope is inverted (face right): the vertical circle comes to the observer's right and the bubble is down.

    1. National Highways
    2. State Highways
    3. Major District roads
    4. Street and Village Roads
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) National Highways

    In the functional hierarchy, arterial roads such as National Highways are meant for long-distance through traffic, so they give the highest mobility with restricted access. Mobility falls and accessibility rises down the hierarchy, with village roads and local streets giving the most access.
