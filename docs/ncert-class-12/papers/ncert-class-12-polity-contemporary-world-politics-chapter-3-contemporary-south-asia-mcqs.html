  1. Q1 · Political Science (Polity) · Contemporary World Politics

    Which of the following countries is usually included in "South Asia", as described in the chapter?

    1. Myanmar
    2. Thailand
    3. Iran
    4. Bhutan
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) Bhutan

    South Asia usually means Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, the Maldives, Nepal, Pakistan and Sri Lanka. Afghanistan is sometimes included and is a member of SAARC.

  2. Q2 · Political Science (Polity) · Contemporary World Politics

    Which of the following military rulers took power in Pakistan through a coup in 1999?

    1. Ayub Khan
    2. Pervez Musharraf
    3. Yahya Khan
    4. Zia-ul-Haq
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Pervez Musharraf

    Pakistan has seen several military rulers: Ayub Khan, Yahya Khan, Zia-ul-Haq and Pervez Musharraf, who overthrew Nawaz Sharif's elected government in 1999.

  3. Q3 · Political Science (Polity) · Contemporary World Politics

    Which of the following is mentioned in the chapter as a reason why democracy has not been stable in Pakistan?

    1. The dominance of the army, religious leaders and landowning aristocracy, and frequent conflict with India
    2. The absence of any political parties
    3. The country's small size
    4. A ban on elections in the constitution
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) The dominance of the army, religious leaders and landowning aristocracy, and frequent conflict with India

    The social dominance of the military, clergy and landlords, along with tensions with India and foreign support for military regimes, weakened democratic institutions.

  4. Q4 · Political Science (Polity) · Contemporary World Politics

    Who led the struggle for the independence of Bangladesh and became its first leader?

    1. Ziaur Rahman
    2. Khaleda Zia
    3. Sheikh Mujib-ur Rahman
    4. H.M. Ershad
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Sheikh Mujib-ur Rahman

    Sheikh Mujib-ur Rahman led the Awami League. After independence in 1971, he became Prime Minister and later President; he was assassinated in a military coup in 1975.

  5. Q5 · Political Science (Polity) · Contemporary World Politics

    Nepal became a democratic republic, ending its monarchy, in:

    1. 2008
    2. 1990
    3. 1999
    4. 2015
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) 2008

    After a people's movement in 2006, the monarchy was abolished in 2008. Nepal adopted a new constitution in 2015.

  6. Q6 · Political Science (Polity) · Contemporary World Politics

    The ethnic conflict in Sri Lanka involved a militant organisation fighting for a separate state for the Tamils. It was called:

    1. Awami League
    2. Mukti Bahini
    3. Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam (LTTE)
    4. Maoists
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam (LTTE)

    The LTTE fought for "Tamil Eelam" in the north and east of Sri Lanka. The Sri Lankan army defeated it in 2009.

  7. Q7 · Political Science (Polity) · Contemporary World Politics

    The Indian Peace Keeping Force (IPKF) was sent to Sri Lanka in 1987 under an agreement between India and Sri Lanka. What happened to it?

    1. It stayed permanently
    2. It got involved in fighting with the LTTE, became unpopular, and was withdrawn in 1989–90 without achieving its aim
    3. It took control of Sri Lanka
    4. It was never deployed
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) It got involved in fighting with the LTTE, became unpopular, and was withdrawn in 1989–90 without achieving its aim

    The IPKF was meant to help implement the accord, but it ended up fighting the LTTE. Many Sri Lankans saw it as interference, and it was withdrawn.

  8. Q8 · Political Science (Polity) · Contemporary World Politics

    The Maldives became a republic in:

    1. 1947
    2. 1965
    3. 2005
    4. 1968
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) 1968

    The Maldives was a sultanate until 1968, when it became a republic with a presidential system. Multi-party democracy was introduced in 2005.

  9. Q9 · Political Science (Polity) · Contemporary World Politics

    The South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation (SAARC) was set up in:

    1. 1975
    2. 1985
    3. 1995
    4. 2005
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) 1985

    SAARC was founded in Dhaka in 1985 to promote cooperation among South Asian countries. Its secretariat is in Kathmandu.

  10. Q10 · Political Science (Polity) · Contemporary World Politics

    The South Asian Free Trade Area (SAFTA) agreement was signed in 2004. Its aim is to:

    1. Create a common army
    2. Abolish national currencies
    3. Create a single parliament
    4. Lower trade tariffs among the SAARC countries
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) Lower trade tariffs among the SAARC countries

    SAFTA came into effect in 2006. Some smaller neighbours have feared that it would let Indian businesses dominate their markets.
