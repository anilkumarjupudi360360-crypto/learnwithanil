  1. Q1 · Political Science (Polity) · Contemporary World Politics

    Globalisation, as defined in the chapter, is fundamentally about:

    1. Flows of ideas, capital, commodities and people across different parts of the world
    2. Only the movement of goods
    3. Only the movement of armies
    4. The creation of a world government
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) Flows of ideas, capital, commodities and people across different parts of the world

    Globalisation involves many kinds of flows that create worldwide interconnectedness. It has political, economic and cultural dimensions.

  2. Q2 · Political Science (Polity) · Contemporary World Politics

    Which of the following is the most important cause of globalisation, according to the chapter?

    1. The end of all wars
    2. The growth of agriculture
    3. Technology, especially advances in telegraph, telephone, the internet and transport
    4. Population decline
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Technology, especially advances in telegraph, telephone, the internet and transport

    Technology has made it possible to move ideas, capital, goods and people faster and more cheaply than ever before.

  3. Q3 · Political Science (Polity) · Contemporary World Politics

    Which statement about the movement of people in a globalised world is correct, according to the chapter?

    1. People move across borders as freely as capital and goods
    2. Developed countries have guarded their borders carefully, so the movement of people is much more restricted than that of capital and goods
    3. Migration has stopped completely
    4. Only developing countries restrict migration
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Developed countries have guarded their borders carefully, so the movement of people is much more restricted than that of capital and goods

    Visa and immigration rules limit the movement of people. The chapter notes this uneven nature of globalisation.

  4. Q4 · Political Science (Polity) · Contemporary World Politics

    How has globalisation affected the capacity of the state, according to the chapter?

    1. It has abolished states
    2. It has only increased the state's power in every respect
    3. It has had no effect
    4. It has led to a decline in the welfare functions of some states, but the state remains important and technology has also increased its capacity
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) It has led to a decline in the welfare functions of some states, but the state remains important and technology has also increased its capacity

    States have withdrawn from some welfare roles and the market has become more important, but states still maintain law and order and national security, and have new tools to gather information.

  5. Q5 · Political Science (Polity) · Contemporary World Politics

    The term "McDonaldisation" in the debate on cultural globalisation refers to:

    1. Restaurants opening in every city
    2. The spread of fast food only
    3. Diversity of world cultures
    4. The fear that the spread of Western, especially American, culture will make cultures across the world uniform
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) The fear that the spread of Western, especially American, culture will make cultures across the world uniform

    Critics fear cultural homogenisation, where the dominant culture of powerful countries spreads everywhere. The chapter also discusses how cultures can mix and enrich each other.

  6. Q6 · Political Science (Polity) · Contemporary World Politics

    The positive cultural effect of globalisation, where outside influences enlarge our choices and mix with local traditions, is called:

    1. Cultural homogenisation
    2. Cultural heterogenisation
    3. Cultural imperialism
    4. Cultural isolation
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Cultural heterogenisation

    The example of blue jeans worn with a khadi kurta shows how cultures combine. Globalisation can broaden cultural choices, not only make them uniform.

  7. Q7 · Political Science (Polity) · Contemporary World Politics

    India began its programme of economic reforms, opening up to globalisation, in:

    1. 1991
    2. 1971
    3. 1985
    4. 2001
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) 1991

    Facing a financial crisis in 1991, India reduced controls, opened up to foreign investment and trade, and gave more room to the private sector.

  8. Q8 · Political Science (Polity) · Contemporary World Politics

    The World Social Forum, a platform for those opposed to neo-liberal globalisation, held its first meeting in 2001 at:

    1. Mumbai
    2. Seattle
    3. Porto Alegre, Brazil
    4. Davos
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Porto Alegre, Brazil

    The WSF brings together human rights activists, environmentalists, labour, youth and women's groups. Its fourth meeting was held in Mumbai in 2004.

  9. Q9 · Political Science (Polity) · Contemporary World Politics

    Large protests against unfair trading practices at a WTO ministerial meeting took place in 1999 in:

    1. Geneva
    2. Seattle
    3. Davos
    4. Doha
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Seattle

    The Seattle protests criticised the power of rich countries in the WTO and the effects of economic globalisation on workers and developing countries.

  10. Q10 · Political Science (Polity) · Contemporary World Politics

    Which of the following is a criticism of economic globalisation discussed in the chapter?

    1. It has increased the welfare spending of every state
    2. It has ended all inequality
    3. It has stopped trade between countries
    4. It has benefited a small section of the population and may increase inequality, while reducing protection for the poor
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) It has benefited a small section of the population and may increase inequality, while reducing protection for the poor

    Critics argue that the gains are unevenly shared. Supporters say it creates growth and opportunities. The chapter asks students to weigh both views.
