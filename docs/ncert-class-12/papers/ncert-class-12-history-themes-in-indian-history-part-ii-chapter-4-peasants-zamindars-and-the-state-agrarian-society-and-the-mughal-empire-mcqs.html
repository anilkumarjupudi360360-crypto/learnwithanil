  1. Q1 · History · Themes in Indian History, Part II

    The Ain-i Akbari, the main source for the agrarian history of the Mughal empire, was written by:

    1. Badauni
    2. Babur
    3. Abul Fazl, Akbar's court historian
    4. Al-Biruni
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Abul Fazl, Akbar's court historian

    It was completed in 1598 and forms the third part of the Akbar Nama. It records arrangements for the empire's administration, revenue and agriculture.

  2. Q2 · History · Themes in Indian History, Part II

    Indo-Persian sources of the Mughal period commonly used which terms for a peasant?

    1. Zamindar and jagirdar
    2. Raiyat (plural riaya), muzarian, kisan or asami
    3. Mansabdar and amil
    4. Patwari and muqaddam
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Raiyat (plural riaya), muzarian, kisan or asami

    Sources of the seventeenth century refer to two kinds of peasants: khud-kashta and pahi-kashta.

  3. Q3 · History · Themes in Indian History, Part II

    "Khud-kashta" peasants were those who:

    1. Were non-resident cultivators who farmed land elsewhere on a contractual basis
    2. Were landless labourers
    3. Were residents of the village in which they held their lands
    4. Were village headmen
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Were residents of the village in which they held their lands

    Pahi-kashta peasants were non-resident cultivators who belonged to some other village. They moved for better terms or because of famine.

  4. Q4 · History · Themes in Indian History, Part II

    Which of the following crops were introduced into India from the New World during the Mughal period?

    1. Wheat and rice
    2. Cotton and sugarcane
    3. Indigo and opium
    4. Maize, tomatoes, potatoes and chillies
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) Maize, tomatoes, potatoes and chillies

    Maize came through Africa and Spain, and by the seventeenth century it was listed among the major crops of western India.

  5. Q5 · History · Themes in Indian History, Part II

    In Mughal sources, "jins-i kamil" (perfect crops) referred to:

    1. Cash crops such as cotton and sugarcane, which brought more revenue to the state
    2. Wheat and barley grown for food
    3. Fruits from Central Asia
    4. Crops grown only in Bengal
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) Cash crops such as cotton and sugarcane, which brought more revenue to the state

    The state encouraged cultivation of such crops. Oilseeds and pulses were also cash crops.

  6. Q6 · History · Themes in Indian History, Part II

    Match the categories of land in the Ain-i Akbari: A. Polaj 1. Land left out of cultivation for five years or more B. Parauti 2. Land annually cultivated, never left fallow C. Chachar 3. Land left fallow for a time to recover its strength D. Banjar 4. Land that had lain fallow for three or four years

    1. A-1, B-2, C-3, D-4
    2. A-3, B-4, C-1, D-2
    3. A-4, B-1, C-2, D-3
    4. A-2, B-3, C-4, D-1
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) A-2, B-3, C-4, D-1

    The classification was used to assess land revenue. Land in each category was further graded into good, middling and bad.

  7. Q7 · History · Themes in Indian History, Part II

    The village panchayat in Mughal times was headed by a person known as:

    1. Patwari
    2. Muqaddam or mandal
    3. Zamindar
    4. Qazi
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Muqaddam or mandal

    The headman supervised the preparation of village accounts with the help of the accountant (patwari). The panchayat dealt with disputes and ensured that caste boundaries were maintained.

  8. Q8 · History · Themes in Indian History, Part II

    In Mughal revenue terms, "jama" and "hasil" referred to:

    1. Jama - land tax on wheat; hasil - tax on rice
    2. Jama - the amount of revenue assessed; hasil - the amount actually collected
    3. Jama - tax on trade; hasil - tax on cattle
    4. Both meant the same thing
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Jama - the amount of revenue assessed; hasil - the amount actually collected

    Akbar's revenue minister Todar Mal carried out careful surveys of crop yields, prices and areas under cultivation over ten years, which formed the basis of the revenue system.

  9. Q9 · History · Themes in Indian History, Part II

    The zamindars of the Mughal period were:

    1. Landless labourers
    2. Officials appointed only for one year
    3. Moneylenders without land
    4. People who held extensive personal lands (milkiyat) and had the right to collect revenue from peasants on behalf of the state
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) People who held extensive personal lands (milkiyat) and had the right to collect revenue from peasants on behalf of the state

    Zamindars also had military resources, such as forts and armed retainers. They were an exploitative class, but their relationship with the peasants also had elements of reciprocity and patronage.

  10. Q10 · History · Themes in Indian History, Part II

    Babur described a device used for irrigation in the northern Indian plains, where a wheel lifted water from a well. This was the:

    1. Persian wheel (saqiya)
    2. Canal lock
    3. Water mill
    4. Shaduf only
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) Persian wheel (saqiya)

    The Mughal state also repaired old canals, such as the Shah Nahr in Punjab, and dug new ones.
