  1. Q1 · History · Themes in Indian History, Part II

    Al-Biruni, the author of Kitab-ul-Hind, was born in 973 in:

    1. Tangier, Morocco
    2. Baghdad
    3. Herat
    4. Khwarizm, in present-day Uzbekistan
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) Khwarizm, in present-day Uzbekistan

    Al-Biruni was well versed in several languages, including Syriac, Arabic, Persian and Hebrew. He came to India after Sultan Mahmud of Ghazni invaded Khwarizm and took scholars to Ghazni.

  2. Q2 · History · Themes in Indian History, Part II

    Al-Biruni's Kitab-ul-Hind was written in:

    1. Persian
    2. Sanskrit
    3. Turkish
    4. Arabic
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) Arabic

    It is divided into 80 chapters on subjects such as religion, philosophy, festivals, astronomy, alchemy, social life, weights and measures, laws and metrology.

  3. Q3 · History · Themes in Indian History, Part II

    Al-Biruni identified several barriers to understanding India. Which of the following was among them? 1. Language, since Sanskrit was very different from Arabic and Persian 2. Differences in religious beliefs and practices 3. The self-absorption and insularity of the local population Select the correct answer using the code given below:

    1. 1 only
    2. 1 and 2 only
    3. 2 and 3 only
    4. 1, 2 and 3
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) 1, 2 and 3

    To overcome these, he relied heavily on Sanskrit texts, especially Brahmanical works like the Vedas, the Puranas, the Bhagavad Gita, the works of Patanjali and the Manusmriti.

  4. Q4 · History · Themes in Indian History, Part II

    In describing the caste system, Al-Biruni:

    1. Accepted the idea of pollution completely
    2. Compared it with the four social categories of ancient Persia, and rejected the notion of pollution as contrary to the laws of nature
    3. Said there was no caste system in India
    4. Praised untouchability
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Compared it with the four social categories of ancient Persia, and rejected the notion of pollution as contrary to the laws of nature

    He tried to explain the caste system by looking for parallels in other societies, showing that social divisions were not unique to India.

  5. Q5 · History · Themes in Indian History, Part II

    Ibn Battuta, whose travel account is called the Rihla, was born in:

    1. Tangier, Morocco
    2. Samarqand
    3. Cairo
    4. Venice
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) Tangier, Morocco

    Born in 1304 into a family of Islamic legal scholars, Ibn Battuta considered travel an important source of knowledge. He reached Sind in 1333.

  6. Q6 · History · Themes in Indian History, Part II

    Which Sultan of Delhi appointed Ibn Battuta as the qazi (judge) of Delhi?

    1. Alauddin Khalji
    2. Iltutmish
    3. Muhammad bin Tughlaq
    4. Firuz Shah Tughlaq
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Muhammad bin Tughlaq

    The Sultan was impressed by his scholarship. In 1342, Ibn Battuta was sent as the Sultan's envoy to the Mongol ruler of China.

  7. Q7 · History · Themes in Indian History, Part II

    Ibn Battuta described two strange things he saw in India in detail. They were:

    1. The coconut and the paan (betel leaf)
    2. The elephant and the tiger
    3. The temple and the mosque
    4. Tea and coffee
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) The coconut and the paan (betel leaf)

    His descriptions of these unfamiliar things to readers in Morocco show how travellers tried to make the unknown understandable.

  8. Q8 · History · Themes in Indian History, Part II

    According to Ibn Battuta, the postal system in India had two kinds. The horse post was called "uluq". The foot post, with stations at every third of a mile, was called:

    1. Barid
    2. Dawa
    3. Yam
    4. Sarai
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Dawa

    The postal system was very efficient. Letters reached their destinations quickly, and even fruit could be sent from Khurasan.

  9. Q9 · History · Themes in Indian History, Part II

    Ibn Battuta wrote that Indian cities were:

    1. Empty and poor
    2. All in ruins
    3. Densely populated and prosperous, full of exciting opportunities for those with the necessary drive and resources
    4. Without markets
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Densely populated and prosperous, full of exciting opportunities for those with the necessary drive and resources

    He described Delhi as a vast city with a great population, and Daulatabad as being no less. Markets were also places of social and economic activity.

  10. Q10 · History · Themes in Indian History, Part II

    Francois Bernier, who was in India from 1656 to 1668, was a:

    1. French physician, political philosopher and historian
    2. Portuguese sailor
    3. Italian Jesuit priest
    4. Dutch trader
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) French physician, political philosopher and historian

    He was closely associated with the Mughal court, first as physician to Prince Dara Shukoh and later as an intellectual and scientist with an Armenian Mughal noble, Danishmand Khan.
