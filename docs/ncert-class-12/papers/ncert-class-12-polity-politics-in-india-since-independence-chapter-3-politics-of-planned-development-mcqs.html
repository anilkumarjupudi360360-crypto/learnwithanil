  1. Q1 · Political Science (Polity) · Politics in India since Independence

    The Planning Commission of India was set up in 1950 by:

    1. An Act of Parliament
    2. A constitutional amendment
    3. A simple resolution of the Government of India
    4. An order of the Supreme Court
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) A simple resolution of the Government of India

    The Planning Commission was not created by the Constitution or a law; it was set up by a government resolution in March 1950, with the Prime Minister as its chairperson.

  2. Q2 · Political Science (Polity) · Politics in India since Independence

    In 1944 a group of big industrialists drew up a plan for setting up a planned economy in India, in which the state would make major investments in industry. This is known as the:

    1. Bombay Plan
    2. Gandhian Plan
    3. People's Plan
    4. Harrod-Domar Plan
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) Bombay Plan

    The Bombay Plan shows that there was broad agreement, even among industrialists, that the state should play a big role in economic development.

  3. Q3 · Political Science (Polity) · Politics in India since Independence

    The First Five Year Plan (1951–1956) gave the highest priority to:

    1. Heavy industries
    2. Space research
    3. Defence production
    4. Agriculture, including dams and irrigation
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) Agriculture, including dams and irrigation

    Drafted under economist K.N. Raj's influence, the First Plan focused on agriculture, which had suffered from Partition, and invested in dams and irrigation such as the Bhakra Nangal project.

  4. Q4 · Political Science (Polity) · Politics in India since Independence

    The Second Five Year Plan, which stressed heavy industries, was drafted under the leadership of:

    1. K.N. Raj
    2. P.C. Mahalanobis
    3. J.C. Kumarappa
    4. Chaudhary Charan Singh
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) P.C. Mahalanobis

    The statistician P.C. Mahalanobis designed the Second Plan (1956–61), which aimed at quick structural change through heavy industry, with the public sector in the lead.

  5. Q5 · Political Science (Polity) · Politics in India since Independence

    Which of the following was a key debate in India's early planning, according to the chapter?

    1. Whether to have elections or not
    2. Agriculture versus industry, and the role of the public versus the private sector
    3. Whether to adopt a monarchy
    4. Whether to abolish the Constitution
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Agriculture versus industry, and the role of the public versus the private sector

    Critics argued that the Second Plan neglected agriculture. There was also a debate about how much of the economy the state should control and how much should be left to private enterprise.

  6. Q6 · Political Science (Polity) · Politics in India since Independence

    A Gandhian economist, J.C. Kumarappa, proposed an alternative model of development that emphasised:

    1. Heavy industries in the public sector
    2. Rapid urbanisation
    3. Foreign investment
    4. Rural industrialisation and a decentralised, village-centred economy
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) Rural industrialisation and a decentralised, village-centred economy

    Kumarappa's ideas, in works like Economy of Permanence, favoured small-scale and village industries, in line with Gandhi's vision of self-reliant villages.

  7. Q7 · Political Science (Polity) · Politics in India since Independence

    Which leader, who left the Congress, argued that planning was urban and industry-centred and neglected agriculture and farmers?

    1. Chaudhary Charan Singh
    2. P.C. Mahalanobis
    3. Jawaharlal Nehru
    4. K.N. Raj
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) Chaudhary Charan Singh

    Charan Singh was a strong voice for the interests of farmers and argued that agriculture should be at the centre of planning.

  8. Q8 · Political Science (Polity) · Politics in India since Independence

    India's economic model in the early decades, with both a public and a private sector, is called:

    1. A capitalist economy
    2. A communist economy
    3. A mixed economy
    4. A barter economy
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) A mixed economy

    India combined features of capitalism and socialism: the private sector worked alongside a large public sector. Critics argued that it combined the disadvantages of both.

  9. Q9 · Political Science (Polity) · Politics in India since Independence

    Which of the following was a criticism of India's development strategy mentioned in the chapter?

    1. It spent too little on industry
    2. It abolished all private property
    3. It gave too much power to state governments
    4. Too many controls and licences hampered the private sector, while the public sector often became inefficient, and the poor did not benefit enough
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) Too many controls and licences hampered the private sector, while the public sector often became inefficient, and the poor did not benefit enough

    The "licence-permit raj" restricted private enterprise; public sector units protected from competition often made losses; and inequalities persisted. These criticisms later led to economic reforms.

  10. Q10 · Political Science (Polity) · Politics in India since Independence

    The abolition of zamindari and land reforms in the early years of independence aimed at:

    1. Ending the system of large landowners who did not cultivate the land, and giving land to cultivators
    2. Increasing the power of landlords
    3. Selling land to foreign companies
    4. Ending agriculture altogether
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) Ending the system of large landowners who did not cultivate the land, and giving land to cultivators

    Zamindari abolition was carried out in most states, but land ceilings and tenancy reforms were less successful because landowners resisted them.
