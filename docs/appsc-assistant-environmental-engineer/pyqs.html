  1. 1 of 10 · APPSC Assistant Environmental Engineer 2025 Paper 2 Common Subject · Q1 · Ecosystems

    Why are ecosystems important?

    Show answer

    Answer: (2) They regulate climate and provide oxygen.

    Ecosystems provide regulating and supporting services: plants release oxygen through photosynthesis, forests and oceans store carbon, and vegetation and water bodies moderate local and global climate. The other options are too narrow or wrong: ecosystems are not a habitat for wildlife only, soil formation also depends on weathering of rocks and climate, and recreation is only a minor cultural service.

  2. 2 of 10 · APPSC Assistant Environmental Engineer 2025 Paper 2 Common Subject · Q3 · Natural Resources

    Which of the following is an indirect utilisation of the forest ecosystem?

    Show answer

    Answer: (4) Paper

    Fruits, wood for farm implements and grass for grazing are taken from the forest and used directly by local people. Paper is an indirect use: wood or bamboo is first converted into pulp in an industry, and only the processed product reaches the user. Hence paper represents indirect utilisation of the forest ecosystem.

  3. 3 of 10 · APPSC Assistant Environmental Engineer 2025 Paper 2 Common Subject · Q2 · Ecosystems

    Which of the following is a part of terrestrial ecosystems?

    Show answer

    Answer: (4) Grasslands

    Terrestrial (land-based) ecosystems include forests, grasslands and deserts. Grasslands are dominated by grasses with few trees and occur on land, so they are terrestrial. Rivers are freshwater (lotic) aquatic ecosystems, estuaries are where rivers meet the sea, and wetlands are waterlogged transitional areas, so all three are treated as aquatic ecosystems.

  4. 4 of 10 · APPSC Assistant Environmental Engineer 2025 Paper 2 Common Subject · Q4 · Ecosystems

    In an ecosystem, some animals feed on other organisms, while others feed on dead organic matter. What is this type of food chain called?

    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Detritus food chain

    A food chain that begins with dead organic matter (detritus such as fallen leaves and dead animals) is called the detritus food chain: detritivores and decomposers like earthworms, bacteria and fungi feed on detritus and are in turn eaten by other animals. The other main type is the grazing food chain, which starts from green plants. Herbivorous, carnivorous and omnivorous food chains are not standard ecological terms.

  5. 5 of 10 · APPSC Assistant Environmental Engineer 2025 Paper 2 Common Subject · Q5 · Ecosystems

    Which of the following best describes a food web?

    Show answer

    Answer: (3) A complex network of interconnected food chains in an ecosystem

    A food web is the network formed when many food chains in an ecosystem interconnect, because most organisms eat, and are eaten by, more than one species. It provides alternative pathways for energy flow and makes the ecosystem more stable. Options 1 and 2 describe a single food chain, and nutrient cycles are biogeochemical cycles, not food webs.

  6. 6 of 10 · APPSC Assistant Environmental Engineer 2025 Paper 2 Common Subject · Q6 · Ecosystems

    Which is the most basic and the first trophic level of an ecological pyramid?

    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Producers

    Producers (autotrophs such as green plants and phytoplankton) make food from sunlight by photosynthesis and form the first trophic level, the base of every ecological pyramid. Primary consumers (herbivores) form the second level, secondary consumers (carnivores) the third, and tertiary consumers the higher levels.

  7. 7 of 10 · APPSC Assistant Environmental Engineer 2025 Paper 2 Common Subject · Q7 · Ecosystems

    In an ecological pyramid, what is the primary direction of energy flow?

    Show answer

    Answer: (2) From producers to consumers

    Energy enters the ecosystem through producers, which fix solar energy, and then passes to herbivores and carnivores. The flow is unidirectional: energy is lost as heat at each step (only about 10% passes on, Lindeman's ten per cent law) and never returns to the producers. So the pyramid of energy is always upright, with energy moving from producers to consumers.

  8. 8 of 10 · APPSC Assistant Environmental Engineer 2025 Paper 2 Common Subject · Q8 · Ecosystems

    Which of the following best describes an ecosystem?

    Show answer

    Answer: (4) Involves interaction between living organisms and the environment

    An ecosystem (a term coined by A.G. Tansley in 1935) is a functional unit in which the living (biotic) community interacts with its non-living (abiotic) environment, with energy flowing and nutrients cycling between them. Option 1 lists only the biotic part, option 2 only the abiotic part and option 3 only climate; none covers the interaction that defines an ecosystem.

  9. 9 of 10 · APPSC Assistant Environmental Engineer 2025 Paper 2 Common Subject · Q9 · Ecosystems

    Which of the following best describes bio-accumulation?

    Show answer

    Answer: (1) The gradual accumulation of pollutants or toxins in the tissues of living organisms over time

    Bioaccumulation is the build-up of a persistent substance such as DDT, mercury or lead in the tissues of an organism over time, because it is taken in faster than it is broken down or excreted. It differs from biomagnification, the rise in concentration from one trophic level to the next. Option 2 describes excretion or detoxification and option 4 describes nutrient flow.

  10. 10 of 10 · APPSC Assistant Environmental Engineer 2025 Paper 2 Common Subject · Q10 · Ecosystems

    What does the term ‘biogeochemical’ signify?

    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Cycling of elements between living and nonliving components

    'Biogeochemical' joins bio (living organisms), geo (rocks, soil, water and air) and chemical (elements). Biogeochemical cycles such as the carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus and water cycles describe how elements move from the non-living environment into organisms and back again. Energy flows one way and is not cycled, so option 2 is wrong; movement of organisms and purely geological processes are not what the term means.
