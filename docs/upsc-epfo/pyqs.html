  1. 1 of 10 · UPSC EPFO APFC EO/AO 2025 · Q1 · General English

    Read the passage given below and answer the question that follows. Africa is on the move. At least 20 million (i) ______ live outside the continent, a three-fold increase since 1990. Europe accounts for about half of African (ii) ______ outside Africa, but its share has steadily declined since 1990. America, China, Saudi Arabia and Turkey have all seen surges in their African-born populations. It may seem otherworldly to predict that this will continue, given the fierce backlash against (iii) ______ across the West. Notwithstanding pushback, African (iv) ______ is an unstoppable force that will long outlast today's populists and help define the 21st century. Demography is one cause. It is well-known that in the developed world the number of people of working age will decline, exacerbating labour shortages. What is less widely appreciated is that emerging countries that are associated with exporting people, such as Mexico and the Philippines, are also getting older and richer — meaning smaller shares of their populations will, in future, wish to emigrate in search of work. In Africa, by contrast, the working-age population is forecast to increase by around 700 million people by 2050. Another cause is economics. Many African countries are entering a phase in their development in which people are still poor enough to wish to leave but, for the first time, rich enough to be able to travel long distances. African countries create only around a fifth of the number of formal jobs required to absorb their expanding workforce. In the above passage there are four blanks numbered (i), (ii), (iii) and (iv). Select the option from those given below in which the most appropriate term has been used for each of the blanks respectively:

    Show answer

    Provisional answer (Learn with Anil; UPSC key not yet out): (2) (i) emigrants; (ii) migrants; (iii) immigration; (iv) migration

    Seen from Africa, people who have left the continent are emigrants, so (i) is "emigrants". (ii) "African migrants outside Africa" is the neutral word for those people living abroad. (iii) The West objects to people coming in, so its backlash is against "immigration". (iv) The overall movement that will "help define the 21st century" is African "migration". Options 1 and 3 wrongly call those who left Africa "immigrants", option 3 also puts the plural "migrants" before the singular verb "is", and option 4's backlash against "migration" misses that the West objects to arrivals.

  2. 2 of 10 · UPSC EPFO PA 2024 · Q1 · General English

    Read the passage given below and answer the question that follows. The responsibility that devolves on the press is very great. The freedom of the press enjoys a sacred privilege, but it must not be exercised to serve communal and sectional interests. Passions and prejudices must not be allowed to dominate this privilege. It should not be abused to foster a partisan spirit and encourage fissiparous tendencies. It should mirror public opinion, but it also should guard public welfare. It must remember to exercise restraint and wisdom while throwing light on contemporary events. Which one of the following statements best reflects the critical message conveyed by the author of the passage?

    Show answer

    Answer: (1) The press must be responsible as it shapes public opinion.

    The passage opens with "The responsibility that devolves on the press is very great" and then lists what that responsibility demands: no communal or partisan use of its freedom, guarding public welfare, and restraint and wisdom in reporting. Because the press mirrors and influences public opinion, it must act responsibly, so (1) captures the central message. (2) is not discussed; (3) ignores the passage's stress on restraint and limits; (4) contradicts the passage, which expects the press to guard public welfare, not merely report events.

  3. 3 of 10 · UPSC EPFO APFC 2023 · Q1 · General English

    Rearrange the parts P, Q, R and S to form a meaningful sentence. P: before the judge Q: given that he was clearly innocent R: was incomprehensible S: his decision to plead guilty

    Show answer

    Answer: (3) QSPR

    The sentence is "Given that he was clearly innocent, his decision to plead guilty before the judge was incomprehensible." Q is an introductory clause ("given that" = considering that) that sets the context, S supplies the subject "his decision to plead guilty", P must follow it directly because one pleads guilty before a judge, and R "was incomprehensible" is the predicate. QPSR wrongly attaches "before the judge" to "innocent" instead of to "plead guilty", while SQRP and SRQP leave "before the judge" stranded at the end, where it attaches to "incomprehensible" or "innocent" and distorts the meaning.

  4. 4 of 10 · UPSC EPFO EO/AO 2023 · Q1 · General English

    The first and sixth sentences of a passage are given as S1 and S6. The four sentences in between have been jumbled up and labelled P, Q, R and S. Choose the proper sequence of the four sentences. S1: Geological mapping, when properly done, demands skill and judgment. S6: Nothing is more naive than to believe that a field geologist should gather only 'facts', the interpretation of which is to be made at a later date. P: While the field work progresses he should conceive as many interpretations as are consistent with the known facts. Q: As the field work progresses and the larger geological picture begins to unfold, experience and judgment are essential if the geologist is to evaluate properly the vast number of facts gathered from thousands of outcrops. R: Such mapping requires keen observation and a knowledge of what data are significant. S: Above all, the field geologist must use the method of "working multiple hypotheses" to deduce the geological structure.

    Show answer

    Answer: (2) RQSP

    The order is RQSP. R must come first because "Such mapping" refers back to the geological mapping of S1 and spells out the skill it demands: keen observation and knowing which data matter. Q moves on to the progress of the field work, when experience and judgment are needed to evaluate the facts. S ("Above all") gives the most important requirement, the method of multiple hypotheses, and P explains it: "he", the field geologist of S, should form many interpretations while the work goes on. That leads into S6, which rejects gathering facts now and interpreting them later. Only RQSP begins with R.

  5. 5 of 10 · UPSC EPFO EO/AO 2021 · Q1 · General English

    Select the most appropriate option for the blank space. It's time you went out and ______ your own living.

    Show answer

    Answer: (2) earned

    The fixed collocation is "earn a living", meaning to get the money you need to live by working: "It's time you went out and earned your own living." The past form "earned" matches "went", because "It's time" is followed by the past tense when it means "you should do this now". "Afford" means to have enough money for something, so "afford a living" makes no sense. "Gain" goes with experience, weight or an advantage, and "win" with prizes, contests or votes; neither is the idiomatic verb with "living" in modern English.

  6. 6 of 10 · UPSC EPFO EO/AO 2017 · Q1 · General English

    Which one of the following is the synonym of the word written in capital letters in the sentence, "The FUNDAMENTAL character of an individual defines his entire life"?

    Show answer

    Answer by Learn with Anil (no official key): (2) Essential

    "Fundamental" means forming the base or core of something: basic and of central importance. So the "fundamental character" of a person is his essential, innermost nature, which shapes his whole life. "Essential" (belonging to the very essence of a thing; basic, indispensable) is therefore the synonym. "Productive" means yielding results or output, "successful" means achieving an aim, and "effective" means producing the intended effect. All three describe results or performance, not the basic nature of something.

  7. 7 of 10 · UPSC EPFO APFC EO/AO 2025 · Q2 · General English

    Read the passage given below and answer the question that follows. Africa is on the move. At least 20 million (i) ______ live outside the continent, a three-fold increase since 1990. Europe accounts for about half of African (ii) ______ outside Africa, but its share has steadily declined since 1990. America, China, Saudi Arabia and Turkey have all seen surges in their African-born populations. It may seem otherworldly to predict that this will continue, given the fierce backlash against (iii) ______ across the West. Notwithstanding pushback, African (iv) ______ is an unstoppable force that will long outlast today's populists and help define the 21st century. Demography is one cause. It is well-known that in the developed world the number of people of working age will decline, exacerbating labour shortages. What is less widely appreciated is that emerging countries that are associated with exporting people, such as Mexico and the Philippines, are also getting older and richer — meaning smaller shares of their populations will, in future, wish to emigrate in search of work. In Africa, by contrast, the working-age population is forecast to increase by around 700 million people by 2050. Another cause is economics. Many African countries are entering a phase in their development in which people are still poor enough to wish to leave but, for the first time, rich enough to be able to travel long distances. African countries create only around a fifth of the number of formal jobs required to absorb their expanding workforce. Which of the following sentences with respect to the passage is/are correct? 1. All rich western nations have been seeing an increase in emigration from Africa over the past few decades. 2. Over the past thirty-five odd years, the number of African origin people living outside Africa has gone up by a factor of three. 3. The fierce resistance to labour incursion into the West will temper emigration from Africa in the 21st century. Select the answer using the codes given below:

    Show answer

    Provisional answer (Learn with Anil; UPSC key not yet out): (4) 2 only

    Only statement 2 is supported. The passage says at least 20 million Africans live outside the continent, "a three-fold increase since 1990", which is about 35 years before 2025, so their number has gone up by a factor of three. Statement 1 overgeneralises: the passage names America, China, Saudi Arabia and Turkey (not all western) and says Europe's share of African migrants has declined; it never says that all rich western nations saw increases. Statement 3 contradicts the passage, which calls African migration "an unstoppable force" notwithstanding the backlash.

  8. 8 of 10 · UPSC EPFO PA 2024 · Q2 · General English

    Consider the following statements: I. He regretted for his mistake. II. We ought to make exercise daily. III. This has been going on for a long time. Which of the statements given above is/are grammatically correct?

    Show answer

    Answer: (4) III only

    Only III is correct: "has been going on for a long time" is the present perfect continuous with "for" plus a period of time. Sentence I is wrong because "regret" is a transitive verb and takes a direct object without "for": "He regretted his mistake" (one "repents of" a mistake, but simply "regrets" it). Sentence II is wrong because the accepted collocation is "take exercise" or "do exercise", not "make exercise": "We ought to take exercise daily."

  9. 9 of 10 · UPSC EPFO APFC 2023 · Q2 · General English

    Rearrange the parts P, Q, R and S to form a meaningful sentence. P: when your stated principles Q: it is difficult R: are at odds with your ground realities S: to practise what you preach

    Show answer

    Answer: (4) QSPR

    The sentence is "It is difficult to practise what you preach when your stated principles are at odds with your ground realities." Q is an anticipatory "it" clause and needs the infinitive S ("to practise what you preach") as its real subject immediately after it; the "when" clause then follows, with P giving its subject and R its verb. PRSQ and SPRQ end with a stranded "it is difficult", and QPRS separates "it is difficult" from the infinitive it introduces. To practise what you preach means to act as you advise others to act.

  10. 10 of 10 · UPSC EPFO EO/AO 2023 · Q2 · General English

    The first and sixth sentences of a passage are given as S1 and S6. The four sentences in between have been jumbled up and labelled P, Q, R and S. Choose the proper sequence of the four sentences. S1: Under the colonial regime, basic infrastructure such as railways, ports, water transport, posts and telegraphs did develop. S6: Naturally, therefore, these areas remained inaccessible to the people. P: There always remained an acute shortage of all-weather roads to reach out to the rural areas during the rainy season. Q: Roads constructed in India prior to the advent of British rule were not fit for modern transport. R: However, the real motive behind this development was not necessarily to provide basic amenities to the people but to subserve various colonial interests. S: The roads that were built primarily served the purposes of mobilizing the army within India and drawing out raw materials from the countryside to the nearest railway station.

    Show answer

    Answer: (4) RQSP

    The order is RQSP. R comes first: "However" contrasts with S1's statement that infrastructure "did develop", and "this development" refers back to it before revealing the real colonial motive. Q and S narrow this down to roads: roads built before British rule were unfit for modern transport, and the roads the British built mainly served army movement and the carrying of raw materials to railway stations. P gives the result, a shortage of all-weather roads to rural areas, and S6's "these areas" means those rural areas, so P must come last. Only RQSP starts with R and ends with P.
