Which one of the following is the figure of speech in the expression "Africa is on the move"?

    1. Personification
    2. Irony
    3. Anthropomorphism
    4. Antithesis
    Show answer

    Provisional answer (Learn with Anil; UPSC key not yet out): (1) Personification

    Personification gives human actions or qualities to something that is not human. Here a continent is said to be "on the move", as if it were a person setting out on a journey, when what is meant is that Africans are migrating. Anthropomorphism is the broader idea of attributing human form or behaviour to animals, gods or objects, usually literally in stories (talking animals), and is not the usual name of this figure of speech. There is no irony (saying the opposite of what is meant) and no antithesis (contrasting ideas in balanced phrases). Strictly, "Africa" for "Africans" is also metonymy, which is not offered.

  6. Q6 · General English · Grammar & Usage

    Select the most appropriate option for the blank space. The evening looked ______ against the stretched out sky.

    1. red, beautiful and soul-quenching
    2. beautiful, red and soul-quenching
    3. soul-quenching, beautiful and red
    4. beautiful, soul-quenching and red
    Show answer

    Provisional answer (Learn with Anil; UPSC key not yet out): (4) beautiful, soul-quenching and red

    Adjectives of opinion come before adjectives of fact such as colour (the usual order is opinion, size, age, shape, colour, origin, material, purpose), so "red" goes last; this rules out options 1 and 2. Between the two opinion adjectives, the general one ("beautiful", which can describe almost anything) comes before the more specific one ("soul-quenching"), as in "a lovely, comfortable chair". Hence: "The evening looked beautiful, soul-quenching and red against the stretched out sky." Option 3 reverses the two opinion adjectives.

  7. Q7 · General English · Grammar & Usage

    Select the most appropriate option for the blank space. Summers ______ be really hot at times, so make sure you stay hydrated.

    1. can
    2. could
    3. might
    4. may
    Show answer

    Provisional answer (Learn with Anil; UPSC key not yet out): (1) can

    "Can" expresses general or theoretical possibility, that is, what is sometimes true of something: "Summers can be really hot at times, so make sure you stay hydrated." The phrase "at times" signals this typical, recurring possibility. "May" and "might" express a possibility about a particular situation (it may be hot tomorrow) and sound odd in a general statement about summers, while "could" suggests a past or more remote, conditional possibility.

  8. Q8 · General English · Grammar & Usage

    Select the most appropriate option for the blank space. I was wondering which festival ______ we celebrate together this year?

    1. will
    2. might
    3. shall
    4. do
    Show answer

    Provisional answer (Learn with Anil; UPSC key not yet out): (3) shall

    "Shall" is used with "I" and "we" in questions that ask for a suggestion or a decision: "Which festival shall we celebrate together this year?" The speaker is wondering what the two of them should choose, not predicting a fact, so "shall" is the most appropriate. "Will we celebrate" asks about a future fact, "might" weakens it into a vague possibility, and "do we celebrate" is the simple present used for habits, which does not fit "this year".

  9. Q9 · General English · Grammar & Usage

    Select the most appropriate option for the blank space. I was ______ exhausted by the end of the week spent in the library doing research work.

    1. exceedingly
    2. inordinately
    3. very
    4. completely
    Show answer

    Provisional answer (Learn with Anil; UPSC key not yet out): (4) completely

    "Exhausted" is an extreme (non-gradable) adjective: it already means "extremely tired". Such adjectives are intensified with "completely", "totally", "utterly" or "absolutely", not with grading adverbs such as "very" or "exceedingly", which go with gradable adjectives like "tired". Hence: "I was completely exhausted by the end of the week spent in the library doing research work." "Inordinately" means excessively, beyond reasonable limits, which does not suit tiredness either.

  10. Q10 · General English · Grammar & Usage

    Select the most appropriate option for the blank space. They ______ be very far away. I saw them a little while ago.

    1. shall not
    2. cannot
    3. should not
    4. ought not
    Show answer

    Provisional answer (Learn with Anil; UPSC key not yet out): (2) cannot

    "Cannot" (can't) expresses a logical deduction that something is impossible: since the speaker saw them a little while ago, they cannot be very far away. The positive deduction would use "must" (They must be nearby). "Should not" and "ought not" express advice, obligation or at most a weaker expectation, and "ought not" would also need "to" (ought not to be). "Shall not" expresses a future intention or prohibition. Only "cannot" conveys the certainty drawn from the evidence in the second sentence.
