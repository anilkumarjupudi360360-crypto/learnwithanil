  4. FRO Screening 2019 · Q7 · General Science & Technology · Physics

    The temperature at which a conductor becomes a super conductor is called:

    Show answer

    Answer: Transition Temperature

    When certain materials are cooled, their electrical resistance suddenly falls to zero at a particular temperature called the transition (or critical) temperature, Tc. For mercury, in which Kamerlingh Onnes discovered superconductivity in 1911, Tc is about 4.2 K. Below Tc a superconductor also expels magnetic fields (Meissner effect).

  4. FRO Mains 2020 Paper I (GS & MA) · Q1 · General Science & Technology · Chemistry

    What is the result when we add salt in water?

    1. The boiling point decreases and freezing point increases.
    2. The boiling point increases and freezing point decreases.
    3. Both the boiling point and the freezing point increase.
    4. Both the boiling point and freezing point decrease.
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) The boiling point increases and freezing point decreases.

    Salt is a non-volatile solute: it lowers the vapour pressure of water, so the solution must be heated above 100 °C to boil (elevation of boiling point) and cooled below 0 °C to freeze (depression of freezing point). These are colligative properties, which is why sea water freezes near −2 °C and salt is spread to melt ice on roads.

  6. FRO Mains 2020 Paper I (GS & MA) · Q5 · General Science & Technology · Chemistry

    Which group of elements generally form coloured compounds?

    1. Alkali metals
    2. Transition metals
    3. Metalloids
    4. Non-metals
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Transition metals

    Transition (d-block) metal ions have partly filled d-orbitals; they absorb part of visible light when electrons jump between d-levels (d–d transitions), so their compounds look coloured, e.g. blue copper sulphate and purple potassium permanganate. Alkali metal compounds are generally white or colourless because their ions have no unpaired d-electrons.

  11. FRO Screening 2019 · Q5 · General Science & Technology · Chemistry

    Arrange the following in order of decreasing magnetic moments: Fe²⁺, Mn²⁺, Cu²⁺, V³⁺

    Show answer

    Answer: Mn²⁺ > Fe²⁺ > V³⁺ > Cu²⁺

    The spin-only magnetic moment is μ = √[n(n + 2)] BM, where n is the number of unpaired d-electrons. Mn²⁺ (3d⁵) has 5 unpaired electrons (5.92 BM), Fe²⁺ (3d⁶) has 4 (4.90 BM), V³⁺ (3d²) has 2 (2.83 BM) and Cu²⁺ (3d⁹) has 1 (1.73 BM). Hence Mn²⁺ > Fe²⁺ > V³⁺ > Cu²⁺.

  12. FRO Screening 2019 · Q6 · General Science & Technology · Chemistry

    Which of the following is/are correct about sigma bond? 1. Formed by axial overlapping 2. Weaker 3. Independent existence 4. Free rotation possible

    Show answer

    Answer: 1, 3 and 4

    A sigma bond forms by head-on (axial) overlap of orbitals along the internuclear axis, so it can exist on its own and the bonded atoms can rotate freely about it. Statement 2 is wrong: the greater overlap makes a sigma bond stronger than a pi bond, which forms by sideways overlap, exists only alongside a sigma bond and prevents free rotation.
