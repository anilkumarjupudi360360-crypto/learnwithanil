Ecosystem Concepts

  1. FRO Mains 2023 General Forestry I · Q3 · Ecosystems & Wildlife · Ecosystem Concepts

    Match the following ecological processes that occur in forests.

    ProcessOrganism 1 and Organism 2
    (A) Commensalism(1) Organism 1: +, Organism 2: -
    (B) Amensalism(2) Organism 1: +, Organism 2: +
    (C) Predation(3) Organism 1: +, Organism 2: 0
    (D) Mutualism(4) Organism 1: -, Organism 2: 0
    1. (A-1), (B-2), (C-3) and (D-4)
    2. (A-4), (B-3), (C-2) and (D-1)
    3. (A-1), (B-2), (C-4) and (D-3)
    4. (A-3), (B-4), (C-1) and (D-2)
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) (A-3), (B-4), (C-1) and (D-2)

    In commensalism one species benefits and the other is unaffected (+/0). In amensalism one is harmed and the other unaffected (-/0), in predation the predator gains and the prey loses (+/-), and in mutualism both gain (+/+). These give A-3, B-4, C-1, D-2.

  2. FRO Mains 2023 General Forestry I · Q4 · Ecosystems & Wildlife · Ecosystem Concepts

    Which of the following ecological pyramids is always upright in a forest?

    1. Pyramid of number of individuals
    2. Pyramid of number of species
    3. Pyramid of energy
    4. Pyramid if biomass
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Pyramid of energy

    Energy is lost as heat at every trophic level (roughly 90% by the ten-per-cent law), so the pyramid of energy is always upright. Pyramids of numbers and of biomass can be inverted in a forest, for example when a single large tree supports thousands of insects.

    Match the following authors and succession theories

    AuthorTheory

    (B) Tansley(2) Climax pattern and vegetation gradation theory
    (C) Aubreville(3) Mosaic theory
    (D) Whittaker(4) Climatic climax theory
    1. (A-3), (B-2), (C-1), (D-4)
    2. (A-1), (B-2), (C-3), (D-4)

    Clements proposed the monoclimax or climatic climax theory, Tansley the polyclimax theory, Aubreville the mosaic theory of tropical forest regeneration, and Whittaker the climax pattern (vegetation gradation) theory. This gives A-4, B-1, C-3, D-2.

  4. FRO Mains 2023 General Forestry I · Q7 · Ecosystems & Wildlife · Ecosystem Concepts

    ‘Ecesis’ is process of succession that represents:

    1. migration of plants and animals to a new habitat
    2. reaction of plants and animals to a new habitat
    3. plants and animals refuse the new habitat
    4. establishment of plants and animals in a new habitat
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) establishment of plants and animals in a new habitat

    In a succession (sere), ecesis is the stage in which migrated seeds or propagules germinate, grow and become established in the new habitat. Migration is the earlier stage of arrival, and reaction is the later stage in which the established species modify the habitat.

  6. FRO Mains 2023 General Forestry II · Q1 · Ecosystems & Wildlife · Ecosystem Concepts

    Select the correct option based on the following assertion (A) and reason (R). Assertion (A): The pyramid of energy in an ecosystem is always upright. Reason (R): The flow of energy from one trophic level to the next is unidirectional.

    1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true, but R is false.
    4. Both A and R are false.
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.

    The pyramid of energy is always upright because a large part of the energy (roughly 90%) is lost as heat in respiration at each trophic transfer, so every higher level receives less. Energy flow is indeed unidirectional (it cannot be recycled), so R is true, but the narrowing of the pyramid is explained by the loss at each step, not by the direction of flow alone.

  7. FRO Mains 2023 General Forestry II · Q2 · Ecosystems & Wildlife · Ecosystem Concepts

    In the forest ecosystem, maximum biomass accumulates at which trophic level?

    1. Producers
    2. Herbivores
    3. Primary consumers
    4. Secondary consumers
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) Producers

    In a forest the producers (trees) form the first trophic level and hold the largest standing biomass, because only a fraction of the energy and matter is passed to each higher level. The pyramid of biomass in a forest is therefore upright, with producers at the base. Herbivores and carnivores carry far less biomass.

  8. FRO Mains 2023 General Forestry II · Q8 · Ecosystems & Wildlife · Ecosystem Concepts

    The ultradian rhythm refers to a cycle that lasts:

    1. less than 24 hours
    2. exactly 24 hours
    3. more than 24 hours
    4. varies in duration
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) less than 24 hours

    Ultradian rhythms are biological cycles shorter than 24 hours and so repeat more than once a day, for example the heartbeat, breathing or the roughly 90-minute sleep cycle. A cycle of about 24 hours is circadian and one longer than 24 hours is infradian.

  9. FRO Mains 2023 General Forestry II · Q9 · Ecosystems & Wildlife · Ecosystem Concepts

    The term ‘entrainment’ refers to the process of:

    1. synchronisation of biological rhythms to external cues
    2. disruption of biological rhythms due to jet lag
    3. altering the duration of the sleep-wake cycle
    4. regulating hormonal fluctuations during puberty
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) synchronisation of biological rhythms to external cues

    Entrainment is the synchronisation of an organism's internal biological clock to external time cues (zeitgebers) such as the light-dark cycle. Jet lag is the opposite situation, a temporary mismatch between the internal clock and the new local time.

  10. FRO Mains 2023 General Forestry II · Q10 · Ecosystems & Wildlife · Ecosystem Concepts

    The menstrual cycle in monkeys is an example of:

    1. ultradian rhythm
    2. circadian rhythm
    3. infradian rhythm
    4. diurnal rhythm
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) infradian rhythm

    The menstrual cycle of monkeys lasts about a month, much longer than 24 hours, so it is an infradian rhythm. Circadian rhythms last about 24 hours and ultradian rhythms are shorter than a day, while 'diurnal' describes day-time activity rather than a rhythm type.

  14. FRO Mains 2020 General Forestry II · Q3 · Ecosystems & Wildlife · Ecosystem Concepts

    A standing dead tree devoid of leaves and branches is called:

    1. litter
    2. down woody
    3. talus
    4. snag
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) snag

    A snag is a standing dead (or dying) tree that has lost its leaves and most branches; it is valuable habitat for cavity-nesting birds and other wildlife. Litter and down woody material lie on the ground, and talus is a slope of broken rock debris.

Types of Ecosystems

  1. FRO Mains 2023 General Forestry II · Q3 · Ecosystems & Wildlife · Types of Ecosystems

    The anthropogenic activity that poses a threat to grassland ecosystems is:

    1. planting more trees to increase biodiversity
    2. promoting controlled grazing by herbivores
    3. overgrazing by livestock
    4. implementing conservation measures
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) overgrazing by livestock

    Overgrazing by livestock removes the vegetative cover faster than it can regrow, compacts and erodes the soil, and lets unpalatable weeds replace good grasses, which degrades the grassland. Controlled grazing and conservation measures are management tools that help grasslands, and planting trees is not a typical threat in the sense asked here.

  2. FRO Mains 2023 General Forestry II · Q4 · Ecosystems & Wildlife · Types of Ecosystems

    What is the primary factor responsible for the formation of grasslands?

    1. High annual precipitation
    2. Rich soil with high organic content
    3. Extreme temperature fluctuations
    4. Insufficient rainfall to support tree growth
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) Insufficient rainfall to support tree growth

    Grasslands occur where rainfall is too low and too erratic (typically about 250-750 mm a year) for forests to establish, but high enough to prevent desert. High annual rainfall favours forests, so option 1 is wrong, and soil richness is a result of grassland rather than its cause.

  4. FRO Mains 2020 General Forestry I · Q5 · Ecosystems & Wildlife · Types of Ecosystems

    Rhizophora species are characteristic of which kind of forest type?

    1. Grassland
    2. Alpine forest
    3. Mangrove
    4. Tropical rainforest
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Mangrove

    Rhizophora (e.g. R. mucronata, R. apiculata) is a typical mangrove genus of tidal coasts and estuaries, recognised by its prop (stilt) roots and viviparous seedlings. Mangroves are tidal halophytic forests, not rainforests, alpine forests or grasslands.

    Which of the following national parks is a UNESCO World Heritage Site?

    1. Jim Corbett National Park
    2. Keoladeo National Park
    3. Namdapha National Park
    4. Bhitarkanika National Park
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Keoladeo National Park

    Keoladeo National Park (Bharatpur, Rajasthan) was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1985 for its wetland bird habitat. Jim Corbett, Namdapha and Bhitarkanika are important protected areas but are not inscribed World Heritage Sites (Bhitarkanika is a Ramsar site).

  3. FRO Mains 2023 General Forestry II · Q6 · Ecosystems & Wildlife · Wildlife & Protected Areas

    Which sanctuary/national park is known for its population of the Great Indian bustard?

    1. Dachigam Wildlife Sanctuary
    2. Mudumalai Wildlife Sanctuary
    3. Desert National Park
    4. Ranthambore National Park
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Desert National Park

    Desert National Park near Jaisalmer, Rajasthan, is the main stronghold of the critically endangered Great Indian Bustard (Ardeotis nigriceps), which prefers open arid grassland. Dachigam is known for the hangul, Mudumalai for elephants and tigers, and Ranthambore for tigers.

  4. FRO Mains 2023 General Forestry II · Q7 · Ecosystems & Wildlife · Wildlife & Protected Areas

    Which of the following is the oldest national park in India?

    1. Bandipur National Park
    2. Kaziranga National Park
    3. Jim Corbett National Park
    4. Kanha National Park
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Jim Corbett National Park

    Jim Corbett National Park in Uttarakhand was established in 1936 as Hailey National Park and is India's oldest national park. Kanha became a national park in 1955, while Kaziranga and Bandipur were notified as national parks in 1974.

  10. FRO Mains 2020 General Forestry II · Q4 · Ecosystems & Wildlife · Wildlife & Protected Areas

    Skinner's box is associated with which type of behaviour?

    1. Operant conditioning
    2. Trial and error learning
    3. Classical conditioning
    4. Habituation
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) Operant conditioning

    B.F. Skinner's operant conditioning chamber (Skinner box) lets an animal such as a rat press a lever to obtain a reward, showing that behaviour is shaped by its consequences. Classical conditioning is associated with Pavlov, and trial and error learning with Thorndike's puzzle box.

  11. FRO Mains 2020 General Forestry II · Q9 · Ecosystems & Wildlife · Wildlife & Protected Areas

    ______ is an example of secondary hole nesters.

    1. Indian pangolin
    2. Rose ringed parakeet
    3. Greater yellow naped woodpecker
    4. Pygmy hog
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Rose ringed parakeet

    Secondary hole nesters do not excavate their own cavities but use holes already made by others; the rose-ringed parakeet nests in existing tree hollows, often old woodpecker or barbet holes. The greater yellow-naped woodpecker is a primary excavator, while the pangolin and pygmy hog are mammals, not birds.
