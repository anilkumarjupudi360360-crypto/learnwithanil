Digitisation of rural economy

Syllabus: Indian Village and Peasantry · Globalisation and rural change
Model answer · free sample

DigitisationJAM trinityDigital divideTechnoscapes

Digitisation of the rural economy is the shift of village credit, payments, markets, land records and welfare delivery onto digital platforms. Since Digital India (2015) and the JAM trinity (Jan Dhan accounts, Aadhaar and mobile phones), the Indian village has entered what Arjun Appadurai (1990) called global technoscapes, which are reshaping old patron–client ties and creating new divides.

1. Instruments of digitisation

  • Money and welfare: Jan Dhan accounts (2014), Aadhaar-based Direct Benefit Transfer and UPI (2016) now carry MGNREGA wages, pensions and PM-KISAN instalments (₹6,000 a year since 2019) straight to rural households.
  • Markets and farming: e-NAM (2016) links agricultural mandis online, and the Digital Agriculture Mission (2024) is building an Agri Stack of farmer IDs and digital crop surveys.
  • Land and governance: SVAMITVA (2020) maps village house sites by drone and gives owners property cards usable as collateral; Common Service Centres and BharatNet take e-governance to gram panchayats.

2. Changes in village society and economy

  • Fewer middlemen: direct transfers reduce leakages and weaken the hold of the moneylender, ration dealer and trader; Robin Jeffrey and Assa Doron's Cell Phone Nation (2013) shows how the mobile phone gave farmers, fishers and small traders price information and new bargaining power.
  • New roles for women: women of self-help groups work as banking correspondents and, under the Namo Drone Didi scheme (2023), as drone operators for spraying crops, entering public economic roles once closed to them.
  • Translocal households: instant transfers tie migrant workers in cities to families in the village, so the village economy now extends far beyond its boundary.

3. Concerns from an anthropological view

  • Digital divide: women who had ever used the internet rose from 33% in NFHS-5 (2019–21) to 64% in NFHS-6 (2023–24), yet over a third had never gone online; older, poorer and remote Adivasi households lag further behind.
  • Exclusion errors: failed biometric authentication or unlinked records can cut off rations and wages; the death of eleven-year-old Santoshi Kumari in Simdega, Jharkhand (2017), after her family's ration card was cancelled for want of Aadhaar linking, became a symbol of this risk.
  • New dependencies: kiosk operators and agents emerge as fresh intermediaries, while the collection of personal data raises issues of privacy and surveillance.

Digitisation is turning the village as a social system from a face-to-face, patron-based economy into a networked one. Its gains will be inclusive only if digital literacy (PMGDISHA), local-language platforms and offline grievance options reach women, the elderly and remote Adivasi hamlets.
