UPSC Civil Services (Main) 2021 · GS Paper 3 · Q1

Question · Q1 · 10 marks · 150 words

Explain the difference between computing methodology of India’s Gross Domestic Product (GDP) before the year 2015 and after the year 2015.

Syllabus: Indian Economy · Growth, development and employment
Model answer · free sample

Base year 2011-12GVA at basic pricesGDP at market pricesMCA-21 database

Gross Domestic Product (GDP) is the value of all final goods and services produced within a country in a year. India's national accounts are compiled by the National Statistical Office (formerly the CSO), which periodically shifts the base year to reflect the changing structure of the economy. In January 2015 the base year moved from 2004-05 to 2011-12, and the method itself changed, bringing India closer to the UN System of National Accounts (SNA) 2008.

1. What is measured: factor cost to market prices

  • Before 2015: growth was headlined as GDP at factor cost, the income earned by the factors of production (wages, rent, interest and profits); sector-wise output was also valued at factor cost.
  • After 2015: sector-wise output is measured as Gross Value Added (GVA) at basic prices, which adds production taxes net of production subsidies, and the headline is GDP at market prices, which also adds product taxes (such as GST) net of product subsidies.

2. Data sources and coverage

  • Corporate sector: the old series relied on the RBI's sample studies of a few thousand companies; the new one uses the MCA-21 database of annual filings of lakhs of companies with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs.
  • Establishment to enterprise approach: earlier, manufacturing value added was counted factory by factory from the Annual Survey of Industries and the IIP; now the whole enterprise is counted, including its head-office, marketing and other activities.
  • Financial sector: stock brokers, stock exchanges, mutual funds, pension funds and regulators such as SEBI, PFRDA and IRDAI were added, beyond banks and insurers.
  • Unorganised sector: an "effective labour input" method weights owners, hired workers and helpers by their productivity instead of treating all workers alike; local bodies and autonomous institutions are covered better.

3. Effects and debate

  • Higher growth: the new series raised growth for 2013-14 from 4.7% to 6.9%, at a time when credit, industrial output and exports were weak.
  • Criticism: experts questioned the quality of MCA-21 data (non-filing and shell companies), the use of single deflation and the gap with other indicators. In 2019 former Chief Economic Adviser Arvind Subramanian argued that growth had been overstated by about 2.5 percentage points; the Economic Advisory Council to the PM rejected this.

4. The latest revision (as of 2026)

  • Base year 2022-23 (released 27 February 2026): the new series uses double deflation for manufacturing and agriculture, supply and use tables, wider GST data, and direct estimates of the informal sector from the ASUSE and PLFS surveys. It puts nominal GDP about 3–4% lower than the old series, and growth for 2025-26 at 7.7% (provisional estimate, June 2026).

Each revision brings India's accounts closer to global standards. Regular base updates, timely surveys and transparent methods are essential for the credibility of statistics on which policy and investment depend.
