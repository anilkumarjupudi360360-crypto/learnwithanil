What is the main task of India’s third moon mission which could not be achieved in its earlier mission? List the countries that have achieved this task. Introduce the subsystems in the spacecraft launched and explain the role of the ‘Virtual Launch Control Centre’ at the Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre which contributed to the successful launch from Sriharikota.

Syllabus: Science and Technology · Space and defence technology

Model answer · free

Chandrayaan-3Soft landingVikram and PragyanVirtual Launch Control Centre

Chandrayaan-3, India's third Moon mission, was launched by LVM3-M4 from Sriharikota on 14 July 2023. It followed Chandrayaan-2 (2019), whose orbiter still works but whose Vikram lander crashed during its final descent. On 23 August 2023 the new Vikram lander touched down near the lunar south pole, at a spot named Shiv Shakti point, making India the fourth country to soft-land on the Moon and the first near its south pole.

1. The main task not achieved earlier

  • Objectives (ISRO): to demonstrate a safe and soft landing on the lunar surface, to demonstrate roving by a rover, and to conduct in-situ experiments. Chandrayaan-2 lost its lander, so none of these was achieved in 2019.
  • Failure-based design: Chandrayaan-3's lander had stronger legs, more fuel, larger solar panels, a bigger landing zone (4 km × 2.4 km), a Laser Doppler Velocimeter and a hazard-detection camera, so that it could survive errors.
  • Results: the Pragyan rover moved about 100 m, confirmed sulphur in the soil and measured elements; Vikram even made a short "hop" (September 2023). 23 August is now National Space Day.

2. Countries that have soft-landed on the Moon (as of 2026)

  • Other attempts: Russia's Luna-25 crashed in August 2023, days before Chandrayaan-3 landed. The US firms Intuitive Machines (IM-1 Odysseus, February 2024; IM-2 Athena, March 2025) and Firefly Aerospace (Blue Ghost, March 2025) have since landed; both Intuitive Machines landers came down farther south than Vikram but tipped over. China's south-pole mission Chang'e-7 was postponed in August 2026 and had not landed by October 2026.

3. Subsystems of the spacecraft

  • Launch vehicle: LVM3, with two S200 solid boosters, an L110 liquid core stage and a C25 cryogenic upper stage, placed the spacecraft in an elliptical Earth orbit; the propulsion module then raised the orbit and sent it to the Moon (lunar orbit insertion on 5 August 2023).

4. Role of the Virtual Launch Control Centre at VSSC

  • What it is: the Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre (VSSC), Thiruvananthapuram, the lead centre for ISRO's launch vehicles, developed a Virtual Launch Control Centre during the COVID-19 pandemic (reported in October 2020). It replicates at VSSC the systems of the launch control centre at Sriharikota, so that rocket systems can be tested from Thiruvananthapuram and fewer people need to travel to the launch port (VSSC director S. Somanath, 2020).
  • Contribution to launches: because rocket systems can be checked from VSSC, the experts who design the launch vehicle can take part in pre-launch testing without all travelling to Sriharikota; as the question notes, this contributed to the successful LVM3-M4 launch of Chandrayaan-3.
  • Benefits: fewer people at the launch port, continuity during disruptions such as a pandemic, and the design centre's expertise available for checks, which is useful as ISRO's launch rate rises.

Chandrayaan-3 turned the failure of 2019 into a frugal, resilient success. Its lessons in autonomy, landing technology and remote operations prepare ISRO for Chandrayaan-4's sample return (targeted for 2028) and LUPEX with Japan.
