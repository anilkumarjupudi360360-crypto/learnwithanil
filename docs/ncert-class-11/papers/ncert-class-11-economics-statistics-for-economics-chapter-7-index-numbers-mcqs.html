  1. Q1 · Economics · Statistics for Economics

    An index number is:

    1. A statistical device for measuring changes in the magnitude of a group of related variables
    2. The serial number of a page in a book
    3. The average of all prices in one year
    4. A measure of correlation between two variables
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) A statistical device for measuring changes in the magnitude of a group of related variables

    An index number measures relative change, for example in prices or quantities, between a base period and a current period, for a group of related items.

  2. Q2 · Economics · Statistics for Economics

    Which period should be chosen as the base period of an index number?

    1. The latest year available
    2. A year with very high prices
    3. A normal period, free from extreme fluctuations
    4. A year of war or drought
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) A normal period, free from extreme fluctuations

    The base period is the reference with which other periods are compared. It should be a normal year, so that comparisons are not distorted.

  3. Q3 · Economics · Statistics for Economics

    The value of an index number for the base period is always taken as:

    1. 0
    2. 100
    3. 1,000
    4. 1
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) 100

    The base period index is 100. An index of 120 for the current period means a 20 per cent rise over the base.

  4. Q4 · Economics · Statistics for Economics

    The prices of three goods in the base year are ₹10, ₹20 and ₹30, and in the current year ₹12, ₹25 and ₹35. What is the simple aggregative price index?

    1. 112
    2. 72
    3. 83.3
    4. 120
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) 120

    Simple aggregative index = (Σp1 ÷ Σp0) × 100 = (72 ÷ 60) × 100 = 120.

  5. Q5 · Economics · Statistics for Economics

    What is a weakness of the simple aggregative price index?

    1. It cannot be calculated
    2. It always equals 100
    3. It uses current year quantities as weights
    4. It is affected by the units in which prices are quoted and gives no weight to the relative importance of items
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) It is affected by the units in which prices are quoted and gives no weight to the relative importance of items

    In a simple aggregative index, an item with a high price per unit dominates the result, and all items are treated as equally important. Weighted indices correct this.

  6. Q6 · Economics · Statistics for Economics

    Laspeyres' price index uses as weights:

    1. Current period quantities
    2. Base period quantities
    3. Equal weights for all goods
    4. Current period prices
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Base period quantities

    Laspeyres' index = (Σp1q0 ÷ Σp0q0) × 100. It measures how much the base period basket would cost at current prices.

  7. Q7 · Economics · Statistics for Economics

    Paasche's price index uses as weights:

    1. Current period quantities
    2. Base period quantities
    3. Base period prices only
    4. No weights
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) Current period quantities

    Paasche's index = (Σp1q1 ÷ Σp0q1) × 100. It uses the quantities of the current period as weights.

  8. Q8 · Economics · Statistics for Economics

    Good A: base price ₹10, current price ₹12, base quantity 5. Good B: base price ₹20, current price ₹25, base quantity 2. What is Laspeyres' price index?

    1. 120
    2. 122
    3. 122.22
    4. 110
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) 122.22

    Σp1q0 = 12 × 5 + 25 × 2 = 60 + 50 = 110. Σp0q0 = 10 × 5 + 20 × 2 = 50 + 40 = 90. Index = (110 ÷ 90) × 100 = 122.22.

  9. Q9 · Economics · Statistics for Economics

    Good A: base price ₹10, current price ₹12, current quantity 6. Good B: base price ₹20, current price ₹25, current quantity 2. What is Paasche's price index?

    1. 122.22
    2. 120
    3. 122
    4. 100
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) 122

    Σp1q1 = 12 × 6 + 25 × 2 = 72 + 50 = 122. Σp0q1 = 10 × 6 + 20 × 2 = 60 + 40 = 100. Index = (122 ÷ 100) × 100 = 122.

  10. Q10 · Economics · Statistics for Economics

    The Consumer Price Index (CPI) measures:

    1. The average change in the retail prices of a fixed basket of goods and services consumed by a particular group of people
    2. The change in industrial production
    3. The change in wholesale prices only
    4. The change in share prices
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) The average change in the retail prices of a fixed basket of goods and services consumed by a particular group of people

    The CPI, also called the cost of living index, shows how the retail prices paid by a group of consumers, such as industrial workers or agricultural labourers, have changed.
