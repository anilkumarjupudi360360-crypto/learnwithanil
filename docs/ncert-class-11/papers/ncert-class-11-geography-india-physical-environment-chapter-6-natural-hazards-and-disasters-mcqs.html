  1. Q1 · Geography · India: Physical Environment

    How does the chapter distinguish a natural hazard from a disaster?

    1. There is no difference between the two
    2. A hazard always causes more damage than a disaster
    3. A disaster is always man-made, while a hazard is always natural
    4. A hazard is an element of the natural environment with the potential to cause harm; a disaster is an actual event that causes large-scale loss of life and property
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) A hazard is an element of the natural environment with the potential to cause harm; a disaster is an actual event that causes large-scale loss of life and property

    Hazards are potential threats; when they strike and cause serious disruption beyond the coping capacity of the community, they become disasters.

  3. Q3 · Geography · India: Physical Environment

    Why do earthquakes occur frequently along the Himalayan arc?

    1. Because of volcanic activity in the Himalayas
    2. Because the Indian plate, moving about 1 cm a year northwards, is locked against the Eurasian plate, and stress builds up until it is released suddenly
    3. Because of heavy rainfall
    4. Because of mining in the mountains
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Because the Indian plate, moving about 1 cm a year northwards, is locked against the Eurasian plate, and stress builds up until it is released suddenly

    The accumulated energy is released when the lock between the two plates breaks, causing earthquakes along the Himalayan arc.

  5. Q5 · Geography · India: Physical Environment

    On the basis of an analysis of more than 1,200 earthquakes, India has been divided into how many earthquake damage risk zones?

    1. Three
    2. Four
    3. Five
    4. Seven

    Answer: (3) Five

  6. Q6 · Geography · India: Physical Environment

    1. 1 and 2 only
    2. 2 and 3 only
    3. 1 and 3 only
    4. 1, 2 and 3

  7. Q7 · Geography · India: Physical Environment

    Which of the following is NOT suggested in the chapter for earthquake hazard mitigation?

    1. Establishing earthquake monitoring centres
    2. Preparing vulnerability maps and spreading risk information
    3. Making earthquake-resistant designs mandatory and using light materials
    4. Encouraging high-rise buildings and big urban centres in vulnerable areas
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) Encouraging high-rise buildings and big urban centres in vulnerable areas

    The chapter recommends discouraging high-rise buildings, large industrial establishments and big urban centres in vulnerable areas.

  8. Q8 · Geography · India: Physical Environment

    Why is a ship at sea not much affected by a tsunami, while coastal areas suffer huge damage?

    1. Over deep water a tsunami has a very long wavelength and limited wave height; in shallow water its wavelength shortens and its height increases
    2. Tsunamis occur only on land
    3. Ships move faster than tsunamis
    4. Tsunamis lose all energy in the open ocean
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) Over deep water a tsunami has a very long wavelength and limited wave height; in shallow water its wavelength shortens and its height increases

    The speed of a tsunami depends on water depth. Near the coast its height can reach 15 m or more, which is why tsunamis are also called shallow water waves.

  9. Q9 · Geography · India: Physical Environment

    After the tsunami of 26 December 2004, which killed more than 300,000 people, India volunteered to join:

    1. The Ramsar Convention
    2. The Kyoto Protocol
    3. The International Tsunami Warning System
    4. The World Meteorological Organisation's monsoon programme
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) The International Tsunami Warning System

    Mitigating tsunami damage is beyond the capacity of a single country, so international cooperation is needed.

  10. Q10 · Geography · India: Physical Environment

    Tropical cyclones are not formed between 0° and 5° latitude. The main reason is:

    1. The absence of moisture there
    2. The very low sea surface temperature there
    3. The absence of the Coriolis force near the equator
    4. Strong westerly winds there
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) The absence of the Coriolis force near the equator

    Cyclones need a strong Coriolis force to prevent the low pressure from filling up, along with warm moist air, unstable conditions and no strong vertical wind wedge.
