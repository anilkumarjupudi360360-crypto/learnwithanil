  1. Q1 · Geography · India: Physical Environment

    On the basis of geological structure and formations, India is divided into which three geological divisions?

    1. The Peninsular Block, the Himalayas and other Peninsular mountains, and the Indo-Ganga-Brahmaputra Plain
    2. The Thar Desert, the Deccan Plateau and the Islands
    3. The Western Ghats, the Eastern Ghats and the Coastal Plains
    4. The Northern Plains, the Central Highlands and the Andaman Islands
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) The Peninsular Block, the Himalayas and other Peninsular mountains, and the Indo-Ganga-Brahmaputra Plain

    These three geological divisions differ in rock type, age and stability.

  2. Q2 · Geography · India: Physical Environment

    Consider the following statements about the Peninsular Block: 1. It is made up mostly of gneisses and granites and has stood like a rigid block since the Cambrian period. 2. The rift valleys of the Narmada, Tapi and Mahanadi and the Satpura block mountains are examples of block faulting in it. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

    1. 1 only
    2. 2 only
    3. Both 1 and 2
    4. Neither 1 nor 2
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Both 1 and 2

    The block is part of the Indo-Australian plate. Except for the western coast that sank below the sea, its original basement has stayed largely unaffected.

  3. Q3 · Geography · India: Physical Environment

    Which of the following are mentioned as relict and residual mountains of the Peninsular Block?

    1. Patkai Bum, Naga hills and Mizo hills
    2. Shiwalik, Pir Panjal and Zaskar ranges
    3. Aravali hills, Nallamala hills, Javadi hills and Mahendragiri hills
    4. Karakoram and Ladakh ranges
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Aravali hills, Nallamala hills, Javadi hills and Mahendragiri hills

    The Peninsula has old, worn-down mountains such as the Aravali, Nallamala, Javadi, Veliconda, Palkonda and Mahendragiri hills.

  4. Q4 · Geography · India: Physical Environment

    How do the Himalayas differ geologically from the Peninsular Block?

    1. They are older and more stable
    2. They are young, weak and flexible in structure, and are still subject to endogenic and exogenic forces
    3. They are made entirely of basalt
    4. They have no rivers
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) They are young, weak and flexible in structure, and are still subject to endogenic and exogenic forces

    The young Himalayas are tectonic in origin and are dissected by fast-flowing rivers in their youthful stage, forming gorges, V-shaped valleys, rapids and waterfalls.

  5. Q5 · Geography · India: Physical Environment

    Which of the following correctly lists the three parallel ranges of the Himalayas from north to south?

    1. Shiwalik, Lesser Himalaya, Greater Himalaya
    2. Greater Himalaya, Lesser Himalaya, Shiwalik
    3. Lesser Himalaya, Greater Himalaya, Shiwalik
    4. Karakoram, Zaskar, Pir Panjal
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Greater Himalaya, Lesser Himalaya, Shiwalik

    The Greater (Central axial) Himalaya is the northernmost, the Lesser Himalaya lies in the middle and the Shiwalik forms the outermost foothills.

  6. Q6 · Geography · India: Physical Environment

    Karewa formations, known for the cultivation of Zafran (a local variety of saffron), are found in:

    1. Darjiling and Sikkim Himalayas
    2. Arunachal Himalayas
    3. Kashmir Himalayas
    4. Western Ghats
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Kashmir Himalayas

    Karewas are thick deposits of glacial clay and other materials embedded with moraines in the Kashmir valley.

  8. Q8 · Geography · India: Physical Environment

    Which of the following pairs of lakes in the Kashmir Himalayas are salt water lakes?

    1. Dal and Wular
    2. Pangong Tso and Tso Moriri
    3. Dal and Pangong Tso
    4. Wular and Tso Moriri
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Pangong Tso and Tso Moriri

    Dal and Wular are freshwater lakes, while Pangong Tso and Tso Moriri are salt water lakes.

  9. Q9 · Geography · India: Physical Environment

    Which is the largest of the "duns" (longitudinal valleys between the Lesser Himalaya and the Shiwalik) mentioned in the chapter?

    1. Kota dun
    2. Nalagarh dun
    3. Dehra Dun
    4. Jammu dun
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Dehra Dun

    Dehra Dun is the largest, about 35–45 km long and 22–25 km wide. Other duns include Chandigarh-Kalka, Nalagarh, Harike and Kota.

  10. Q10 · Geography · India: Physical Environment

    The Bhotia tribes of the Himachal and Uttarakhand Himalayas migrate in summer to "Bugyals". Bugyals are:

    1. River confluences
    2. Glacial lakes
    3. Hill stations in the Lesser Himalaya
    4. Summer grasslands in the higher reaches
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) Summer grasslands in the higher reaches

    Bhotias are nomadic groups living in the Great Himalayan range; they move to the high summer pastures called bugyals and return to the valleys in winter.
