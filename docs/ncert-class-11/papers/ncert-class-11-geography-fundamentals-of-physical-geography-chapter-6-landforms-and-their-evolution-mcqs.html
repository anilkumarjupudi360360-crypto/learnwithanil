  1. Q1 · Geography · Fundamentals of Physical Geography

    Meanders, natural levees and oxbow lakes are characteristic of which stage in the evolution of a landscape by running water?

    1. Youth
    2. Mature
    3. Old
    4. None of the stages; they form only in deserts
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Old

    In old age, rivers flow on gentle gradients and work sideways, forming meanders, natural levees and oxbow lakes. Youth has V-shaped valleys, waterfalls and rapids.

  2. Q2 · Geography · Fundamentals of Physical Geography

    At the old stage of erosion by running water, most of the landscape is reduced to an almost level surface. This surface is called a:

    1. Pediment
    2. Peneplain
    3. Plateau
    4. Playa
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Peneplain

    A peneplain ("almost plain") forms when divides are worn down; a few resistant rock remnants called monadnocks may stand above it.

  3. Q3 · Geography · Fundamentals of Physical Geography

    Consider the following statements: 1. A gorge has very steep to straight sides and is almost equally wide at its top and bottom. 2. A canyon has steep, step-like side slopes and is wider at its top than at its bottom. 3. Canyons commonly form in horizontally bedded sedimentary rocks, while gorges form in hard rocks. Which of the statements given above are correct?

    1. 1 and 2 only
    2. 2 and 3 only
    3. 1 and 3 only
    4. 1, 2 and 3
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) 1, 2 and 3

    Both are deep valleys; a canyon is a variant of a gorge. The chapter gives the Kaveri near Hogenakkal (Tamil Nadu) as a gorge and the Colorado in the USA as a canyon.

  4. Q4 · Geography · Fundamentals of Physical Geography

    Large, deep holes at the base of waterfalls, formed by the impact and swirling of falling water and stones, are called:

    1. Potholes
    2. Plunge pools
    3. Sinkholes
    4. Cirques
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Plunge pools

    Potholes are circular hollows drilled into the rocky river bed; the large, deep ones at the foot of waterfalls are plunge pools, which also help the valley deepen.

  5. Q5 · Geography · Fundamentals of Physical Geography

    River terraces that occur at the same elevation on both sides of a river are called:

    1. Paired terraces
    2. Unpaired terraces
    3. Incised meanders
    4. Point bars
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) Paired terraces

    Terraces mark old valley floors or floodplain levels. When they appear at matching heights on both banks, they are paired terraces.

  6. Q6 · Geography · Fundamentals of Physical Geography

    Alluvial fans form when streams from higher levels break into foot-slope plains of low gradient. In arid and semi-arid climates, these fans are usually:

    1. Low cones with gentle slopes
    2. High cones with steep slopes
    3. Absent altogether
    4. Made only of fine clay
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) High cones with steep slopes

    In humid areas alluvial fans are low cones with gentle slopes from head to toe; in arid and semi-arid areas they are high cones with steep slopes.

  7. Q7 · Geography · Fundamentals of Physical Geography

    How do deltas differ from alluvial fans?

    1. Deltas are made of unsorted boulders
    2. Delta deposits are well sorted with clear stratification, the coarsest settling first
    3. Deltas form only in deserts
    4. Deltas never have distributaries
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Delta deposits are well sorted with clear stratification, the coarsest settling first

    Rivers carry material to the sea or a lake and drop it in order of size, so delta deposits are sorted and stratified, unlike the coarse, poorly sorted deposits of fans.

  8. Q8 · Geography · Fundamentals of Physical Geography

    Meanders are described in the chapter as:

    1. A type of landform made of hard rock
    2. Deposits left by glaciers
    3. Not a landform but a type of channel pattern
    4. Hollows formed by wind deflation
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Not a landform but a type of channel pattern

    Meanders are loops in a river's course. They develop where water flows over gentle gradients and works laterally on soft alluvial banks; the Coriolis force is also cited as a cause.

  9. Q9 · Geography · Fundamentals of Physical Geography

    In a meander of a large river, the concave bank is marked by active undercutting and appears as a steep scarp. This bank is known as the:

    1. Cut-off bank
    2. Slip-off bank
    3. Natural levee
    4. Point bar
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) Cut-off bank

    The concave bank is the cut-off or undercut bank where erosion is active. The convex bank has a long, gentle slope where deposition takes place.

  10. Q10 · Geography · Fundamentals of Physical Geography
