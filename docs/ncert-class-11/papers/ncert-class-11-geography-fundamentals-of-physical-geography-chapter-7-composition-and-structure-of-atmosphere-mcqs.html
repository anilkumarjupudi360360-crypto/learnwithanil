  1. Q1 · Geography · Fundamentals of Physical Geography

    After nitrogen and oxygen, which gas has the largest share by volume in the atmosphere, according to the chapter's table?

    1. Carbon dioxide
    2. Helium
    3. Neon
    4. Argon
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) Argon

    Nitrogen is about 78 per cent and oxygen about 21 per cent; argon comes next at about 0.93 per cent. Carbon dioxide is only about 0.036 per cent.

  2. Q2 · Geography · Fundamentals of Physical Geography

    About 99 per cent of the total mass of the atmosphere lies within what height from the earth's surface?

    1. 8 km
    2. 32 km
    3. 80 km
    4. 400 km
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) 32 km

    The atmosphere is dense near the surface and thins rapidly with height; 99 per cent of its mass lies within 32 km.

  3. Q3 · Geography · Fundamentals of Physical Geography

    Consider the following statements: 1. The proportion of oxygen becomes almost negligible at a height of about 120 km. 2. Carbon dioxide and water vapour are found only up to about 90 km from the surface. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

    1. 1 only
    2. 2 only
    3. Both 1 and 2
    4. Neither 1 nor 2
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Both 1 and 2

    The chapter notes that the composition of the higher layers changes; oxygen nearly disappears by 120 km, and CO2 and water vapour do not go beyond about 90 km.

  4. Q4 · Geography · Fundamentals of Physical Geography

    Carbon dioxide is meteorologically important because it is:

    1. Opaque to incoming solar radiation but transparent to outgoing terrestrial radiation
    2. Transparent to incoming solar radiation but opaque to outgoing terrestrial radiation
    3. Opaque to both incoming and outgoing radiation
    4. Transparent to both incoming and outgoing radiation
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Transparent to incoming solar radiation but opaque to outgoing terrestrial radiation

    CO2 lets sunlight in but absorbs part of the heat radiated by the earth and sends some back to the surface. This is the greenhouse effect; its rising volume, mainly from burning fossil fuels, raises air temperature.

  5. Q5 · Geography · Fundamentals of Physical Geography

    The ozone in the atmosphere is mostly found between which heights above the earth's surface?

    1. 0 and 5 km
    2. 10 and 50 km
    3. 80 and 400 km
    4. 400 and 1,000 km
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) 10 and 50 km

    Ozone, found between 10 and 50 km, acts as a filter that absorbs ultraviolet rays from the sun and stops them from reaching the surface.

  6. Q6 · Geography · Fundamentals of Physical Geography

    Consider the following statements about water vapour: 1. It may account for up to four per cent of the air by volume in the warm and wet tropics. 2. It makes up less than one per cent of the air in dry and cold areas of deserts and polar regions. 3. Its amount increases with altitude. Which of the statements given above are correct?

    1. 1 and 2 only
    2. 2 and 3 only
    3. 1 and 3 only
    4. 1, 2 and 3
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) 1 and 2 only

    Water vapour is a variable gas that decreases with altitude and from the equator towards the poles. So statement 3 is wrong.

  7. Q7 · Geography · Fundamentals of Physical Geography

    Water vapour is said to act "like a blanket" around the earth because it:

    1. Reflects all sunlight back to space
    2. Absorbs part of the insolation and preserves the heat radiated by the earth
    3. Removes heat from the earth's surface at night
    4. Blocks the formation of clouds
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Absorbs part of the insolation and preserves the heat radiated by the earth

    By absorbing some incoming insolation and holding the earth's radiated heat, water vapour keeps the earth from becoming too hot or too cold. It also affects the stability of the air.

  8. Q8 · Geography · Fundamentals of Physical Geography

    Dust particles are found in higher concentration in subtropical and temperate regions than in equatorial and polar regions. The reason given is:

    1. Greater rainfall in subtropical regions
    2. Volcanic eruptions at the poles
    3. Absence of vegetation at the equator
    4. Dry winds in subtropical and temperate regions
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) Dry winds in subtropical and temperate regions

    Dry winds lift and carry more dust in subtropical and temperate areas. Dust is mostly concentrated in the lower layers of the atmosphere.

  9. Q9 · Geography · Fundamentals of Physical Geography

    Why are dust and salt particles in the air important for clouds?

    1. They act as hygroscopic nuclei around which water vapour condenses
    2. They raise the temperature of the air
    3. They absorb ozone
    4. They prevent rainfall
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) They act as hygroscopic nuclei around which water vapour condenses

    Water vapour condenses around these tiny particles, called hygroscopic nuclei, to produce clouds.

  10. Q10 · Geography · Fundamentals of Physical Geography

    The average height of the troposphere is about 13 km. It is thickest at the equator, about 18 km, because:

    1. The equator has the highest air pressure
    2. The earth's gravity is strongest at the equator
    3. There is more ozone at the equator
    4. Heat is carried to great heights by strong convectional currents at the equator
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) Heat is carried to great heights by strong convectional currents at the equator

    Strong convection lifts heat high over the equator, so the troposphere is about 18 km thick there and only about 8 km near the poles.
