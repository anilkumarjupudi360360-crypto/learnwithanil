Engine Fundamentals & Cycles

  1. AMVI 2023 Paper 2 (Automobile) · Q3 · IC Engines, Fuels & Emissions · Engine Fundamentals & Cycles

    In context of I.C. engines, what is the ratio of shaft energy to the energy available at the piston is called?

    1. Brake thermal efficiency
    2. Indicated thermal efficiency
    3. Mechanical efficiency
    4. Volumetric efficiency
    Show answer

    Answer (TSPSC's preliminary key): (3) Mechanical efficiency

    Mechanical efficiency ηm = brake power / indicated power, i.e. the useful output at the shaft divided by the power developed by the gases on the piston; the difference is lost in friction and pumping. Brake thermal efficiency compares brake power with the fuel energy supplied, indicated thermal efficiency compares indicated power with the fuel energy, and volumetric efficiency compares the charge actually inducted with the swept volume.

  2. AMVI 2023 Paper 2 (Automobile) · Q4 · IC Engines, Fuels & Emissions · Engine Fundamentals & Cycles

    For the I.C. engine, the ratio of total volume of the cylinder to clearance volume is called ______.

    1. clearance ratio
    2. efficiency
    3. expansion ratio
    4. compression ratio
    Show answer

    Answer (TSPSC's preliminary key): (4) compression ratio

    Compression ratio r = total cylinder volume / clearance volume = (Vs + Vc)/Vc, where Vs is the swept (stroke) volume and Vc the clearance volume left above the piston at TDC. Typical values are about 6–10 for petrol (SI) engines and 14–22 for diesel (CI) engines. The clearance ratio is Vc/Vs, and a higher compression ratio raises the air-standard efficiency.

  3. AMVI 2023 Paper 2 (Automobile) · Q5 · IC Engines, Fuels & Emissions · Engine Fundamentals & Cycles

    Which of the following is an advantage of reciprocating internal combustion engines over external combustion engines?

    1. Starting torque is high
    2. Weight to power ratio is generally low
    3. Require more space
    4. Cheaper fuel can be used
    Show answer

    Answer (TSPSC's preliminary key): (2) Weight to power ratio is generally low

    Because the fuel burns inside the cylinder, an IC engine needs no boiler, condenser or separate furnace, so it is compact, with a low weight-to-power ratio and higher overall efficiency, which suits vehicles. External combustion engines such as steam engines give high starting torque and can burn cheaper fuels like coal or wood, but they need more space; these are not advantages of IC engines.

  4. AMVI 2023 Paper 2 (Automobile) · Q6 · IC Engines, Fuels & Emissions · Engine Fundamentals & Cycles

    Which of the following is a function of Gudgeon pin in internal combustion engines?

    1. It acts as a face to receive gas pressure and transmits the thrust to connecting rod
    2. Provide a good sealing fit between the piston and cylinder
    3. It supports and allows the swivel of the connecting rod
    4. It reduces friction and takes care of the fluctuations of speed during a cycle
    Show answer

    Answer (TSPSC's preliminary key): (3) It supports and allows the swivel of the connecting rod

    The gudgeon (piston or wrist) pin joins the piston to the small end of the connecting rod and acts as a pivot, supporting the rod and allowing it to swivel as the crank turns. The piston crown receives the gas pressure, the piston rings give the seal between piston and cylinder, and the flywheel takes care of speed fluctuations during a cycle.

  5. AMVI 2023 Paper 2 (Automobile) · Q7 · IC Engines, Fuels & Emissions · Engine Fundamentals & Cycles

    During which of the following stroke, the flywheel stores energy so that it can supply the energy during the idle strokes?

    1. Suction stroke
    2. Expansion stroke
    3. Compression stroke
    4. Exhaust stroke
    Show answer

    Answer (TSPSC's preliminary key): (2) Expansion stroke

    In a four-stroke engine only the expansion (power) stroke produces work. During this stroke the flywheel absorbs the surplus energy as kinetic energy, its speed rising slightly, and gives it back during the idle suction, compression and exhaust strokes, keeping the crankshaft speed nearly uniform. A multi-cylinder engine overlaps power strokes and so needs a lighter flywheel.

  6. AMVI 2023 Paper 2 (Automobile) · Q8 · IC Engines, Fuels & Emissions · Engine Fundamentals & Cycles

    To allow the fresh charge to enter the cylinder, the inlet valve is opened in advance of the T.D.C position. The inlet valve is opened in advance by ______.

    1. 10° to 30°
    2. 50° to 60°
    3. 30° to 70°
    4. 0° to 10°
    Show answer

    Answer (TSPSC's preliminary key): (1) 10° to 30°

    In the actual valve timing of a four-stroke engine the inlet valve opens about 10°–30° of crank rotation before TDC, so that it is fully open when the suction stroke begins and the fresh charge can enter without restriction. It closes some degrees after BDC, using the inertia of the incoming charge to improve volumetric efficiency. The period when both valves are open near TDC is the valve overlap.

  7. AMVI 2023 Paper 2 (Automobile) · Q10 · IC Engines, Fuels & Emissions · Engine Fundamentals & Cycles

    In a two-stroke engine, the process of clearing the cylinder after the expansion stroke is called ______.

    1. expansion
    2. scavenging
    3. compressing
    4. knocking
    Show answer

    Answer (TSPSC's preliminary key): (2) scavenging

    Scavenging is the sweeping out of the burnt gases from the cylinder of a two-stroke engine by the fresh charge or air entering through the transfer ports at the end of the expansion stroke. Common methods are cross-flow, loop (reverse-flow) and uniflow scavenging. Knocking is abnormal combustion, while expansion and compression are the normal working processes.

Fuel Supply & Combustion

  1. AMVI 2023 Paper 2 (Automobile) · Q9 · IC Engines, Fuels & Emissions · Fuel Supply & Combustion

    Which one of the following factors affect the ignition timing of an internal combustion engine?

    1. Type of fuel
    2. Flywheel
    3. Piston material
    4. Cylinder material
    Show answer

    Answer (TSPSC's preliminary key): (1) Type of fuel

    The spark must occur early enough for the flame to burn the charge near TDC, and the time needed depends on the fuel: its octane rating (knock resistance) and burning rate decide how much spark advance can be used. Engine speed, load and mixture strength also affect the timing, which is why distributors have centrifugal and vacuum advance. The flywheel and the piston or cylinder materials do not decide ignition timing.
