    Identify the incorrect sentence.

    1. The principles of science is difficult to understand.
    2. Reactions to the annual play have been favourable.
    3. The author of the stories in these books is Mary Gomes.
    4. Many lines in the poem are especially good.
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) The principles of science is difficult to understand.

    The verb agrees with the head noun of the subject, not with the noun inside the 'of' phrase. The subject 'principles' is plural, so it should be 'The principles of science are difficult to understand.' The other sentences agree correctly (Reactions…have, author…is, lines…are).

    1. stepped out the car
    2. stepped off the car
    3. stepped out of
    4. No substitution
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) stepped out of

    We get or step 'out of' a car (an enclosed vehicle); 'step off' is used for a bus, train, platform or stage, and 'stepped out the car' drops the needed 'of'. SSC's answer is 'stepped out of' (the car): Everyone clapped as she stepped out of the car.

  7. Q7 · English Comprehension · Voice & Narration

    From the alternatives given below, select the correct passive voice of the following sentence. Do not put your hand over the stove lest you should burn your fingers.

    1. Let your hand be not put over the stove lest your fingers should be burnt.
    2. Lest your hand be not put over the stove let your fingers should be burnt.
    3. Let your hand not put over the stove lest your fingers should be burnt.
    4. Let your hand be not put over the stove lest your fingers should burn.
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) Let your hand be not put over the stove lest your fingers should be burnt.

    A negative imperative becomes passive as 'Let + object + be not + past participle': 'Let your hand be not put over the stove'. The 'lest' clause also turns passive: 'you should burn your fingers' → 'your fingers should be burnt'.

    2. Let the car covered by him with that pink cloth be lying on the table.
    3. Let the car be covered with that pink cloth by him lying on the table.
    4. Let the car be covered by him with that pink cloth lying on the table.
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) Let the car be covered by him with that pink cloth lying on the table.

    'Let + agent + verb + object' becomes 'Let + object + be + past participle + by + agent': Let the car be covered by him. The phrase 'with that pink cloth lying on the table' must stay together; option 3 wrongly splits it with 'by him'.
