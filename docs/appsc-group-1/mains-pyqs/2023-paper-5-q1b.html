APPSC Group-1 Mains 2023 · Paper V · Q1(b)

Question · Q1(b) · 10 marks

Technology plays a key role in transforming healthcare sector and in addressing India's healthcare challenges - Discuss.

Syllabus: Biotechnology and health · Health and diseases
Model answer · free sample

Digital healthAyushman Bharat Digital MissionTelemedicineAI in healthcare

India's health system faces a triple challenge: too few doctors and specialists in rural areas, a rising burden of non-communicable diseases alongside TB and infections, and high out-of-pocket spending, because public spending is still below the 2.5% of GDP target of the National Health Policy, 2017. Technology, from digital health IDs and telemedicine to AI, genomics and drones, can stretch scarce resources and bring care closer to people.

1. Matching technology to India's health challenges

2. How technology is transforming healthcare

  • Access: e-Sanjeevani offers doctor-to-doctor and online OPD consultations where specialists are absent; Tele-MANAS (14416) gives 24×7 mental-health support in many languages.
  • Continuity and efficiency: the ABHA ID lets records follow the patient; ABDM's Scan and Share OPD tokens (over 23 crore by June 2026) cut registration waits from about an hour to a few minutes.
  • Public-health intelligence: CoWIN managed over 220 crore COVID-19 doses, U-WIN records routine immunisation, and the Integrated Health Information Platform tracks outbreaks.
  • Precision and lower costs: the government's AI strategy for healthcare (SAHI) and the BODH benchmarking platform (February 2026), Genome India's 10,000 genomes and indigenous devices cut costs and import dependence.
  • Financial protection: PM-JAY's IT platform runs cashless, portable treatment: over 48.5 crore Ayushman cards and 13.25 crore admissions by August 2026.

3. Limitations and concerns

  • Digital divide: only about half of women used a mobile phone of their own (NFHS-5); the elderly, poor and tribal people need assisted access.
  • Privacy and security: health data are highly sensitive; the DPDP Act, 2023 must be enforced, and the ransomware attack on AIIMS Delhi (2022) showed the risk.
  • No substitute for staff: teleconsultation needs working health centres, drugs and tests; AI must be validated on Indian data, with clear accountability for errors.
  • Uneven adoption: many private providers are yet to link records, and high-end therapies remain costly.

AP angle: AIIMS Mangalagiri, the AP MedTech Zone at Visakhapatnam (India's first medical-devices park, 2016), the cashless Dr NTR Vaidya Seva network and the proposed Universal Health Policy (2025) can use digital records and telemedicine to reach tribal and Rayalaseema areas.

Technology is a force multiplier, not a replacement, for doctors, nurses and public spending. With stronger primary care, data protection and digital literacy, it can carry India towards universal health coverage (SDG 3.8) by 2030.
