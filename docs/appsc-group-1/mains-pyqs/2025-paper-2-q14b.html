Discuss the vulnerability of Andhra Pradesh to cyclones and floods, highlighting the causes, regional patterns, and impacts.

Syllabus: Wildlife, Environment and Disasters · Cyclones, floods, droughts and disaster management

Model answer · free

Cyclone vulnerabilityStorm surgeDeltaic floodsDisaster risk reduction

Andhra Pradesh is one of India's most multi-hazard states. Its 1,053 km coastline (2025 re-measurement) faces the warm Bay of Bengal, and its flat, low-lying Godavari–Krishna deltas lie in the path of tropical cyclones. The same deltas also receive the floodwaters of two of peninsular India's largest river basins. Together, these make the State highly vulnerable to cyclones, storm surges and floods, as Cyclone Montha (October 2025) showed once again.

1. Causes of vulnerability

  • Warm Bay of Bengal: sea-surface temperatures above about 26.5°C, high humidity and weak vertical wind shear make the Bay the main breeding ground of cyclones in the North Indian Ocean, unlike the cooler Arabian Sea.
  • Two cyclone seasons: the post-monsoon season (October–December) brings the most intense storms; a second, smaller season occurs in April–May.
  • Shallow continental shelf and flat deltas: a gently sloping sea floor piles up water ahead of a cyclone, so storm surges push several kilometres inland over the deltas.
  • Large river basins: heavy monsoon rain over the Godavari and Krishna basins, and releases from upstream reservoirs in other States, bring floods down to the deltas.
  • Human factors: loss of mangroves, conversion of wetlands into aquaculture ponds, encroached drains and urban sprawl (as in Vijayawada) magnify the damage.

2. Regional patterns

3. Impacts

  • Human: loss of life (now much lower thanks to early warning and evacuation), displacement and water-borne diseases after floods.
  • Economic: damage to houses, roads, power lines and telecom; Hudhud crippled Visakhapatnam's city infrastructure for weeks.
  • Agriculture and fisheries: loss of paddy, banana, coconut and aquaculture ponds in the deltas; boats and nets destroyed; sea-water intrusion leaves fields saline.
  • Fiscal: large relief and reconstruction spending diverts resources from development.

4. Way forward

Early warning by the IMD and the State's Real Time Governance Society, cyclone shelters, evacuation roads and saline embankments under the National Cyclone Risk Mitigation Project, mangrove bio-shields such as Coringa, floodplain zoning and clean drains, and community preparedness through the AP State Disaster Management Authority.

In short, Andhra Pradesh's geography makes cyclones and floods unavoidable, but disasters are not: shifting from relief to risk reduction, in line with the Sendai Framework, is the way forward.
