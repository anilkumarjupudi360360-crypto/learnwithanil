Q3(b)10 marks2020 · Paper IV

Give an account of the mineral and forest resources of Andhra Pradesh?

Syllabus: Andhra Pradesh's money and the bifurcation · AP's minerals, forests and water disputes with neighbours

Model answer · free

Mineral resourcesBarytes and beach sandsForest coverSustainable use

Andhra Pradesh (about 1.63 lakh sq km) has rich minerals, from the beach sands of its 1,053-km coast to the barytes of Kadapa and the limestone of Kurnool, and valuable forests on the Eastern Ghats and the Nallamala–Seshachalam hills. Geology explains the variety: the Cuddapah basin holds barytes, limestone and uranium, the old Dharwar rocks of Anantapur hold gold, and the khondalites of the Eastern Ghats are capped with bauxite.

1. Mineral resources

  • Barytes: Mangampet (Kadapa region) is among the world's largest deposits and holds most of India's barytes, used mainly in oil-well drilling mud. APMDC mines it.
  • Limestone: Kurnool, Nandyal, YSR Kadapa and Palnadu supply AP's large cement industry.
  • Beach-sand minerals: ilmenite, rutile, zircon, garnet, sillimanite and monazite along the north coast and the Godavari–Krishna coast; monazite is an atomic mineral (thorium).
  • Bauxite: Eastern Ghats (Alluri Sitharama Raju district); mining is sensitive in these Fifth Schedule tribal areas.
  • Others: uranium (Tummalapalle), manganese (Vizianagaram), Nellore mica, Chimakurthi granite, gold (Ramagiri), diamonds (Wajrakarur), and Krishna–Godavari basin oil and gas.

2. Forest resources

  • Extent: recorded forest area is about 37,000 sq km (around 23% of the area). Forest cover is about 30,085 sq km, about 18.5% of the area (ISFR 2023, the latest). ISFR 2021 showed the largest gain of any State.
  • Types: tropical dry deciduous (most extensive), moist deciduous in the Eastern Ghats agency areas, thorn forests in Rayalaseema, and over 400 sq km of mangroves (Coringa).
  • Products: red sanders (endemic to the Seshachalam and adjoining hills), teak, bamboo, beedi leaves, tamarind and honey; the Girijan Cooperative Corporation markets minor forest produce.
  • Protected areas: Nagarjunasagar–Srisailam Tiger Reserve (India's largest), Seshachalam Biosphere Reserve, Papikonda National Park, and the Kolleru wetland.

3. Issues

  • Red-sanders smuggling from the Seshachalam hills threatens an endemic, high-value species despite a special anti-smuggling task force.
  • Illegal mining of sand, granite and limestone causes revenue loss, road damage and pollution.
  • Tribal rights: the rights of forest-dwellers under the Forest Rights Act, 2006 and the consent of gram sabhas in Fifth Schedule areas must come before any bauxite mining.
  • Mangrove loss to aquaculture ponds weakens the coast's natural defence against cyclones.

Scientific mining, District Mineral Foundation funds for affected communities and community forest management can make these resources a basis for sustainable, inclusive growth.
