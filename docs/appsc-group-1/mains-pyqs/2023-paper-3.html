  1. 2023 · Paper III · Q1(a) · 10 marks
    The Constitution and our rights · Fundamental Rights, Directive Principles and how rights are protected

    Examine the scope of 'reasonable restrictions' and the 'test of reasonableness' in connection with Article 19 of the Indian Constitution.

    Model answer · free sample

    Article 19Reasonable restrictionsTest of reasonablenessProportionality

    Article 19(1) guarantees six freedoms to citizens: speech and expression, peaceful assembly, association, movement, residence, and profession, trade or business (the right to property was deleted by the 44th Amendment, 1978). These freedoms are not absolute. Clauses (2) to (6) allow the State to impose "reasonable restrictions" by law on specified grounds, and the courts, applying the test of reasonableness, decide whether a restriction is valid. The aim is to balance individual liberty with social control.

    1. Scope of reasonable restrictions

    • Only on listed grounds: each freedom has its own grounds (see table). In Kaushal Kishore (2023) a Constitution Bench held the grounds in Article 19(2) to be exhaustive, so no new ground can be added by invoking other rights.
    • Only by law: a restriction must be imposed by a valid law, not by an executive instruction (Bijoe Emmanuel, 1986, the national anthem case).
    • Grounds widened by amendment: after Romesh Thappar (1950), the First Amendment (1951) added public order, friendly relations with foreign States and incitement to an offence, and inserted the word "reasonable"; the 16th Amendment (1963) added sovereignty and integrity of India.
    • Special situations: Article 33 lets Parliament restrict the rights of the armed forces, and Article 358 suspends Article 19 during an Emergency declared on the ground of war or external aggression.

    2. The test of reasonableness

    • No fixed standard (V.G. Row, 1952): the court weighs the nature of the right, the purpose of the restriction, the extent and urgency of the evil, proportionality and the prevailing conditions.
    • Not arbitrary or excessive (Chintaman Rao, 1950): a restriction that goes beyond what its object requires is unreasonable.
    • Substance and procedure: both the law and the procedure it lays down must be reasonable; Maneka Gandhi (1978) read Articles 14, 19 and 21 together.
    • Clarity: Shreya Singhal (2015) struck down Section 66A of the IT Act as vague and overbroad, separating discussion and advocacy, which are protected, from incitement.

    3. Proportionality: the modern test

    • Privacy: Puttaswamy (2017) adopted the test for the right to privacy, and the Aadhaar judgment (2018) applied it to uphold the scheme with limits.
    • Digital age: Anuradha Bhasin (2020) applied proportionality to internet shutdowns: they must be temporary, necessary, published and periodically reviewed.

    AP angle: in the protective spirit of Article 19(5), the AP Scheduled Areas Land Transfer Regulation (1 of 1959, amended 1970) bars the transfer of land in Scheduled Areas to non-tribals; in Samatha (1997) the Supreme Court held leases of such land to private mining firms void.

    Reasonableness is thus a judicially tested balance: freedom remains the rule and restriction the exception, and proportionality ensures that the State uses the least intrusive means to protect the public interest.

  2. 2023 · Paper III · Q1(b) · 10 marks
    Centre and States: sharing power and money · Federalism: a strong Centre, Centre–State friction and cooperation

    Explain the genesis of the Inter-State Council and discuss its relevance in the context of Centre-State and Inter-State coordination and cooperation in India.

    Model answer · free sample

    Article 263Inter-State CouncilSarkaria CommissionCooperative federalism

    India's federal system needs forums where the Union and the States can settle friction through dialogue. Article 263 empowers the President to establish an Inter-State Council (ISC), if public interest so requires, to inquire into and advise on disputes between States, investigate subjects of common interest to the Union and States, and recommend better coordination of policy and action. For four decades this provision was not used for a general forum, until the Sarkaria Commission pressed for one.

    1. Genesis

    • A dormant provision: until 1990 Article 263 was used only for sectoral advisory bodies such as the Central Council of Health (1952).
    • Demand for a forum: the Administrative Reforms Commission (1966-70) and Tamil Nadu's Rajamannar Committee (1969) recommended a standing body for Centre-State consultation.
    • Sarkaria Commission (set up 1983, report 1988): recommended a permanent Inter-State Council under Article 263, which it called the Inter-Governmental Council.
    • Constitution: the ISC was set up by a Presidential Order on 28 May 1990; a Secretariat followed in 1991 and a Standing Committee under the Union Home Minister in 1996.

    2. Composition and working

    • Members: the Prime Minister as Chairman, Chief Ministers of all States and of Union Territories with legislatures, Administrators of other UTs, and six Union Cabinet Ministers nominated by the Prime Minister.
    • Standing Committee: chaired by the Union Home Minister, with some Union Ministers and Chief Ministers, it prepares matters for the Council and follows up its recommendations.
    • Method: it is a recommendatory body; questions are decided by consensus, which suits a forum meant for trust-building.

    3. Relevance for coordination and cooperation

    • Neutral platform: issues such as the Governor's role, use of central agencies, river waters, cesses and Centrally Sponsored Schemes need structured dialogue.
    • Policy review: it examined the Sarkaria recommendations and, at its 11th meeting (July 2016), the Punchhi Commission report.
    • Complement to other forums: it works alongside bodies with narrower mandates (see table).

    4. Limitations

    • Advisory and irregular: it met only 11 times between 1990 and 2016, and follow-up on its recommendations has been weak.
    • Reform proposals: the Punchhi Commission urged at least three meetings a year to make it a vibrant forum.

    AP angle: post-2014 issues between Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, such as the division of Schedule IX and X institutions and power dues, are discussed at the Southern Zonal Council and Union Home Ministry meetings; a regularly meeting ISC could settle such issues faster.

    A permanent, regularly meeting ISC with a strong secretariat would turn cooperative federalism from a principle into a working habit of dialogue between the Union and the States.
