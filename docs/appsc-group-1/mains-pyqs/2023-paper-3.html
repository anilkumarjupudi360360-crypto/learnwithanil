  1. 2023 · Paper III · Q1(a) · 10 marks
    The Constitution and our rights · Fundamental Rights, Directive Principles and how rights are protected

    Examine the scope of 'reasonable restrictions' and the 'test of reasonableness' in connection with Article 19 of the Indian Constitution.

    Model answer · free sample

    Article 19(1) guarantees six freedoms to citizens: speech and expression, assembly, association, movement, residence and profession. Clauses (2) to (6) permit "reasonable restrictions" by law, and courts decide what is reasonable.

    Scope of reasonable restrictions

    • 19(2), speech: sovereignty and integrity, security of the State, friendly relations with foreign States, public order, decency or morality, contempt of court, defamation, incitement to an offence. The First Amendment (1951), after Romesh Thappar (1950), added public order, friendly relations and incitement and the word "reasonable"; the Sixteenth Amendment (1963) added sovereignty and integrity.
    • 19(3)-(4), assembly and association: public order, morality, sovereignty and integrity.
    • 19(5), movement and residence: general public interest and protection of Scheduled Tribes.
    • 19(6), profession: public interest, qualifications, State monopoly.
    • The grounds are exhaustive (Kaushal Kishore, 2023).

    Test of reasonableness

    • V.G. Row (1952): the court weighs the nature of the right, purpose of the restriction, extent and urgency of the evil, proportionality and prevailing conditions.
    • Chintaman Rao (1950): a restriction must not be arbitrary or excessive.
    • Both substance and procedure must be reasonable; Maneka Gandhi (1978) linked Articles 14, 19 and 21.
    • Proportionality: Modern Dental College (2016), Puttaswamy (2017), Anuradha Bhasin (2020) on internet shutdowns. Vague laws fail: Shreya Singhal (2015) struck down Section 66A of the IT Act.

    AP angle: in the protective spirit of 19(5), AP's Scheduled Areas Land Transfer Regulation (1 of 1959, amended 1970) bars land transfer to non-tribals; Samatha (1997) held leases of such land to private mining firms void.

    Reasonableness balances liberty with social control; strict scrutiny keeps freedom the rule and restriction the exception.

  2. 2023 · Paper III · Q1(b) · 10 marks
    Centre and States: sharing power and money · Federalism: a strong Centre, Centre–State friction and cooperation

    Explain the genesis of the Inter-State Council and discuss its relevance in the context of Centre-State and Inter-State coordination and cooperation in India.

    Model answer · free sample

    Article 263 empowers the President to establish an Inter-State Council (ISC), if public interest so requires, to advise on inter-State disputes, investigate subjects of common interest to the Union and States, and recommend better coordination of policy.

    Genesis

    • For four decades the provision was used only for sectoral bodies such as the Central Council of Health.
    • The Administrative Reforms Commission (1966-70) and Tamil Nadu's Rajamannar Committee (1969) sought a Centre-State forum.
    • The Sarkaria Commission (set up 1983) recommended a permanent ISC.
    • It was constituted by Presidential Order on 28 May 1990: the Prime Minister as chairman, Chief Ministers of all States and of UTs with legislatures, Administrators of other UTs and six Union Cabinet Ministers; a Secretariat (1991) and a Standing Committee under the Home Minister (1996) support it.

    Relevance

    • A neutral platform for issues such as the Governor's role, central agencies, river waters, cesses and Centrally Sponsored Schemes.
    • It examined the Sarkaria recommendations and, at its 11th meeting (July 2016), the Punchhi Commission report.
    • Complements the GST Council (Article 279A), NITI Aayog's Governing Council and Zonal Councils (States Reorganisation Act, 1956).
    • Limitations: only advisory, meets irregularly (only 11 meetings between 1990 and 2016) and lacks follow-up; the Punchhi Commission urged at least three meetings a year.

    AP angle: post-2014 issues between Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, such as division of Schedule IX and X institutions and power dues, are discussed at the Southern Zonal Council and Union Home Ministry meetings; a regular ISC could settle such issues faster.

    A permanent, regularly meeting ISC would make cooperative federalism a working habit.
