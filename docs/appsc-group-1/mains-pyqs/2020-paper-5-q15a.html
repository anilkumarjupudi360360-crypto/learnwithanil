Q15(a)10 marks2020 · Paper V

Explain the mechanism of action of vaccines. Give two examples where vaccines have eradicated diseases in India. What do you know about the Global Vaccine Action Plan 2011-2020 and Mission Indradhanush?

Syllabus: Biotechnology and health · Health and diseases

Model answer · free

Active immunityImmunological memorySmallpox and polioMission Indradhanush

A vaccine is a preparation of a weakened or killed microbe, a part of it, an inactivated toxin or its genetic code, which trains the immune system to recognise a pathogen without causing the disease. It produces active immunity and long-lasting immunological memory. Since Edward Jenner's smallpox vaccine (1796), vaccines have saved more lives than almost any other health measure.

1. Mechanism of action

  • Primary response: the first dose produces antibodies slowly; boosters strengthen and prolong the memory.
  • Secondary response: when the real microbe arrives, memory cells respond within days and neutralise it before it causes illness.
  • Types: live attenuated (BCG, measles–rubella), inactivated (IPV, Covaxin), subunit (hepatitis B, HPV), toxoid (tetanus), conjugate (Hib, pneumococcal), viral vector (Covishield), mRNA, and DNA (ZyCoV-D, 2021, the world's first human DNA vaccine).
  • Herd immunity: when enough people are immune, transmission stops and even the unvaccinated are protected; measles needs about 95% coverage.

2. Diseases eradicated from India by vaccines

  • Smallpox is the only human disease eradicated worldwide; India succeeded through "search and contain" vaccination around every case.
  • Polio: India, once home to the largest number of cases, was certified polio-free in 2014. Maternal and neonatal tetanus was also eliminated (2015) by vaccinating mothers.

3. Global Vaccine Action Plan (GVAP) 2011–2020

  • What: a WHO-led framework for the "Decade of Vaccines", endorsed by the World Health Assembly's 194 member states in 2012, with UNICEF, Gavi and the Gates Foundation as partners.
  • Goals: a polio-free world; 90% national and 80% district coverage for every vaccine; elimination of measles, rubella and maternal and neonatal tetanus; new vaccines; and fewer child deaths.
  • Outcome: progress on polio and new vaccines, but most targets were missed, as global DTP3 coverage stalled at about 85%. Its successor, the Immunization Agenda 2030, aims to halve the number of "zero-dose" children.

4. Mission Indradhanush

  • Launch: 25 December 2014, to vaccinate children and pregnant women left out of the Universal Immunisation Programme (UIP); named after the rainbow's seven colours for the seven diseases first targeted; goal: 90% full immunisation.
  • Reach: 12 phases of Mission Indradhanush and Intensified Mission Indradhanush (from 2017) up to 2023 vaccinated 5.46 crore children and 1.32 crore pregnant women.
  • Results: full immunisation coverage reached 93.5% in 2023-24, and zero-dose children fell from 26 lakh (2013) to 16 lakh (2023).
  • Now: the UIP covers 12 diseases, and the U-WIN registry (2024) and HPV vaccine for 14-year-old girls (2026) strengthen it.

Vaccines have wiped smallpox and polio out of India; reaching the last zero-dose child, countering hesitancy and adding new vaccines will decide the next victories.
