  1. Q1 · English · Synonyms & Antonyms

    Directions: Select the option that is opposite in meaning to the underlined word. She accepted the prospect of her medical treatment with equanimity. (underlined: equanimity)

    1. tranquillity
    2. agitation
    3. composure
    4. placidity
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) agitation

    "Equanimity" means calmness and evenness of mind, especially under stress, so its opposite is "agitation"; tranquillity, composure and placidity are all synonyms.

  2. Q2 · English · Synonyms & Antonyms

    Directions: Select the option that is opposite in meaning to the underlined word. He had been vehement in opposing the idea. (underlined: vehement)

    1. ardent
    2. subdued
    3. fervent
    4. impassioned
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) subdued

    "Vehement" means showing strong, forceful feeling; "subdued" (quiet, restrained) is its opposite, while ardent, fervent and impassioned are synonyms.

  3. Q3 · English · Synonyms & Antonyms

    Directions: Select the option that is opposite in meaning to the underlined word. He indulges in nefarious activities. (underlined: nefarious)

    1. flagitious
    2. execrable
    3. admirable
    4. abominable
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) admirable

    "Nefarious" means wicked or criminal; "admirable" (deserving praise) is the opposite, whereas flagitious, execrable and abominable all mean extremely wicked or hateful.

  4. Q4 · English · Synonyms & Antonyms

  5. Q5 · English · Synonyms & Antonyms

    Directions: Select the option that is opposite in meaning to the underlined word. All of us are living in turbulent times. (underlined: turbulent)

    1. capricious
    2. desolate
    3. tranquil
    4. obstreperous
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) tranquil

    "Turbulent" means full of disorder and unrest; "tranquil" (calm, peaceful) is the opposite, while "obstreperous" (noisy, unruly) is close to the original meaning.

  6. Q6 · English · Fill in the Blanks

    Directions: Select the most appropriate choice to fill in the blank. I shall not be going to the theatre _____.

    1. if it may snow
    2. if it snow
    3. if it snows
    4. if snowed
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) if it snows

    In a first conditional (real future condition), the if-clause takes the simple present while the main clause has a future form: "I shall not be going ... if it snows."

  7. Q7 · English · Fill in the Blanks

    Directions: Select the most appropriate choice to fill in the blank. _____ than a hundred passengers were killed in the recent train accident.

    1. Less
    2. No less
    3. Fewest
    4. No fewer
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) No fewer

    "Passengers" is a countable plural noun, so "fewer" (not "less") is required; "No fewer than a hundred" stresses that the number was as large as a hundred.

  8. Q8 · English · Fill in the Blanks

    Directions: Select the most appropriate choice to fill in the blank. Each of these paintings _____ made by a famous painter.

    1. are
    2. is
    3. be
    4. should
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) is

    The subject is "Each", which is singular, so it takes the singular verb "is"; the plural "paintings" in the of-phrase does not change the number of the subject.

  9. Q9 · English · Fill in the Blanks

    Directions: Select the most appropriate choice to fill in the blank. This is an area of _____ dense population.

    1. extremely
    2. totally
    3. tangentially
    4. languidly
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) extremely

    An adverb of degree is needed to intensify the adjective "dense"; "extremely dense" is the natural collocation, while "totally", "tangentially" and "languidly" do not fit.

  10. Q10 · English · Fill in the Blanks

    Directions: Select the most appropriate choice to fill in the blank. Reading ability in young children _____ by speech disorders.

    1. effected
    2. affected
    3. is effected
    4. is affected
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) is affected

    The sentence needs a passive verb ("is" + past participle) with "affect" (to influence); "effect" as a verb means to bring about, which does not fit here.
