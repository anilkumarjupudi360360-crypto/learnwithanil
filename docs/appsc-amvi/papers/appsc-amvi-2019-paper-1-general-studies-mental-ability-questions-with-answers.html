  1. Q1 · General Science & Technology · Physics

    Who among the following Indian scientists is the first scientist to get Nobel Prize?

    Show answer

    Answer: C. V Raman

    Sir C. V. Raman won the 1930 Nobel Prize in Physics for discovering the Raman effect, the scattering of light announced on 28 February 1928 (now National Science Day). He was the first Indian and the first Asian to win a Nobel Prize in science; Rabindranath Tagore (Literature, 1913) was the first Indian Nobel laureate overall.

  2. Q2 · Indian Polity & Governance · Constitution & Rights

    ‘Liberty, Equality and Fraternity’ inspirations was derived from the:

    Show answer

    Answer: French Revolution

    Liberty, equality and fraternity was the slogan of the French Revolution (1789). The Preamble of the Indian Constitution takes these ideals, along with the idea of a republic, from the French model, while the ideal of social, economic and political justice is drawn from the Russian Revolution and the Soviet constitution.

  4. Q4 · Indian Polity & Governance · Constitution & Rights

    Match the List (I) and List (II) and select the correct answer from the code given below:

    List IList II
    ArticleIdea
    1. 19 (1) aa. To assemble peacefully and without arms
    2. 19 (1) bb. To form associations or unions
    3. 19 (1) cc. To freedom of speech and expressions
    4. 19 (1) dd. To move freely throughout the territory of India
    Show answer

    Answer: 1-c, 2-a, 3-b, 4-d

    Article 19(1) guarantees citizens six freedoms: (a) speech and expression; (b) to assemble peaceably and without arms; (c) to form associations or unions (co-operative societies were added by the 97th Amendment, 2011); (d) to move freely throughout India; (e) to reside and settle anywhere in India; and (g) to practise any profession, trade or business. Clause (f), property, was deleted by the 44th Amendment (1978). So 1-c, 2-a, 3-b, 4-d.

  5. Q5 · Indian Polity & Governance · Union & State Government

    Which of the following statements is/are correct about Zero Hour? 1. Zero Hour has special provision mentioned in the rule of procedure 2. India has adopted Zero Hour from UK 3. Zero Hour starts immediately after the Question Hour and lasts until the agenda from the day is taken up 4. Before raising question in Zero Hour the members of parliament required to give prior notice

    Show answer

    Answer: 3 only

    Zero Hour is an Indian parliamentary innovation, in use since 1962, not borrowed from Britain, and it is not mentioned in the Rules of Procedure. In the Lok Sabha it begins immediately after Question Hour (12 noon, hence the name) and lasts until the regular business of the day is taken up. Standard texts describe it as an informal device for raising urgent matters without prior notice. (Official final key; some sources differ: in practice members must give the Speaker or Chairman notice on the morning of the sitting, which would make statement 4 true as well.)

  6. Q6 · Mental Ability & Data Interpretation · Reasoning

    Complete the series 1, 2, 8, 33, 148, _?_

    Show answer

    Answer: 765

    Each term is the previous term multiplied by n and then increased by n², for n = 1, 2, 3, and so on: 1×1+1 = 2, 2×2+4 = 8, 8×3+9 = 33 and 33×4+16 = 148. The multipliers rise by one at each step and the added numbers are the squares 1, 4, 9, 16, so the next term is 148×5+25 = 740+25 = 765.

  7. Q7 · Mental Ability & Data Interpretation · Reasoning

    Complete the series 18, 22, 21, 20, 24, 18, _?_

    Show answer

    Answer: 27

    The series is made of two alternating sequences. The terms in the odd places, 18, 21, 24, rise by 3 each time, while the terms in the even places, 22, 20, 18, fall by 2 each time. The missing number is the seventh term, which is in an odd place, so it is 24 + 3 = 27 (the next even-place term would be 16).

  9. Q9 · History & Culture · Modern India & Freedom Struggle

    Which of the following statements is/are correct regarding land revenue policy of British? 1. Land revenue policies made the land saleable, mortgageable and alienable. 2. Land revenue was periodically revised in ryotwari areas but fixed in Mahalwari System. 3. Areas covered by Mahalwari system included North Western Provinces and parts of Punjab. Select the correct answer using the code given below.

    Show answer

    Answer: 1 and 3 only

    British land revenue settlements turned land into a commodity that could be sold, mortgaged and alienated (statement 1). The Mahalwari system, introduced by Holt Mackenzie in 1822, covered the North-Western Provinces, parts of Central India and Punjab (statement 3). Statement 2 is wrong: revenue was revised periodically in both ryotwari and Mahalwari areas; it was fixed permanently only under the Permanent Settlement (zamindari) of Bengal, 1793.

  10. Q10 · History & Culture · Modern India & Freedom Struggle

    The Treaty of Salbai was signed between:

    Show answer

    Answer: British and Marathas

    The Treaty of Salbai (17 May 1782), negotiated through Mahadji Scindia, was signed between the English East India Company and the Marathas and ended the First Anglo-Maratha War (1775–82). The British kept Salsette, recognised Madhav Rao II as Peshwa and gave up the cause of Raghunath Rao, who was pensioned off. It secured peace between the two powers for about twenty years.
