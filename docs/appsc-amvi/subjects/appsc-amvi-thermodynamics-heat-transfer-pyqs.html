  1. AMVI 2026 Paper 2 (Automobile) · Q1 · Thermodynamics & Heat Transfer · Thermodynamics

    A system is in thermodynamic equilibrium when

    1. It exchanges mass but not energy with its surroundings
    2. It exchanges both mass and energy with its surroundings
    3. Macroscopic variables like pressure, volume and temperature do not change with time
    4. It exchanges energy but not mass with its surroundings
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Macroscopic variables like pressure, volume and temperature do not change with time

    A system is in thermodynamic equilibrium when it is at the same time in thermal, mechanical and chemical equilibrium, so its macroscopic properties such as pressure, volume and temperature are uniform and do not change with time, with no net internal flows. Whether a system exchanges mass, energy or both with its surroundings only tells us whether it is open or closed; it does not decide whether the system is in equilibrium.

  2. AMVI 2026 Paper 2 (Automobile) · Q2 · Thermodynamics & Heat Transfer · Thermodynamics

    The Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics states that if two systems are each in thermal equilibrium with a third system, then

    1. They are in mechanical equilibrium
    2. They are in chemical equilibrium
    3. They are in thermal equilibrium with each other
    4. They are in a state of total thermodynamic equilibrium
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) They are in thermal equilibrium with each other

    The Zeroth Law says that if bodies A and B are each in thermal equilibrium with a third body C, then A and B are in thermal equilibrium with each other: there is no net heat flow when they are brought into contact, because they are at the same temperature. It is the basis of temperature measurement, the third body being the thermometer. It says nothing about mechanical or chemical equilibrium.

  3. AMVI 2026 Paper 2 (Automobile) · Q3 · Thermodynamics & Heat Transfer · Thermodynamics

    Which of the following is not an example of a pure substance ?

    1. Water (H₂O)
    2. Nitrogen (N₂)
    3. Air (a mixture of gases like O₂, N₂, Ar)
    4. Helium (He)
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Air (a mixture of gases like O₂, N₂, Ar)

    A pure substance has a fixed and uniform chemical composition throughout. Water, nitrogen and helium are single chemical species and qualify. Air is a mixture of nitrogen, oxygen, argon and other gases. It behaves like a pure substance only while it remains a gas of uniform composition; when it is partly liquefied, its components condense at different temperatures and the liquid and vapour have different compositions, so air is not a true pure substance.

  4. AMVI 2026 Paper 2 (Automobile) · Q4 · Thermodynamics & Heat Transfer · Thermodynamics

    The Mollier diagram is a plot of

    1. Temperature and entropy
    2. Temperature and enthalpy
    3. Pressure and enthalpy
    4. Enthalpy and entropy
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) Enthalpy and entropy

    The Mollier diagram, devised by Richard Mollier, plots enthalpy (h) on the vertical axis against entropy (s) on the horizontal axis, usually for steam. On this h–s chart an isentropic expansion is a vertical line and the enthalpy drop can be read directly, which makes it convenient for nozzle and turbine calculations. The temperature–entropy plot is the T–s diagram, and the pressure–enthalpy (p–h) chart is mainly used for refrigerants.

  17. AMVI 2019 Paper 2 (Automobile) · Q1 · Thermodynamics & Heat Transfer · Thermodynamics

    Which of the following is true for open system?

    Show answer

    Answer: Mass can transfer, energy can transfer

    An open system (control volume) is one whose boundary allows both mass and energy (heat and work) to cross, for example an engine cylinder during intake and exhaust, a compressor, a turbine or a nozzle. A closed system allows only energy transfer with a fixed mass (a piston-cylinder with the valves shut), and an isolated system allows neither mass nor energy to cross its boundary.

  18. AMVI 2019 Paper 2 (Automobile) · Q2 · Thermodynamics & Heat Transfer · Thermodynamics

    A piston cylinder contains air at 600 kPa, 290 K and a volume of 0.01m^3. A constant pressure process gives 60 kJ of work out. Find the final volume of the air.

    Show answer

    Answer: 0.11 m^3

    In a constant-pressure process the boundary work is W = p(V₂ − V₁). So 60 kJ = 600 kPa × (V₂ − 0.01) m³, giving V₂ − 0.01 = 60/600 = 0.1 m³ and V₂ = 0.11 m³. The temperature (290 K) is not needed for the volume; it would be used to find the mass of air, m = pV/RT = 600 × 0.01/(0.287 × 290) ≈ 0.072 kg.

  19. AMVI 2019 Paper 2 (Automobile) · Q3 · Thermodynamics & Heat Transfer · Thermodynamics

    As per kinetic theory of gases, value of gamma for monoatomic gases, diatomic gases and polyatomic gases are

    Show answer

    Answer: 5/3, 7/5, 4/3

    Kinetic theory gives γ = cp/cv = 1 + 2/f, where f is the number of degrees of freedom of the molecule. A monatomic gas (He, Ar) has 3 translational degrees, so γ = 5/3 ≈ 1.67; a diatomic gas (O₂, N₂, air) has 5 (3 translational + 2 rotational), so γ = 7/5 = 1.4; a non-linear polyatomic gas has 6, so γ = 8/6 = 4/3 ≈ 1.33 (vibrational modes neglected).

  20. AMVI 2019 Paper 2 (Automobile) · Q5 · Thermodynamics & Heat Transfer · Thermodynamics

    The ratio of specific heat at constant volume (cv) and specific heat at constant pressure (cp) is

    Show answer

    Answer: < 1

    cp − cv = R, so cp is always larger than cv: at constant pressure part of the heat supplied does expansion work, while at constant volume all of it raises the internal energy. Hence cp/cv = γ > 1 (1.4 for air), and the ratio asked, cv/cp = 1/γ, is less than 1 (about 0.714 for air).

  21. AMVI 2019 Paper 2 (Automobile) · Q9 · Thermodynamics & Heat Transfer · Thermodynamics

    Availability of a system is depending on the

    Show answer

    Answer: State of a system and surrounding

    Availability (exergy) is the maximum useful work obtainable as a system is brought into equilibrium with its surroundings (the dead state). It therefore depends both on the state of the system (p, T, composition) and on the state of the surroundings (p₀, T₀); for a closed system, availability = (U − U₀) + p₀(V − V₀) − T₀(S − S₀). A system already at the dead state has zero availability.

  22. AMVI 2019 Paper 2 (Automobile) · Q10 · Thermodynamics & Heat Transfer · Thermodynamics

    Calculate the enthalpy of 25 kg of fluid that occupies a volume of 12000 litre, if the internal energy is 28000000 J /kg and the pressure is 0.3 MN/m²

    Show answer

    Answer: 703.6 MJ

    Enthalpy H = U + pV. The internal energy U = 25 kg × 28,000,000 J/kg = 700 MJ. The volume is 12,000 litres = 12 m³, so pV = 0.3 × 10⁶ N/m² × 12 m³ = 3.6 MJ. Therefore H = 700 + 3.6 = 703.6 MJ, and the specific enthalpy is 703.6/25 = 28.144 MJ/kg.

    2. Refrigerant

    4. Condenser
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Turbo charger

    A car air-conditioning system is a vapour-compression refrigeration circuit: the compressor (belt- or motor-driven), condenser, receiver-drier, expansion valve or orifice tube and evaporator, with a refrigerant such as R-134a or R-1234yf circulating through them.   5. AMVI 2023 Paper 2 (Automobile) · Q2 · Thermodynamics & Heat Transfer · Refrigeration & Air Conditioning

    Select the option that is true regarding the following two statements labelled Assertion (A) and Reason (R). Assertion (A): The COP of an air-conditioning plant is lower than that of an ice plant. Reason (R): The temperatures required in the ice plant are lower than those required for an air-conditioning plant.

    1. Both A and R are individually true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are individually true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true, but R is false.
    4. A is false, but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) A is false, but R is true.

    COP falls as the evaporator temperature falls, because the compressor has to lift heat through a larger temperature difference (Carnot COP = T_L/(T_H − T_L)). An ice plant evaporates below 0 °C, while an air-conditioning plant evaporates at about 5–10 °C, so R is true. For example, with a 45 °C condenser the Carnot COP is about 7.0 at 5 °C but only 4.3 at −15 °C. Hence an air-conditioning plant has the higher COP and A is false.
