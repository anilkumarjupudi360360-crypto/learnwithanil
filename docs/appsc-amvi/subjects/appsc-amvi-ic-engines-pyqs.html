  1. AMVI 2026 Paper 2 (Automobile) · Q5 · IC Engines, Fuels & Emissions · Engine Fundamentals & Cycles

    For a fixed amount of heat supplied and a fixed compression ratio, the thermal efficiency of the air-standard cycle follows the order

    1. Otto > Diesel > Dual
    2. Otto > Dual > Diesel
    3. Diesel > Otto > Dual
    4. Dual > Otto > Diesel
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Otto > Dual > Diesel

    For the same compression ratio and the same heat input, the Otto cycle adds all its heat at constant volume at the end of compression, so the gas then expands through the full ratio, rejects the least heat and is the most efficient. The Diesel cycle adds heat at constant pressure while the gas expands, so it rejects the most heat. The dual cycle adds part at constant volume and part at constant pressure and lies between: η Otto > η Dual > η Diesel.

  2. AMVI 2026 Paper 2 (Automobile) · Q6 · IC Engines, Fuels & Emissions · Engine Fundamentals & Cycles

    The Stirling cycle consists of

    1. Two isothermal and two constant pressure processes
    2. Two isothermal and two constant volume processes
    3. Two isothermal and two adiabatic processes
    4. Two adiabatic processes and one constant volume processes
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Two isothermal and two constant volume processes

    The Stirling cycle consists of isothermal compression, constant-volume heat addition, isothermal expansion and constant-volume heat rejection. With a perfect regenerator, which stores the heat rejected at constant volume and returns it later, its efficiency equals that of the Carnot cycle working between the same temperatures. Two isothermal and two constant-pressure processes make the Ericsson cycle, and two isothermal and two adiabatic processes make the Carnot cycle.

    The diagram factor is defined as the ratio of

    1. Brake power to indicated power
    2. Indicated work done to brake work done
    3. Area of the actual indicator diagram to the area of the theoretical indicator diagram
    4. Thermal efficiency to mechanical efficiency
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Area of the actual indicator diagram to the area of the theoretical indicator diagram

    Diagram factor = area of the actual indicator diagram ÷ area of the theoretical (hypothetical) indicator diagram, which equals the ratio of the actual to the theoretical mean effective pressure. It allows for losses such as valve throttling, heat transfer and the rounding of the diagram's corners, and it is always less than 1. The ratio of brake power to indicated power (option 1) is the mechanical efficiency.

  18. AMVI 2023 Paper 2 (Automobile) · Q7 · IC Engines, Fuels & Emissions · Engine Fundamentals & Cycles

    Select the option that is true regarding the following two statements labelled Assertion (A) and Reason (R). Assertion (A): The C.I. engine is found to be more efficient than the S.I. engine. Reason (R): Modern C.I. engines operate on a dual-cycle, which has efficiency greater than the Otto cycle.

    1. Both A and R are individually true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
    2. Both A and R are individually true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
    3. A is true, but R is false.
    4. A is false, but R is true.
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Both A and R are individually true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.

    C.I. engines are more efficient mainly because they run at much higher compression ratios (about 14–22 against 8–12 for S.I. engines), with lean mixtures and no throttling losses. Modern high-speed C.I. engines are modelled by the dual (limited-pressure) cycle, which is more efficient than the Otto cycle when both work to the same peak pressure; for the same compression ratio, however, the Otto cycle is the most efficient. So R holds only for the same peak pressure; the real reason for A is the higher compression ratio, and R does not explain A.

    List IList II

    (B) Actual cycle(ii) Generation of energy by burning of fuel with air
    (iii) Air is working fluid
    (iv) No internal heat generation
    (v) This cycle is completely closed
    (vi) Energy is generated inside of the engine
    1. (A)-(iii), (iv) and (v); (B)-(i), (ii) and (vi)
    2. (A)-(i), (ii) and (vi); (B)-(iii), (iv) and (v)
    3. (A)-(ii), (i) and (iv); (B)-(iii), (v) and (vi)
    4. (A)-(i), (ii) and (iii); (B)-(iv), (v) and (vi)
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) (A)-(iii), (iv) and (v); (B)-(i), (ii) and (vi)

    The air-standard cycle is an idealisation: the working fluid is air alone, treated as a perfect gas, heat is added from an external source instead of by combustion (no internal heat generation), and the same air is recirculated in a closed cycle. In the actual engine cycle the working fluid is an air–fuel mixture, energy is released by burning the fuel with air, and this happens inside the engine cylinder.

    C) Flash point value(iii) Always lower than the fire point

    1. (A)-(ii), (B)-(iv), (C)-(iii) and (D)-(i)
