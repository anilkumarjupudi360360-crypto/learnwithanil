Grammar

  1. LDA (Heads of Dept) 2026 · Q1 · General English · Grammar

    Choose the correct option to fill in the gaps : We ______ some terrifying news last night.

    1. hear
    2. heard
    3. get
    4. got
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) heard

    “Last night” is a finished past time, so the simple past is needed, which rules out “hear” and “get”. One hears news (learns it by being told), so the standard answer is “We heard some terrifying news last night.” “Got” is also grammatical in everyday speech, but it is informal, so the key takes “heard”.

  2. LDA (Heads of Dept) 2026 · Q2 · General English · Grammar

    Choose the correct option to fill in the gaps : My teacher ______ a book last year.

    1. write
    2. wrote
    3. written
    4. had wrote
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) wrote

    “Last year” marks a finished past time, so the simple past “wrote” is used. “Written” is a past participle and needs “has/had”, and “had wrote” is ungrammatical (it would be “had written”).

  3. LDA (Heads of Dept) 2026 · Q3 · General English · Grammar

    Choose the correct option to fill in the gaps : The train ______ at 8:00 at the station yesterday.

    1. did not arrive
    2. did not arrived
    3. did not pass
    4. did not passed
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) did not arrive

    After the auxiliary “did/did not”, the main verb stays in its base form: “did not arrive”, never “did not arrived” or “did not passed”. A train arrives at a station; “pass at the station” is not idiomatic (a train passes, or passes through, a station).

    1. wear
    2. wore
    3. wearing
    4. worn
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) wearing

  6. LDA (Heads of Dept) 2026 · Q6 · General English · Grammar

    Choose the correct option to fill in the gaps : The house ______ to him since his birth.

    1. belong
    2. belonged
    3. had belonged
    4. has belonged
    Show answer

    Answer: (4) has belonged

    “Since his birth” shows a state that began in the past and continues now, which needs the present perfect: “The house has belonged to him since his birth.” “Belong” is a stative verb, so it is not used in the continuous.

  9. LDA (Heads of Dept) 2026 · Q9 · General English · Grammar

    Choose the correct option to fill in the gaps : Last night, the dog ______ for a long time.

    1. barked
    2. is barking
    3. has barked
    4. barking
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) barked

    “Last night” is a definite, finished past time, so the simple past “barked” is used. The present perfect and present continuous cannot go with a definite past time, and “barking” alone has no auxiliary.

  10. LDA (Heads of Dept) 2026 · Q10 · General English · Grammar

    Choose the correct option to fill in the gaps : I ______ she will be late.

    1. won’t think
    2. thinking
    3. don’t think
    4. will think
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) don’t think

    An opinion held now takes the simple present of “think”, and English normally puts the negative on “think”: “I don’t think she will be late.” “Won’t think” and “will think” wrongly make the opinion future, and “thinking” has no auxiliary.

Comprehension & Usage

  1. MCS Prelims 2026 · Paper II · Q1 · General English · Comprehension & Usage

    Science has discovered that the ant does not lay up anything for winter use. He is a deceiver who does not work, except when people are looking, and only then when an observer has a green, naturalistic look and seems to be taking notes. He cannot stroll around a stump and find his way home again. This amounts to idiocy. His vaunted industry is but a vanity, since he never gets home with anything he starts with. This disposes of the last remnant of his reputation and wholly destroys his main usefulness as a moral agent. It is strange, beyond comprehension, that so manifest a humbug as the ant has been able to fool so many nations, so many ages without being found out. Which claim about the ant is central to the passage?

    1. The ant prepares diligently for winter
    2. The ant only appears industrious when observed
    3. The ant navigates stumps better than other insects
    4. The ant’s industry makes it a reliable moral model
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) The ant only appears industrious when observed

    The author’s core charge is that the ant is “a deceiver who does not work, except when people are looking” – its industry is only a show for observers. The passage denies (A) and (D) outright and never compares the ant with other insects (C).

    Imagine you had a bank account that deposited ₹ 86,400 each morning. The account carries over no balance from day to day, allows you to keep no cash balance, and every evening cancels whatever part of the amount you had failed to use during the day. What would you do? Draw out every rupee each day! We all have such a bank. Its name is Time. Every morning, it credits you with 86400 seconds. Every night it writes off, as lost, whatever time you have failed to use wisely. It carries over no balance from day to day. It allows no overdraft so you can’t borrow against yourself or use more time than you have. Each day, the account starts fresh. Each night, it destroys an unused time. If you fail to use the day’s deposits, it’s your loss and you can’t appeal to get it back. There is never any borrowing time. You can’t take a loan out on your time or against someone else’s. The time you have is the time you have and that is that. Time management is yours to decide how you spend the time, just as with money you decide how you spend the money. It is never the case of us not having enough time to do things, but the case of whether we want to do them and where they fall in our priorities.
