  1. 2025 · Paper III English & Odia · Q2 · English · Grammar

    Directions : Fill in the blanks in the following sentences by selecting the most appropriate option from the choices given below each. Each of the buildings ______ unique design.

    1. have
    2. has
    3. are
    4. were
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) has

    "Has" is correct because "each" is singular and takes a singular verb. In "Each of the buildings has a unique design", the real subject is "Each" (each one), and "of the buildings" is only a phrase describing it, so the verb agrees with "Each", not with "buildings". "Have" is the plural form, "are" is plural and also gives no sense of possession, and "were" is plural past tense. The same rule applies to every, either, neither, one of, everyone and each one: "One of my friends lives in Cuttack"; "Neither of the answers is correct." The printed sentence leaves out the article "a" before "unique design", but that does not change the choice of verb: only "has" agrees with the subject. Trap: don't let the plural noun just before the verb ("buildings") pull the verb into the plural; this "proximity" error is a favourite trap in competitive exams.

  2. 2025 · Paper III English & Odia · Q4 · English · Grammar

    Directions : Fill in the blanks in the following sentences by selecting the most appropriate option from the choices given below each. It was ______ honour to meet the author.

    1. a
    2. an
    3. the
    4. some
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) an

    "An" is correct because the choice between "a" and "an" depends on the first sound of the next word, not its first letter. In "honour" the h is silent, so the word begins with a vowel sound ("on-er"), and vowel sounds take "an": "It was an honour to meet the author." "A" is used before consonant sounds, so "a honour" is wrong. "The" would point to a particular, already-known honour, which does not suit the general sense of "It was an honour to ...", and "some" is used with uncountable or plural nouns and would change the meaning to "a certain amount of honour". Other silent-h words that take "an": an hour, an honest man, an heir, an honorarium. The reverse case: words that start with a vowel letter but a consonant sound take "a": a university, a European, a one-day match, a unique idea. Trap: "a hotel", "a historic event" and "a hospital" take "a" because their h is pronounced.

  3. 2025 · Paper III English & Odia · Q9 · English · Grammar

    Directions : Fill in the blanks in the following sentences by selecting the most appropriate option from the choices given below each. The pair of shoes ______ on sale at the store.

    1. is
    2. are
    3. be
    4. were
    Show answer

    Answer: (1) is

    "Is" is correct because the true subject is "pair", which is singular; "of shoes" only tells us what the pair consists of. So the verb must be singular and, since the sale is happening now, in the present tense: "The pair of shoes is on sale at the store." "Are" and "were" are plural forms, and "were" is also past tense; "be" is the base form and cannot follow a singular subject in a normal statement. The same rule works for other "a pair of" and "a set of" phrases: "A pair of scissors is on the table", "A set of books was gifted to the library", "A bouquet of flowers looks lovely." But nouns such as shoes, trousers, scissors and spectacles take a plural verb when used alone: "My shoes are new." Trap: the plural noun "shoes" sits right before the verb and tempts you to write "are"; always find the head noun of the subject first.

  4. 2025 · Paper III English & Odia · Q10 · English · Grammar

    Directions : Fill in the blanks in the following sentences by selecting the most appropriate option from the choices given below each. They ______ the Asia Cup trophy in 1993.

    1. win
    2. has won
    3. won
    4. winning
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) won

    "Won" is correct: when a sentence names a definite past time, such as "in 1993", "yesterday", "last year" or "two days ago", the simple past tense is used: "They won the Asia Cup trophy in 1993." The present perfect "has won" links the past to the present and cannot be used with a specific past date; it is also wrong in number, because "They" needs "have", not "has". "Win" is present tense and clashes with 1993, and "winning" is a participle that needs a helping verb ("were winning"). Compare: "India has won the Asia Cup several times" (no time stated, so the present perfect is fine) with "India won the Asia Cup in 2023" (definite time, so simple past). Trap: never pair "has/have + past participle" with a finished time expression; "I have seen him yesterday" is a classic error-spotting mistake. Tip: words like already, ever, never, just and since usually go with the present perfect.

  44. 2022 · Paper III English & Odia · Q1 · English · Grammar

    Change the active voice into passive voice : I will clean the house every Saturday. The house ______ by me every Saturday.

    1. cleaned
    2. will cleaned
    3. will be cleaned
    4. None of these
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) will be cleaned

    The passive form is 'will be cleaned'. In the simple future tense, the passive voice is made with will + be + past participle. The object of the active sentence ('the house') becomes the subject, and the doer ('I') moves to a by-phrase as 'by me'. So 'I will clean the house every Saturday' becomes 'The house will be cleaned by me every Saturday'. The tense stays future, and the time phrase 'every Saturday' stays as it is. 'Cleaned' alone turns the sentence into the simple past and even makes the house the doer ('The house cleaned'). 'Will cleaned' is ungrammatical, since a modal such as will must be followed by a base verb, here 'be'. 'None of these' is wrong because option C is correct. Another example: 'She will write a letter' becomes 'A letter will be written by her'. Memory tip for passives: keep the tense of the active verb, add the right form of 'be', then the past participle (V3), and change the pronoun 'I' to 'me' in the by-phrase.

  45. 2022 · Paper III English & Odia · Q2 · English · Grammar

    People ______ walk on grass.

    1. couldn't
    2. needn't
    3. mustn't
    4. may not
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) mustn't

    'Mustn't' (must not) expresses a prohibition, which is exactly what a public rule such as 'People mustn't walk on grass' means: walking on the grass is not allowed. 'Needn't' means something is not necessary, so 'People needn't walk on grass' would make walking on it optional, which is not a rule at all. 'Couldn't' refers to inability, usually in the past ('I couldn't swim as a child'), not to a rule. 'May not' can refuse permission in formal English, but it is softer and is also used for possibility ('It may not rain'), so it does not carry the firm, general ban the sentence needs. Key contrast to remember: mustn't = it is forbidden; needn't or don't have to = it is not necessary. Example: 'You mustn't use your phone in the exam hall' (forbidden) versus 'You needn't bring a pen; we will provide one' (not necessary). Signboards such as 'Keep off the grass' express the same idea as the sentence in the question.

  46. 2022 · Paper III English & Odia · Q3 · English · Grammar

    Choose the correct word order :

    1. Why she did leave so early ?
    2. Why did she leave so early ?
    3. Why did so early she leave ?
    4. Why so early did she leave ?
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) Why did she leave so early ?

    The correct order is 'Why did she leave so early?' In English wh-questions the order is: question word + auxiliary + subject + main verb + the rest. Here: Why (question word) + did (auxiliary) + she (subject) + leave (main verb in base form) + so early (adverbial). When the statement has no auxiliary ('She left early'), we add do, does or did and change the main verb to its base form, so 'left' becomes 'did ... leave'. Option A, 'Why she did leave so early?', keeps the statement order (subject before auxiliary); that order is used only in indirect questions such as 'I wonder why she left so early.' Option C wrongly puts 'so early' between the auxiliary 'did' and the subject 'she', and option D moves 'so early' in front of the auxiliary, which is unnatural in ordinary English. Another example: 'Where does he work?' (not 'Where he does work?'). Memory tip: QASV, that is, Question word, Auxiliary, Subject, Verb.

  47. 2022 · Paper III English & Odia · Q5 · English · Grammar

    ______ his principles, he has to be very careful.

    1. With regard of
    2. With regard on
    3. With regard to
    4. None of these
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) With regard to

    'With regard to' is the fixed phrase, meaning 'concerning', 'about' or 'in respect of'. The sentence becomes: 'With regard to his principles, he has to be very careful.'
