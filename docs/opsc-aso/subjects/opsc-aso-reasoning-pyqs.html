  1. 2025 · Paper II Reasoning & Maths · Q1 · Reasoning & Mental Ability · Verbal Reasoning

    Choose the most appropriate option to complete the following series. V, R, N, ______, F, B

    1. K
    2. H
    3. J
    4. P
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) J

    J completes the series. Write each letter's position in the alphabet: V = 22, R = 18, N = 14, F = 6, B = 2. Each step goes back by 4: 22 → 18 → 14 → ? → 6 → 2. So the missing term is 14 − 4 = 10, and the 10th letter is J. Check forwards from the blank: 10 − 4 = 6 = F, and 6 − 4 = 2 = B, so the series holds. The other options break the pattern: K (11) would make a gap of 3, H (8) a gap of 6, and P (16) would make the series go forward instead of backward. Shortcut: remember the EJOTY positions, E = 5, J = 10, O = 15, T = 20, Y = 25; with them you can find any letter's number in a second. Trap: three letters are skipped each time (V, skip U T S, R), so always count the gap, not just the next letter.

  2. 2025 · Paper II Reasoning & Maths · Q2 · Reasoning & Mental Ability · Verbal Reasoning

    In a certain code language, STRAIGHT is written as TSARGITH. How will THURSDAY be written as in that language?

    1. AYSDURTH
    2. HTRUDSYA
    3. UHTDRSYA
    4. HTRUDSAY
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) HTRUDSYA

    HTRUDSYA follows the same code. Step 1: split STRAIGHT into pairs of letters: ST | RA | IG | HT. Step 2: split the code TSARGITH the same way: TS | AR | GI | TH. Each pair has simply been reversed: ST → TS, RA → AR, IG → GI, HT → TH. Step 3: apply the same rule to THURSDAY: TH | UR | SD | AY becomes HT | RU | DS | YA, which joins up as HTRUDSYA, option (B). Check the others: (A) AYSDURTH keeps each pair as it is but reverses the order of the pairs (AY SD UR TH), a different rule; (C) UHTDRSYA moves the letters in a way that matches nothing in the example; (D) HTRUDSAY swaps the first three pairs but leaves the last pair AY unchanged, so it breaks the rule at the end. Tip: when a word and its code use exactly the same letters, look for a fixed pattern of moving letters, such as swapping pairs, reversing each half, or reversing the whole word. Both words here have eight letters, so splitting them into four pairs is the natural first try.

  3. 2025 · Paper II Reasoning & Maths · Q3 · Reasoning & Mental Ability · Verbal Reasoning

    A class of boys is standing in only one row. A boy is at thirty third position from both the ends in this sequence. How many boys are there in the class?

    1. 64
    2. 65
    3. 69
    4. 66
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) 65

    There are 65 boys. The boy is 33rd from the left end, so there are 32 boys to his left; he is also 33rd from the right end, so there are 32 boys to his right. Total = boys to the left + the boy himself + boys to the right = 32 + 1 + 32 = 65. In formula form: total = (position from left) + (position from right) − 1 = 33 + 33 − 1 = 65. We subtract 1 because adding the two positions counts the boy twice. The wrong options come from common slips: 66 (D) forgets to subtract 1, 64 (A) subtracts 2, and 69 (C) has no basis. Related uses of the same idea: if there are 65 boys and one is 20th from the left, he is 65 − 20 + 1 = 46th from the right; and a boy standing exactly in the middle of an odd row of n is (n + 1) ÷ 2 from either end, here (65 + 1) ÷ 2 = 33, which confirms the answer. Remember: add the two positions, then remove the one person counted twice.

  4. 2025 · Paper II Reasoning & Maths · Q4 · Reasoning & Mental Ability · Verbal Reasoning

    Suraj drove his bicycle towards the north. Then he turned to the right and covered a distance of 2 km. Then, he turned to the right and covered a distance of 4 km. He found himself 2 km east of his starting point. How far did he go by bicycle in the north direction?

    1. 2 km
    2. 4 km
    3. 6 km
    4. 8 km
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) 4 km

    He rode 4 km towards the north. Let the northward distance be x km. Step 1: he rides x km north. Step 2: facing north, a right turn makes him face east; he rides 2 km east. Step 3: facing east, another right turn makes him face south; he rides 4 km south. Now compare with the start. East–west: he has gone 2 km east and nothing west, which matches '2 km east of his starting point'. North–south: he went x km north and 4 km south, and since he ends up exactly east of the start (neither north nor south of it), these must cancel: x − 4 = 0, so x = 4 km. A quick sketch shows the path: up 4, right 2, down 4. Option (A) 2 km would leave him 2 km south of that point, while (C) 6 km and (D) 8 km would leave him north of it. Tip: in direction problems, handle north–south and east–west movements separately, and remember that when facing north, right = east, and when facing east, right = south.

  5. 2025 · Paper II Reasoning & Maths · Q5 · Reasoning & Mental Ability · Verbal Reasoning

    At present, A is younger than B by 8 years. If 4 years ago, their ages were in the ratio 1 : 2, then what is the present age of B (in years)?

    1. 12
    2. 20
    3. 11
    4. 18
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) 20

    B's present age is 20 years. Let A's present age be x, so B = x + 8, because A is younger by 8 years. Four years ago their ages were x − 4 and x + 4. Given (x − 4) : (x + 4) = 1 : 2, cross-multiply: 2(x − 4) = x + 4, so 2x − 8 = x + 4, which gives x = 12. So A = 12 and B = 12 + 8 = 20 years. Check: four years ago A was 8 and B was 16, and 8 : 16 = 1 : 2. Quicker method: the age difference never changes. In the ratio 1 : 2 the difference is 2 − 1 = 1 part, and this part equals 8 years, so four years ago A was 8 and B was 16; adding 4 gives B = 20 now. Other options: 12 (A) is A's age, not B's, a common slip; 11 (C) and 18 (D) do not give 1 : 2 four years ago. Tip: in age problems, combine the constant difference with the ratio; it saves time.

    1. (IV), (I), (II), (V), (III)
    2. (IV), (I), (II), (III), (V)

  34. 2022 · Paper II Reasoning & Maths · Q6 · Reasoning & Mental Ability · Verbal Reasoning

    David gets on the elevator at the 11th floor of a building and rides up at the rate of 57 floors per minute. At the same time, Albert gets on an elevator at the 51st floor of the same building and rides down at the rate of 63 floors per minute. If they continue travelling at these rates, then at which floor will their paths cross ?

    1. 19
    2. 28
    3. 30
    4. 37
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) 30

    Their paths cross at the 30th floor. Step 1: the gap between them = 51 − 11 = 40 floors. Step 2: they move towards each other, David going up and Albert coming down, so the gap closes at the sum of their speeds: 57 + 63 = 120 floors per minute (relative speed). Step 3: time to meet = gap ÷ relative speed = 40 ÷ 120 = 1/3 minute, that is 20 seconds. Step 4: in 1/3 minute David rises 57 × 1/3 = 19 floors, so he reaches 11 + 19 = 30. Check with Albert: he drops 63 × 1/3 = 21 floors, and 51 − 21 = 30. Both give 30, so the paths cross at the 30th floor. Shortcut: the meeting point splits the 40-floor gap in the ratio of the speeds, 57 : 63 = 19 : 21, so David covers 19 of the 40 floors. Trap: 19 is the number of floors David climbs, not the floor where they meet; 28 and 37 do not pass either check. The same relative-speed idea solves questions on two trains or two people moving towards each other.

  35. 2022 · Paper II Reasoning & Maths · Q7 · Reasoning & Mental Ability · Verbal Reasoning

    An egg vendor calls on his first customer and sells half his eggs and half an egg. To the second customer, he sells half of what he has left with and half an egg, and to the third customer, he sells half of what he was then left with and half an egg. However, he did not break any egg. If in the end, the vendor was left with three eggs. How many eggs did he have initially ?

    1. 26
    2. 31
    3. 39
    4. None of these
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) 31

    He started with 31 eggs. Step 1: if he has x eggs and sells half of them plus half an egg, he sells x/2 + 1/2 and is left with x − (x/2 + 1/2) = (x − 1)/2. Step 2: so, working backwards, eggs before a sale = 2 × (eggs left) + 1. Step 3: after the third customer he had 3 eggs, so before that sale he had 2 × 3 + 1 = 7. Before the second sale: 2 × 7 + 1 = 15. At the start: 2 × 15 + 1 = 31. Forward check: from 31 he sells 15.5 + 0.5 = 16, leaving 15; then 7.5 + 0.5 = 8, leaving 7; then 3.5 + 0.5 = 4, leaving 3. Every sale is a whole number, so no egg is broken. Why not the others: 26 would need a first sale of 13.5 eggs (a broken egg), and 39 ends with 4, not 3. Shortcut: for n such sales, start = (end + 1) × 2ⁿ − 1 = (3 + 1) × 2³ − 1 = 32 − 1 = 31.

    Directions (Q. Nos. 3 to 5) : One hundred and twenty five cubes of the same size are arranged in the form of a cube on a table. Then a column of five cubes is removed from each of the four corners. All the exposed faces of the rest of the solid (except the face touching the table) are coloured red. Now, answer these questions based on the above statement : How many small cubes are there in the solid after the removal of the columns ?

    1. 120
    2. 110
    3. 105
    4. 100
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) 105

    105 cubes remain. Step 1: 125 cubes arranged as a cube means 5 × 5 × 5, since 5³ = 125, so each edge has 5 cubes and there are 5 layers. Step 2: a column at a corner is the vertical stack standing on one corner of the base; it is 5 cubes tall, one cube in each layer. Step 3: there are 4 vertical corner columns, so cubes removed = 4 × 5 = 20. Step 4: cubes left = 125 − 20 = 105. Layer check: each layer had 5 × 5 = 25 cubes; taking away its 4 corner cubes leaves 21, and 21 × 5 layers = 105. Why not the others: 120, 110 and 100 would mean 5, 15 or 25 cubes removed, not 20. This solid is the base for the next two questions: every layer is now a 5 × 5 square with its corners cut off, and only the top and the four sides are painted, not the base on the table. Tip: draw one layer as a 5 × 5 grid, cross out the corners, then multiply by the number of layers.

Then a column of five cubes is removed from each of the four corners. All the exposed faces of the rest of the solid (except the face touching the table) are coloured red. Now, answer these questions based on the above statement : How many cubes do not have any coloured face ?

    1. 12
    2. 24
    3. 36
    4. 48
    Show answer

    Answer: (3) 36

    36 cubes have no coloured face. A cube stays unpainted only if none of its faces is exposed, apart from a bottom face resting on the table, which is not coloured. Step 1: look at one layer. After the corners go, each 5 × 5 layer has 21 cubes: 12 on the outer border (3 on each side) and an inner 3 × 3 block of 9. Step 2: every border cube shows at least one side face, so all are painted. Removing the corner columns exposes faces only of the border cubes next to the corners; the inner 3 × 3 block stays hidden, because its corner cube touches a removed corner only diagonally, not face to face. Step 3: the top layer is painted on top, so its inner 9 are coloured. Step 4: each of the lower four layers keeps 9 unpainted cubes, including the bottom layer, whose under-faces rest on the table. Total = 9 × 4 = 36. Quick formula when the base is unpainted: (n − 2)² × (n − 1) = 3² × 4 = 36. The options 12, 24 and 48 come from miscounting the layers or the inner block.

    1. 40
    2. 25
    3. 20
    4. 15
    Show answer

    Answer: (2) 25

    25 cubes have exactly one red face. Count layer by layer. Top layer: of its 21 cubes, the inner 3 × 3 block (9 cubes) shows only its top face, so these 9 have one red face; the 12 border cubes show the top and at least one side, so they have two or more. Lower four layers: each has 12 border cubes, 3 on each side. On every side the two end cubes stand next to a removed corner column, so they show their outer side face and the newly exposed face, which makes two red faces. Only the middle cube of each side shows a single face, giving 4 per layer. The bottom layer behaves the same way, because its under-faces touch the table and stay unpainted. So 4 × 4 layers = 16. Total = 9 + 16 = 25. The other options (40, 20, 15) come from counting all border cubes as one-face cubes or from leaving out the top block or some layers.
