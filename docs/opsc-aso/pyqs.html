  1. 1 of 10 · OPSC ASO 2025 Paper 1 General Awareness · Q1 · History & National Movement

    What is the correct sequence of the following ? i. The Hartog Committee ii. The Hunter Education Commission iii. The Sadler University Commission iv. Kothari Education Commission

    Show answer

    Answer: (4) ii, iii, i, iv

    The correct order is ii, iii, i, iv: Hunter Education Commission (1882) → Sadler University Commission (1917–19) → Hartog Committee (1929) → Kothari Education Commission (1964–66). The Hunter Commission, appointed under Lord Ripon, reviewed the working of Wood's Despatch (1854) and stressed primary education, leaving more of secondary and higher education to private effort. The Sadler Commission (Calcutta University Commission) under Michael Sadler studied Calcutta University and recommended a 12-year school course, with an intermediate stage before a three-year degree. The Hartog Committee was an auxiliary committee of the Simon Commission; it pointed out the 'wastage and stagnation' in primary schools and favoured consolidation rather than rapid expansion. The Kothari Commission, chaired by D.S. Kothari after independence, proposed the 10+2+3 pattern and spending 6% of national income on education, and led to the first National Policy on Education in 1968. Option A puts Hartog first, while B and C place the post-1947 Kothari Commission before older colonial bodies. Memory tip: Hunter, Sadler, Hartog, Kothari runs 1882, 1917, 1929, 1964.

  2. 2 of 10 · OPSC ASO 2025 Paper 2 Reasoning & Maths · Q1 · Reasoning & Mental Ability

    Choose the most appropriate option to complete the following series. V, R, N, ______, F, B

    Show answer

    Answer: (3) J

    J completes the series. Write each letter's position in the alphabet: V = 22, R = 18, N = 14, F = 6, B = 2. Each step goes back by 4: 22 → 18 → 14 → ? → 6 → 2. So the missing term is 14 − 4 = 10, and the 10th letter is J. Check forwards from the blank: 10 − 4 = 6 = F, and 6 − 4 = 2 = B, so the series holds. The other options break the pattern: K (11) would make a gap of 3, H (8) a gap of 6, and P (16) would make the series go forward instead of backward. Shortcut: remember the EJOTY positions, E = 5, J = 10, O = 15, T = 20, Y = 25; with them you can find any letter's number in a second. Trap: three letters are skipped each time (V, skip U T S, R), so always count the gap, not just the next letter.

  3. 3 of 10 · OPSC ASO 2025 Paper 3 English & Odia · Q1 · English

    Directions : Fill in the blanks in the following sentences by selecting the most appropriate option from the choices given below each. The intricate design ______ meticulous attention during manufacturing.

    Show answer

    Answer: (1) calls for

    "Calls for" is the right phrasal verb: "call for" means to require, need or demand. An intricate (very detailed, complicated) design naturally requires meticulous (very careful, precise) attention, so "The intricate design calls for meticulous attention during manufacturing" makes perfect sense. "Comes to" means to reach a total or to regain consciousness ("The bill comes to ₹500"), which does not fit. "Gets over" means to recover from something ("She got over her illness"), and "puts off" means to postpone or to discourage ("They put off the meeting"); neither can take "attention" in this sense. Another example of the rule: "This situation calls for quick action." Tip: a phrasal verb can change its meaning completely with the particle, so learn them in sets: call for (require), call off (cancel), call on (visit), call up (phone or summon). Trap: "come to" looks similar in form but refers to totals, decisions or waking up.

  4. 4 of 10 · OPSC ASO 2022 Paper 1 General Awareness · Q1 · Indian Economy

    In the guidelines announced by Union Finance Minister in the 2022-2023 Budget, instead of using the term ‘Crypto Currency’, the term ______ is used.

    Show answer

    Answer: (4) Virtual Digital Assets

    Virtual Digital Assets (VDAs) is the term Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman used in the Union Budget 2022-23 (presented on 1 February 2022) instead of 'cryptocurrency'. VDA is a wide legal category that covers cryptocurrencies, NFTs (non-fungible tokens) and similar digital tokens. The Budget set up a tax regime for them: income from transferring a VDA is taxed at a flat 30%, with no deduction except the cost of acquisition; losses on VDAs cannot be set off against other income; a VDA received as a gift is taxed in the receiver's hands; and 1% TDS applies on transfers above a threshold (from 1 July 2022). The same Budget announced a Digital Rupee, a central bank digital currency to be issued by the RBI; its pilots began in late 2022. Why the other options are wrong: Bitcoin, Ether and altcoins are only kinds of crypto — Bitcoin is the first cryptocurrency (2009), Ether is the token of the Ethereum network, and 'altcoins' means all coins other than Bitcoin. None was the official term. Memory tip: Budget 2022 → VDA → 30% tax + 1% TDS.

  5. 5 of 10 · OPSC ASO 2022 Paper 2 Reasoning & Maths · Q1 · Reasoning & Mental Ability

    Select the correct option that indicates the arrangement of the given words in the order in which they appear in a telephone directory : (I) Krishanmurty (II) Krishnamurthy (III) Krishnmurthi (IV) Krishanmurthy (V) Krishnamurti

    Show answer

    Answer: (1) (IV), (I), (II), (V), (III)

    The order is (IV), (I), (II), (V), (III). A telephone directory lists names letter by letter, as a dictionary does. All five names begin with 'Krish', so compare the 6th letter: (I) and (IV) have 'a' (Krisha…), while (II), (III) and (V) have 'n' (Krishn…); so (I) and (IV) come first. Between Krishanmurthy (IV) and Krishanmurty (I), the first eleven letters 'Krishanmurt' match; the 12th letter is 'h' in (IV) and 'y' in (I), and h comes before y, so (IV) leads. Among the 'Krishn…' names, look at the 7th letter: (II) and (V) have 'a', but (III) has 'm', so (III) goes last. Between Krishnamurthy (II) and Krishnamurti (V), the 12th letter is 'h' against 'i', so (II) comes before (V). Final order: IV, I, II, V, III. Option B wrongly puts (III) before (V), option C misplaces (V) and (I), and option D even repeats (III). Tip: move from left to right and decide at the first letter that differs; ignore everything after it.

  6. 6 of 10 · OPSC ASO 2022 Paper 3 English & Odia · Q1 · English

    Change the active voice into passive voice : I will clean the house every Saturday. The house ______ by me every Saturday.

    Show answer

    Answer: (3) will be cleaned

    The passive form is 'will be cleaned'. In the simple future tense, the passive voice is made with will + be + past participle. The object of the active sentence ('the house') becomes the subject, and the doer ('I') moves to a by-phrase as 'by me'. So 'I will clean the house every Saturday' becomes 'The house will be cleaned by me every Saturday'. The tense stays future, and the time phrase 'every Saturday' stays as it is. 'Cleaned' alone turns the sentence into the simple past and even makes the house the doer ('The house cleaned'). 'Will cleaned' is ungrammatical, since a modal such as will must be followed by a base verb, here 'be'. 'None of these' is wrong because option C is correct. Another example: 'She will write a letter' becomes 'A letter will be written by her'. Memory tip for passives: keep the tense of the active verb, add the right form of 'be', then the past participle (V3), and change the pronoun 'I' to 'me' in the by-phrase.

  7. 7 of 10 · OPSC ASO 2025 Paper 1 General Awareness · Q2 · Indian Polity

    Which of the following Articles are related to the Right to Religion ?

    Show answer

    Answer: (3) Articles 25 to 28

    Articles 25 to 28 together form the Right to Freedom of Religion in Part III of the Constitution. Article 25 gives every person freedom of conscience and the right to freely profess, practise and propagate religion, subject to public order, morality and health. Article 26 lets religious denominations manage their own affairs and institutions. Article 27 bars compulsory taxes whose proceeds are used to promote a particular religion. Article 28 forbids religious instruction in educational institutions wholly maintained out of State funds. The other groups belong to different rights: Articles 14–18 are the Right to Equality, Articles 19–22 the Right to Freedom (including life and personal liberty in Article 21), and Articles 29–30 the Cultural and Educational Rights, such as the right of minorities to set up and run their own educational institutions. Note that Article 25 protects every person, citizens and foreigners alike. Memory tip for the Part III blocks: Equality 14, Freedom 19, Against Exploitation 23, Religion 25, Culture and Education 29, Remedies 32.

  8. 8 of 10 · OPSC ASO 2025 Paper 2 Reasoning & Maths · Q2 · Reasoning & Mental Ability

    In a certain code language, STRAIGHT is written as TSARGITH. How will THURSDAY be written as in that language?

    Show answer

    Answer: (2) HTRUDSYA

    HTRUDSYA follows the same code. Step 1: split STRAIGHT into pairs of letters: ST | RA | IG | HT. Step 2: split the code TSARGITH the same way: TS | AR | GI | TH. Each pair has simply been reversed: ST → TS, RA → AR, IG → GI, HT → TH. Step 3: apply the same rule to THURSDAY: TH | UR | SD | AY becomes HT | RU | DS | YA, which joins up as HTRUDSYA, option (B). Check the others: (A) AYSDURTH keeps each pair as it is but reverses the order of the pairs (AY SD UR TH), a different rule; (C) UHTDRSYA moves the letters in a way that matches nothing in the example; (D) HTRUDSAY swaps the first three pairs but leaves the last pair AY unchanged, so it breaks the rule at the end. Tip: when a word and its code use exactly the same letters, look for a fixed pattern of moving letters, such as swapping pairs, reversing each half, or reversing the whole word. Both words here have eight letters, so splitting them into four pairs is the natural first try.

  9. 9 of 10 · OPSC ASO 2025 Paper 3 English & Odia · Q2 · English

    Directions : Fill in the blanks in the following sentences by selecting the most appropriate option from the choices given below each. Each of the buildings ______ unique design.

    Show answer

    Answer: (2) has

    "Has" is correct because "each" is singular and takes a singular verb. In "Each of the buildings has a unique design", the real subject is "Each" (each one), and "of the buildings" is only a phrase describing it, so the verb agrees with "Each", not with "buildings". "Have" is the plural form, "are" is plural and also gives no sense of possession, and "were" is plural past tense. The same rule applies to every, either, neither, one of, everyone and each one: "One of my friends lives in Cuttack"; "Neither of the answers is correct." The printed sentence leaves out the article "a" before "unique design", but that does not change the choice of verb: only "has" agrees with the subject. Trap: don't let the plural noun just before the verb ("buildings") pull the verb into the plural; this "proximity" error is a favourite trap in competitive exams.

  10. 10 of 10 · OPSC ASO 2022 Paper 1 General Awareness · Q2 · Current Affairs

    Which State in India presented ‘Child Budget’ as part of the Annual State Budget ?

    Show answer

    Answer: (4) Madhya Pradesh

    Madhya Pradesh presented a separate 'Child Budget' for the first time along with its annual budget for 2022-23. Finance Minister Jagdish Devda tabled the budget on 9 March 2022, and the child budget brought together in one statement the money set aside across many departments — such as school education, health, and women and child development — for schemes meant for children below 18. A child budget is not a separate fund; it tags existing spending so that the government and the public can track how much actually reaches children, much as a gender budget does for women. The key picks D because the question is based on the March 2022 news, when Madhya Pradesh's move was widely reported. Gujarat and Sikkim were not part of that news. Note, however, that Andhra Pradesh had already presented a child budget with its 2021-22 budget, so option A is not strictly wrong. Tip: in current-affairs questions, link the fact to the year of the news (here, 2022-23 and Madhya Pradesh). (Official key; some sources differ.)
