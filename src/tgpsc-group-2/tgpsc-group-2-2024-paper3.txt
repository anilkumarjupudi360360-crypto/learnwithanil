Q 1 | eco | Poverty, Employment & Human Development
Which of the following statements is/are correct in relation to the infant and neonatal mortality rates in India as per the National Family Health Survey (NFHS)–5 (2019–21) ?
A. Infant mortality rate (IMR) decreased when compared to National Family Health Survey (NFHS) – 4 (2015–16).
B. Neonatal mortality rate (NNMR) decreased when compared to NFHS – 4 (2015–16).
Choose the correct answer :
(1) A only
(2) B only
(3) Both A and B
(4) Neither A nor B
= 3
~ NFHS-5 (2019–21) recorded an infant mortality rate of 35.2 per 1,000 live births, down from 40.7 in NFHS-4 (2015–16). The neonatal mortality rate fell from 29.5 to 24.9 over the same period. Since both rates decreased, both statements are correct.

Q 2 | eco | Poverty, Employment & Human Development
Which of the following statements are true in relation to India’s population ?
A. Sex ratio of population is expressed as number of females per 1000 males.
B. If sex ratio of population is more than thousand, it is called unfavourable sex ratio.
C. The sex ratio of population in India in 2011 was less than 1000.
Choose the correct answer :
(1) A and B only
(2) A and C only
(3) B and C only
(4) A, B and C
= 2
~ In India the sex ratio is expressed as the number of females per 1,000 males, so A is correct. A ratio above 1,000 means there are more females than males and is called a favourable sex ratio, not an unfavourable one, so B is wrong. Census 2011 recorded a sex ratio of 943, below 1,000, so C is correct. Hence A and C only.

Q 3 | eco | Poverty, Employment & Human Development
In which of the following years was the National Population Policy announced in India ?
(1) 2014
(2) 2016
(3) 2000
(4) 2020
= 3
~ The National Population Policy was announced in 2000. Its immediate objective was to meet the unmet need for contraception and health infrastructure, its medium-term objective was to bring the total fertility rate down to replacement level (2.1) by 2010, and its long-term objective was a stable population by 2045.

Q 4 | eco | Poverty, Employment & Human Development
The total fertility rate (TFR) in India during 2015–16 as per the National Family Health Survey (NFHS)–4 was nearly :
(1) 1·6
(2) 1·9
(3) 2·2
(4) 2·5
= 3
~ NFHS-4 (2015–16) put India's total fertility rate, the average number of children a woman would bear in her lifetime, at 2.2. This was just above the replacement level of 2.1. In NFHS-5 (2019–21) the TFR fell further to 2.0, below replacement level.

Q 5 | eco | Poverty, Employment & Human Development
Which of the following statements is/are true in relation to crude death rate (CDR) of India’s population ?
A. During 2014–19, the crude death rate was less than 7.
B. During 2006–11, the crude death rate was more than 7.
Choose the correct answer :
(1) A only
(2) B only
(3) Both A and B
(4) Neither A nor B
= 3
~ The crude death rate is the number of deaths per 1,000 population in a year. According to the Sample Registration System, it stayed above 7 throughout 2006–11 (7.5 in 2006, 7.1 in 2011). It fell to 6.7 in 2014 and 6.0 in 2019, so it was below 7 throughout 2014–19. Both statements are true.

Q 6 | eco | Poverty, Employment & Human Development
The rate of natural population growth in India had been steadily declining since the early 1980s, despite an increase in absolute number of population, owing to :
(1) The falling of birth rate was faster than death rate.
(2) The falling of birth rate was slower than death rate.
(3) The increase of birth rate was faster than death rate.
(4) The increase of birth rate and death rate was almost same.
= 1
~ The natural growth rate is the birth rate minus the death rate. The death rate had already fallen sharply after 1921, and from the early 1980s the birth rate began to fall faster than the death rate, so the gap between them narrowed. Births still exceed deaths, so the absolute population keeps rising even though its growth rate declines.

Q 7 | eco | Poverty, Employment & Human Development
Which of the following schemes are implemented in India to reduce population growth ?
A. Accredited Social Health Activist (ASHA) scheme
B. Scheme for provision of Pregnancy Testing Kits
C. Mission Parivar Vikas
D. Clinical Outreach Teams (COT) scheme
Choose the correct answer :
(1) A and C only
(2) B and D only
(3) A, C and D only
(4) A, B, C and D
= 4
~ All four are family-planning measures of the Union Health Ministry. ASHAs deliver contraceptives to households and counsel couples on spacing births, and Pregnancy Testing Kits are supplied in ASHA kits for early detection of pregnancy. Mission Parivar Vikas (2016) targets high-fertility districts, and Clinical Outreach Teams provide family-planning services in remote, underserved areas. Hence A, B, C and D.

Q 11 | eco | Growth, Planning & Public Finance
In the national income accounting concepts, any excess of tax revenue over government spending is called :
(1) Public goods
(2) Public deficit
(3) Public saving
(4) Public expenditure
= 3
~ When tax revenue (T) exceeds government spending (G), the surplus T – G is public (government) saving; national saving is private saving plus public saving. A public deficit is the opposite case, where spending exceeds revenue, and public goods and public expenditure are not names for this excess.

Q 12 | eco | Growth, Planning & Public Finance
Which of the following is not a component of total demand for domestic income/output ?
(1) Consumption spending by households
(2) Investment spending by business firms
(3) Government purchases of goods and services
(4) Net borrowings from other countries
= 4
~ Aggregate demand for domestic output is the sum of household consumption (C), business investment (I), government purchases of goods and services (G) and net exports (X – M). Net borrowing from other countries is a capital-account financing flow, not spending on domestic goods and services, so it is not a component.

Q 13 | eco | Growth, Planning & Public Finance
What is the term used in national income accounting when depreciation charges are deducted from a country’s Gross Domestic Product ?
(1) Net National Product
(2) Net Domestic Product
(3) Real Net Domestic Product
(4) National Income at constant prices
= 2
~ Net Domestic Product = Gross Domestic Product – depreciation (consumption of fixed capital). Net National Product is GNP (GDP plus net factor income from abroad) minus depreciation, while "real" and "at constant prices" refer to removing the effect of price changes, not depreciation.

Q 14 | eco | Growth, Planning & Public Finance
Which of the following statements is/are true in relation to the concepts of personal and disposable incomes ?
A. The consumption expenditure + savings of an individual is equal to disposable income of an individual.
B. If the personal taxes are added to disposable income of an individual, we get personal income.
Choose the correct answer :
(1) A only
(2) B only
(3) Both A and B
(4) Neither A nor B
= 1
~ Disposable income is the income a person can either spend or save, so consumption + saving = disposable income and A is correct. As NCERT defines it, personal disposable income = personal income – personal tax payments – non-tax payments (fees, fines), so adding back only personal taxes does not give personal income; the key treats B as incomplete. Some textbooks treat B as correct and give "both A and B". (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 15 | eco | Growth, Planning & Public Finance
Who were the chairman and members of the National Income Committee set up in 1949 by the Government of India ?
(1) V.K.R.V. Rao as the chairman and P.C. Mahalanobis and D.R. Gadgil as the members.
(2) P.C. Mahalanobis as the chairman and D.R. Gadgil and V.K.R.V. Rao as the members.
(3) D.R. Gadgil as the chairman and Dadabhai Naoroji and P.C. Mahalanobis as the members.

Q 16 | eco | Growth, Planning & Public Finance
Which of the following statements are true in relation to national income in India ?
A. The first official estimates of national income were prepared by the Ministry of Statistics, Programme Implementation.
B. The first official estimates of national income were prepared with base year 1948–49 at constant prices.
C. The estimates of national income published in 1956 were known as the conventional series.
D. National income under the title conventional series was published till 1976.
Choose the correct answer :
(1) A, B and C only
(2) B and C only
(3) A and D only
(4) A, C and D only
= 2
~ The first official estimates of national income, for 1948–49, were prepared by the Ministry of Commerce and then by the National Income Committee; the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation was formed only in 1999, so A is wrong. In 1956 the CSO published estimates with 1948–49 as the base year at constant prices, known as the conventional series. This series was replaced in 1967 by a revised series with base year 1960–61, so D (till 1976) is wrong. Hence B and C only.

Q 17 | eco | Agriculture & Rural Economy
As per the second advance estimate of national income 2022–23 released by the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation of India, arrange the following years in ascending order based on the share of agriculture and allied sectors in gross value added (at current prices) in each year :
A. 2020–21
B. 2021–22
C. 2022–23
Choose the correct answer :
(1) C, A, B
(2) B, C, A
(3) C, B, A
(4) A, C, B
= 3

Q 19 | eco | Growth, Planning & Public Finance
Match the following List-I with List-II :
List-I | List-II
A. Gross National Product at market prices | I. Net Domestic Product at market prices + Depreciation
B. Net National Product at market prices | II. Gross Domestic Product at market prices – Depreciation
C. Gross Domestic Product at market prices | III. Net National Product at factor cost + Indirect taxes – Subsidies
 | IV. Net National Product at market prices + Depreciation
Choose the correct answer :
(1) A-III; B-I; C-II
(2) A-IV; B-III; C-I
(3) A-II; B-IV; C-III
(4) A-I; B-II; C-IV
= 2
~ GNP at market prices = NNP at market prices + depreciation (A-IV). NNP at market prices = NNP at factor cost + indirect taxes – subsidies (B-III). GDP at market prices = NDP at market prices + depreciation (C-I). Item II (GDP at market prices – depreciation) gives NDP at market prices, which is not in List-I.

Q 20 | eco | Growth, Planning & Public Finance
Which of the following statements is/are true in relation to national income ?
A. National income calculated with a base year is known as national income at constant prices.
B. National income estimated at current prices is not inclusive of price changes.
C. During war period, national income is estimated at future prices also.
D. National income divided by total population gives personal income.
Choose the correct answer :
(1) B and C only
(2) A only
(3) A and D only
(4) C and D only
= 2
~ National income at constant prices is valued at the prices of a chosen base year, so A is true. Estimates at current prices use each year's own prices and therefore include price changes, so B is false. National income is not estimated at "future prices", so C is false, and national income divided by population gives per capita income, not personal income, so D is false. Hence A only.

Q 21 | eco | Agriculture & Rural Economy
Which of the following is not the primary goal of the Indian Government’s Minimum Support Price Policy, which has been fixed at an incentive level ?
(1) To induce more investment by the farmers in the farm sector.
(2) To enable the poor to buy foodgrains at very low prices.
(3) To motivate farmers to adopt improved crop production technologies.
(4) To enhance production, thereby increasing farmers’ income.
= 2
~ MSPs, recommended by the Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices, are announced before sowing and fixed at an incentive level so that farmers invest more, adopt improved technology and raise production and income. Supplying foodgrains to the poor at low prices is the goal of the Public Distribution System, which works through low issue prices, not of the MSP policy.
