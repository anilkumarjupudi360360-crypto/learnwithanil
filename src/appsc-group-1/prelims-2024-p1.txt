Q 1 | ih | Ancient India
Consider the following statements on the late Harappan cultures:
I. The late Harappan cultures are primarily Chalcolithic in which tools of stone and copper are used.
II. They do not show metal objects requiring complicated casting.
III. The Chalcolithic people in the later Harappan phase lived in villages.
IV. In the late Harappan phase at Bhagwanpura in Haryana, burnt bricks were not found.
Which of the above statements are correct?
(1) I, II and IV
(2) I, II and III
(3) II, III and IV
(4) I, II, III and IV
= 2
~ Statements I-III follow the standard (R.S. Sharma) description of the late Harappan phase: village-based chalcolithic cultures using stone and copper tools and lacking metal objects that need complicated casting. Statement IV is wrong because the late Harappan level at Bhagwanpura did have houses of burnt brick, so the answer is I, II and III.

Q 31 | pol | Parliament
Which of the following statement/statements is/are valid regarding the office of the 'Whip'?
I. Although not mentioned in the Constitution of India, the office of the 'Whip' has however been incorporated in the Rules of the House by the 'Leaders and Chief Whips of Recognised Parties and Groups in Parliament (Facilities) Act, 1998'.
II. The role of the Chief Whip of the government party in the Lok Sabha is served by the Minister of State for Parliamentary Affairs.
III. All political parties - whether ruling or opposition, will have their own Whips in the Parliament.
IV. The Chief Whip is not responsible to the leader of the House but is responsible only to the leader of the party that has appointed him/her to the office of Chief Whip.
Select the correct answer using the codes given below:
(1) I and IV
(2) Only III
(3) II and III
(4) II and IV
= 2
~ The office of whip is not provided for in the Constitution or in the Rules of the House; it rests on parliamentary convention, and the 1998 Act only gives facilities to leaders and chief whips of recognised parties, so I is wrong. In the Lok Sabha the ruling party's Chief Whip is the Minister of Parliamentary Affairs (not a Minister of State) and is responsible to the Leader of the House, so II and IV are wrong, while every party, ruling or opposition, appoints its own whips, so only III is valid.

Q 32 | pol | Parliament
In the context of distinctions existing between a Public Bill and a Private Bill, select the statement/statements that is/are valid:
I. A Private Bill which has been introduced by a Member of Parliament sitting in the opposition and which has been approved by the House will have to be referred to the law department for consultation.
II. A Public Bill is always introduced in the Parliament by the Minister or a member of the government party.
III. A Private Bill will not reflect the policy of the government but upon its approval by the House, a Private Bill would have reflected the policy of the government.
IV. Public Bills and Private Bills, regardless of the differences between them, are governed by the same general procedure, and are required to be passed through the same stages in the House.
Select the correct answer using the codes given below:
(1) II and III
(2) I, III and IV
(3) Only I
(4) Only IV
= 4
~ A public (government) bill is introduced only by a minister, while a private (member's) bill is introduced by any MP who is not a minister, even one from the ruling party, so II is wrong; drafting in consultation with the law department is a feature of government bills, and a private bill does not become government policy merely because the House passes it, so I and III are wrong. Both kinds of bill, however, follow the same general procedure and pass through the same stages in the House, so only IV is valid.

Q 33 | pol | State Government
Which of the following State Bill/Bills will have to be mandatorily reserved for the President's consideration?
I. A Bill that proposes to give effect to certain Directive Principles of State Policy.
II. A Bill requiring prior Presidential sanction but which was however not obtained under Article 304.
III. A Bill that attracts the provisions of Article 288.
IV. A Bill seeking to operate a provision that belongs to one of the Concurrent List entries, but which is however repugnant to any Union law.
Select the correct answer using the codes given below:
(1) I and IV
(2) I, II and IV
(3) Only III
(4) I and III
= 3
~ Article 288(2) expressly says that a State law taxing water or electricity stored, generated, consumed, distributed or sold by an authority set up by Parliament for an inter-State river or river valley has no effect unless it is reserved for the President's consideration and receives his assent, so III must be reserved. The Constitution lays down no such express requirement for the others: a DPSP-based bill is reserved only if it seeks Article 31C protection, a repugnant Concurrent List bill only if it is to prevail under Article 254(2), and Article 304(b) calls for the President's previous sanction before introduction rather than reservation.

Q 34 | pol | Constitutional & Statutory Bodies
Consider the following statements in connection with the office of Comptroller and Auditor General of India (CAG):
I. CAG can be removed by the President only in accordance with the procedure mentioned in the Constitution but this does not mean that the CAG can be impeached from office in the same manner as a Judge of the Supreme Court.
II. Some public corporations are audited exclusively by private professional auditors, and the CAG may have no involvement at all.
III. The CAG can conduct legal, regulatory and propriety audit but not performance audit.
IV. The Constitution of India has not specified any criterion for the appointment of CAG.
Which of the above statement/statements is/are valid?
(1) I and III
(2) Only IV
(3) II and III
(4) II and IV
= 4
~ Under Article 148 the CAG can be removed only in the same manner and on the same grounds as a Supreme Court judge, so I is wrong, and besides legal, regulatory and propriety audit the CAG also conducts performance (efficiency) audit, so III is wrong. Some public corporations are audited solely by private professional auditors with no role for the CAG, and the Constitution prescribes no qualifications for the CAG, so II and IV are valid.

Q 35 | pol | Constitutional & Statutory Bodies
In connection with the Goods and Services Tax (GST) Council, which of the following statement/statements is/are valid?
I. The GST Council was established as a Statutory body, but it was later turned into a Constitutional federal body by an amendment of the Constitution.
II. The proceedings of the GST Council will be invalidated in the eventuality of any defect in the appointment of a person as a member of the Council.
III. In the event of any dispute between more than two states over GST issues, the GST Council has to refer the matter to be adjudicated by the Public Accounts Committee of the Parliament.
IV. The GST Council is required to make recommendations to the Centre and the States on the threshold limit of turnover below which goods and services may be exempted from GST.
Select the correct answer using the codes given below:
(1) II and III
(2) I and IV
(3) Only IV
(4) Only II
= 3
~ The GST Council was created directly as a constitutional body under Article 279A, inserted by the 101st Amendment Act, 2016, so I is wrong; Article 279A(10) says its proceedings are not invalidated by any vacancy or defect in its constitution or in the appointment of a member, and disputes are settled by a mechanism the Council itself sets up (Article 279A(11)), not by the PAC. Article 279A(4) does require the Council to recommend the threshold turnover below which goods and services may be exempted from GST, so only IV is valid.

(3) Only II
(4) I, II and IV
