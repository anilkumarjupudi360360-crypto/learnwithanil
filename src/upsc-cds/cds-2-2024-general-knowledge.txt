# UPSC Combined Defence Services Examination (II) 2024: General Knowledge (booklet RAKU-S-GKL, Series A), held 1 September 2024.
# Source: the official UPSC question paper. UPSC has not published its answer key: answers worked out and checked by Learn with Anil. Explanations by Learn with Anil.

Q 1 | sci | Biology
Which one among the following is not a micronutrient for plants ?
(1) Iron
(2) Manganese
(3) Magnesium
(4) Chlorine
= 3
~ Magnesium is a macronutrient (secondary nutrient) needed in large amounts as the central atom of chlorophyll. Iron, manganese and chlorine are micronutrients needed only in traces.

Q 2 | sci | Biology
Xylem in the plants are responsible for the transport of :
(1) Water
(2) Water and dissolved minerals
(3) Gases
(4) Food
= 2
~ Xylem carries water together with the minerals dissolved in it from the roots to the leaves. Food is carried by phloem.

Q 3 | sci | Biology
Which one among the following hormones inhibits the growth activity in plants ?
(1) Auxins
(2) Cytokinins
(3) Abscisic acid
(4) Gibberellins
= 3
~ Abscisic acid (ABA) is the growth-inhibiting "stress hormone" that causes dormancy and stomatal closure. Auxins, cytokinins and gibberellins promote growth.

Q 4 | sci | Biology
Which of the following category of biomolecules does Starch belong to ?
(1) Protein
(2) Carbohydrate
(3) Lipid
(4) Nucleic acid
= 2
~ Starch is a polysaccharide made of many glucose units, so it is a carbohydrate (the storage food of plants).

Q 5 | sci | Biology
Which one among the following is not a feature of Xerophytes ?
(1) Smaller leaves
(2) Large number of stomata
(3) Waxy cuticle
(4) Stomata in pits
= 2
~ Xerophytes (desert plants) reduce water loss, so they have small leaves, thick waxy cuticle, sunken stomata and fewer stomata. A large number of stomata would increase transpiration.

Q 6 | geo | Physical Geography
Halophytes are the plants that grow on which one, among the following categories of soil ?
(1) Acidic soil
(2) Saline soil
(3) Black soil
(4) Sandy soil
= 2
~ Halophytes (e.g. mangroves) are salt-tolerant plants that grow in saline soils and salt marshes.

Q 7 | sci | Biology
Which one among the following is not a part of vascular bundle in plants ?
(1) Phloem
(2) Xylem
(3) Cambium
(4) Endodermis
= 4
~ A vascular bundle consists of xylem, phloem and (in open bundles of dicots) cambium. The endodermis is the innermost layer of the cortex surrounding the stele, not part of the bundle.

Q 8 | sci | Physics
In which one among the following mediums would the speed of sound be maximum ?
(1) Distilled water
(2) Sea water
(3) Drinking water
(4) Methanol
= 2
~ Sound travels at about 1530 m/s in sea water against about 1480 m/s in pure water and about 1100 m/s in methanol; dissolved salts make sea water less compressible, so sound is fastest there.

Q 9 | sci | Biology
Which organelle in animal cells can digest foreign materials ?
(1) Endoplasmic reticulum
(2) Lysosome
(3) Golgi body
(4) Mitochondria
= 2
~ Lysosomes contain hydrolytic (digestive) enzymes that break down foreign material, worn-out organelles and microbes; they are called the "suicide bags" of the cell.

Q 10 | sci | Biology
Photosynthesis that converts light energy into chemical energy by the plants, involves the use of green pigments of leaves called chlorophyll. The only structure in a leaf that hosts chlorophyll is :
(1) Nucleus
(2) Ribosome
(3) Chloroplast
(4) Chromosome
= 3
~ Chlorophyll is located in the thylakoid membranes of chloroplasts, the site of photosynthesis.

Q 11 | sci | Chemistry
In which of the following colloids is the dispersing medium a liquid ?
(1) Mist
(2) Foam
(3) Jelly
(4) Shaving cream
= 2
~ A foam is a gas dispersed in a liquid, so its dispersion medium is liquid; mist is liquid in gas (medium gas) and jelly is a gel, liquid in solid (medium solid). Shaving cream is itself a kind of foam, so (4) is also defensible, but "foam" is the textbook colloid type with a liquid medium. (Answer by Learn with Anil; UPSC has not published its key.)

Q 12 | sci | Chemistry
The two isotopes of elemental chlorine are :
(1) ³⁵₁₇C and ³⁶₁₇C
(2) ³⁴₁₇C and ³⁶₁₈C
(3) ³⁵₁₇C and ³⁷₁₈C
(4) ³⁵₁₇C and ³⁷₁₇C
= 4
~ The symbol printed as "C" stands for chlorine (Cl). Chlorine (atomic number 17) has two stable isotopes, Cl-35 and Cl-37; isotopes must have the same atomic number, which rules out (2) and (3).

Q 13 | sci | Chemistry
Which one among the following metals is not stored under oil ?
(1) Sodium
(2) Rubidium
(3) Potassium
(4) Lithium
= 4
~ Lithium is so light that it floats on kerosene/oil, so it is usually stored wrapped in paraffin wax (or under inert gas). Sodium, potassium and rubidium are kept under kerosene oil to keep them away from air and moisture.

Q 14 | sci | Chemistry
Potassium nitrate is not used :
(1) as a fertilizer
(2) in medicine
(3) in making gunpowder
(4) as a preservative of meat products
= 2
~ Potassium nitrate (saltpetre) is a major fertilizer, the oxidiser in gunpowder and a curing salt for preserving meat. Its medicinal role is minor (e.g. in toothpaste for sensitive teeth), so "medicine" is the intended odd one out. (Answer by Learn with Anil; UPSC has not published its key.)

Q 15 | sci | Chemistry
The surface of the concrete structure of a new construction is covered with straw/gunny bags by wetting. This is done to :
(1) prevent fast evaporation, until hydration has proceeded well
(2) protect the concrete structure from contamination by dust particles
(3) prevent development of any fungus on the surface
(4) give smoother and cleaner surface over cement structure
= 1
~ This is "curing": the setting of cement is a hydration reaction, so wet gunny bags keep the surface moist and stop water evaporating before hydration is complete, giving strength and preventing cracks.

Q 16 | sci | Chemistry
Which one among the following statements for the gases mentioned below is not correct ?
(1) Hydrogen has 3 isotopes.
(2) Helium is a commonly used gas in electric bulbs.
(3) Oxygen is used to burn fuel in rocket engines in spacecraft.
(4) Nitrogen can be used in gaseous form to replace air in food packaging.
= 2
~ Incandescent bulbs are filled with argon or nitrogen, not helium. Hydrogen does have three isotopes (protium, deuterium, tritium), liquid oxygen is the oxidiser in rockets, and nitrogen flushing is used in food packets.

Q 18 | sci | Chemistry
Which one among the following statements is not true for glass ?
(1) Sodium silicate is known as water glass.
(2) Glass is also called super cooled liquid.
(3) Crystallinity develops on cooling of the molten mass of the glass.
(4) Coloured glasses can be made by adding different colouring materials.
= 3
~ Glass is an amorphous solid (a supercooled liquid): the molten mass cools without forming an ordered crystal lattice, so statement (3) is false. Sodium silicate is indeed "water glass" and metal oxides are added to colour glass.

Q 19 | sci | Chemistry
Which one among the following is responsible for determining the chemical properties of an element ?
(1) Protons
(2) Electrons
(3) Neutrons
(4) Nucleus
= 2
~ Chemical reactions involve losing, gaining or sharing electrons, so the electronic configuration (especially valence electrons) decides an element's chemical properties.

Q 21 | sci | Physics
X-rays can be used to :
1. inspect welded joints between two metal parts of a machine.
2. study structure of crystals.
Select the answer using the code given below :
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 3
~ X-ray radiography finds hidden cracks and flaws in welds and castings, and X-ray diffraction (Bragg's law) reveals the atomic arrangement in crystals. Both uses are correct.

Q 22 | sci | Physics
Which one of the following heat transfers is an example of convection ?
(1) Heating of food in a microwave oven
(2) Boiling water in a pot on a gas stove
(3) Feeling the warmth in sun
(4) Heating a brass rod at one end and observing the temperature rise at the other end
= 2
~ In a pot of water, heated water at the bottom rises and cooler water sinks, setting up convection currents. Sunlight warmth is radiation, a heated brass rod shows conduction, and a microwave heats food by absorbing radiation.

Q 23 | sci | Physics
The phenomenon of rainbow formation by water droplets involves :
(1) single refraction of sunlight
(2) one internal reflection of sunlight
(3) two internal reflections of sunlight
(4) both refraction and internal reflection of sunlight
= 4
~ In a raindrop, sunlight is refracted (and dispersed) on entering, internally reflected at the back, and refracted again on leaving, so a rainbow involves both refraction and internal reflection.

Q 24 | geo | Environment & Ecology
Ozone hole is a region over :
(1) the Antarctic caused mainly by CFC gases.
(2) the Antarctic caused mainly by carbon dioxide gas.
(3) the Arctic caused mainly by CFC gases.
(4) the Arctic caused mainly by carbon dioxide gas.
= 1
~ The ozone hole forms each spring over Antarctica, where chlorine released from chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) destroys stratospheric ozone on polar stratospheric clouds. CO₂ is a greenhouse gas, not the cause of ozone depletion.

Q 26 | sci | Physics
Which one of the following is the most appropriate reason for aircraft being equipped with conducting tyres ?
(1) They can transfer the charge accumulated on their surfaces during lightning
(2) They can transfer the charge accumulated on their surfaces being rubbed by air particles
(3) They can transfer the charge accumulated on their surfaces due to inflight operation of several gadgets
(4) For preventing sparks while they land on the ground
= 2
~ An aircraft's body becomes charged by friction with air during flight; conducting tyres pass this static charge to the earth on landing. Option (4) is the consequence (avoiding sparks), but the charge-transfer reason in (2) is the standard textbook answer. (Answer by Learn with Anil; UPSC has not published its key.)

Q 27 | sci | Biology
Which of the following statements for persons suffering from presbyopia is/are correct ?
1. They cannot distinguish colours.
2. It usually develops with ageing.
3. They wear either bi-focal lenses consisting of both concave and convex lenses or progressive lenses.
Select the answer using the code given below :
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) 1 and 3
(4) 2 and 3
= 4
~ Presbyopia comes with age as the ciliary muscles weaken and the lens loses flexibility; it is corrected with bifocal (concave upper, convex lower) or progressive lenses. Inability to distinguish colours is colour blindness, not presbyopia.

Q 28 | sci | Physics
A sound wave having frequency of 300 Hz is travelling in an unknown medium. Its wavelength is not known. It travels a distance equal to 150 times its wavelength in time t. The value of t is :
(1) 0·5 s
(2) 1 s
(3) 1·5 s
(4) 2 s
= 1
~ The wave covers one wavelength in one period T = 1/f, so t = 150 × T = 150/300 = 0·5 s, whatever the medium.

Q 30 | sci | Physics
In Sun-Earth system, the Sun, the Earth and the Lagrange point L4 form :
(1) an isosceles triangle
(2) an equilateral triangle
(3) a straight line

Q 100 | eco | Growth, Planning & National Income
Suppose there are only two normal goods in the economy, X and Y. If price of good X increases, which would be the correct statement from below ?
(1) Demand for good X decreases and demand for Y is indeterminate.
(2) Demand for good X decreases and demand for Y decreases.
(3) Demand for good X increases and demand for Y is indeterminate.
(4) Demand for good X increases and demand for Y decreases.
= 1
~ For a normal good both the substitution and income effects cut demand when its price rises, so demand for X falls. For Y, the substitution effect raises demand but the lower real income reduces it (Y is normal), so the net change is indeterminate.

Q 101 | eco | Money, Banking & Inflation

(1) 10%
(2) 32%
(3) 20%
(4) Cannot be determined
= 3
~ Inflation is measured year on year: 2022 = (110 − 100)/100 = 10% and 2023 = (132 − 110)/110 = 20%. The current (latest) rate of inflation is therefore 20%, while 32% is the total rise over two years. (Answer by Learn with Anil; UPSC has not published its key.)
