Q 1 | eng | Synonyms & Antonyms
Directions: Select the option that is opposite in meaning to the underlined word.
The leader's views were equivocal on the contentious issue. (underlined: equivocal)
(1) obscure
(2) ambiguous
(3) clear
(4) vague
= 3
~ "Equivocal" means open to more than one interpretation or deliberately unclear, so its opposite is "clear"; obscure, ambiguous and vague are all near-synonyms.

Q 2 | eng | Synonyms & Antonyms
Directions: Select the option that is opposite in meaning to the underlined word.
The authorities interacted with a group of mutinous students. (underlined: mutinous)
(1) unruly
(2) contumacious
(3) seditious
(4) obedient
= 4
~ "Mutinous" means rebellious or refusing to obey authority, so "obedient" is its opposite; unruly, contumacious (stubbornly disobedient) and seditious are synonyms.

Q 6 | eng | Fill in the Blanks
Directions: Select the most appropriate choice to fill in the blank.
Working in dim light ________ eye issues such as glaucoma.
(1) contributes
(2) precipitates
(3) eradicates
(4) eliminates
= 2
~ "Precipitates" means causes or brings on (something bad), which fits "eye issues"; "contributes" would need "to", and eradicates/eliminates give the opposite sense.

Q 8 | eng | Fill in the Blanks
Directions: Select the most appropriate choice to fill in the blank.
Those medicines, ________ are used to treat kidney infection, have been withdrawn from the market.
(1) what
(2) which
(3) when
(4) who
= 2
~ A non-defining relative clause about things (medicines) is introduced by "which"; "who" is for people and "what" cannot follow a noun as a relative pronoun.

Q 9 | eng | Fill in the Blanks

________ intake of sugar can cause health problems.
(1) Access
(2) Excess of
(3) Excessive
(4) Excessively
= 3

Q 10 | eng | Fill in the Blanks

(1) hurting
(2) excruciating
(3) consuming

Q 11 | eng | Fill in the Blanks
Directions: Select the most appropriate choice to fill in the blank.
The country's politics ________ complex, with more than ten parties competing for power.
(1) are
(2) am
(3) is
(4) were
= 3
~ Nouns ending in -ics such as "politics" are usually treated as singular when they denote a field or the political life of a place, so "is" (present tense, matching "competing") fits; "were" is the wrong tense and "am" is impossible. Note that "politics" meaning a set of political views or activities can also take "are", so (1) is defensible. (Answer by Learn with Anil; UPSC has not published its key.)

Q 12 | eng | Fill in the Blanks
Directions: Select the most appropriate choice to fill in the blank.
The job advertisement stipulates that the applicant ________ three years' experience.
(1) have
(2) had
(3) shall have
(4) must have
= 4
~ A stipulation states a requirement, and "must have" expresses that obligation clearly in the present; "had" breaks the tense sequence. The formal subjunctive "(that the applicant) have" is also grammatical, so (1) is defensible. (Answer by Learn with Anil; UPSC has not published its key.)

Q 13 | eng | Fill in the Blanks
Directions: Select the most appropriate choice to fill in the blank.
Newspapers have reported heavy flooding in the coastal areas. The damage ________ to be extensive.
(1) expected
(2) is expecting
(3) has been expected
(4) is expected
= 4
~ Damage cannot itself expect anything, so a passive is needed, and the simple present "is expected to be" states the current expectation; "has been expected" wrongly implies a continuing past expectation.

Q 14 | eng | Fill in the Blanks

(1) four of whom
(2) four of these
(3) four of those
(4) four whom
= 1

Q 15 | eng | Fill in the Blanks
Directions: Select the most appropriate choice to fill in the blank.
The country ________ several economic changes in the past two decades.
(1) underwent
(2) had undergone
(3) will be undergoing
(4) have been undergoing
= 1
~ "Underwent" (simple past) reports completed changes; "have been undergoing" does not agree with the singular "country", "will be undergoing" clashes with "in the past", and the past perfect has no later past event to refer to.
