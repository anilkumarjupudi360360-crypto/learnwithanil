Q 1 | alg | Polynomials & Quadratics
A real number x is such that the sum of the number and four times its square is the least. What is that number?
(1) −0·625
(2) −0·125
(3) 0·125
(4) 1
= 2
~ f(x) = 4x² + x is a upward parabola with minimum at x = −b/(2a) = −1/8 = −0·125 (minimum value −1/16).

Q 2 | ari | Number System
The difference of the square of two natural numbers m and n (m > n) is 72. How many pairs of natural numbers will satisfy?
(1) 3
(2) 4
(3) 5
(4) 6
= 1
~ (m − n)(m + n) = 72 needs both factors of the same parity, i.e. both even: 2×36, 4×18, 6×12. These give (m, n) = (19, 17), (11, 7), (9, 3), so 3 pairs.

Q 3 | ari | Number System
Let N be a 5-digit number. When N is divided by 6, 12, 15, 24 it leaves respectively 2, 8, 11, 20 as remainders. What is the greatest value of N?
(1) 99960
(2) 99956
(3) 99950
(4) 99946
= 2
~ In each case divisor − remainder = 4, so N + 4 is a multiple of LCM(6, 12, 15, 24) = 120. The largest 5-digit multiple of 120 is 99960, so N = 99956.

Q 4 | ari | Number System
What is the remainder when 111²²² + 222³³³ + 333⁴⁴⁴ is divided by 5?

(2) 2
(3) 3
(4) 4

Q 9 | ari | Speed & Distance
Train X crosses a man standing on the platform in 24 seconds and train Y crosses a man standing on the platform in 18 seconds. They cross each other while running in opposite directions in 20 seconds. What is the ratio of speed of X to speed of Y?
(1) 1 : 2
(2) 2 : 3
(3) 1 : 3
(4) 3 : 4

~ Lengths are 24x and 18y, so 24x + 18y = 20(x + y), giving 4x = 2y, i.e. x : y = 1 : 2.

Q 10 | alg | Polynomials & Quadratics
Let p, q be the roots of the equation x² + mx − n = 0 and m, n be the roots of the equation x² + px − q = 0 (m, n, p, q are non-zero numbers). Which of the following statements is/are correct?
I. m(m + n) = −1
II. p + q = 1
Select the answer using the code given below:
(1) I only
(2) II only
(3) Both I and II
(4) Neither I nor II
= 3
~ p + q = −m, pq = −n, m + n = −p, mn = −q. The two sum relations give q = n; then pq = −n gives p = −1 and mn = −n gives m = −1, so q = n = 2. Hence m(m + n) = −1 × 1 = −1 and p + q = 1; both hold.

Q 16 | trg | Ratios & Identities

(1) 1
(2) 2
(3) 3
(4) 4

Q 17 | trg | Ratios & Identities
If cosecθ − cotθ = m and secθ − tanθ = n, then what is cosecθ + secθ equal to?
(1) (1/2)(m + n + 1/m + 1/n)
(2) (m + n + 1/m + 1/n)
(3) (1/2)(m + n − 1/m − 1/n)
(4) (m + n − 1/m − 1/n)
= 1
~ Since cosec²θ − cot²θ = 1, cosecθ + cotθ = 1/m, so cosecθ = (m + 1/m)/2; similarly secθ = (n + 1/n)/2. Adding gives (1/2)(m + n + 1/m + 1/n).

Q 18 | trg | Heights & Distances
From a point X on a bridge across a river, the angles of depression of two points P and Q on the banks on opposite side of the river are α and β respectively. If the point X is at a height h above the surface of the river, what is the width of the river if α and β are complementary?
(1) 2h(tanα + cotα)
(2) H tanα·tanβ
(3) h cotα·cotβ
(4) h secα·cosecα
= 4
~ Width = h cotα + h cotβ = h(cotα + tanα) since β = 90° − α. And cotα + tanα = 1/(sinα cosα), so width = h secα·cosecα.

Q 20 | alg | Polynomials & Quadratics

(2) 1

Q 23 | sts | Statistics & Data

Q 54 | sts | Statistics & Data
