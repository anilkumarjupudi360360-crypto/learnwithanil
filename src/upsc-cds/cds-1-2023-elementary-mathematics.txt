Q 1 | ari | Number System
What is the largest number which divides both 2³⁵ − 1 and 2⁹¹ − 1?
(1) 34
(2) 90
(3) 127
(4) 129
= 3
~ gcd(2ᵐ − 1, 2ⁿ − 1) = 2^gcd(m, n) − 1. Here gcd(35, 91) = 7, so the HCF is 2⁷ − 1 = 127.

Q 2 | ari | Number System
What is the largest power of 10 that divides the product 29 × 28 × 27 × . . . 2 × 1?
(1) 4
(2) 5
(3) 6
(4) 7
= 3
~ The product is 29!, and the power of 10 is fixed by the number of 5s: ⌊29/5⌋ + ⌊29/25⌋ = 5 + 1 = 6. There are plenty of 2s, so 10⁶ is the largest power.

Q 3 | ari | Number System
What is the remainder when 65⁹⁹ is divided by 11?
(1) 0
(2) 5
(3) 9
(4) 10
= 4
~ 65 = 66 − 1 ≡ −1 (mod 11), so 65⁹⁹ ≡ (−1)⁹⁹ = −1 ≡ 10 (mod 11).

Q 4 | alg | Polynomials & Quadratics
If the roots of the equation x² − bx + c = 5 differ by 5, then which one of the following is correct?
(1) b² = 4c + 5
(2) c² = 4b − 5
(3) b² + c² = 5
(4) b² − c² = 5
= 1
~ The equation is x² − bx + (c − 5) = 0, so (difference of roots)² = b² − 4(c − 5) = 25. This gives b² − 4c + 20 = 25, i.e. b² = 4c + 5.

Q 5 | alg | Sets, Logarithms & Inequalities
In a party of 150 persons, 75 persons take tea, 60 persons take coffee and 50 persons take milk. 15 of them take both tea and coffee, but no one taking milk takes tea. If each person in the party takes at least one drink, then what is the number of persons taking milk only?
(1) 50
(2) 40
(3) 30
(4) 20
= 3
~ Since nobody takes both milk and tea, 150 = 75 + 60 + 50 − 15 − n(coffee ∩ milk), so n(coffee ∩ milk) = 20. Milk only = 50 − 20 = 30.

Q 6 | ari | Ratio, Time & Work
A, B, C, D and E enter into a business. They invest money in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4 : 5 : 6. However, the time invested by them is in the ratio 6 : 5 : 4 : 3 : 2. If the profit distributed is directly proportional to time and money invested, then who receives the highest amount of profit?
(1) C
(2) Both B and D
(3) Both C and D
(4) All get equal profit
= 1
~ Profit shares are money × time: 2×6 = 12, 3×5 = 15, 4×4 = 16, 5×3 = 15, 6×2 = 12. C has the largest product (16).

Q 7 | ari | Number System
Consider the following numbers:
1. 437
2. 797
3. 1073
How many of the above numbers are prime?
(1) Only one
(2) Only two
(3) All three
(4) None

Q 8 | ari | Ratio, Time & Work
A can do a certain work at twice the speed of B. Further, B can do the same work at 1·5 times the speed of C. All of them together can finish the work in 12 days. In how many days can C alone finish the work?
(1) 36 days
(2) 45 days
(3) 60 days
(4) 66 days
= 4
~ If C's rate is c, then B's rate is 1·5c and A's is 3c, so together they do 5·5c = 1/12 of the work per day. That gives c = 1/66, so C alone takes 66 days.

Q 9 | ari | Number System
The sum of digits of a 2-digit number is 12. When the digits are reversed, the number becomes greater by eighteen. What is the difference between the digits in the number?
(1) 1
(2) 2
(3) 3
(4) 4
= 2
~ For the number 10a + b, reversing gives 9(b − a) = 18, so b − a = 2. With a + b = 12 the number is 57, and the digits differ by 2.

Q 10 | ari | Speed & Distance
The time taken by a train to cross a man travelling in another train is 10 seconds, when the other train is travelling in the opposite direction. However, it takes 20 seconds, if both the trains are travelling in the same direction. The length of the first train is 200 m and that of the second train is 150 m. What is the speed of the first train?
(1) 60 km/hr
(2) 56 km/hr
(3) 54 km/hr
(4) 52 km/hr
= 3
~ To pass a man, the first train covers only its own 200 m: u + v = 200/10 = 20 m/s and u − v = 200/20 = 10 m/s. So u = 15 m/s = 54 km/hr. The 150 m length of the second train plays no part.

(4) 9/2

Q 12 | alg | Polynomials & Quadratics
If a, b, c are non-zero real numbers such that a + b + c = 0, then what are the roots of the equation ax² + bx + c = 0?

Q 13 | ari | Percentage, Profit & Interest
Twelve percent of bananas bought by a fruit vendor got lost during transportation. On selling the remaining bananas, the vendor's overall profit turned out to be 4%. If the vendor had not lost any bananas and had sold them at the price of the remaining bananas, what would have been his profit percentage?
(1) 8 1/9 %
(2) 9 1/11 %
(3) 18 2/11 %
(4) None of the above
= 3
~ Say 100 bananas cost ₹100. Selling the 88 left brings in ₹104, so the price is 104/88 per banana. Selling all 100 at that price brings in ₹1300/11 ≈ 118·18, a profit of 18 2/11 %.

Q 14 | alg | Equations & Expressions
If the positive square root of (5 + 3√2)(5 − 3√2) is α, then what is the positive square root of 8 + 2α?
(1) 2 + √3
(2) 3 − √2
(3) √7 − 1
(4) √7 + 1
= 4
~ (5 + 3√2)(5 − 3√2) = 25 − 18 = 7, so α = √7. Then 8 + 2√7 = (√7 + 1)², and its positive square root is √7 + 1.

Q 15 | ari | Number System
When every even power of every odd integer (greater than 1) is divided by 8, what is the remainder?
(1) 3
(2) 2
(3) 1
(4) The remainder is not necessarily 1.
= 3
~ Every odd square has the form (2k + 1)² = 4k(k + 1) + 1, and k(k + 1) is even, so it is ≡ 1 (mod 8). Any even power is a power of an odd square, so it is also ≡ 1 (mod 8).

Q 16 | ari | Number System
Consider the following statements:
1. If n is a natural number, then the number n(n² + 2)/3 is also a natural number.
2. If m is an odd integer, then the number (m⁴ + 4m² + 11)/16 is an integer.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 3
~ n(n² + 2) = n(n² − 1) + 3n = (n − 1)n(n + 1) + 3n, which is divisible by 3. For odd m, m⁴ + 4m² + 11 = (m² − 1)(m² + 5) + 16, and m² − 1 and m² + 5 are both even, with one of them divisible by 8, so the expression is divisible by 16. Python confirms both statements.

Q 17 | ari | Number System
It is given that 5 does not divide n − 1, n and n + 1, where n is a positive integer. Which one of the following is correct?
(1) 5 divides (n² + 1)
(2) 5 divides (n² − 1)
(3) 5 divides (n² + n)
(4) 5 divides (n² − n)
= 1
~ The condition leaves n ≡ 2 or 3 (mod 5), so n² ≡ 4 ≡ −1 (mod 5). That makes 5 a divisor of n² + 1.

Q 18 | ari | Number System
What is the largest 5-digit number, which leaves remainder 7, when divided by 18 as well as by 11?
(1) 99981
(2) 99988
(3) 99997
(4) 99999

Q 19 | ari | Percentage, Profit & Interest
In a business dealing, A owes B ₹ 20,000 payable after 5 years, whereas B owes A ₹ 12,000 payable after 4 years. They want to settle it now at the rate of 5% simple interest. Who gives how much money in this settlement?
(1) Both are at par
(2) B gives ₹ 6,000 to A
(3) A gives ₹ 6,000 to B
(4) A gives ₹ 4,000 to B
= 3
~ Present values at 5% simple interest: A's debt is 20000/1·25 = ₹16,000 and B's debt is 12000/1·20 = ₹10,000. So A pays B the difference, ₹6,000.

Q 20 | ari | Averages & Mixtures
Average marks in Mathematics of Section A comprising 30 students is 65 and that of Section B comprising 35 students is 70. What are the average marks (approximately) of both the sections if it was detected later that an entry of 47 marks was wrongly made as 74?
(1) 67·28
(2) 67·58
(3) 68·11
(4) 68·63

~ Correct total = 30 × 65 + 35 × 70 − (74 − 47) = 4400 − 27 = 4373. The average is 4373/65 ≈ 67·28.

Q 21 | alg | Polynomials & Quadratics

Q 22 | ari | Number System
Consider the following statements in respect of all factors of 360:

2. The sum of all factors is 1170.
Which of the above statements is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only

(4) Neither 1 nor 2

Q 23 | ari | Number System

Q 24 | ari | Number System
What is the HCF of 3²⁹ − 9 and 3³⁸ − 9?

Q 26 | ari | Number System
Let m and n be natural numbers. What is the minimum value of (m + n) such that 33m + 22n is divisible by 121?
(1) 3
(2) 4
(3) 5
(4) 10
= 2
~ 33m + 22n = 11(3m + 2n), so 3m + 2n must be a multiple of 11. The smallest case is m = 3, n = 1 (3m + 2n = 11), giving m + n = 4. No pair with m + n = 2 or 3 works.

Q 27 | ari | Number System
The product of two numbers is 2160 and their HCF is 12. If the sum of the squares of the two numbers is 4896, then what is the mean of the two numbers?
(1) 24
(2) 36
(3) 48
(4) 96
= 3
~ (a + b)² = 4896 + 2 × 2160 = 9216, so a + b = 96 and the mean is 48. The numbers are 36 and 60, whose HCF is indeed 12.

Q 28 | alg | Equations & Expressions
The age of Q exceeds the age of P by 3 years. The age of R is twice the age of P and the age of Q is twice the age of S. Further, the age difference of R and S is 30 years. What is the sum of the ages of P and Q?
(1) 35 years
(2) 38 years
(3) 39 years
(4) 45 years
= 4
~ Q = P + 3, R = 2P and S = (P + 3)/2. From 2P − (P + 3)/2 = 30 we get P = 21 and Q = 24, so P + Q = 45 years.

Q 29 | gm | Lines, Angles & Triangles
If a, b and c are the sides of a triangle ABC, then √a + √b − √c is always:
(1) Negative
(2) Positive
(3) Non-negative
(4) Non-positive
= 2
~ (√a + √b)² = a + b + 2√(ab) > a + b > c by the triangle inequality. So √a + √b > √c, and the expression is always positive.

Q 30 | ari | Number System

Q 35 | ari | Ratio, Time & Work
Three taps A, B and C together can fill a tank in 6 hours. Tap C alone can fill the tank in 12 hours. To fill the tank, when it is empty, all the three taps are started together. After working t hours, tap C is closed and the tank is filled in 8 more hours. What is t equal to?

(2) 2

Q 37 | ari | Number System
Consider the following statements:
1. n³ − n is divisible by 6.
2. n⁵ − n is divisible by 5.
3. n⁵ − 5n³ + 4n is divisible by 120.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ n³ − n is a product of 3 consecutive integers, so it is divisible by 6. n⁵ − n is divisible by 5 by Fermat's little theorem. n⁵ − 5n³ + 4n = (n − 2)(n − 1)n(n + 1)(n + 2) is a product of 5 consecutive integers, so it is divisible by 5! = 120. All three are true.

Q 42 | alg | Polynomials & Quadratics
Consider the following statements in respect of the polynomial a(b − c)(x − b)(x − c) + b(c − a)(x − c)(x − a) + c(a − b)(x − a)(x − b):

Q 43 | alg | Polynomials & Quadratics
Consider the following statements in respect of the polynomial 1 − x − xⁿ + xⁿ⁺¹, where n is a natural number:
1. It is divisible by 1 − 2x + x².
2. It is divisible by 1 − xⁿ.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 3
~ 1 − x − xⁿ + xⁿ⁺¹ = (1 − x)(1 − xⁿ). Since (1 − x) also divides 1 − xⁿ, the polynomial is divisible by (1 − x)² = 1 − 2x + x². So both statements are true.

Q 44 | alg | Polynomials & Quadratics

Q 45 | alg | Equations & Expressions
If x = a + b + (a − b)²/(4a + 4b) and y = (a + b)/4 + ab/(a + b), then what is the value of (x − a)² − (y − b)²?

Q 46 | trg | Ratios & Identities
Consider the following:
1. cos⁴θ − sin⁴θ = 2 tan θ/(1 − tan²θ), 0 < θ < π/2
2. cosec θ + cot θ = 1/(cosec θ − cot θ), 0 < θ < π/2

(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 2
~ cos⁴θ − sin⁴θ = cos 2θ, but 2 tan θ/(1 − tan²θ) = tan 2θ, so 1 is not an identity. Statement 2 follows from cosec²θ − cot²θ = 1. Statement 3 holds because both sides equal cos 2θ.
