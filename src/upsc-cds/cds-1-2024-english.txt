Q 1 | eng | Synonyms & Antonyms
Directions: Select the option that is opposite in meaning to the underlined word.
She accepted the prospect of her medical treatment with equanimity. (underlined: equanimity)
(1) tranquillity
(2) agitation
(3) composure
(4) placidity
= 2
~ "Equanimity" means calmness and evenness of mind, especially under stress, so its opposite is "agitation"; tranquillity, composure and placidity are all synonyms.

Q 2 | eng | Synonyms & Antonyms
Directions: Select the option that is opposite in meaning to the underlined word.
He had been vehement in opposing the idea. (underlined: vehement)
(1) ardent
(2) subdued
(3) fervent
(4) impassioned
= 2
~ "Vehement" means showing strong, forceful feeling; "subdued" (quiet, restrained) is its opposite, while ardent, fervent and impassioned are synonyms.

Q 3 | eng | Synonyms & Antonyms
Directions: Select the option that is opposite in meaning to the underlined word.
He indulges in nefarious activities. (underlined: nefarious)
(1) flagitious
(2) execrable
(3) admirable
(4) abominable
= 3
~ "Nefarious" means wicked or criminal; "admirable" (deserving praise) is the opposite, whereas flagitious, execrable and abominable all mean extremely wicked or hateful.

Q 6 | eng | Fill in the Blanks
Directions: Select the most appropriate choice to fill in the blank.
I shall not be going to the theatre _____.
(1) if it may snow
(2) if it snow
(3) if it snows
(4) if snowed
= 3
~ In a first conditional (real future condition), the if-clause takes the simple present while the main clause has a future form: "I shall not be going ... if it snows."

Q 7 | eng | Fill in the Blanks
Directions: Select the most appropriate choice to fill in the blank.
_____ than a hundred passengers were killed in the recent train accident.
(1) Less
(2) No less
(3) Fewest
(4) No fewer
= 4
~ "Passengers" is a countable plural noun, so "fewer" (not "less") is required; "No fewer than a hundred" stresses that the number was as large as a hundred.

Q 8 | eng | Fill in the Blanks
Directions: Select the most appropriate choice to fill in the blank.
Each of these paintings _____ made by a famous painter.
(1) are
(2) is
(3) be
(4) should
= 2
~ The subject is "Each", which is singular, so it takes the singular verb "is"; the plural "paintings" in the of-phrase does not change the number of the subject.

Q 9 | eng | Fill in the Blanks
Directions: Select the most appropriate choice to fill in the blank.
This is an area of _____ dense population.
(1) extremely
(2) totally
(3) tangentially
(4) languidly
= 1
~ An adverb of degree is needed to intensify the adjective "dense"; "extremely dense" is the natural collocation, while "totally", "tangentially" and "languidly" do not fit.

Q 10 | eng | Fill in the Blanks
Directions: Select the most appropriate choice to fill in the blank.
Reading ability in young children _____ by speech disorders.
(1) effected
(2) affected
(3) is effected
(4) is affected
= 4
~ The sentence needs a passive verb ("is" + past participle) with "affect" (to influence); "effect" as a verb means to bring about, which does not fit here.

Q 11 | eng | Fill in the Blanks
Directions: Select the most appropriate choice to fill in the blank.
She has faced almost _____ difficulty in arranging the funds for the event.
(1) big
(2) insurmountable
(3) gigantic
(4) large
= 2
~ "Almost" qualifies an absolute adjective, so "almost insurmountable" (nearly impossible to overcome) is the idiomatic choice; "almost big/large/gigantic difficulty" is not natural English.

Q 12 | eng | Fill in the Blanks
Directions: Select the most appropriate choice to fill in the blank.
Our school building is _____ dilapidated.
(1) visibly
(2) little
(3) deeply
(4) satisfactorily
= 1
~ "Visibly dilapidated" means obviously run-down to anyone who looks; "deeply" and "satisfactorily" do not collocate with "dilapidated", and "little dilapidated" lacks the article "a".

Q 13 | eng | Fill in the Blanks
Directions: Select the most appropriate choice to fill in the blank.
Ranikhet is a place of remarkably _____ beauty.
(1) scenery
(2) scenic
(3) scene
(4) scenically
= 2
~ An adjective is needed to modify the noun "beauty" (and to be modified by the adverb "remarkably"), so "scenic" is correct; "scenery" and "scene" are nouns and "scenically" is an adverb.

Q 14 | eng | Fill in the Blanks
Directions: Select the most appropriate choice to fill in the blank.
The criminal on the run managed to _____ capture by the police officer.
(1) invade
(2) evade
(3) slip
(4) run away
= 2
~ "Evade capture" means to escape or avoid being caught; "slip" and "run away" would need "from", and "invade" (to enter by force) makes no sense.

Q 117 | eng | Parts of Speech

Q 118 | eng | Parts of Speech

Q 119 | eng | Parts of Speech

Q 120 | eng | Parts of Speech
Directions: Read the two sentences and choose the response that denotes the correct use of the underlined words.
S1: The teacher enquired whether there is any problem with the question paper. (underlined: any)
S2: He asked her whether she had some pets at home. (underlined: some)
(1) S1 is right and S2 is wrong
(2) S1 is wrong and S2 is right
(3) Both S1 and S2 are wrong
(4) Both S1 and S2 are right
= 3
~ In a question (including a reported "whether" question) "any" is used, so S2 should read "whether she had any pets"; S1 uses "any" correctly but, being reported speech after a past reporting verb, should read "whether there was any problem", which is presumably why UPSC marks it wrong too.
