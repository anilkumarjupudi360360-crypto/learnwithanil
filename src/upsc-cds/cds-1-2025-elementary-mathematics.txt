Q 1 | alg | Polynomials & Quadratics
A real number M is squared to give the value N. What is the minimum value of (M + N)?
(1) −0·25
(2) −0·50
(3) 0
(4) 0·25
= 1
~ M + N = M² + M = (M + 1/2)² − 1/4, which is least when M = −1/2. So the minimum value is −1/4 = −0·25.

Q 2 | ari | Number System
What is the sum of all 3-digit numbers that give a remainder of 5 when they are divided by 50?
(1) 9005
(2) 9540
(3) 9600
(4) 9640
= 2
~ The numbers are 105, 155, …, 955: an AP of 18 terms. Sum = 18 × (105 + 955)/2 = 9 × 1060 = 9540.

Q 3 | ari | Averages & Mixtures
If the average of 64, 69, 72, 75, x lies between 62 and 76 (excluding 62 and 76), then what is the number of possible integer values of x?
(1) 68
(2) 69
(3) 70
(4) 71
= 2
~ 62 < (280 + x)/5 < 76 gives 310 < 280 + x < 380, i.e. 30 < x < 100. The integers 31 to 99 give 69 values.

Q 4 | alg | Equations & Expressions
Let x, y, z be variables such that (x + y + z) = k, where k is a constant. If (x + z − y) × (x − z + y) is proportional to yz, then (y + z − x) is proportional to:
(1) x
(2) y
(3) yz
(4) xz
= 3
~ (x + z − y)(x − z + y) = (k − 2y)(k − 2z) = k(k − 2y − 2z) + 4yz = k(x − y − z) + 4yz. If this equals c·yz, then k(y + z − x) = (4 − c)yz, so (y + z − x) is proportional to yz.

Q 5 | ari | Number System
Let p be the remainder when 7⁸⁴ is divided by 342 and q be the remainder when 7⁸⁴ is divided by 344. What is (p − q) equal to?
(1) 0
(2) 1
(3) 2
(4) 6
= 1
~ 7³ = 343 = 342 + 1 = 344 − 1. So 7⁸⁴ = (343)²⁸ ≡ 1²⁸ = 1 (mod 342) and ≡ (−1)²⁸ = 1 (mod 344). Hence p = q = 1 and p − q = 0.

Q 6 | ari | Number System
N is the smallest 5-digit number which when divided by 2, 2², 2³, 2⁴, …, 2ⁿ leaves a remainder 1. What is the value of n?
(1) 12
(2) 13
(3) 14
(4) 15
= 3
~ N − 1 must be a multiple of 2ⁿ. Taking N = 2ⁿ + 1 as the smallest 5-digit such number gives 2¹⁴ + 1 = 16385 (2¹³ + 1 = 8193 has only 4 digits), so n = 14. Strictly, n = 13 also leads to N = 16385, so the question is loosely framed; 14 is the intended answer. (Answer by Learn with Anil; UPSC has not published its key.)

Q 7 | ari | Number System
What is the minimum value of p for which 1/532900 + p²/266450 + p⁴/523900 is an integer?
(1) 729
(2) 243
(3) 27
(4) 1
= 3
~ 532900 = 730² and 266450 = 532900/2, so with the last denominator read as 532900 the sum is (1 + 2p² + p⁴)/730² = ((p² + 1)/730)². The least p with p² + 1 divisible by 730 is p = 27 (27² + 1 = 730), giving the integer 1. The last denominator printed as 523900 appears to be a misprint for 532900; as printed, none of the options gives an integer. (Answer by Learn with Anil; UPSC has not published its key.)

Q 8 | alg | Polynomials & Quadratics
If α and β are the roots of the equation x + a + b = abx/(ab + ax + bx), then what is (αβ + α + β) equal to?
(1) ab + a + b
(2) ab − a − b
(3) a + b − ab
(4) −(ab + a + b)
= 2
~ Cross-multiplying, (x + a + b)(ab + (a + b)x) = abx simplifies to (a + b)[x² + (a + b)x + ab] = 0, so x² + (a + b)x + ab = 0. Then α + β = −(a + b) and αβ = ab, giving αβ + α + β = ab − a − b.

Q 9 | ari | Number System
Consider a 2-digit number N. Let P be the product of the digits of the number. If P is added to square of the digit in the tens place of N, we get 84. If P is added to the square of the digit in the unit place of N, we get 60. What is the value of P + N?
(1) 100
(2) 110
(3) 115
(4) 120
= 2
~ With digits a (tens) and b (units): a² + ab = 84 and b² + ab = 60. Adding gives (a + b)² = 144, so a + b = 12, a = 84/12 = 7 and b = 5. Then N = 75, P = 35 and P + N = 110.

Q 10 | ari | Averages & Mixtures
A mixture of 100 L contains kerosene and turpentine oil in the ratio 3 : 2. What is the minimum quantity of kerosene in litres (whole number) that should be mixed in the mixture so that the resulting mixture has 20% of kerosene?
(1) 10 L
(2) 20 L
(3) 25 L
(4) Not possible
= 4
~ The mixture already has 60 L of kerosene, which is 60%. Adding more kerosene can only raise this share, so it can never fall to 20%.

Q 11 | gm | Lines, Angles & Triangles
A lamp is kept on a vertical pole. The height of the top of the lamp above the ground is 5√3/2 m. The perpendicular distances of the bottom of the pole from two adjacent walls meeting perpendicularly are 0·7 m and 2·4 m. What is the distance of the top of the lamp from the corner point of the walls on the ground?
(1) 3 m
(2) 5 m
(3) 6 m
(4) 7 m
= 2
~ The foot of the pole is √(0·7² + 2·4²) = 2·5 m from the corner. The distance to the top of the lamp is √(2·5² + (5√3/2)²) = √(6·25 + 18·75) = √25 = 5 m.

Q 12 | gm | Circles & Quadrilaterals
C is the centre of a circle of radius 20 cm. AB is a chord of length 32 cm. E is a point on AB such that CE = 13 cm. What is AE × EB equal to?
(1) 231 square cm
(2) 256 square cm
(3) 272 square cm
(4) 297 square cm
= 1
~ By the power of a point, AE × EB = r² − CE² = 400 − 169 = 231. As a check, C is 12 cm from AB, so E is 5 cm from the midpoint and AE × EB = (16 − 5)(16 + 5) = 231.

Q 13 | gm | Circles & Quadrilaterals
The inside of a bowl is part of a sphere. When water is put into the bowl to a depth d, the water surface becomes a circle of radius 2d. What is the radius of the sphere?
(1) 2·5d
(2) 2·75d
(3) 3d
(4) 3·25d
= 1
~ The centre of the sphere is at a height r − d above the water surface, so r² = (2d)² + (r − d)². This gives 2rd = 5d², so r = 2·5d.

Q 14 | gm | Lines, Angles & Triangles
In a triangle ABC, AB = 2 cm, BC = 4 cm and AC = 3 cm. The bisector of angle A meets BC at D and the bisector of angle B meets AD at E. What is AE : ED equal to?
(1) 5 : 4
(2) 5 : 3
(3) 4 : 3
(4) 3 : 2
= 1
~ BD = BC × AB/(AB + AC) = 4 × 2/5 = 1·6 cm. In triangle ABD, BE bisects angle B, so AE : ED = AB : BD = 2 : 1·6 = 5 : 4.

Q 15 | gm | Lines, Angles & Triangles
In a triangle ABC, the bisector of angle A cuts BC at D. If AB + AC = 10 cm and BD : DC = 3 : 1, then what is the length of AC?

(2) 6 cm
(3) 7·5 cm
(4) 8 cm

Q 16 | gm | Mensuration
In a triangle ABC, AB + BC = 7·1 cm, BC + CA = 12·1 cm and CA + AB = 7·2 cm. What is the area of the triangle?
(1) 3 square cm
(2) 32 square cm
(3) 33 square cm
(4) 3·3 square cm
= 4
~ Adding the three gives AB + BC + CA = 13·2, so CA = 6·1, AB = 1·1 and BC = 6·0. With s = 6·6, Heron's formula gives area = √(6·6 × 5·5 × 0·6 × 0·5) = √10·89 = 3·3 sq cm.

Q 21 | trg | Ratios & Identities
If 2 sin⁴ α + 2 cos⁴ α − 1 = 0, where 0 ≤ α < π/2, then what is sin 2α + cos 2α equal to?
(1) 0
(2) 1
(3) (√3 + 1)/2
(4) (√3 − 1)/2
= 2
~ Let s = sin²α. Then 2s² + 2(1 − s)² − 1 = 4s² − 4s + 1 = (2s − 1)² = 0, so sin²α = 1/2 and α = π/4. Hence sin 2α + cos 2α = 1 + 0 = 1.

Q 22 | trg | Ratios & Identities
Consider the following:
I. 1 − sin⁶ α = cos² α (cos⁴ α − 3 cos² α + 3)
II. cos⁸ α − sin⁸ α = 2 sin² α (1 − cos⁴ α + sin² α cos² α)
Which of the above is/are identities?
(1) I only
(2) II only
(3) Both I and II
(4) Neither I nor II
= 1
~ With c = cos²α, 1 − sin⁶α = 1 − (1 − c)³ = c(c² − 3c + 3), so I is an identity. But cos⁸α − sin⁸α = (cos²α − sin²α)(1 − 2 sin²α cos²α), and the given right side of II differs from it (e.g. at α = 0 the left side is 1 and the right side is 0), so II is not an identity.

Q 23 | trg | Ratios & Identities
If p = 1/(cosec θ + cot θ) and q = cosec θ, then what is p² − 2pq equal to?
(1) −1
(2) 0
(3) 1
(4) 2
= 1
~ Since cosec²θ − cot²θ = 1, p = cosec θ − cot θ. Then p² − 2pq = (p − q)² − q² = cot²θ − cosec²θ = −1.

Q 24 | trg | Ratios & Identities
Consider the following statements:
I. (cosec α − sec α) is always positive in the first quadrant.
II. (tan α − cot α) is always negative in the first quadrant.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) I only
(2) II only
(3) Both I and II
(4) Neither I nor II
= 4
~ Both expressions change sign at α = 45°. For α > 45°, cosec α < sec α, so I fails. For α > 45°, tan α > cot α, so II fails. Neither statement is always true.

Q 25 | trg | Heights & Distances
A tower subtends an angle 60° at a point A on the same level as the foot of the tower. B is a point vertically above A and AB = h. The angle of depression of the foot of the tower, measured from B is 30°. What is the height of the tower?
(1) 2h
(2) 2·5h
(3) 3h
(4) 3·5h
= 3
~ Let d be the distance of A from the foot. From B, tan 30° = h/d, so d = h√3. From A, the height H = d tan 60° = h√3 × √3 = 3h.

Q 28 | trg | Ratios & Identities
If cot² θ − 3√3 cot θ + 6 = 0, where π/6 ≤ θ < π/2, then what is a value of sin θ + cos 2θ?
(1) 0
(2) 1

Q 29 | trg | Ratios & Identities
Which of the following equations is/are possible?
I. sin² θ = (x + y)²/4xy, where x, y are positive unequal real quantities.
II. sin θ + cos θ = x + 1/x, where x is a positive real quantity.
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
(1) I only
(2) II only
(3) Both I and II
(4) Neither I nor II
= 4
~ For unequal positive x, y, (x + y)² > 4xy, so the right side of I exceeds 1, which sin²θ cannot be. In II, x + 1/x ≥ 2 but sin θ + cos θ ≤ √2, so II is also impossible.

Q 30 | trg | Ratios & Identities
If m² (sin θ − 1) + n² (sin θ + 1) = 0, where 0 < θ < π/2, then what is (m² + n²) cos θ − (m² − n²) cot θ equal to?

(2) 2mn
(3) 1
(4) 0
= 4
~ The condition gives sin θ = (m² − n²)/(m² + n²). So (m² − n²) cot θ = (m² + n²) sin θ cot θ = (m² + n²) cos θ, and the expression is 0.

Q 32 | trg | Ratios & Identities

(3) 2
(4) 4

Q 33 | trg | Ratios & Identities
If tan θ = (sin α − cos α)/(sin α + cos α), where θ and α (α ≠ π/4) are acute angles, then what is √2 sin θ equal to?

(3) cos α − sin α
(4) ±(sin α − cos α)
= 1

Q 36 | alg | Equations & Expressions
What is (a − b)²/((b − c)(c − a)) + (b − c)²/((c − a)(a − b)) + (c − a)²/((a − b)(b − c)) − 3 equal to, where a ≠ b ≠ c?
(1) 0
(2) 3
(3) a + b + c
(4) 3(a − b)(b − c)(c − a)
= 1
~ Over the common denominator (a − b)(b − c)(c − a), the first three terms give [(a − b)³ + (b − c)³ + (c − a)³]/[(a − b)(b − c)(c − a)]. The three differences add to 0, so the sum of cubes is 3(a − b)(b − c)(c − a). The terms therefore add to 3, and 3 − 3 = 0.

Q 37 | ari | Number System

(1) 20
(2) 21
(3) 24
(4) None of the above

Q 38 | gm | Lines, Angles & Triangles
A man starting from a place P went x metre (x > 120 m) East before turning South. He went 40 m straight before turning to West. He went 60 m to reach a place Q. From Q he went 200 m North and reached a place R. If PR = 200 m, then what is x equal to?

Q 50 | ari | Speed & Distance
A train of certain length takes time t to pass completely through a station of length x. The same train with same speed takes time 2t to pass completely through another station of length y. What is the time taken by the train to pass completely through a station of length (x + y)?
(1) (2yt + xt)/(y − x)
(2) (yt + xt)/(y − x)
(3) (3yt − xt)/(2y − x)
(4) (2yt − xt)/(y − x)
= 4
~ With train length L and speed v: L + x = vt and L + y = 2vt. So v = (y − x)/t and L = y − 2x. The time for (x + y) is (L + x + y)/v = (2y − x)t/(y − x) = (2yt − xt)/(y − x).
