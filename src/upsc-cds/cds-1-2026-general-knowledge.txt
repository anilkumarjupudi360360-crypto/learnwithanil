Q 1 | sci | Biology
The central cytoplasmic region of a prokaryotic cell containing nucleic acids is called :
(1) Nucleus
(2) Nucleoid
(3) Nucleolus
(4) Plastid
= 2
~ Prokaryotes have no nuclear membrane; their genetic material (a single circular DNA) lies in an undefined region of the cytoplasm called the nucleoid. The nucleolus is a structure inside the true nucleus of eukaryotes.

Q 2 | sci | Biology
Nearly all infants benefit from milk, but most adults cannot properly digest milk. If an elderly person can still digest milk, his body is producing enough of which enzyme ?
(1) Cellulase
(2) Amylase
(3) Pectase
(4) Lactase
= 4
~ Lactase breaks down lactose (milk sugar) into glucose and galactose. Its production usually falls after childhood, causing lactose intolerance; a person who still digests milk continues to make enough lactase.

Q 3 | sci | Biology
The antibiotic penicillin can kill invading bacteria without harming human cells because it targets bacterial :
(1) Cell wall
(2) Plasma membrane
(3) Ribosome
(4) Chromosome
= 1
~ Penicillin blocks the synthesis of the peptidoglycan cell wall of bacteria. Human (animal) cells have no cell wall, so they are not affected.

Q 4 | sci | Biology
A researcher is studying different types of cells. In which cell type will he/she encounter the highest number of lysosomes ?
(1) Pancreatic cell
(2) Ovarian cell
(3) Muscle cell in the thigh of a runner
(4) White blood cell
= 4
~ Lysosomes contain digestive (hydrolytic) enzymes, so they are most numerous in phagocytic white blood cells such as neutrophils and macrophages, which engulf and digest microbes. Pancreatic cells are rich in RER/secretory vesicles and muscle cells in mitochondria.

Q 5 | sci | Biology
Which one of the following is not a function of the pigment carotenoid ?
(1) Responsible for yellow-orange hues of autumnal leaves
(2) Protecting chlorophyll from photo-oxidation
(3) Photoprotective role in our eyes
(4) Reaction centre in the pigment system
= 4
~ The reaction centre of a photosystem is a special chlorophyll a molecule (P680/P700); carotenoids are only accessory pigments that pass energy to it. Carotenoids do give autumn leaves their yellow-orange colour, protect chlorophyll from photo-oxidation, and (as lutein/zeaxanthin) protect the retina.

Q 6 | sci | Biology
A world chess champion has curly hair, attached earlobes and freckles. Which one of the following traits is not likely to be passed on to his/her offsprings ?
(1) Chess skills
(2) Curly hair
(3) Attached earlobes
(4) Freckles
= 1
~ Chess skill is an acquired trait developed by learning and practice; it is not coded in the genes and so is not inherited. Curly hair, attached earlobes and freckles are genetically inherited traits.

Q 7 | sci | Biology
In humans, which chamber of the heart receives oxygen-rich blood from the lungs first ?
(1) Left atrium
(2) Right atrium
(3) Left ventricle
(4) Right ventricle
= 1
~ Oxygenated blood from the lungs returns through the pulmonary veins into the left atrium, then passes to the left ventricle, which pumps it to the body. The right atrium receives deoxygenated blood from the body.

Q 10 | sci | Biology

(2) Athlete's foot – Fungal disease
(3) Dengue fever – Non-viral disease

Q 11 | sci | Physics
Which one of the following statements qualitatively explains the second law of motion ?
(1) The rate of change of angular momentum of a body is equal to the applied force.
(2) The rate of change of displacement of a body is directly proportional to the applied force.
(3) The rate of change of acceleration of a body is directly proportional to the applied force.
(4) The rate of change of momentum of a body is equal to the applied force.
= 4
~ Newton's second law says the rate of change of momentum of a body is proportional to (in SI units, equal to) the applied force: F = dp/dt. Rate of change of angular momentum equals torque, not force.

Q 13 | sci | Physics
Which one among the following has the least coefficient of thermal conductivity ?
(1) H₂
(2) H₂O
(3) Cu
(4) Al
= 1
~ Gases are poor conductors of heat. Hydrogen gas has k ≈ 0·18 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹, while water has ≈ 0·6, aluminium ≈ 237 and copper ≈ 400 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹. So H₂ has the lowest value.

(1) Second Generation
(2) Third Generation
(3) Fourth Generation
(4) Fifth Generation

~ Fourth-generation computers (from about 1971) were built around the microprocessor (VLSI chips such as the Intel 4004). Second-generation computers used transistors and third-generation computers used integrated circuits.

Q 15 | sci | Physics
An X-ray beam can be deflected by :
(1) An electric field
(2) A magnetic field
(3) An electric as well as a magnetic field
(4) Neither an electric field nor a magnetic field
= 4
~ X-rays are electromagnetic waves (photons) with no electric charge, so neither electric nor magnetic fields deflect them. Charged beams such as cathode rays can be deflected.

Q 16 | sci | Physics
An object is placed at a distance of 15 cm in front of a concave mirror with a radius of curvature of 20 cm. Which one among the following statements is correct for the image formed ?
(1) The image is 30 cm from the mirror on the same side as the object.
(2) The image is formed at a distance of 30 cm behind the mirror.
(3) The image is 60 cm from the mirror on the same side as the object.
(4) The image is formed at a distance of 60 cm behind the mirror.
= 1
~ f = R/2 = −10 cm and u = −15 cm. 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = −1/10 + 1/15 = −1/30, so v = −30 cm. The negative sign means a real image 30 cm in front of the mirror, on the same side as the object.

Q 17 | sci | Physics
Which one among the following statements regarding the electrical resistivity of a conducting wire is correct ?
(1) The resistivity of the conductor increases if the length of the wire is increased.
(2) The resistivity of the conductor decreases if the length of the wire is increased.
(3) The resistivity of the conductor remains the same if the length of the wire is changed.
(4) The resistivity of the conductor increases if the cross-sectional area of the wire is increased.
= 3
~ Resistivity is a property of the material, which depends on the material and temperature but not on the wire's dimensions. Resistance R = ρL/A changes with length and area, but ρ does not.

Q 18 | sci | Physics
Which one among the following statements related to electric cells is correct ?
(1) A primary cell is rechargeable.
(2) A secondary cell can be used only once.
(3) The emf of a battery is the power provided by the battery per unit current.
(4) The unit of emf is Joule.
= 3
~ Power delivered by a source is P = εI, so ε = P/I, the power per unit current (equivalently, the work done per unit charge). The unit of emf is the volt (J/C), not the joule. It is secondary cells that are rechargeable, not primary cells.

Q 19 | sci | Physics
An object is launched horizontally from a height of 45 m above the ground with a speed of 10 m/s. The time at which the object will hit the ground is : (Take g = 10 m/s²)
(1) 6 s
(2) 4 s
(3) 3 s
(4) 2 s
= 3
~ The initial vertical velocity is zero, so h = ½gt² gives 45 = 5t², t² = 9 and t = 3 s. The horizontal speed does not affect the time of fall.

Q 20 | sci | Physics
Consider the following statements regarding waves :
1. If the particles of a medium move along the direction of motion of the wave, the wave is called a longitudinal wave.
2. If the particles of a medium move perpendicular to the direction of motion of the wave, the wave is called a transverse wave.
3. Transverse waves may involve change in the shape of the medium, whereas longitudinal waves involve change in the density of the medium.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct ?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 1 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ Statements 1 and 2 are the standard definitions. Transverse waves involve shearing (change of shape), so they need a medium with rigidity; longitudinal waves involve compressions and rarefactions, which are changes in density. All three are correct.

Q 23 | sci | Chemistry
Consider the following statements :

Q 24 | sci | Chemistry

(2) Carbon : Tetravalent
(3) Chlorine : Two stable isotopic forms

Q 26 | sci | Chemistry

Q 27 | sci | Chemistry
Consider the following statements :
1. The temporary hardness in water is due to the presence of bicarbonates of calcium.
2. The permanent hardness in water is due to the presence of soluble chlorides and sulphates of calcium and magnesium.
3. The permanent hardness in water is removed by adding Na₂CO₃ to water.
Which of the statements given above are correct ?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ Temporary hardness is caused by hydrogencarbonates (bicarbonates) of calcium and magnesium, and permanent hardness by their soluble chlorides and sulphates. Washing soda (Na₂CO₃) removes permanent hardness by precipitating CaCO₃ and MgCO₃. So all three statements are correct.

Q 28 | sci | Chemistry
Which one among the following statements regarding micelles is not correct ?
(1) Soap molecules are sodium or potassium salts of long-chain carboxylic acids.
(2) Micelles precipitate out of solution because of ion-ion repulsion.
(3) Grease gets collected in the centre of the micelle.
(4) Soap micelles scatter light.
= 2
~ The ionic heads on a micelle's surface repel one another, so the micelles stay dispersed as a colloid and do not come together and precipitate. Grease is trapped in the hydrophobic centre, and because micelles are colloid-sized they scatter light (Tyndall effect), which is why soap solution looks cloudy.

Q 29 | sci | Chemistry
Consider the following statements regarding bases and alkalis :
1. All bases dissolve in water.
2. Bases which are soluble in water are called alkalis.
3. An alkali is soapy to touch, bitter and corrosive.
4. Addition of water to an alkali generates heat.
Which of the statements given above are correct ?
(1) 1, 2, 3 and 4
(2) 2, 3 and 4 only
(3) 1 and 2 only
(4) 3 and 4 only
= 2
~ Not all bases dissolve in water (for example, Cu(OH)₂ and Fe(OH)₃ do not), so statement 1 is wrong. Water-soluble bases are called alkalis. Alkalis taste bitter, feel soapy and are corrosive, and dissolving or diluting a base such as NaOH in water is strongly exothermic.

Q 30 | sci | Chemistry
Which of the following statements relating to the Modern Periodic Table is/are correct ?
1. The atomic radius increases on moving from left to right across a period.
2. Germanium, Tellurium, and Copper are transition metals.
3. The two isotopes of Chlorine occupy the same position in the periodic table.

(2) 1 and 4

Q 32 | sci | Science & Technology

(2) It is an electrically erasable programmable ROM.

Q 39 | eco | Growth, Planning & National Income
The Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) is measured by :
(1) Measuring each person's overlapping deprivations across the indicators in the health, education and standard of living dimensions
(2) Measuring each person's separate efficiencies across the indicators in the wealth, income and standard of living dimensions
(3) Measuring each person's poverty on the indicators of health, happiness and ownership of vehicles
(4) Measuring inequalities and deprivations in respect of wealth, income and standard of living dimensions
= 1
~ The global MPI (UNDP–OPHI) records each person's overlapping deprivations across 10 indicators in three equally weighted dimensions: health, education and standard of living. A person deprived in at least one-third of the weighted indicators counts as multidimensionally poor.

Q 50 | his | Ancient India
The Bower Manuscript is known for being a valuable source on which one of the following subjects in ancient India ?
(1) Medicine
(2) Mathematics
(3) Astronomy
(4) Chemistry
= 1
~ The Bower Manuscript, a birch-bark text from Kucha in Central Asia written in Gupta Brahmi script (c. 5th–6th century CE), is best known for its medical treatises such as the Navanitaka. It is among the oldest surviving manuscripts on Ayurveda. Do not confuse it with the Bakhshali Manuscript, which is the famous one on mathematics.
