Q 1 | sci | Biology
Which structure in Rhizopus is directly involved in the process of reproduction through spore formation?
(1) Hyphae
(2) Mycelium
(3) Sporangia
(4) Rhizoids
= 3
~ Rhizopus (bread mould) reproduces asexually by spore formation. Some hyphae grow upward and develop small knob-like sacs at their tips called sporangia, inside which many spores are made; when a sporangium bursts, the spores spread and each can grow into a new mould. Hyphae and mycelium form the body of the fungus, and rhizoids only anchor it and absorb food, so they are not the spore-forming structures.

Q 2 | sci | Chemistry
Which property enables carbon to form long chains?
(1) Polymerisation
(2) Catenation
(3) Tetravalency
(4) Isomerism
= 2
~ Catenation is the ability of an element to form bonds with other atoms of the same element, giving long chains, branched chains and rings. Carbon shows this property to the greatest extent because carbon–carbon bonds are very strong and stable. Tetravalency (a valency of four) lets carbon bond with four other atoms, but it is catenation that specifically explains the long chains; isomerism and polymerisation are results of these properties, not the cause.

Q 3 | sci | Physics
A sound wave has a speed of 320 m/s. If the wavelength of the wave is 0.4 m, what is the frequency of this wave?
(1) 128 Hz
(2) 80 Hz
(3) 1280 Hz
(4) 800 Hz
= 4
~ For any wave, speed = frequency × wavelength (v = νλ), so frequency ν = v/λ. Here ν = 320 ÷ 0.4 = 800 Hz. Option 128 Hz comes from wrongly multiplying 320 × 0.4 instead of dividing.

Q 4 | sci | Physics
The filament of a light bulb is made up of tungsten, because of its ______.
(1) High resistance and low melting point
(2) High resistance and high melting point
(3) Low resistance and low melting point
(4) Low resistance and high melting point
= 2
~ A bulb filament must get hot enough to glow without melting. Tungsten has a high resistance (high resistivity), so it produces a lot of heat when current flows (H = I²Rt), and it has a very high melting point of about 3400 °C, so it can glow white-hot without melting. A metal with a low melting point would simply melt.

Q 5 | sci | Biology
In plants, food made in leaves is transported to other parts by the complex tissue called __________.
(1) Phloem
(2) Sclerenchyma
(3) Xylem
(4) Collenchyma
= 1
~ Phloem is the complex permanent tissue that carries food (mainly sugars made during photosynthesis) from the leaves to other parts of the plant; this process is called translocation. Xylem is also a complex tissue, but it carries water and minerals from the roots upward. Sclerenchyma and collenchyma are simple tissues that give strength and support.

Q 6 | sci | Chemistry
A sealed syringe containing air is pressed from one end while keeping the other end closed. What is the most likely outcome of this action in terms of the state of air inside the syringe?
(1) The volume of air increases and pressure decreases
(2) The air escapes completely from the syringe
(3) The volume of air decreases and its pressure increases
(4) The temperature of air inside drops significantly
= 3
~ Gases are highly compressible because there is a lot of space between their particles. When the plunger is pushed with the nozzle closed, the air cannot escape, so its particles are pushed closer together: the volume decreases and, since the same particles now hit the walls more often, the pressure increases (Boyle's law: at constant temperature, pressure × volume stays constant). The air cannot escape from a sealed syringe, and compressing a gas does not cool it.

Q 7 | sci | Physics
A constant force acts on an object of mass 4 kg for a duration of 2 s, increasing its velocity from 2 m/s to 8 m/s. If the same force is applied for a duration of 4 s, then what would be the final velocity of the object?
(1) 14 m/s
(2) 10 m/s
(3) 20 m/s
(4) 12 m/s
= 1
~ Acceleration a = (v − u)/t = (8 − 2)/2 = 3 m/s², so the force is F = ma = 4 × 3 = 12 N. The same force gives the same acceleration of 3 m/s², so when it acts for 4 s starting from 2 m/s, v = u + at = 2 + 3 × 4 = 14 m/s. The option 20 m/s comes from wrongly adding 3 × 4 to 8 m/s instead of to the initial 2 m/s.

Q 8 | sci | Physics
A boy lifts an object above his head by exerting a force. Which of the following works is negative?
(1) Work done by the force of gravity
(2) Work done by the boy
(3) Work done by the object
(4) Work done by the applied force
= 1
~ Work is negative when the force and the displacement are in opposite directions. While the object is lifted, it moves upward, but the force of gravity acts downward, so the work done by gravity is negative. The boy's applied force acts upward in the same direction as the displacement, so the work done by the boy (the applied force) is positive.

Q 9 | sci | Physics
Which of the following is the correct unit of average speed in SI system?
(1) km/h²
(2) m/s²
(3) s/m²
(4) m/s
= 4
~ Average speed = total distance travelled ÷ total time taken. In the SI system distance is measured in metres (m) and time in seconds (s), so the SI unit of average speed is metre per second (m/s). The units m/s² and km/h² are units of acceleration, not speed (and km/h² is not even an SI unit), while s/m² is not a unit of speed at all.

Q 10 | sci | Chemistry
Which of the following is NOT an olfactory indicator?
(1) Vanilla
(2) Onion
(3) Litmus
(4) Clove oil
= 3
~ Olfactory indicators are substances whose smell changes in acidic or basic medium; vanilla essence, onion and clove oil lose their smell in a basic solution but keep it in an acidic one. Litmus is a natural indicator that shows acids and bases by a change of colour (blue litmus turns red in acid, red litmus turns blue in base), not by smell, so it is not an olfactory indicator.

Q 11 | sci | Physics
Which of the following statements is true about weight and mass?
(1) Only weight changes on the Moon
(2) Only mass changes on the Moon
(3) Neither mass nor weight changes on the Moon
(4) Both mass and weight change on the Moon
= 1
~ Mass is the amount of matter in an object, so it stays the same everywhere. Weight is the force with which a body is pulled by gravity (W = mg); the Moon's gravity is about one-sixth of the Earth's, so an object's weight on the Moon becomes about one-sixth of its weight on the Earth while its mass does not change.

Q 12 | sci | Biology
During a nerve impulse transmission, neurotransmitters are released from one neuron and received by another.
Which part of neurons are primarily involved in this chemical communication?
(1) Axon → Myelin sheath of nerve
(2) Axon terminal → Dendrite of another neuron
(3) Cell body → Axon of same neuron
(4) Dendrite → Axon terminal of same neuron
= 2
~ An impulse travels from the dendrite to the cell body and along the axon to its end (the axon terminal). There the electrical impulse makes the terminal release chemicals called neurotransmitters, which cross the small gap called the synapse and are picked up by the dendrite of the next neuron, starting a new impulse there. The other options describe movement within the same neuron or to the myelin sheath, which is only an insulating covering, not a receiving part.

Q 13 | sci | Biology
During the birth, the ovaries of a girl child contain ____________.
(1) Only mature eggs
(2) Both mature and immature eggs
(3) Only immature eggs
(4) No eggs
= 3
~ When a girl is born, her ovaries already contain thousands of immature eggs. No egg matures before puberty; after puberty, usually one egg matures and is released by one of the ovaries every month. So at birth the ovaries hold only immature eggs.

Q 14 | sci | Physics
In a toaster, the heating wire glows red because __________.
(1) The current is very high.
(2) It resists the current and gets hot.
(3) It has low melting point.
(4) It has zero resistance.
= 2
~ The heating element of a toaster is made of an alloy such as nichrome, which has a high resistance. When current flows through it, the wire opposes the current and electrical energy is changed into heat (Joule's law of heating, H = I²Rt), so the wire becomes red-hot. A wire with zero resistance would produce no heat, and a heating wire needs a high melting point, not a low one.

Q 15 | sci | Chemistry
Read the following statements carefully and select the correct option.
Assertion (A): Keeping oily food in airtight containers prevents rancidity.
Reason (R): Airtight containers slow down the oxidation of fats and oils.
(1) Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A.
(2) A is true, but R is false.
(3) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(4) A is false, but R is true.
= 3
~ Rancidity is the change in smell and taste of oily or fatty food when the fats and oils in it get oxidised by oxygen in the air. An airtight container keeps fresh air away from the food, so oxidation is slowed down and rancidity is prevented (delayed). Thus both statements are true and R correctly explains A; other methods with the same idea are flushing chip packets with nitrogen and adding antioxidants.

Q 16 | sci | Chemistry
How does the space between particles change from solid to gas?
(1) The space increases as matter changes from solid to gas
(2) Solids and gases always have equal spacing
(3) The space remains unchanged during the change in states of matter
(4) The space decreases as matter changes from solid to gas
= 1
~ In a solid the particles are packed very closely with very little space between them, in a liquid the spaces are a little larger, and in a gas the particles are very far apart. So as matter changes from solid to liquid to gas, the space between particles increases and the forces of attraction between them decrease, which is why gases are easily compressed and fill any container.

Q 18 | sci | Chemistry
What is the general name of the reaction between an acid and a base?
(1) Oxidation
(2) Combustion
(3) Neutralisation
(4) Decomposition
= 3
~ When an acid reacts with a base, they cancel each other's effect and form a salt and water, for example HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O. This is called a neutralisation reaction. Combustion is burning in oxygen, oxidation is the gain of oxygen or loss of hydrogen, and decomposition is the breaking of one compound into simpler substances.

Q 19 | sci | Biology
The complex tissue that transports food in plants is called __________, and it consists of living cells like sieve tubes and __________.
(1) Xylem, tracheids
(2) Collenchyma, sclerenchyma
(3) Phloem, companion cells
(4) Xylem, xylem fibres
= 3
~ Phloem carries food from the leaves to other parts of the plant. It is made of four kinds of elements: sieve tubes, companion cells, phloem parenchyma and phloem fibres; all of these except the phloem fibres are living cells. Xylem carries water, and its tracheids and vessels are dead cells, while collenchyma and sclerenchyma are simple tissues, not food-conducting ones.

Q 21 | sci | Physics
A real and inverted image is formed at a distance of 30 cm from a concave mirror when the object is placed 40 cm from the mirror. What will be the magnification produced by the mirror?
(1) −4/3
(2) −3/4
(3) +3/4
(4) +4/3
= 2
~ Using the New Cartesian sign convention, distances measured in front of the mirror are negative, so u = −40 cm and, since a real image forms in front of the mirror, v = −30 cm. Magnification m = −v/u = −(−30)/(−40) = −3/4. The negative sign shows the image is real and inverted, and the value 3/4 (less than 1) shows it is smaller than the object; −4/3 comes from wrongly using u/v.

Q 22 | sci | Physics
A student shows a concave lens forming a diminished real image on the screen. The mistake is:
(1) Concave lens cannot form a real image
(2) Concave lens cannot form a magnified image
(3) Concave lens does not refract light
(4) Concave lens always converges light
= 1
~ A concave lens is a diverging lens: it spreads out the rays passing through it, so the refracted rays never actually meet. For a real object placed anywhere, a concave lens always forms a virtual, erect and diminished image on the same side as the object, which cannot be caught on a screen. So the mistake is claiming a real image; a diminished image itself is correct, and the lens does refract light, but it diverges rather than converges it.

Q 23 | sci | Biology
Which connective tissue is known for supporting internal organs?
(1) Squamous epithelium
(2) Ligament
(3) Tendon
(4) Areolar tissue
= 4
~ Areolar connective tissue is found between the skin and muscles, around blood vessels and nerves, and in the bone marrow; it fills the space inside organs, supports internal organs and helps in the repair of tissues. Tendons join muscles to bones and ligaments join bones to bones. Squamous epithelium is not a connective tissue at all; it is a covering (epithelial) tissue.

Q 25 | sci | Biology

Q 32 | qa | Speed, Time & Distance
A and B started moving at the same time towards each other from points X and Y, respectively. After meeting on the way, A and B took 95.4 hours and p hours, respectively, to reach Y and X, respectively. If the speed of B is 50% more than that of A, then what is the value of p?

(2) 40.4
(3) 43.8
(4) 42.4
= 4

Q 43 | qa | Geometry & Mensuration

~ The UPSC began as the Public Service Commission, set up on 1 October 1926 on the recommendation of the Lee Commission (1924) under the Government of India Act, 1919, with Sir Ross Barker as its first Chairman.
