Q 1 | sci | Chemistry
Which of the following salts will have a pH close to 7 when dissolved in water?
(1) Na₂CO₃
(2) NaCl
(3) CH₃COONa
(4) NH₄Cl
= 2
~ NaCl is the salt of a strong acid (HCl) and a strong base (NaOH), so its ions do not react with water and the solution stays neutral, with pH close to 7. Na₂CO₃ and CH₃COONa come from a strong base and a weak acid, so their solutions are basic (pH above 7), while NH₄Cl comes from a strong acid and a weak base (NH₄OH), so its solution is acidic (pH below 7).

Q 2 | sci | Physics
If the distance-time graph of an object is a straight line parallel to the time axis, what is the object's speed?
(1) Zero
(2) Uniform
(3) Non-zero constant
(4) Increasing
= 1
~ A line parallel to the time axis means the distance stays the same as time passes, so the object is not moving at all. The slope of a distance-time graph gives speed, and the slope of a horizontal line is zero. A uniform (non-zero) speed would instead give a straight line sloping upwards.

Q 3 | sci | Biology
Which of the following best explains the division of labour seen in multicellular organisms?
(1) All cells perform the same function in an organism
(2) Different cells have specialized functions
(3) Multicellular organisms have only one type of cell
(4) Each body part is made up of a single cell
= 2
~ In multicellular organisms, cells are specialised into different types (muscle cells, nerve cells, blood cells and so on), and groups of such cells form tissues and organs that each do a particular job. This sharing of work among specialised cells is called division of labour. If all cells did the same work, as in option 1, there would be no division of labour.

Q 4 | sci | Chemistry
The concentration of H⁺ ions in a solution primarily determines its:
(1) Degree of acidity in aqueous phase
(2) Rate of solid crystal formation
(3) Level of gas compressibility factor
(4) Extent of metallic bonding strength
= 1
~ The acidity of an aqueous solution depends on how many hydrogen ions (H⁺) it contains: the higher the H⁺ concentration, the more acidic the solution. This is measured on the pH scale, where pH = −log[H⁺], so a lower pH means a higher H⁺ concentration and a stronger acid. The other options have nothing to do with H⁺ concentration.

Q 5 | sci | Physics
How is the change in Gravitational Potential Energy (ΔPEg) of a system related to the work (Wg) done by the gravitational force?
(1) Wg = ΔPEg
(2) The two are unrelated
(3) Wg = 0
(4) Wg = −ΔPEg
= 4
~ Gravity is a conservative force, and the work done by such a force equals the negative of the change in potential energy, so Wg = −ΔPEg. For example, when a body falls, gravity does positive work while the potential energy decreases (ΔPEg is negative); when a body is lifted, gravity does negative work while the potential energy increases. Option 1 has the sign wrong.

Q 6 | sci | Biology
Which contraceptive method is most appropriate for a couple who wishes to avoid both hormonal and surgical methods?
(1) Using barrier methods like condoms
(2) Natural methods (periodic abstinence)
(3) Taking oral pills
(4) Inserting a copper-T
= 1
~ Barrier methods such as condoms simply stop the sperm from reaching the egg, so they involve no hormones and no surgery, and they are reliable and also protect against sexually transmitted infections. Oral pills are hormonal, and a copper-T has to be inserted into the uterus by a doctor. Natural methods also avoid hormones and surgery, but they are much less reliable, so barrier methods are the most appropriate choice.

Q 7 | sci | Chemistry
The law of conservation of mass states that:
(1) Matter can be destroyed during reactions
(2) Mass can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction
(3) Matter can be created in reactions
(4) Mass always decreases during a reaction
= 2
~ The law of conservation of mass, given by Lavoisier, says that mass can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction. The atoms only rearrange, so the total mass of the products equals the total mass of the reactants. That is also why chemical equations must be balanced.

Q 8 | sci | Chemistry
Copper reacts with moist carbon dioxide in the air and slowly loses its shiny brown surface and gains a _________ colour coating of basic copper carbonate.
(1) black
(2) white
(3) reddish brown
(4) green
= 4
~ When copper is exposed to moist air containing carbon dioxide, it slowly corrodes and forms a green layer of basic copper carbonate, CuCO₃·Cu(OH)₂. This green coating is why old copper vessels and copper statues turn green. A black coating (copper oxide) forms when copper is heated in air, not in this slow reaction with moist CO₂.

Q 9 | sci | Biology
A decrease in oxygen availability in the environment is most likely to first impact which step of aerobic respiration in animal cells?
(1) Krebs cycle
(2) Electron transport chain
(3) Glycolysis
(4) Lactic acid fermentation
= 2
~ Oxygen is used directly in only one step of aerobic respiration: it is the final electron acceptor at the end of the electron transport chain in the mitochondria, where it combines with electrons and H⁺ to form water. So when oxygen falls, the electron transport chain is the first step to slow down; the Krebs cycle slows only afterwards because it runs out of NAD⁺. Glycolysis does not need oxygen at all, and lactic acid fermentation is not a step of aerobic respiration.

Q 10 | sci | Physics
If the current in a straight conductor is doubled, the magnetic field at a given distance from it:
(1) Becomes zero
(2) Doubles
(3) Halves
(4) Remains the same
= 2
~ The magnetic field around a straight current-carrying conductor is directly proportional to the current and inversely proportional to the distance from the wire (B = μ₀I/2πr). So at the same distance, doubling the current doubles the magnetic field. The field would halve only if the distance were doubled.

Q 11 | sci | Physics
The SI unit of average speed is:
(1) km/hr
(2) m/s
(3) cm/s
(4) m/s²
= 2
~ Average speed is total distance divided by total time, and in the SI system distance is measured in metres (m) and time in seconds (s), so its SI unit is m/s. km/hr and cm/s are also units of speed but they are not SI units, and m/s² is the unit of acceleration.

Q 12 | sci | Physics
A girl clapped her hands near a cliff and heard the echo after 8 s. What is the distance of the cliff from the girl if the speed of the sound is taken as 320 m s⁻¹?
(1) 640 m
(2) 80 m
(3) 320 m
(4) 1280 m
= 4
~ In an echo the sound travels to the cliff and back, so it covers twice the distance d. Total distance = speed × time = 320 × 8 = 2560 m, so 2d = 2560 m and d = 2560/2 = 1280 m. 640 m would come from wrongly dividing by 2 twice (2560/4).

Q 13 | sci | Biology
What is the advantage of cross-breeding local and exotic cattle breeds?
(1) Higher meat and milk yield
(2) Faster reproduction rate
(3) Lower adaptability to environment
(4) Denser wool yield
= 1
~ Exotic (foreign) breeds such as Jersey and Brown Swiss give a high milk yield and have a long lactation period, while local (Indian) breeds such as Red Sindhi and Sahiwal resist diseases and suit the local climate. Cross-breeding the two gives animals that combine high yield with good adaptability, so production of milk (and meat) goes up. Lower adaptability would be a disadvantage, and cattle are not reared for wool.

Q 14 | sci | Chemistry
Corrosion of iron is commonly known as rusting, and it requires:
(1) salt and strong acid presence
(2) iron and very cold temperatures
(3) water and atmospheric oxygen
(4) moisture and high acidity
= 3
~ Rusting is the slow reaction of iron with oxygen of the air in the presence of water (moisture), forming hydrated iron(III) oxide (Fe₂O₃·xH₂O), the reddish-brown rust. Both water and oxygen are essential: iron kept in dry air or in boiled water without air does not rust. Salt and acids only speed up rusting; they are not the basic requirement.

Q 15 | sci | Biology
Arrange the following in the correct sequences of development after fertilisation in humans.
Embryo, Zygote and Foetus
(1) Foetus - Embryo - Zygote
(2) Zygote - Foetus - Embryo
(3) Embryo - Foetus - Zygote
(4) Zygote - Embryo - Foetus
= 4
~ Fertilisation of the egg by a sperm first forms a single-celled zygote. The zygote divides repeatedly and develops into an embryo, which implants in the wall of the uterus. Once the body parts and organs become recognisable (from about the ninth week in humans), the developing baby is called a foetus. So the order is Zygote - Embryo - Foetus.

Q 16 | sci | Biology
Which of the following plants is a weed?
(1) Oryza sativa
(2) Vigna unguiculata
(3) Cyperus rotundus
(4) Pisum sativum
= 3
~ Cyperus rotundus (nut grass or motha) is a common weed that grows uninvited in crop fields and competes with crops for water, nutrients and light. The other three are crop plants: Oryza sativa is rice, Vigna unguiculata is cowpea (lobia) and Pisum sativum is garden pea.

Q 17 | sci | Biology
Two children of the same parents resemble each other but are not exactly alike because ___________.
(1) their parents show very little genetic variation
(2) each of them inherits all genetic characters from one parent only
(3) they show different combinations of the genetic variation existing in their parents
(4) each of them shows variation in their DNA copying mechanisms
= 3
~ In sexual reproduction each child gets half of its genetic material from the mother and half from the father, and the gametes of each parent carry different combinations of the parents' genes. So every child receives a new combination of the variations already present in the parents, which is why siblings look similar but not identical. A child does not inherit all characters from one parent, and small errors in DNA copying are not the main reason for these differences.

Q 18 | sci | Chemistry
A compound is a substance in which elements are:
(1) Easily separable
(2) Chemically combined in fixed proportion
(3) Physically mixed in any ratio
(4) Mixed temporarily
= 2
~ In a compound, two or more elements are chemically combined in a fixed proportion by mass; for example, water always has hydrogen and oxygen in the mass ratio 1:8. Its components cannot be separated by physical methods. Elements physically mixed in any ratio make a mixture, not a compound.

Q 19 | sci | Chemistry
Which statement best describes the Law of Constant Proportion of the compound water?
(1) The total mass of products is always greater than reactants in a reaction.
(2) A chemical compound always contains the same elements combined in a fixed ratio by mass.
(3) Elements combine in any ratio to form compounds.
(4) The properties of compounds are the same as those of elements.
= 2
~ The law of constant proportions (Proust) says that a pure chemical compound always contains the same elements combined in the same fixed ratio by mass, whatever its source. For water, hydrogen and oxygen are always in the mass ratio 1:8, whether the water comes from a river, rain or a laboratory. Option 1 contradicts the law of conservation of mass, and option 3 describes a mixture.

Q 20 | sci | Physics
Which of the following circuit elements is used to vary the amount of current following through a closed circuit?
(1) Ammeter
(2) Plug key
(3) Voltmeter
(4) Rheostat
= 4
~ A rheostat is a variable resistance; changing its resistance changes the current flowing in the circuit (by Ohm's law, I = V/R). An ammeter only measures current, a voltmeter only measures potential difference, and a plug key only switches the circuit on or off.

Q 21 | sci | Physics
The random wavering or flickering of objects viewed through a turbulent stream of hot air rising above a fire or radiator is an effect of ______________.
(1) Diffraction of light
(2) Heat radiation causing visual distortion
(3) Tyndall effect
(4) Atmospheric refraction on a small scale
= 4
~ Air just above a fire or radiator is hotter and optically rarer than the cooler air further away, and because this hot air keeps moving, its refractive index keeps changing from point to point. Light from objects behind it is bent (refracted) by different amounts from moment to moment, so the objects appear to waver or flicker. This is atmospheric refraction on a small, local scale, the same effect that makes stars twinkle; it is not caused by diffraction or by scattering (Tyndall effect).

Q 22 | sci | Biology
What is the method of removal of metabolic waste in many unicellular organisms?
(1) Through a simple diffusion process
(2) Through osmosis
(3) Through the use of special organs
(4) Through filtration through kidneys
= 1
~ Unicellular organisms have no special excretory organs; their whole body surface is in contact with the surrounding water. So wastes such as carbon dioxide and ammonia simply diffuse out from the cell into the water. Kidneys and other special organs are found only in complex multicellular organisms, and osmosis is the movement of water, not the removal of wastes.

Q 23 | sci | Physics
Which of the following quantities determines the magnitude of the buoyant force on a body?
(1) Surface area of the body
(2) Density of the fluid and volume displaced
(3) Temperature of the fluid
(4) Weight of the body
= 2
~ By Archimedes' principle, the buoyant force equals the weight of the fluid displaced by the body: buoyant force = density of fluid × volume displaced × g. So it depends on the density of the fluid and the volume of fluid displaced. The weight of the body decides whether it floats or sinks, but it does not set the size of the buoyant force.

Q 24 | sci | Physics
What type of image is always formed by a concave lens, no matter where the object is placed?
(1) Virtual, erect, and diminished
(2) Real, erect, and same size
(3) Virtual, inverted, and enlarged
(4) Real, inverted, and enlarged
= 1
~ A concave lens is a diverging lens: the refracted rays spread out and never actually meet, so they only appear to come from a point on the same side as the object. For any position of the object, the image is therefore virtual, erect and diminished, formed between the optical centre and the focus. Real, inverted and enlarged images are formed by a convex lens.

Q 25 | sci | Chemistry
In electrolytic refining, the impure metal is used as the:
(1) anode
(2) electrolyte
(3) separator
(4) cathode
= 1
~ In electrolytic refining, a thick block of the impure metal is made the anode and a thin strip of the pure metal is made the cathode, with a solution of a salt of the metal as the electrolyte. On passing current, the metal dissolves from the anode into the solution and pure metal is deposited on the cathode, while insoluble impurities settle below the anode as anode mud. For example, copper is refined this way using acidified copper sulphate solution.

Q 27 | qa | Percentage, Profit & Discount
Find the single discount equivalent to two successive discounts of 5% and 8%.
(1) 14.67%
(2) 12.6%
(3) 10.8%
(4) 14.63%
= 2
~ For successive discounts of a% and b%, the single equivalent discount = a + b − ab/100 = 5 + 8 − (5 × 8)/100 = 13 − 0.4 = 12.6%. Q 31 | qa | Time & Work, Pipes & Cisterns
P and Q together can fill a cistern with water in 8 hours. If P alone can fill the cistern with water in 20 hours, then in how many hours will Q alone fill three-fourth of the same cistern with water?
(1) 10
(2) 11
(3) 21
(4) 20
= 1
~ In one hour P and Q together fill 1/8 of the cistern and P alone fills 1/20, so Q alone fills 1/8 − 1/20 = (5 − 2)/40 = 3/40 of the cistern per hour. Q alone therefore needs 40/3 hours for the full cistern, and for three-fourth of it Q needs (40/3) × (3/4) = 10 hours.

Q 32 | qa | HCF, LCM & Fractions
If the numerator of a fraction is increased by 25% and the denominator is decreased by 9%, the value of new fraction becomes 5/7. Which of the following is the correct statement w.r.t. the original fraction?
(1) The numerator is greater than denominator by 10
(2) The sum of numerator and denominator is 37
(3) The value of denominator is 13
(4) The difference between denominator and numerator is 12
= 4
~ Let the fraction be N/D. Then (1.25N)/(0.91D) = 5/7, so N/D = (5 × 0.91)/(7 × 1.25) = 4.55/8.75 = 13/25. Taking the fraction in its lowest form, N = 13 and D = 25, so the denominator exceeds the numerator by 25 − 13 = 12. The other statements fail: the numerator is smaller than the denominator, the sum is 38 (not 37) and the denominator is 25 (not 13). Check: 13 × 1.25 = 16.25 and 25 × 0.91 = 22.75, and 16.25/22.75 = 5/7.

Q 33 | qa | Geometry & Mensuration
A field is in the shape of a quadrilateral. One of its diagonals is 10 m long. The perpendicular distances from the other two vertices to this diagonal are 12 m and 13 m, respectively. What is the area of the field?
(1) 110 m²
(2) 100 m²
(3) 130 m²
(4) 125 m²
= 4
~ The diagonal divides the quadrilateral into two triangles that share the diagonal as their base, with heights 12 m and 13 m. Area = 1/2 × diagonal × (sum of the perpendiculars) = 1/2 × 10 × (12 + 13) = 5 × 25 = 125 m².

Q 34 | qa | Geometry & Mensuration

(2) 1175 cm²

Q 36 | qa | Algebra & Equations

Q 39 | qa | Age, Calendar & Clock

(2) 15 years and 20 years

Q 40 | qa | Speed, Time & Distance

(1) 133 1/3 m
(2) 170 m
(3) 181 2/3 m
(4) 176 m

Q 45 | qa | Time & Work, Pipes & Cisterns
