Q 1 | sci | Physics
When a gun is fired, the bullet moves forward because:
(1) the barrel exerts no force
(2) air resistance acts forward
(3) the recoil of the gun acts backward as reaction
(4) gravity pushes it forward
= 3
~ This is Newton's third law of motion: the gun (through the expanding gases) pushes the bullet forward, and the bullet pushes back on the gun with an equal and opposite force, which is felt as the backward recoil. These two forces, forward on the bullet and backward on the gun, are an action-reaction pair, which is what option 3 describes. Air resistance acts against the bullet's motion (backward), and gravity acts downward, so neither can push it forward.

Q 2 | sci | Chemistry
Which of the following observations best supports that liquids have a definite volume but no fixed shape?
(1) Air expands to fill the entire container
(2) A solid iron block retains its shape in any container
(3) A sponge changes its shape when squeezed
(4) Water takes the shape of its container but retains its volume
= 4
~ A liquid has a fixed volume but flows to take the shape of whatever container it is poured into, and water shows exactly this. Air spreading to fill the whole container is a property of gases, the iron block keeping its shape is a property of solids, and a sponge changes shape only because air is squeezed out of its pores, so it says nothing about liquids.

Q 3 | sci | Chemistry
Which of the following methods involves coating steel and iron with a layer of zinc to prevent rusting?
(1) Passivation
(2) Cathodic protection
(3) Electroplating
(4) Galvanisation
= 4
~ Galvanisation is the process of coating iron or steel with a thin layer of zinc, usually by dipping it in molten zinc. Zinc is more reactive than iron, so even if the coating is scratched, zinc corrodes first and protects the iron. Cathodic protection is a broader method in which the iron is connected to a block of a more reactive metal or to an electric supply, electroplating uses electricity to deposit metals such as chromium or nickel, and passivation forms a thin protective oxide layer, so none of them is the specific name for a zinc coating.

Q 4 | sci | Chemistry
Which among the following organic compounds is saturated?
(1) 2-Bromopropene
(2) Propene
(3) Propyne
(4) Bromopropane
= 4
~ A saturated compound has only single bonds between its carbon atoms. Bromopropane (C₃H₇Br) is propane with one hydrogen replaced by bromine, so all its carbon-carbon bonds are single and it is saturated. Propene and 2-bromopropene have a carbon-carbon double bond (the ending "-ene"), and propyne has a triple bond (the ending "-yne"), so they are unsaturated.

Q 5 | sci | Chemistry
Which of the following has straight-chain hydrocarbons?
(1) 3-Methylpentane
(2) 2-Methylpropane
(3) Butane
(4) Isobutane
= 3
~ Butane (n-butane, CH₃–CH₂–CH₂–CH₃) has its four carbon atoms joined one after another in a single unbranched chain. 2-Methylpropane, which is the same compound as isobutane, and 3-methylpentane each carry a methyl (–CH₃) branch on the main chain, so they are branched-chain hydrocarbons.

Q 6 | sci | Physics
The relation between electric energy, power and time is:
(1) Energy = Time – Power
(2) Energy = Power / Time
(3) Energy = Power × Time
(4) Energy = Power + Time
= 3
~ Power is the rate at which energy is used, P = E/t, so electric energy E = P × t. This is why household electricity is billed in kilowatt-hours (kWh): a 1 kW appliance running for 1 hour uses 1 kWh, or 3.6 × 10⁶ J. Adding or subtracting power and time makes no sense because they are different physical quantities.

Q 7 | sci | Physics
The weight of a body on the moon is less than on Earth because:
(1) Gravitational acceleration on the moon is smaller
(2) Mass of the body decreases on the moon
(3) The moon has no atmosphere
(4) Mass of the moon is equal to Earth’s mass
= 1
~ Weight is W = mg, so it depends on the acceleration due to gravity. The acceleration due to gravity on the moon is about 1.6 m/s², roughly one-sixth of Earth's 9.8 m/s², so the same body weighs about one-sixth as much there. The mass of the body stays the same everywhere, and the absence of an atmosphere has nothing to do with weight.

Q 8 | sci | Physics
When we hit a table lightly, the sound produced is soft because the:
(1) frequency of vibration is low
(2) amplitude of vibration is small
(3) speed of sound decreases
(4) wavelength becomes shorter
= 2
~ The loudness of a sound depends on the amplitude of vibration: a light tap makes the table vibrate with a small amplitude, so the sound is soft, while a hard hit gives a larger amplitude and a louder sound. Frequency decides the pitch (shrillness), not the loudness, and the speed of sound depends on the medium, not on how hard the table is struck.

Q 9 | sci | Biology
Which of the following statements is true about HIV transmission?
(1) HIV can be transmitted through aerosols.
(2) HIV can be transmitted through sexual acts.
(3) HIV can be transmitted through mosquito bites.
(4) HIV can be transmitted through casual contact.
= 2
~ HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus), which causes AIDS, spreads through body fluids such as blood, semen and vaginal fluids, mainly by unprotected sexual contact, sharing infected needles, transfusion of infected blood, and from an infected mother to her child. It does not spread through air or droplets, through mosquito bites, or by casual contact such as shaking hands, hugging or sharing food.

Q 10 | sci | Biology
Why can tiny molecules like oxygen easily pass through the cell membrane, but not the big ones like starch or proteins?
(1) Small molecules have special energy to push through.
(2) The cell membrane is permeable to selective substances.
(3) The cell membrane is completely solid.
(4) Big molecules have to break down for entry.
= 2
~ The cell (plasma) membrane is selectively permeable: it lets some substances, such as small molecules of oxygen, carbon dioxide and water, pass through freely, while it blocks large molecules like starch and proteins. If the membrane were completely solid, nothing at all could pass through it.

Q 11 | sci | Physics
A car starts from rest and accelerates uniformly at 2 m/s² for 5 seconds. What is the final velocity of the car?
(1) 20 m/s
(2) 5 m/s
(3) 10 m/s
(4) 15 m/s
= 3
~ Use the first equation of motion, v = u + at. The car starts from rest, so u = 0, a = 2 m/s² and t = 5 s, giving v = 0 + 2 × 5 = 10 m/s.

Q 12 | sci | Biology
Generally which of the following trophic levels have the least number of organisms?
(1) Secondary consumers
(2) Tertiary consumers
(3) Primary consumers
(4) Producers
= 2
~ In a food chain only about 10% of the energy at one trophic level passes on to the next, so less and less energy is available at each higher level and fewer organisms can be supported there. In a typical pyramid of numbers, producers are the most numerous and tertiary (top) consumers, which sit at the highest of the levels given, are the fewest.

Q 13 | sci | Physics
The distance between the pole and the centre of curvature of a convex mirror is + 40 cm. What will be the distance between the pole and the focus of this mirror?
(1) −20 cm
(2) −40 cm
(3) + 20 cm
(4) + 40 cm
= 3
~ The distance between the pole and the centre of curvature is the radius of curvature, R = +40 cm. For a spherical mirror the focal length is half the radius of curvature, f = R/2 = +40/2 = +20 cm. The sign stays positive because, for a convex mirror, the focus lies behind the mirror.

Q 14 | sci | Biology
Which of the following human diseases can be transmitted during sexual act?
(1) Protein Energy Malnutrition
(2) Acquired Immuno Deficiency Syndrome
(3) Diabetes
(4) Cancer
= 2
~ AIDS (Acquired Immuno Deficiency Syndrome) is caused by HIV, which passes from person to person through body fluids, and sexual contact is one of its main routes; it is a sexually transmitted infection. Protein energy malnutrition is a deficiency disease, and diabetes and cancer are non-communicable diseases, so none of them spreads from one person to another.

Q 15 | sci | Chemistry
Which of the following is a characteristic of gases?
(1) No fixed shape and no fixed volume
(2) Fixed shape and volume
(3) Compressible but not fluid
(4) No fixed shape but fixed volume
= 1
~ The particles of a gas are far apart and move freely in all directions, so a gas has neither a fixed shape nor a fixed volume and spreads to fill its whole container. A fixed shape and volume describes solids, and a fixed volume without a fixed shape describes liquids. Gases are highly compressible and they also flow, so they are fluids.

Q 16 | sci | Physics
Magnification produced by a lens is related to object distance (u) and image distance (v) by the formula:
(1) m = v – u
(2) m = u / v
(3) m = u + v
(4) m = v / u
= 4
~ Magnification of a lens is the ratio of image height to object height, and it also equals the ratio of image distance to object distance: m = h′/h = v/u. For a mirror the formula carries a minus sign (m = −v/u), so it is easy to confuse the two. Sums or differences of distances do not give magnification.

Q 17 | sci | Chemistry
Which of the following has the highest acidity?
(1) Vinegar
(2) Water
(3) Milk
(4) Lemon juice
= 4
~ The lower the pH, the more acidic a substance is. Lemon juice has a pH of about 2 (it contains citric acid), vinegar about 2.5 to 3 (acetic acid), milk about 6.5 (slightly acidic) and pure water 7 (neutral), so lemon juice is the most acidic of these.

Q 18 | sci | Physics
A body of mass ‘m’ kg is kept at a height of ‘h’ m from the surface of the Earth. What will be the potential energy possessed by this body?
(1) m²gh
(2) hg/m
(3) mh/g
(4) mgh
= 4
~ Lifting a body of mass m to a height h requires work against its weight mg, and this work is stored as gravitational potential energy: PE = force × height = mg × h = mgh, where g is the acceleration due to gravity. The other expressions do not even give the unit of energy (joule).

Q 19 | sci | Physics
How much work (W) is done in moving a charge (Q) of 5 C across two points that have a potential difference of 15 V?
(1) 1/3 J
(2) 3 J
(3) 15 J
(4) 75 J
= 4
~ Potential difference is the work done per unit charge, V = W/Q, so W = V × Q. Here W = 15 V × 5 C = 75 J. Dividing instead (5/15 = 1/3 or 15/5 = 3) gives the wrong options.

Q 20 | sci | Biology
Which of the following cell organelles have enough surface area for ATP-generating chemical reactions?
(1) Cell wall
(2) Vacuoles
(3) Lysosomes
(4) Mitochondria
= 4
~ Mitochondria are the "powerhouses of the cell" where cellular respiration releases energy in the form of ATP. Their inner membrane is deeply folded into cristae, which gives a large surface area for the ATP-generating chemical reactions. The cell wall gives support, vacuoles store materials, and lysosomes digest waste, so none of them makes ATP.

Q 21 | sci | Biology
Mitochondria are special because they have their own DNA and ribosomes. What can they do on this basis?
(1) Move around the cell on their own
(2) Digest materials like lysosomes do
(3) Make food using sunlight like plants do
(4) Make some of their own proteins
= 4
~ DNA carries the instructions for making proteins and ribosomes are the sites where proteins are made, so having both lets mitochondria make some of their own proteins (most of their proteins still come from the nucleus). Making food using sunlight is the job of chloroplasts, and digesting materials is the job of lysosomes.

Q 22 | sci | Biology
Which of the following plants reproduces asexually by budding along the margins of a leaf?
(1) Mango
(2) Hydra
(3) Bryophyllum
(4) Spirogyra
= 3
~ Bryophyllum has buds in the notches along the margins of its leaves; when a leaf falls on moist soil, these buds grow into new plants, a form of vegetative propagation. Hydra also reproduces by budding but it is an animal, not a plant, and Spirogyra (an alga) reproduces by fragmentation.

Q 23 | sci | Chemistry
Which of the following will show diffusion fastest?
(1) Liquid in gas
(2) Solid in liquid
(3) Gas in gas
(4) Solid in solid
= 3
~ Diffusion is fastest when particles move fastest and have the most space between them. Gas particles have the highest kinetic energy and the largest gaps between them, so one gas spreads through another gas quickly (for example, the smell of incense filling a room). Diffusion of a solid in a solid is the slowest because the particles are tightly packed.

Q 24 | sci | Biology
How is intercropping beneficial over single crop farming?
(1) Utilises nutrients efficiently and prevents spread of diseases
(2) Increases irrigation needs
(3) Requires less land
(4) Reduces labour cost
= 1
~ In intercropping, two or more crops with different nutrient needs (for example, soyabean with maize) are grown together in a set row pattern on the same field. Because the crops draw different nutrients from the soil, the nutrients are used efficiently, and because each crop's plants are not all next to one another, pests and diseases cannot spread easily through the whole field. Higher irrigation needs would be a drawback, not a benefit.

Q 25 | sci | Chemistry
__________ is precipitated, when carbon dioxide is passed through lime water.
(1) Ca(HCO₃)₂
(2) Ca(OH)₂
(3) CaSO₄
(4) CaCO₃
= 4
~ Lime water is a solution of calcium hydroxide. When carbon dioxide is passed through it, insoluble calcium carbonate forms and turns the solution milky: Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O. If excess CO₂ is passed, the precipitate dissolves again as soluble calcium hydrogen carbonate, Ca(HCO₃)₂, which is why that option is not the precipitate.

Q 28 | qa | Ratio, Proportion & Average
Anmol and Ayushi together invested ₹46,600 in a business. At the end of the year, out of a total profit of ₹7,500, Anmol's share was ₹2,700. What was Anmol's investment?
(1) ₹16,080
(2) ₹17,240

Q 29 | qa | Algebra & Equations

(1) 53
(2) 56
(3) 65
(4) 55
= 3

Q 31 | qa | Ratio, Proportion & Average
The average age of 51 students in a class is 24 years. If the age of the teacher is also included, the average age of the whole group becomes 25 years. The age (in years) of the teacher is:
(1) 76
(2) 75
(3) 77
(4) 71
= 1
~ Total age of 51 students = 51 × 24 = 1,224 years. With the teacher there are 52 people with total age 52 × 25 = 1,300 years. Teacher's age = 1,300 − 1,224 = 76 years. Shortcut: the teacher brings his own share of 25 plus 1 extra year for each of the 51 students, so 25 + 51 = 76.

Q 32 | qa | Time & Work, Pipes & Cisterns
A, B and C can do a piece of work in 3, 8 and 24 days, respectively. How long (in days) will they take to do thrice the same work, working together?
(1) 6
(2) 18
(3) 12
(4) 7
= 1
~ One day's work of A, B and C together = 1/3 + 1/8 + 1/24 = 8/24 + 3/24 + 1/24 = 12/24 = 1/2. So together they finish the work once in 2 days, and three times the work in 3 × 2 = 6 days.

Q 33 | qa | Speed, Time & Distance
A train P going at the speed of 84 km/hr completely passes train Q of length 447 m, going in the same direction on parallel tracks, at 54 km/hr, in 4.5 minutes. How much time (in seconds) will P take to completely cross train R of length 287 m, going at 87 km/hr in the opposite direction?
(1) 36
(2) 39
(3) 44
(4) 34
= 3
~ Same direction: relative speed = 84 − 54 = 30 km/hr = 30 × 5/18 = 25/3 m/s, and 4.5 minutes = 270 s, so P covers (25/3) × 270 = 2,250 m relative to Q; length of P = 2,250 − 447 = 1,803 m. Opposite direction: relative speed = 84 + 87 = 171 km/hr = 171 × 5/18 = 47.5 m/s. Distance to cover = 1,803 + 287 = 2,090 m. Time = 2,090 ÷ 47.5 = 44 seconds.

Q 34 | qa | Percentage, Profit & Discount

Q 36 | qa | Ratio, Proportion & Average
What is the average of the first 50 even natural numbers?
(1) 52
(2) 50
(3) 54
(4) 51
= 4
~ The first 50 even natural numbers are 2, 4, 6, …, 100. Their sum = 50 × 51 = 2,550 (sum of first n even numbers = n(n + 1)), so the average = 2,550 ÷ 50 = 51. Equivalently, for evenly spaced numbers the average = (first + last)/2 = (2 + 100)/2 = 51, i.e. n + 1.

Q 39 | qa | Geometry & Mensuration
In a ΔPMN, ∠P = 76°. The bisectors of the exterior angles at M and N intersect each other at point O. The degree measure of ∠MON is:
(1) 128°
(2) 52°
(3) 104°
(4) 38°
= 2

Q 45 | qa | Geometry & Mensuration
The ratio of the volume of a sphere to the total surface area of a hemisphere is 28 : 3. If the radii of the sphere and the hemisphere are equal, find the volume of the sphere (in cubic centimetres).

(2) 38,808
(3) 38,542
(4) 38,460
