Q 1 | sci | Chemistry
Baking soda releases which gas on heating?
(1) O₂
(2) CO₂
(3) H₂
(4) N₂
= 2
~ Baking soda is sodium hydrogen carbonate (NaHCO₃). On heating it decomposes: 2NaHCO₃ → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂. The carbon dioxide released is what makes cakes and bread rise when baking soda or baking powder is used. Sodium hydrogen carbonate also gives off CO₂ when it reacts with an acid, which is the basis of soda-acid fire extinguishers.

Q 2 | sci | Chemistry
What is the typical oxide used along with aluminium in a thermite reaction?
(1) Magnesium oxide
(2) Iron (III) oxide
(3) Copper oxide
(4) Zinc oxide
= 2
~ In the thermite reaction, aluminium powder reduces iron(III) oxide: Fe₂O₃ + 2Al → 2Fe + Al₂O₃ + heat. So much heat is released that the iron formed is molten, which is why this reaction is used to join (weld) railway tracks and cracked machine parts. Magnesium oxide would not work because magnesium is more reactive than aluminium, so aluminium cannot displace it.

Q 3 | sci | Biology
Which cells in xylem are primarily responsible for the vertical transport of water and minerals in plants?
(1) Sieve tubes
(2) Xylem fibres
(3) Tracheids and vessels
(4) Companion cells
= 3
~ Xylem has four elements: tracheids, vessels, xylem parenchyma and xylem fibres. Tracheids and vessels are long, tube-like dead cells that form continuous channels carrying water and dissolved minerals upward from the roots. Xylem fibres mainly give support, while sieve tubes and companion cells belong to phloem, which transports food.

Q 4 | sci | Physics
Which of the following conditions must be satisfied for work to be done according to science?
(1) Only the object moves, even if no force is applied.
(2) The object is at rest, and a force is applied.
(3) Only a force is applied, even if the object does not move.
(4) A force must act on an object, and the object must be displaced.
= 4
~ In science, work is done only when a force acts on an object and the object is displaced (moves) in the direction of the force; work = force × displacement (W = F × s). If either the force or the displacement is zero, the work done is zero. That is why pushing a wall that does not move does no work, however tiring it may be.

Q 5 | sci | Biology
Imagine a country continues to use CFCs despite the global agreement. What could be the consequences for that country and the world?
(1) Environmental harm and global criticism
(2) Faster industrial development
(3) No effect, as other countries have stopped using CFCs
(4) Increase in rainfall
= 1
~ Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs), once used in refrigerators, air conditioners and aerosol sprays, break down the ozone layer in the stratosphere that shields the Earth from harmful ultraviolet (UV) rays. The Montreal Protocol (1987) is the global agreement to phase them out. Ozone depletion affects the whole planet (more skin cancer, eye damage and harm to crops and ecosystems), so a country that keeps using CFCs would cause environmental harm and face international criticism; the damage does not stop just because other countries have stopped.

Q 6 | sci | Chemistry
What is a colloidal solution?
(1) Mixture in which particles fully dissolve and settle
(2) Transparent mixture with gases only
(3) Heterogeneous mixture with fine particles
(4) Uniform mixture with large particles
= 3
~ A colloid is a heterogeneous mixture whose particles (about 1–100 nm) are too small to be seen with the naked eye but large enough to scatter a beam of light (Tyndall effect). The particles do not settle on standing and cannot be separated by ordinary filtration. Milk, fog, smoke and jelly are common examples. Option 4 is wrong because colloid particles are not large; a mixture with large particles that settle down is a suspension.

Q 7 | sci | Biology
A flexible support to developing plant parts is provided by _______.
(1) Xylem
(2) Phloem
(3) Collenchyma
(4) Chlorenchyma
= 3
~ Collenchyma is a living simple permanent tissue whose cells are thickened at the corners with cellulose and pectin. It gives mechanical support along with flexibility, so young stems, leaf stalks and growing parts can bend without breaking. Xylem and phloem are conducting tissues, and chlorenchyma is parenchyma containing chloroplasts that carries out photosynthesis.

Q 8 | sci | Chemistry
In which state of matter is the space between particles maximum ?
(1) Solid
(2) Liquid
(3) All have equal space
(4) Gas
= 4
~ In solids the particles are tightly packed, in liquids they are a little farther apart, and in gases they are very far apart with very weak forces of attraction between them. This large empty space is why gases can be compressed easily and spread to fill any container. So the space between particles is the most in the gaseous state.

Q 9 | sci | Physics
In household electricity supply, which of the following statements correctly describes the colour codes of the wires?
(1) The live wire is black, the neutral wire is green, and the earth wire is blue
(2) The live wire is red, the neutral wire is black, and the earth wire is green
(3) The live wire is blue, the neutral wire is green, and the earth wire is red

Q 10 | sci | Physics
A race car starting from rest accelerates uniformly. If the car covers 22.5 m in the 5th second of its motion, what is the total distance covered at the end of 10 seconds?
(1) 375 m
(2) 300 m
(3) 250 m
(4) 450 m
= 3
~ Distance covered in the nth second is sₙ = u + (a/2)(2n − 1). With u = 0 and n = 5: 22.5 = (a/2) × 9 = 4.5a, so a = 5 m/s². Distance in 10 s is s = ut + ½at² = 0 + ½ × 5 × 10² = 250 m. Check: distance in the first 5 s is ½ × 5 × 25 = 62.5 m and in the first 4 s is 40 m, and 62.5 − 40 = 22.5 m, as given.

Q 11 | sci | Physics
An object is thrown vertically upwards and rises to a height of 20 m. What was the velocity with which the object was thrown? (Take g =10 m/s²).
(1) 40 m/s
(2) 10 m/s
(3) 30 m/s
(4) 20 m/s
= 4
~ At the highest point the final velocity is zero. Using v² = u² − 2gh: 0 = u² − 2 × 10 × 20, so u² = 400 and u = 20 m/s. 40 m/s is too much: it would carry the object up to 40² ÷ (2 × 10) = 80 m, four times as high.

Q 12 | sci | Chemistry
Which of the following types of ore is pyrite?
(1) Halide
(2) Oxide
(3) Nitrate
(4) Sulphide
= 4
~ Pyrite (iron pyrites, FeS₂) is a compound of iron and sulphur, so it is a sulphide ore; its shiny golden colour gives it the name "fool's gold". Sulphide ores such as pyrite, galena (PbS), zinc blende (ZnS) and cinnabar (HgS) are first roasted in air to convert them into oxides before the metal is extracted. Haematite (Fe₂O₃) is an example of an oxide ore of iron.

Q 13 | sci | Physics
Which of the following is NOT caused by atmospheric refraction?
(1) Sun appearing higher in sky than its actual geometric position
(2) Advanced sunrise and delayed sunset
(3) Twinkling of stars
(4) Reddish colour of sun at sunrise / sunset
= 4
~ Atmospheric refraction is the bending of light as it passes through air layers of changing density; it makes stars twinkle, makes the Sun appear higher than its true position, and lets us see the Sun about 2 minutes before actual sunrise and after actual sunset. The reddish colour of the Sun at sunrise and sunset is due to scattering of light: sunlight travels a longer path through the atmosphere near the horizon, so most blue light is scattered away and mainly the longer-wavelength red light reaches our eyes.

Q 14 | sci | Physics
The heating effect of electric current is least in _________.
(1) Iron
(2) Copper
(3) Nichrome
(4) Tungsten
= 2
~ Heat produced by a current is H = I²Rt, so for the same current and time, a conductor with lower resistance produces less heat. Copper has the lowest resistivity among these (about 1.7 × 10⁻⁸ Ω m), lower than tungsten, iron and especially nichrome (about 100 × 10⁻⁸ Ω m). That is why copper is used for connecting wires, while nichrome, with its high resistivity, is used in heating elements of heaters and irons.

Q 15 | sci | Physics
If the velocity of a car increases from 20 m/s to 40 m/s in 4 seconds, the acceleration of the car is _____.
(1) 5 m/s²
(2) 5 m/s
(3) 10 m/s
(4) 10 m/s²
= 1
~ Acceleration = change in velocity ÷ time = (v − u)/t = (40 − 20)/4 = 20/4 = 5 m/s². The unit of acceleration is m/s², so options with m/s (the unit of velocity) are wrong even if the number looks right.

Q 16 | sci | Chemistry
When a drop of ink is added to water and left undisturbed, the ink gradually spreads throughout the water. Which property of matter does this observation best demonstrate?
(1) Fixed positions of particles
(2) Spaces between particles
(3) Attraction between particles
(4) Continuous movement of particles
= 4
~ The ink spreads on its own, without stirring, because the particles of ink and water are always moving and keep intermixing; this spontaneous intermixing is called diffusion. Diffusion is the standard evidence that particles of matter are continuously moving (and it becomes faster on heating, as particles move faster). Spaces between particles allow the mixing, but the spreading itself is caused by the movement of particles.

Q 18 | sci | Physics
In a longitudinal wave, particle’s motion is ___________.
(1) random
(2) parallel to the wave direction
(3) circular
(4) perpendicular to the wave motion
= 2
~ In a longitudinal wave, the particles of the medium vibrate back and forth along (parallel to) the direction in which the wave travels, forming compressions and rarefactions; sound in air is the common example. In a transverse wave, such as a wave on a stretched string, the particles move perpendicular to the direction of the wave.

Q 19 | sci | Biology
Fill in the blanks with the correct sequence of organs.
Urine is formed in the _______ → passes through the _______ → stored in the _______ → and finally released through the _______.
(1) Kidney → Ureter → Urinary bladder → Urethra
(2) Urethra → Urinary bladder → Ureter → Kidney
(3) Urinary bladder → Kidney → Ureter → Urethra

Q 20 | sci | Physics
The distance between the pole and focus of a concave mirror is 30 cm. A real and inverted image is formed by this mirror on a screen at 40 cm from the pole of this mirror. What will be the object distance?
(1) −120/7 cm
(2) +120/7 cm
(3) −120 cm
(4) +120 cm
= 3
~ By the sign convention, for a concave mirror f = −30 cm, and a real image forms in front of the mirror, so v = −40 cm. Mirror formula: 1/u = 1/f − 1/v = −1/30 + 1/40 = (−4 + 3)/120 = −1/120, so u = −120 cm. The object lies 120 cm in front of the mirror (beyond C at 60 cm), which matches a real, inverted image between F and C. The value 120/7 comes from wrongly adding 1/30 + 1/40.

Q 21 | sci | Biology
What role does the placenta play during the development of the embryo in the human female reproductive system?
(1) It facilitates the exchange of nutrients and wastes between mother and embryo.
(2) It is the location where fertilisation of egg and sperm occurs.
(3) It produces eggs needed for fertilisation.
(4) It unites the two oviducts into the uterus.
= 1
~ The placenta is a disc-shaped special tissue embedded in the uterine wall that connects the embryo with the mother. Through its large surface of villi, glucose, oxygen and other nutrients pass from the mother's blood to the embryo, and wastes such as carbon dioxide pass from the embryo to the mother's blood for removal. Fertilisation takes place in the oviduct (fallopian tube), and eggs are produced by the ovaries, so the other options do not describe the placenta.

Q 22 | sci | Biology
Which of the following organisms shows the phenomenon of regeneration?
(1) House fly
(2) Honey bee
(3) Mosquito
(4) Planaria
= 4
~ Regeneration is the ability of an organism to grow a complete new body from a cut piece of its body. Planaria, a flatworm, has specialised cells that can divide and form all the tissues, so if it is cut into pieces, each piece can grow into a complete planarian; Hydra also shows this. Insects such as the house fly, honey bee and mosquito are complex organisms that cannot regenerate in this way.

Q 23 | sci | Biology
What is the most important condition for successful fragmentation reproduction in Spirogyra?
(1) Each fragment possessing the capability to perform basic life functions
(2) The ability of fragments to form seeds
(3) Presence of reproductive organs in each fragment
(4) Availability of a fully developed vascular system
= 1
~ Spirogyra is a simple filamentous green alga. When mature, its filament simply breaks into small pieces, and each fragment grows into a new filament. This works because every cell of the filament is similar and can carry out all life functions such as photosynthesis and growth, so a fragment does not need seeds, special reproductive organs or a vascular system (which algae do not have) to become a new individual.

Q 24 | sci | Chemistry
Which of the following is an example of addition reaction?
(1) Chlorination of methane
(2) Hydrolysis of acetic acid
(3) Dehydration of alcohol
(4) Hydrogenation of ethene
= 4
~ In an addition reaction, atoms add across a double or triple bond of an unsaturated compound. In hydrogenation of ethene, hydrogen adds across the C=C double bond in the presence of a nickel catalyst: CH₂=CH₂ + H₂ → CH₃–CH₃ (ethane); the same type of reaction is used to make vegetable ghee from oils. Chlorination of methane is a substitution reaction, and dehydration of alcohol is an elimination reaction.

Q 25 | sci | Chemistry
Which statement best defines a combination reaction?
(1) Two reactants break down into simpler products.
(2) Two or more reactants combine to form a single product.
(3) One element displaces another element from its compound.
(4) Two compounds exchange ions to form new compounds.
= 2
~ A combination reaction is one in which two or more reactants combine to form a single product, for example CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ or 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. Breaking down into simpler products is decomposition, one element replacing another is displacement, and exchange of ions between two compounds is double displacement.

Q 31 | qa | Age, Calendar & Clock

(1) 28 years
(2) 26 years

(4) 25 years

Q 34 | qa | Time & Work, Pipes & Cisterns
Pipe A can fill a cistern in 15 minutes and Pipe B can fill it in 35 minutes, while Pipe C can empty the full cistern in 1 hour. If all the pipes are turned on at the same time, in how much time (in minutes) will the cistern be full?

Q 37 | qa | HCF, LCM & Fractions
There are 156 boy students and 143 girl students in a school. The principal wants to divide them in sections, such that a section can have only boys or only girls. If all sections should have the same number of students, and the principal wants to have as fewer sections as possible, how many sections would be there in the school?
(1) 45
(2) 25
(3) 43
(4) 23
= 4
~ For the fewest sections, each section must be as large as possible, so its size is the HCF of 156 and 143. Since 156 = 12 × 13 and 143 = 11 × 13, the HCF is 13. Number of sections = 156/13 + 143/13 = 12 + 11 = 23.

Q 38 | qa | Geometry & Mensuration
