Q 1 | sci | Physics
Which of the following statements is correct?
(1) Work done can only be negative.
(2) Work done can be positive or negative depending on the direction of force.
(3) Work done does not depend on force or displacement.
(4) Work done can only be positive.
= 2
~ Work done is W = F × s × cos θ, where θ is the angle between the force and the displacement. If the force acts along the displacement (θ = 0°), work is positive, e.g. a falling ball pulled by gravity; if the force acts opposite to the displacement (θ = 180°), work is negative, e.g. friction on a sliding block. So work can be positive or negative (and zero when the force is perpendicular to the displacement), which rules out "only positive" and "only negative"; and since W depends on both F and s, option 3 is also wrong.

Q 2 | sci | Biology
Through which method of cell division are the diploid chromosomes divided into haploid gametes?
(1) Mitosis
(2) Binary Fission
(3) Amitosis
(4) Meiosis
= 4
~ Meiosis is called reduction division because a diploid (2n) cell in the reproductive organs divides twice to form four haploid (n) cells, which become the gametes. When a male and a female gamete fuse at fertilisation, the diploid number is restored in the zygote. Mitosis keeps the chromosome number the same and is used for growth and repair, while binary fission and amitosis are simple direct divisions that do not halve the chromosome number.

Q 3 | sci | Physics
The power of a lens is defined as:
(1) The reciprocal of its focal length in meters
(2) The ratio of image distance to object distance
(3) The product of its focal length and refractive index
(4) The square of its focal length
= 1
~ Power of a lens is its ability to converge or diverge light and is given by P = 1/f, with f in metres. Its SI unit is the dioptre (D); 1 D is the power of a lens of focal length 1 m. A convex lens has positive power and a concave lens has negative power. The ratio of image distance to object distance (option 2) is the magnification, not the power.

Q 4 | sci | Physics
When a thick glass slab is placed over printed matter, the letters appear raised. This is due to ___________.
(1) Scattering of light
(2) Refraction of light
(3) Total internal reflection
(4) Polarization of light
= 2
~ Light from the letters travels up through the glass and bends away from the normal as it passes from the denser glass into the rarer air. To the eye these rays seem to come from a point higher than the actual letters, so the apparent depth is less than the real depth and the letters look raised. This bending of light at the boundary of two media is refraction; total internal reflection would need light to strike the surface beyond the critical angle, which does not happen here.

Q 5 | sci | Physics
The motion of a satellite orbiting the Earth in a circular path at constant speed is a classic example of uniform circular motion. What property of this motion prevents its acceleration from being zero?
(1) The total mechanical energy is conserved, necessitating acceleration.
(2) The direction of motion changes continuously at every point, meaning the velocity is changing.
(3) The object`s mass continually changes due to orbital dynamics.
(4) The speed is too high for the gravitational force to sustain a constant velocity.
= 2
~ Velocity is a vector, so it changes if either its magnitude (speed) or its direction changes. In uniform circular motion the speed stays constant, but the direction of motion keeps changing at every point along the circle, so the velocity is always changing and the body is accelerated. This acceleration (centripetal acceleration) points towards the centre of the circle; for a satellite it is provided by the Earth's gravitational pull.

Q 6 | sci | Biology
Asthma primarily affects which part of the respiratory system?
(1) Diaphragm
(2) Bronchi and bronchioles
(3) Trachea
(4) Alveoli
= 2
~ Asthma is a chronic disease of the airways in which the bronchi and bronchioles become inflamed, their muscles tighten and extra mucus is produced. The narrowed air passages cause wheezing, coughing and breathlessness, especially on breathing out. The alveoli are mainly affected in diseases like emphysema and pneumonia, and the diaphragm is a breathing muscle, not the site of asthma.

Q 7 | sci | Physics
Which of the following describes an example of uniform circular motion?
(1) A train moving on its track
(2) A car accelerating on its way
(3) A ball thrown vertically upwards
(4) A fan blade rotating at constant speed
= 4
~ Uniform circular motion means moving along a circular path with constant speed. Every point on a fan blade rotating at constant speed moves in a circle with unchanging speed, so it is the best example. A car that is accelerating changes its speed, a ball thrown upwards moves in a straight line and slows down, and a train on its track does not, in general, move in a circle.

Q 8 | sci | Biology
During reproduction, why is it important to create DNA copies?
(1) For blood circulation
(2) For digestion
(3) For inheritance of traits
(4) For respiration
= 3
~ DNA in the nucleus carries the information for making proteins, which decide the body design and characteristics of an organism. During reproduction the cell makes a copy of its DNA so that each new cell or offspring receives a full set of this information, and the traits of the parents are passed on (inherited). Small errors while copying DNA also produce variations, which help species survive changing conditions. Circulation, digestion and respiration are life processes not linked to DNA copying in reproduction.

Q 9 | sci | Chemistry
What happens when a naphthalene ball is burnt in a metal plate in open space?
(1) It burns with a blue flame.
(2) It burns with yellow flame with no deposit on the metal plate.
(3) It burns with a yellow flame with black soot deposited on the metal plate.
(4) It burns with a blue flame with black soot deposited on the metal plate.
= 3
~ Naphthalene (C₁₀H₈) is an aromatic compound with a very high proportion of carbon. Even in open air it does not get enough oxygen for complete combustion, so it burns with a yellow, sooty (smoky) flame and unburnt carbon particles settle as black soot on a metal plate held over it. A clean blue flame is given by saturated hydrocarbons or fuels like LPG burning with enough air, and blue flames do not leave soot.

Q 10 | sci | Biology
When water and nutrients are available, algae grow and multiply rapidly by the process of _________.
(1) spore formation
(2) pollination
(3) fragmentation
(4) budding
= 3
~ Filamentous algae such as Spirogyra reproduce by fragmentation: when they mature, the filament simply breaks into small pieces and each piece grows into a new individual. This is why algae spread quickly over ponds when water and nutrients are plentiful. Spore formation is typical of fungi like Rhizopus, budding of Hydra and yeast, and pollination is a step in sexual reproduction of flowering plants.

Q 11 | sci | Physics
A coil has a resistance of 44 Ω. If it is connected across a 220 V supply, what is the power consumed by the coil?
(1) 5 W
(2) 1100 W
(3) 110 W
(4) 0.2 W
= 2
~ Electric power can be found from P = V²/R. Here P = (220 × 220)/44 = 48400/44 = 1100 W. You can check it another way: current I = V/R = 220/44 = 5 A, and P = V × I = 220 × 5 = 1100 W. Option 1 (5) is the current in amperes, not the power in watts.

Q 12 | sci | Biology

Q 13 | sci | Physics
A body is dropped from a height. If it falls for 3.0 s and g = 10 m/s², its final velocity (v) is ______________.
(1) 10 m/s
(2) 30 m/s
(3) 40 m/s
(4) 20 m/s
= 2
~ A dropped body starts from rest, so initial velocity u = 0 and it accelerates downward at g. Using the first equation of motion, v = u + gt = 0 + 10 × 3.0 = 30 m/s. (10 m/s and 20 m/s are its speeds after 1 s and 2 s.)

Q 14 | sci | Chemistry
What happens when a zinc rod is placed in aqueous copper sulphate solution?
(1) The blue colour of the copper sulphate solution fades.
(2) The temperature of the solution falls.
(3) Heat is required to start the reaction.
(4) No chemical change observed.
= 1
~ Zinc is more reactive than copper, so it displaces copper from copper sulphate: Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu. Blue copper sulphate is slowly replaced by colourless zinc sulphate, so the blue colour fades, and reddish-brown copper deposits on the zinc rod. This displacement reaction takes place on its own at room temperature and gives out a little heat, so the temperature does not fall and no heating is needed to start it.

Q 15 | sci | Chemistry
The fractional atomic mass of chlorine is due to which of the following?
(1) Presence of ions
(2) Presence of isotopes
(3) Its compound nature
(4) Experimental error
= 2
~ Natural chlorine is a mixture of two isotopes, Cl-35 and Cl-37, present roughly in the ratio 3 : 1. Its atomic mass is the average of these: (35 × 75 + 37 × 25)/100 = (2625 + 925)/100 = 35.5 u. That is why chlorine's atomic mass is fractional; it is not caused by ions, by measurement error, or by chlorine being a compound (it is an element).

Q 16 | sci | Chemistry
The functional group which does NOT contain oxygen hetero atom is:
(1) halo alkane
(2) ketone
(3) carboxylic acid
(4) alcohol
= 1
~ A hetero atom is any atom other than carbon and hydrogen in an organic compound. In a haloalkane the functional group is a halogen atom (−Cl, −Br or −I), so it contains no oxygen. The other three groups all contain oxygen: ketone (>C=O), carboxylic acid (−COOH) and alcohol (−OH).

Q 17 | sci | Chemistry
What is the main role of micelles in the cleansing action of soap?
(1) To trap and remove oil and dirt from clothes
(2) To reduce the hardness of water
(3) To produce lather with water
(4) To increase the surface tension of water
= 1
~ A soap molecule has an ionic end that dissolves in water and a long hydrocarbon tail that dissolves in oil. In water, soap molecules form clusters called micelles, with the tails pointing inwards into the oily dirt and the ionic ends facing the water. The oil and dirt are thus trapped in the centre of the micelles, which stay suspended in water and are washed away on rinsing. Soap does not remove hardness (it forms scum with hard water), and it lowers rather than raises the surface tension of water.

Q 19 | sci | Physics
Statement 1: The potential difference is measured in volts.
Statement 2: One volt is equal to one newton per coulomb.
Which of the following is true?
(1) Both Statements 1 and 2 are correct.
(2) Only Statement 2 is correct.
(3) Both Statements 1 and 2 are incorrect.
(4) Only Statement 1 is correct.
= 4
~ Potential difference is the work done to move a unit charge between two points, V = W/Q, and its SI unit is the volt, so Statement 1 is correct. Hence 1 volt = 1 joule per coulomb (1 V = 1 J/C), not 1 newton per coulomb; newton per coulomb (N/C) is the unit of electric field. So Statement 2 is incorrect and only Statement 1 is correct.

Q 20 | sci | Physics
The frequency of a sound wave is 50 Hz. How much time will it take to complete one wave?
(1) 50 s
(2) 0.02 s
(3) 0.2 s
(4) 5 s
= 2
~ The time taken to complete one wave (one oscillation) is the time period T, and T = 1/frequency. So T = 1/50 = 0.02 s. A frequency of 50 Hz means 50 waves are completed every second, so each wave takes one-fiftieth of a second.

Q 21 | sci | Chemistry
Which of the following statements best explains the Law of Constant Proportions?
(1) Elements are always found in random ratios in a compound.
(2) Atoms can be divided into smaller particles during a reaction.
(3) Mass can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction.
(4) A chemical compound always contains the same elements in the same proportion by mass.
= 4
~ The Law of Constant (Definite) Proportions, given by Proust, says that a pure chemical compound always contains the same elements combined in the same fixed proportion by mass, whatever its source or method of preparation. For example, water always has hydrogen and oxygen in the mass ratio 1 : 8. Option 3 is the Law of Conservation of Mass, and option 1 contradicts the law.

Q 22 | sci | Chemistry
Which of the following is NOT used for expressing the concentration of a solution?
(1) Volume by Volume percentage of a solution
(2) Volume by Mass percentage of a solution
(3) Mass by volume percentage of a solution
(4) Mass by mass percentage of a solution
= 2
~ Concentration of a solution is commonly expressed as mass by mass percentage (mass of solute/mass of solution × 100), mass by volume percentage (mass of solute/volume of solution × 100) and volume by volume percentage (volume of solute/volume of solution × 100, used for liquid solutes). A "volume by mass" percentage, i.e. volume of solute per mass of solution, is not a standard way of expressing concentration.

Q 23 | sci | Biology
Which of the following plant tissue performs photosynthesis?
(1) Chlorenchyma
(2) Sclerenchyma
(3) Xylem
(4) Phloem
= 1
~ Chlorenchyma is parenchyma tissue whose cells contain chloroplasts; it is found mainly in green leaves and young stems and carries out photosynthesis. Sclerenchyma is made of dead, thick-walled cells that give strength, xylem carries water and minerals, and phloem carries food; none of these make food.

Q 24 | sci | Biology
Which of the following life processes is important to bring variation in species?
(1) Heterotrophic nutrition
(2) Transportation
(3) Autotrophic nutrition
(4) Sexual reproduction
= 4
~ In sexual reproduction, gametes formed by meiosis from two different parents fuse, so the offspring get a new combination of genes from both parents. This mixing of DNA creates variation among individuals, which helps a species survive changes in its environment and is the raw material for evolution. Nutrition and transportation keep an individual alive but do not create new combinations of traits.

Q 25 | sci | Chemistry
Which of the following metals is extracted in the middle of the activity series?
(1) Gold
(2) Potassium
(3) Iron
(4) Sodium
= 3
~ Metals in the middle of the activity series, such as iron, zinc and lead, are moderately reactive; their ores (usually sulphides or carbonates) are first converted to oxides by roasting or calcination and then reduced, often with carbon, to get the metal. Iron is one of these middle metals. Potassium and sodium are at the top of the series and are extracted by electrolysis of their molten chlorides, while gold is at the bottom and is found free in nature.

Q 26 | qa | Geometry & Mensuration
P is any point inside the rectangle ABCD. If PA = 98 cm, PB = 91 cm and PC = 21 cm, then the length of PD (in cm) is equal to:
(1) 38
(2) 41
(3) 46
(4) 42
= 4
~ For any point P inside a rectangle ABCD, the sums of squares of distances to opposite corners are equal (British Flag theorem): PA² + PC² = PB² + PD². So 98² + 21² = 91² + PD², i.e. 9604 + 441 = 8281 + PD², giving PD² = 10045 − 8281 = 1764 and PD = 42 cm.

Q 27 | qa | Geometry & Mensuration
The vertices of a convex pentagon (in order) are P(0,0), Q(6,0), R(8,3), S(4,7), T(0,4). Find the area of the pentagon.
(1) 42 sq. units
(2) 36 sq. units
(3) 39 sq. units
(4) 45 sq. units
= 3
~ Use the shoelace formula: Area = ½ |Σ (xᵢyᵢ₊₁ − xᵢ₊₁yᵢ)|. Taking the vertices in order: P→Q: 0×0 − 6×0 = 0; Q→R: 6×3 − 8×0 = 18; R→S: 8×7 − 4×3 = 44; S→T: 4×4 − 0×7 = 16; T→P: 0×0 − 0×4 = 0. The sum is 78, so the area = 78/2 = 39 sq. units.

Q 28 | qa | Ratio, Proportion & Average
A metal rod is divided into three parts, A, B and C. The lengths of A and B are in the ratio 9 : 20, and the lengths of C and B are in the ratio 5 : 11. If the difference between the lengths of A and C is 10 cm, find the total length (in cm) of the metal rod.
(1) 4202
(2) 4199
(3) 4190
(4) 4180
= 3

Q 29 | qa | Age, Calendar & Clock

Q 44 | qa | Time & Work, Pipes & Cisterns
Three pipes, A, B and C, can fill a tank from empty to full in 40 minutes, 20 minutes and 30 minutes, respectively. When the tank is empty, all the three pipes are opened. (1) 3/4
(2) 6/13
(3) 7/13
(4) 3/13
= 2
~ Take the tank as 120 units (LCM of 40, 20, 30). Per minute A gives 3 units of P, B gives 6 units of Q and C gives 4 units of R, a total of 13 units. Q 45 | qa | HCF, LCM & Fractions
Two-fifth of a container is filled with blue liquid, one-third of the remaining container is filled with black liquid, five-sixth of the still remaining container is filled with yellow liquid and the remaining 4.2 litres is of white colour. Find the total capacity of the container.
(1) 72 litres
(2) 63 litres
(3) 60 litres
(4) 78 litres
= 2
~ After blue, 1 − 2/5 = 3/5 is left. Black takes 1/3 of this, leaving (2/3) × (3/5) = 2/5. Yellow takes 5/6 of this, leaving (1/6) × (2/5) = 1/15 of the container for white. So (1/15) × capacity = 4.2 litres, and capacity = 4.2 × 15 = 63 litres.

Q 46 | qa | Speed, Time & Distance
A 325 m long train overtakes a man moving at a speed of 5 km/hr (in the same direction) in 45 seconds. How much time (in seconds) will it take this train to completely cross another 440 m long train, moving in the opposite direction at a speed of 20 km/hr?
(1) 54
(2) 40
(3) 52
(4) 51
= 1
~ Passing the man, the train covers its own length 325 m in 45 s at the relative speed (train − 5) km/hr: 325/45 m/s = 65/9 m/s = (65/9) × (18/5) = 26 km/hr, so the train's speed = 26 + 5 = 31 km/hr. Against the other train (opposite direction) the relative speed = 31 + 20 = 51 km/hr = 51 × 5/18 = 85/6 m/s. Distance = 325 + 440 = 765 m, so time = 765 ÷ (85/6) = 765 × 6/85 = 54 seconds.

Q 89 | ga | Geography & Environment
As reported in May 2025, the study by Climate Trends, a Delhi-based research consultancy, examined NASA satellite data of the Himalayan glaciers from 2000-2023 to evaluate the impact of which of the following on Himalayan snow?
(1) Ozone
(2) Sulfate Aerosols
(3) Black Carbon
(4) Mineral Dust Particles
= 3
~ The Climate Trends study used NASA satellite data for 2000-2023 to study black carbon (soot) over the Himalayas. Black carbon settling on snow makes it darker, so the snow reflects less sunlight (lower albedo), absorbs more heat and melts faster; the study found the average snow surface temperature rose from about −11.27 °C in 2000-2009 to −7.13 °C in 2020-2023. Black carbon comes mainly from burning biomass and fossil fuels, and the Indo-Gangetic Plain was identified as a major source region.
