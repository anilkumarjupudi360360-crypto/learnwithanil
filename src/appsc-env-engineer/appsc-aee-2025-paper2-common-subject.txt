Q 1 | ecs | Ecosystem Structure & Function
Why are ecosystems important?
(1) They provide a habitat for wildlife only.
(2) They regulate climate and provide oxygen.
(3) They are solely responsible for soil formation.
(4) They serve as source of entertainment for humans.
= 2
~ Ecosystems provide regulating and supporting services: plants release oxygen through photosynthesis, forests and oceans store carbon, and vegetation and water bodies moderate local and global climate. The other options are too narrow or wrong: ecosystems are not a habitat for wildlife only, soil formation also depends on weathering of rocks and climate, and recreation is only a minor cultural service.

Q 2 | ecs | Ecosystem Structure & Function
Which of the following is a part of terrestrial ecosystems?
(1) Estuaries
(2) Rivers
(3) Wetlands
(4) Grasslands
= 4
~ Terrestrial (land-based) ecosystems include forests, grasslands and deserts. Grasslands are dominated by grasses with few trees and occur on land, so they are terrestrial. Rivers are freshwater (lotic) aquatic ecosystems, estuaries are where rivers meet the sea, and wetlands are waterlogged transitional areas, so all three are treated as aquatic ecosystems.

Q 3 | nrr | Forest Resources
Which of the following is an indirect utilisation of the forest ecosystem?
(1) Fruits
(2) Wood for farm implements
(3) Grass for grazing
(4) Paper
= 4
~ Fruits, wood for farm implements and grass for grazing are taken from the forest and used directly by local people. Paper is an indirect use: wood or bamboo is first converted into pulp in an industry, and only the processed product reaches the user. Hence paper represents indirect utilisation of the forest ecosystem.

Q 4 | ecs | Food Chains, Webs & Pyramids
In an ecosystem, some animals feed on other organisms, while others feed on dead organic matter. What is this type of food chain called?
(1) Herbivorous food chain
(2) Detritus food chain
(3) Carnivorous food chain
(4) Omnivorous food chain
= 2
~ A food chain that begins with dead organic matter (detritus such as fallen leaves and dead animals) is called the detritus food chain: detritivores and decomposers like earthworms, bacteria and fungi feed on detritus and are in turn eaten by other animals. The other main type is the grazing food chain, which starts from green plants. Herbivorous, carnivorous and omnivorous food chains are not standard ecological terms.

Q 5 | ecs | Food Chains, Webs & Pyramids
Which of the following best describes a food web?
(1) A linear representation of energy flow from producers to consumers in an ecosystem
(2) A single chain showing the flow of energy from one trophic level to another
(3) A complex network of interconnected food chains in an ecosystem
(4) A graphical representation of the nutrient cycle within an ecosystem
= 3
~ A food web is the network formed when many food chains in an ecosystem interconnect, because most organisms eat, and are eaten by, more than one species. It provides alternative pathways for energy flow and makes the ecosystem more stable. Options 1 and 2 describe a single food chain, and nutrient cycles are biogeochemical cycles, not food webs.

Q 6 | ecs | Food Chains, Webs & Pyramids
Which is the most basic and the first trophic level of an ecological pyramid?
(1) Primary consumers
(2) Secondary consumers
(3) Producers
(4) Tertiary consumers
= 3
~ Producers (autotrophs such as green plants and phytoplankton) make food from sunlight by photosynthesis and form the first trophic level, the base of every ecological pyramid. Primary consumers (herbivores) form the second level, secondary consumers (carnivores) the third, and tertiary consumers the higher levels.

Q 7 | ecs | Food Chains, Webs & Pyramids
In an ecological pyramid, what is the primary direction of energy flow?
(1) From consumers to producers
(2) From producers to consumers
(3) From decomposers to consumers
(4) From predators to producers
= 2
~ Energy enters the ecosystem through producers, which fix solar energy, and then passes to herbivores and carnivores. The flow is unidirectional: energy is lost as heat at each step (only about 10% passes on, Lindeman's ten per cent law) and never returns to the producers. So the pyramid of energy is always upright, with energy moving from producers to consumers.

Q 8 | ecs | Ecosystem Structure & Function
Which of the following best describes an ecosystem?
(1) Only plants and animals in a particular area
(2) Soil, water, and air in a specific location
(3) Climate and weather patterns of a region
(4) Involves interaction between living organisms and the environment
= 4
~ An ecosystem (a term coined by A.G. Tansley in 1935) is a functional unit in which the living (biotic) community interacts with its non-living (abiotic) environment, with energy flowing and nutrients cycling between them. Option 1 lists only the biotic part, option 2 only the abiotic part and option 3 only climate; none covers the interaction that defines an ecosystem.

Q 9 | ecs | Biogeochemical Cycles & Bioaccumulation
Which of the following best describes bio-accumulation?
(1) The gradual accumulation of pollutants or toxins in the tissues of living organisms over time
(2) The process by which organisms eliminate toxins from their bodies
(3) The process by which organisms accumulate food
(4) The movement of nutrients through different trophic levels in an ecosystem
= 1
~ Bioaccumulation is the build-up of a persistent substance such as DDT, mercury or lead in the tissues of an organism over time, because it is taken in faster than it is broken down or excreted. It differs from biomagnification, the rise in concentration from one trophic level to the next. Option 2 describes excretion or detoxification and option 4 describes nutrient flow.

Q 10 | ecs | Biogeochemical Cycles & Bioaccumulation
What does the term ‘biogeochemical’ signify?
(1) Movement of organisms through the ecosystem
(2) Flow of energy from sunlight to heat
(3) Cycling of elements between living and nonliving components
(4) Geological processes affecting only the biosphere
= 3
~ 'Biogeochemical' joins bio (living organisms), geo (rocks, soil, water and air) and chemical (elements). Biogeochemical cycles such as the carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus and water cycles describe how elements move from the non-living environment into organisms and back again. Energy flows one way and is not cycled, so option 2 is wrong; movement of organisms and purely geological processes are not what the term means.

Q 11 | ecs | Food Chains, Webs & Pyramids
Read the following statement and conclusions carefully and decide which of the conclusions logically follow(s) from the given statement.
Statement: In an ecological pyramid, the energy transfer between trophic levels follows a hierarchical structure, with each successive level receiving less energy than the previous one.
Conclusions:
I. Top predators in an ecosystem, such as apex predators, have the lowest energy availability compared to primary producers.
II. Primary producers contribute the most energy to the ecosystem, forming the foundation of the food web.
(1) Only conclusion I follows
(2) Only conclusion II follows
(3) Neither conclusion I nor II follows
(4) Both conclusions I and II follow
= 4
~ The statement says each higher trophic level receives less energy than the one below it. Apex predators sit at the top, so they have the least energy available compared with producers, and conclusion I follows. Producers form the first level and capture the solar energy that supports all other levels, so they hold the most energy and form the base of the food web; conclusion II also follows.

Q 12 | ecs | Ecosystem Structure & Function
Read the following statement and conclusions carefully and decide which of the conclusions logically follow(s) from the given statement.
Statement: Ecosystems provide invaluable services that support life on Earth and contribute to human well-being.
Conclusions:
I. Therefore, preserving ecosystems is essential for maintaining biodiversity and ecological balance.

Q 13 | ecs | Ecosystem Structure & Function
Select the option that is true regarding the following two statements labelled Assertion (A) and Reason (R).
Assertion (A): Ecosystem services are essential for sustaining human well-being and economic development.
Reason (R): The carrying capacity of an ecosystem determines its ability to provide services such as food production, water purification, and climate regulation. As human populations increase, the demand for these services may exceed the ecosystem’s capacity, leading to degradation and loss of biodiversity.
(1) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
(2) Both A and R are true, and R is not the correct explanation of A
(3) A is true, but R is false
(4) A is false, but R is true
= 1
~ Ecosystem services such as food, clean water, pollination and climate regulation sustain human well-being and the economy, so A is true. R is also true: an ecosystem can supply these services only within its carrying capacity, and when growing human demand exceeds it, the ecosystem degrades and biodiversity is lost. R explains why these services are vital and limited, so it is the correct explanation of A.

Q 16 | ecs | Food Chains, Webs & Pyramids
What restricts the number of trophic levels in a food chain to four or five?
(1) Limited availability of producers
(2) Significant energy loss (80-90%) at each transfer
(3) Competition between top predators
(4) Decline in biodiversity at higher levels
= 2
~ According to Lindeman's ten per cent law, only about 10% of the energy at one trophic level is passed on to the next; roughly 80–90% is lost as heat in respiration and in undigested or unused matter. After four or five transfers too little energy remains to support another level of consumers, so food chains are short. The other options do not explain this limit.

Q 17 | nrr | Water Resources & Dams
How can droughts be mitigated?
(1) By increasing water consumption during dry seasons
(2) By implementing water conservation measures, such as rain water harvesting
(3) By promoting deforestation to increase soil moisture retention
(4) By encouraging excessive irrigation practices
= 2
~ Droughts are mitigated by storing and using water wisely: rainwater harvesting, watershed development, check dams, groundwater recharge, afforestation and efficient irrigation such as drip systems. Increasing consumption, deforestation (which reduces infiltration and soil moisture) and excessive irrigation would deplete water further and worsen drought.

Q 18 | nrr | Energy Resources
Which of the following is an example of non-renewable energy sources?
(1) Hydropower
(2) Geothermal
(3) Solar energy
(4) Natural gas
= 4
~ Natural gas is a fossil fuel formed from organic matter buried over millions of years; its reserves are finite and cannot be replenished on a human time scale, so it is non-renewable. Hydropower (driven by the water cycle), geothermal energy (the Earth's internal heat) and solar energy are continuously renewed by natural processes and are renewable sources.

Q 19 | eia | Sustainable Development & Green Concepts
Which of the following is NOT an indicator of sustainable lifestyles in human life?
(1) No income enhancement
(2) Increased longevity
(3) An increase in knowledge
(4) An enhancement of income
= 1
~ The indicators of quality of life and sustainable lifestyles are increased longevity, an increase in knowledge and an enhancement of income, which match the three dimensions of UNDP's Human Development Index (life expectancy, education and per-capita income). 'No income enhancement' shows no improvement in living standards, so it is not an indicator of a sustainable lifestyle.

Q 20 | nrr | Water Resources & Dams
Which of the following is a potential problem caused by dams?
(1) Increased agricultural productivity
(2) Reduced risk of flooding downstream
(3) Disruption of natural river flow
(4) Preservation of aquatic ecosystems
= 3
~ Dams break the natural flow regime of a river: they store water, change downstream discharge and temperature, trap silt, block fish migration and fragment aquatic habitats. They also submerge forests and farmland and displace people. Increased agricultural productivity and reduced downstream flooding are benefits of dams, and dams harm rather than preserve aquatic ecosystems.

Q 21 | nrr | Mineral & Land Resources
Which modern method is used to locate and exploit mineral deposits?
(1) Panning
(2) Satellite imagery and remote sensing technique
(3) Traditional mining techniques
(4) Surface sampling
= 2
~ Modern mineral exploration uses satellite imagery and remote sensing (multispectral and hyperspectral data), together with GIS and geophysical surveys, to map rock types, faults and alteration zones that indicate ore bodies over large areas quickly. Panning, surface sampling and traditional mining techniques are older, ground-based methods.

Q 22 | nrr | Mineral & Land Resources
Which of the following is a potential consequence of the use and exploitation of mineral resources?
(1) Decreases environmental pollution
(2) Enhances biodiversity in affected areas
(3) Ecological balance
(4) Soil erosion and habitat destruction
= 4
~ Mining removes vegetation and topsoil, causing deforestation, soil erosion and loss of wildlife habitat; it also produces dust, acid mine drainage and heavy-metal pollution of water and land. So it increases pollution, reduces biodiversity and disturbs rather than maintains ecological balance, which rules out options 1, 2 and 3.

Q 23 | nrr | Forest Resources
Which of the following best describes the function of forests?
(1) Forests contribute to noise pollution reduction in urban areas.
(2) Forests are mainly used for urban development and construction purposes.
(3) Forests primarily serve as recreational areas for tourists.
(4) Forests prevent flash floods and soil erosion.
= 4
~ Forest cover intercepts rainfall, and roots and leaf litter bind the soil and increase infiltration, so run-off is slowed and released gradually. This watershed function prevents flash floods and soil erosion and keeps streams flowing. Forests are not mainly for construction or tourism, and noise reduction by urban green belts is only a minor role.

Q 24 | bdv | Biodiversity & Its Value
Which of the following represents the living natural resources?
(1) Air
(2) Plants
(3) Soil
(4) Minerals
= 2
~ Natural resources are classified as living (biotic) and non-living (abiotic) resources. Plants, animals and micro-organisms are living resources that grow, reproduce and can regenerate if used sustainably. Air, soil and minerals are non-living (abiotic) resources, and minerals in particular are non-renewable.

Q 25 | nrr | Water Resources & Dams
Which of the following is a consequence of overutilisation of surface and groundwater?
(1) Enhanced water quality
(2) Increased aquifer recharge rates
(3) Drought
(4) Expansion of wetland habitats
= 3
~ When surface and groundwater are withdrawn faster than they are recharged, rivers and lakes dry up and the water table falls, leading to water scarcity and drought conditions; other effects include land subsidence and saline intrusion into coastal aquifers. Overuse lowers rather than raises aquifer recharge, shrinks wetlands and does not improve water quality.

Q 26 | nrr | Mineral & Land Resources
Which of the following activities distinguishes the exploitation stage from other stages of mining operations?
(1) Identification of mineral deposits through geophysical surveys
(2) Evaluation of the economic feasibility of extracting a deposit
(3) Construction of access routes and infrastructure for extraction
(4) Direct removal of minerals from the deposit for commercial use
= 4
~ Mining passes through prospecting (searching for the deposit), exploration (surveys and assessment of size, grade and economic feasibility), development (access roads, shafts and infrastructure), exploitation and finally reclamation. Exploitation is the stage in which ore is actually extracted from the deposit for commercial production. Options 1, 2 and 3 belong to prospecting, exploration and development.

Q 32 | bdv | Hotspots & Threats
Which biogeographical region, known for its high biodiversity, is under threat due to human habitation?
(1) Biodiversity hotspot
(2) Ecosystem
(3) Genetic diversity
(4) All geographic regions
= 1
~ A biodiversity hotspot (concept introduced by Norman Myers in 1988) is a region with at least 1,500 endemic vascular plant species that has already lost 70% or more of its original habitat, largely to human settlement and land use. Hotspots thus combine very high biodiversity with high threat. India has parts of four of the 36 global hotspots: Himalaya, Indo-Burma, Western Ghats and Sri Lanka, and Sundaland.

Q 40 | bdv | Hotspots & Threats
What happens when non-native species, like Lantana, are introduced into ecosystems?
(1) Stabilises local species
(2) Disrupts balance, causing extinctions
(3) Enhances biodiversity
(4) Promotes agriculture
= 2
~ Lantana camara, introduced from tropical America as an ornamental, has become an invasive alien species in India. It forms dense thickets, suppresses native plants through competition and allelopathy, reduces fodder for herbivores and increases fire risk. Such invasive species disrupt the ecological balance and can cause decline or extinction of native species, rather than stabilising or enhancing biodiversity.
