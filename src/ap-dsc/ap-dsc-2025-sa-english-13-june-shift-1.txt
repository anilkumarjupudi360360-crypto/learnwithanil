Q 31 | psy | Intelligence & Individual Differences
The following is NOT a subtest of the Differential Aptitude Test (DAT)

(2) Numerical Ability

Q 32 | psy | Motivation, Attitude & Personality
The following is NOT a limitation of using rating sales to assess personality
(1) Type I error
(2) Generosity error
(3) Hallo effect
(4) Error due to central tendency
= 1
~ Rating scales suffer from the halo effect, the generosity (leniency) error, the error of central tendency, logical error and so on. A Type I error is a statistical term – rejecting a true null hypothesis in hypothesis testing – and is not a limitation of rating scales.

Q 33 | psy | Motivation, Attitude & Personality

Q 34 | psy | Mental Health & Adjustment

(1) Approach-Approach
(2) Approach-Avoidance
(3) Avoidance-Avoidance
(4) Double approach-Avoidance
= 1
~ Having to choose between two equally attractive goals (two important weddings at the same time) is an approach–approach conflict (Lewin). Avoidance–avoidance involves two unpleasant choices, and approach–avoidance one goal with both attractive and unattractive features.

Q 35 | psy | Learning & Learning Theories
A boy can memorise by reading any aspect without understanding its meaning. His memory is
(1) Abnormal memory
(2) Rote memory
(3) Long term memory
(4) Logical memory
= 2
~ Memorising material mechanically by repetition without understanding its meaning is rote memory. Logical memory, by contrast, depends on understanding meaning and relationships; long-term memory refers to how long information is stored, not how it is learnt.

Q 36 | psy | Learning & Learning Theories
A woman fell down from a scooter while her husband was taking her to a market. From then on, she developed a fear of travelling on a scooter with her husband. But she is not scared of travelling on a scooter with others. This is
(1) Stimulus generalization
(2) Stimulus discrimination
(3) High order conditioning
(4) Spontaneous recovery
= 2
~ She fears only the conditioned stimulus (riding with her husband) and not similar stimuli (riding with others) – this is stimulus discrimination in classical conditioning. If she feared every scooter ride, it would be stimulus generalization.
