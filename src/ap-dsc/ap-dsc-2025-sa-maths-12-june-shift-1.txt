(2) Nuclear Physics

(1) Article 10, 12 & 14

(3) Article 14, 19 & 21

Q 31 | psy | Motivation, Attitude & Personality
The tests that assess the personality of a person by eliciting responses to meaningless stimuli are
(1) Rating Scales
(2) Projective tests
(3) Questionnaires
(4) Inventories
= 2
~ Projective tests (e.g. Rorschach inkblots, TAT, sentence completion) present vague or unstructured stimuli; the person projects hidden feelings and motives into his responses. Rating scales, questionnaires and inventories use clear, structured items, so they are not projective.

Q 33 | psy | Learning & Learning Theories
The correct order with respect to the process of memory is
(1) Retention, Encoding, Retrieval
(2) Retrieval, Encoding, Retention
(3) Encoding, Retention, Retrieval
(4) Retention, Retrieval, Encoding
= 3
~ Memory works in three stages: information is first encoded (taken in and converted into a usable form), then retained (stored), and finally retrieved (recalled or recognised) when needed. Nothing can be stored before it is encoded or recalled before it is stored.

Q 35 | psy | Learning & Learning Theories
A boy developed a fear of the doctor, when an injection was given by him. From that day onwards he is scared of not only that doctor but also all other doctors. This is
(1) Higher order conditioning
(2) Operant conditioning
(3) Stimulus Generalization
(4) Stimulus discrimination
= 3
~ In classical conditioning, once a response (fear) is conditioned to one stimulus (the doctor who gave the injection), similar stimuli (all other doctors) also evoke it; this is stimulus generalization. Stimulus discrimination would be fearing only that one doctor and not the others.

= 2

Q 37 | psy | Learning & Learning Theories

(2) b, d, a, c
