Q 32 | psy | Learning & Learning Theories

Q 34 | psy | Learning & Learning Theories
Teacher gives reinforcement for every correct answer given by the child – This is called
(1) Fixed Interval Reinforcement Schedule
(2) Continuous Reinforcement Schedule
(3) Fixed Ratio Reinforcement Schedule
(4) Variable Reinforcement Schedule
= 2
~ In Skinner’s schedules of reinforcement, rewarding every correct response is continuous reinforcement; it is the quickest way to establish a new response. Fixed-ratio reinforces after a set number of responses, fixed-interval after a set time, and variable schedules at unpredictable points.

Q 35 | psy | Learning & Learning Theories
Sequential order of stages in the process of Memory
(1) Encoding-Retention-Retrieval
(2) Retention-Encoding-Retrieval
(3) Retrieval-Retention-Encoding
(4) Retention-Retrieval-Encoding
= 1
~ Memory works in three stages: information is first encoded (taken in and changed into a form the brain can use), then retained or stored, and finally retrieved (recalled or recognised) when needed. Nothing can be stored before it is encoded, or recalled before it is stored.

Q 36 | psy | Learning & Learning Theories
The transfer of learning that takes place in a person who is an expert in swimming, learns Computer education
(1) Zero transfer
(2) Negative transfer
(3) Positive transfer
(4) Bilateral transfer
= 1
~ Swimming and computer learning share no common elements (Thorndike’s identical elements), so the earlier skill neither helps nor hinders the new learning—this is zero transfer. Positive transfer needs similar elements (e.g., cycling helping in learning to ride a motorcycle); bilateral transfer passes from one side of the body to the other.

Q 40 | psy | Child-Centred Pedagogy & Assessment

(1) Frequency/Range
(2) Highest Value/Frequency
(3) Range/No. of Classes
(4) Lowest Value/No. of Classes
= 3
