(1) Manipur
(2) Tripura
(3) Bihar
(4) Uttar Pradesh
= 2

Q 26 | ped | Pedagogy & Methods

‘Play is Life’ theory was promoted by
(1) John Dewey

Q 30 | ped | Psychology, Sociology & Measurement

(2) Growth
(3) Intelligence
(4) Memory

~ Growth means quantitative physical change — increase in height, weight and size — that can be seen and measured. Development is qualitative: it covers changes in functions and behaviour, which cannot be measured directly.

Q 31 | ped | Anatomy, Physiology & Kinesiology
The reason why the women left behind men in long distance running races.
(1) Low Height
(2) Hemoglobin is low
(3) Heavy Weight
(4) Low Weight
= 2
~ Women generally have lower haemoglobin (about 12–15 g/dL against 14–17 g/dL in men) and a smaller heart and blood volume, so less oxygen reaches the working muscles and VO₂ max is lower. This limits performance in long-distance (endurance) running.

Q 34 | ped | Psychology, Sociology & Measurement

(2) Recognition
(3) Cups and Medals
(4) Status
= 1
~ Intrinsic rewards come from within the activity itself — enjoyment, satisfaction and the absorbed attention (concentration) a player feels while playing. Recognition, cups and medals, and status are given by others, so they are extrinsic rewards.

Q 35 | ped | Pedagogy & Methods
This age is NOT used in the classification of boys for physical activities.

= 3
~ Pupils are classified for physical activities on the basis of physical maturity — chronological age, physiological age, anatomical age, height and weight (e.g. McCloy's classification index). Mental age reflects intellectual level, not physical capacity, so it is not used.

Q 36 | ped | Pedagogy & Methods
Student physically, mentally ready and motivate himself to learn are confirmed by this law
(1) Law of Contiguity
(2) Law of Effect
(3) Law of Readiness
(4) Law of Exercise
= 3
~ Thorndike's law of readiness says learning is effective when the learner is physically and mentally ready and wants to learn; forcing an unready learner causes annoyance. The law of exercise concerns repetition and the law of effect concerns satisfying results.

Q 37 | ped | Psychology, Sociology & Measurement
‘The direction and intensity of behaviour’ is explained by

Q 39 | ped | Pedagogy & Methods
The teacher says, “Follow me” (or) “Do as I do,” in this method
(1) Imitation method
(2) Dramatization method
(3) Oral method
(4) Whole method
= 1
~ In the imitation method the teacher demonstrates the movement and the pupils copy it, with cues such as 'Follow me' or 'Do as I do'; it suits young children and simple activities. In the dramatization method children act out stories or characters, and the whole method teaches a skill as one unit.

Q 40 | ped | Sports Training & Officiating
The weight is the main criteria for the classification of competitors in this game
(1) Kabaddi
(2) Basketball
(3) Volleyball
(4) Handball
= 1
~ Kabaddi competitions are held in weight categories (for example, senior men up to 85 kg and senior women up to 75 kg), so weight is the main basis for classifying players. Basketball, volleyball and handball have no weight limits; there players are grouped by age.

Q 41 | ped | Pedagogy & Methods
The following method is suitable to teach activities for children of elementary school
(1) Part method
(2) Oral method
(3) Dramatization method
(4) Progressive part method
= 3
~ Elementary-school children learn best through imagination and make-believe, so the dramatization method — acting out animals, characters or stories (story plays, mimetics) — suits them. The part and progressive-part methods break complex skills into pieces and suit older learners.

Q 42 | ped | Pedagogy & Methods
Command method is divided into
(1) 5 types
(2) 2 types
(3) 4 types
(4) 3 types
= 2
~ A command has two parts: the preparatory (cautionary) command, which tells pupils what is to be done (e.g. 'Squad…'), and the executive command, which tells them when to do it (e.g. '…Halt!'). So the command method is divided into two types.

Q 43 | ped | Pedagogy & Methods
This method is suitable to take attendance accurately and easily with in short time
(1) Squad method
(2) Reading the names method
(3) Calling Roll number
(4) Fixing numbers on walls
= 1
~ In the squad method the class is divided into squads, each with a leader; the leaders quickly check their squads and report absentees, so attendance is accurate and takes little time. Reading names or calling roll numbers one by one wastes much of the period.

Q 44 | ped | Pedagogy & Methods
This method is highly recommended to teach major games
(1) Set drill method
(2) Whole part whole method
(3) Part method
(4) Oral method
= 2
~ Major games have many complex skills, so the whole–part–whole method is preferred: pupils first get an idea of the whole game, then practise the difficult skills separately, and finally put them back into the whole game. The part method alone breaks the continuity of the game.

Q 45 | ped | Pedagogy & Methods
This method has time for close observation, demonstration and discussion
(1) Project method
(2) Set drill method
(3) Lecture method
(4) Exploration method
= 4
~ In the exploration method the teacher sets movement problems and pupils explore their own responses; time is kept for close observation of the pupils, demonstration of good responses and discussion. The lecture method is mainly teacher talk, and set drill is done on command with no room for discussion.

Q 46 | ped | Pedagogy & Methods
This method has the best technique for teaching the combative skills
(1) Demonstration method
(2) Lecturer method
(3) Project method
(4) Whole method
= 1
~ Combatives (wrestling, judo, boxing, fencing, etc.) involve exact holds, grips and counter-moves that pupils must see to copy safely, so the demonstration method is best. A lecture cannot show the technique, and the project method is meant for knowledge-based work.

Q 47 | ped | Pedagogy & Methods
These are the tools in the hands of the teachers for influencing the good class management.
(1) Combatives
(2) Commands
(3) Rhythmics
(4) Gymnastics
= 2
~ Commands are the teacher's main tools of class control: a clear command (preparatory part + executive word) gets a prompt, uniform response from the whole class. Combatives, rhythmics and gymnastics are activities taught in class, not tools of managing it.

Q 48 | ped | Pedagogy & Methods
Mcloy’s classification for elementary grade pupil’s formula is

Q 49 | ped | Pedagogy & Methods

(1) File formation

Q 50 | ped | Pedagogy & Methods
Half of the lesson time is allocated to this part of the general lesson plan.
(1) Recreative activities
(2) Formal activities
(3) Introductory part
(4) Special activities
= 1
~ In a general lesson plan of about 40 minutes, the introductory activity takes about 1/8 (5 min), formal activities about 1/4 (10 min), and the recreative activities (minor games, relays) — the main part of the lesson — about half (20 min); the rest is for recall and dismissal.

Q 51 | ped | Pedagogy & Methods
These exercises are not only corrective but have educative values like well developed muscles, power of attention, sense and accurate response also.
(1) Rhythamics
(2) Combatives
(3) Calisthenics
(4) Minor games
= 3
~ Calisthenics are free-hand exercises done to counts or commands; they correct posture and also have educative value — they develop the muscles and train attention and quick, accurate response to commands. Rhythmics are done to music for rhythm and grace, and minor games are mainly recreational.

Q 52 | ped | Pedagogy & Methods
‘Lead – up activity’ is a part of lesson plan related to
(1) General lesson plan
(2) Knowledge lesson plan
(3) Appreciate lesson plan
(4) Specific lesson plan
= 4
~ Lead-up activities are simplified games and drills that lead pupils towards a particular major game; they form part of the specific (special/coaching) lesson plan, which teaches the skills of one game. The general lesson plan has introductory, formal and recreative activities.

Q 56 | ped | Pedagogy & Methods
Teaching of ‘Pyramid building’ comes under

(2) Dynamic gymnastics
(3) Heavy gymnastics
(4) Static gymnastics

Q 57 | ped | Pedagogy & Methods

= 2
~ The peg system is a method of taking roll call (attendance): each pupil has a numbered peg/tag on a board and hangs or removes it on arrival, so the vacant pegs show the absentees at a glance without calling names.

Q 58 | ped | Pedagogy & Methods
While calculating McCloy’s classification index, height is to be taken in
(1) Inches
(2) Centimeters
(3) Meters
(4) Feet
= 1
~ McCloy's classification index uses age in years, height in inches and weight in pounds, e.g. high school index = 20 × age + 6 × height (inches) + weight (pounds).

Q 59 | ped | Pedagogy & Methods
The manner of artistic execution in presenting a subject matter is
(1) Personal preparation
(2) Presentation technique
(3) Teaching Aids
(4) Technical preparation
= 2
~ Presentation technique is the manner of artistic execution of the lesson — how the teacher presents the subject matter through explanation, demonstration, voice and class handling. Personal and technical preparation are done before the class, and teaching aids are only tools used in it.

Q 61 | ped | Organisation, Management & Recreation

= 3

Q 62 | ped | Organisation, Management & Recreation

(1) Implementation of plan
(2) Low level of function
(3) Completion of work
(4) Initial stage

~ Administration means putting the plan into action — directing and carrying out the programme through the people and resources that organisation has arranged. Organisation prepares the plan; administration implements it.

Q 63 | ped | Organisation, Management & Recreation

(4) Medicine ball

Q 64 | ped | Organisation, Management & Recreation
