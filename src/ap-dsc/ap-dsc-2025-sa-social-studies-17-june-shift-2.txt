(3) Phosphorus
(4) Carbon
= 4

Q 32 | psy | Intelligence & Individual Differences

(2) Cognition
(3) Behaviour
(4) Systems
= 3

Q 35 | psy | Learning & Learning Theories
The three sequential stages of process of memory are
(1) Learning, Recall, Re learning
(2) Recall, Retention, Re learning
(3) Encoding, Relearning, Forgetting
(4) Encoding, Retention, Retrieval
= 4
~ Memory works in three stages: encoding (taking in and registering information), retention or storage (keeping it over time) and retrieval (bringing it back through recall or recognition). Relearning is a method of measuring retention, and forgetting is the failure to retain or retrieve; they are not stages of memory.

Q 36 | psy | Motivation, Attitude & Personality
Maslow’s Hierarchy of Needs indicates that
(1) Safety needs are to be met first
(2) Physiological needs are to be met first
(3) Love and belonging needs are to be met first
(4) Self – actualization is to be met first
= 2
~ Maslow arranged needs in a hierarchy: physiological, safety, love and belonging, esteem and self-actualisation. The basic physiological needs (food, water, sleep, shelter) must be met first; only then do safety and the higher needs motivate behaviour, with self-actualisation at the top.

Q 37 | psy | Learning & Learning Theories
‘Results of teaching learning should provide satisfaction, pleasure and happiness in students’. This statement is given by Thorndike’s
(1) Law of Readiness
(2) Law of Exercise
(3) Law of Effect
(4) Classical Law
= 3
~ Thorndike's law of effect states that a response followed by a satisfying outcome is strengthened and one followed by an annoying outcome is weakened, so learning results should give pupils satisfaction and pleasure. The law of readiness concerns the learner's preparedness to act, and the law of exercise concerns practice and repetition.

Q 38 | psy | Motivation, Attitude & Personality

Q 39 | psy | Motivation, Attitude & Personality

Q 40 | psy | Mental Health & Adjustment
A boy wants to watch a movie. But fears that his father may not permit. This type of conflict is
(1) Approach - Approach
(2) Approach - Avoidance
(3) Avoidance - Avoidance
(4) Double Approach – Avoidance
= 2
~ In Lewin's classification, an approach–avoidance conflict arises when one goal has both attractive and unpleasant sides: the boy is drawn to the movie but fears his father's disapproval. Approach–approach is a choice between two pleasant goals, avoidance–avoidance between two unpleasant ones, and double approach–avoidance involves two goals, each with pros and cons.
