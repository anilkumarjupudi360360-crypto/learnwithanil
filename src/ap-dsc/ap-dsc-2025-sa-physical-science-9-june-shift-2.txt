(2) Kabir Das

(4) Harivamsharoy Bachan

(2) Very superior

Q 35 | psy | Learning & Learning Theories
A woman has good knowledge of English. This did not show any impact on learning car driving. The transfer of learning here is
(1) Positive transfer
(2) Negative transfer
(3) Zero transfer
(4) Bilateral transfer
= 3
~ Zero transfer occurs when earlier learning has no effect, neither helpful nor harmful, on new learning; knowing English neither helps nor hinders learning to drive. Positive transfer helps, negative transfer hinders, and bilateral transfer is transfer of skill from one side of the body to the other.

Q 36 | psy | Child-Centred Pedagogy & Assessment
Choose the correct statement/s
a. Cumulative percentage curve is ‘S’ in shape.
b. Values expanded on right side of normal probability curve is positive skewness
c. Mode is a measure of variability
(1) a
(2) a, b
(3) b
(4) c
= 2
~ The cumulative percentage curve (ogive) is S-shaped, and when scores spread out towards the right (a long tail on the right) the distribution is positively skewed, so a and b are correct. Mode is a measure of central tendency, not of variability (range, quartile deviation, standard deviation).

Q 37 | psy | Learning & Learning Theories
A student learnt 12 meaningless words in 10 attempts. After 10 days he was again asked to learn the same words and it took 6 attempts to relearn. His Saving score is
(1) 20%
(2) 30%
(3) 40%
(4) 50%
= 3
~ Saving score = (original trials − relearning trials) ÷ original trials × 100 = (10 − 6) ÷ 10 × 100 = 40%. This savings (relearning) method of measuring retention was devised by Ebbinghaus.

Q 43 | phs | Mechanics & Matter
Error in the measurement of radius of sphere is 2%. The error in calculated value of its volume is
(1) 8%
(2) 6%
(3) 2%
(4) 2/3 %
= 2
~ V = (4/3)πr³, so the percentage error in V is 3 times that in r: ΔV/V = 3 × Δr/r = 3 × 2% = 6%. 8% would come from wrongly cubing the error (2³).

Q 44 | phs | Mechanics & Matter

Q 46 | phs | Mechanics & Matter
A physical quantity has a value of −3 m/s. It may be

(2) Velocity of particle
(3) Position of a particle
(4) Acceleration of a particle
= 2

Q 47 | phs | Mechanics & Matter
The area under a graph between two physical quantities is given in the unit ‘m’ (metre). The physical quantities are
(1) Acceleration and time
(2) Distance and time
(3) Speed and time
(4) Velocity and distance
= 3
~ The unit of the area under a graph = (unit on y-axis) × (unit on x-axis). Speed × time = (m/s) × s = m, so the area under a speed–time graph gives distance. Acceleration × time gives m/s, distance × time gives m·s, velocity × distance gives m²/s.

Q 48 | phs | Mechanics & Matter
If |ā + b̄| = |ā − b̄|. The angle between ā and b̄ is
(1) 0°
(2) 30°
(3) 60°
(4) 90°
= 4
~ |ā + b̄|² = a² + b² + 2ab cos θ and |ā − b̄|² = a² + b² − 2ab cos θ. Equating them: 4ab cos θ = 0, so cos θ = 0 and θ = 90°. The two vectors are perpendicular.

Q 50 | phs | Mechanics & Matter
Carpet is beaten up with a stick, dust comes out of it. This is an example of Newton’s

(2) Second law
(3) Third law
(4) Law of cooling
= 1
~ When the carpet is beaten it suddenly moves, but the dust particles tend to remain at rest because of inertia of rest, so they separate and fall out. Inertia is the subject of Newton’s first law (law of inertia).

Q 51 | phs | Mechanics & Matter
A body sinks in a liquid if the buoyant force is
(1) Zero
(2) Equal to its weight
(3) Less than its weight
(4) Greater than its weight
= 3
~ A body sinks when its weight is greater than the upward buoyant force, i.e. buoyant force < weight. If the two are equal the body floats; a body immersed in a liquid always experiences some buoyant force, so it is never zero.

Q 52 | phs | Mechanics & Matter
The unit of pressure among the following is

(4) kg m s⁻²

Q 53 | phs | Mechanics & Matter
Gravitational force (F) is related to distance (d) between two masses as
(1) F ∝ 1/d
(2) F ∝ 1/d²
(3) d ∝ 1/F³
(4) d ∝ 1/F²
= 2
~ By Newton’s law of gravitation, F = Gm₁m₂/d², so the force is inversely proportional to the square of the distance: F ∝ 1/d². Doubling the distance makes the force one-fourth.

Q 54 | phs | Mechanics & Matter
The condition for mass to be equal to weight is
(1) g = 0
(2) g = 9.8 m/s²
(3) g = ∞
(4) g = 1 m/s²
= 4
~ Weight W = mg. The numerical value of W equals that of m only when g = 1 m/s² (W = m × 1). With g = 0 the weight becomes zero, and with g = 9.8 m/s² the weight is 9.8 times the mass.

Q 55 | phs | Mechanics & Matter
This among the following is not a conservative force
(1) Spring force
(2) Frictional force
(3) Electrostatic force
(4) Gravitational force
= 2
~ The work done by a conservative force depends only on the initial and final positions and is zero round a closed path (spring, gravitational and electrostatic forces). Work done against friction depends on the path and is lost as heat, so friction is a non-conservative force.

Q 56 | phs | Mechanics & Matter
A stone is tied to a string is whirled in a horizontal circle, the work done on it by the string is
(1) Positive
(2) Undefined
(3) Zero
(4) Negative
= 3
~ The tension in the string acts along the radius towards the centre, while the stone’s displacement at every instant is along the tangent. The angle between force and displacement is 90°, so W = Fs cos 90° = 0.

Q 57 | phs | Mechanics & Matter
The energy consumed in 5 hours by two devices of power 500W each is
(1) 1.8 × 10⁷ J
(2) 5000J
(3) 2500 J
(4) 9 × 10⁶ J
= 1
~ Total power = 2 × 500 W = 1000 W; time = 5 h = 5 × 3600 = 18000 s. Energy = P × t = 1000 × 18000 = 1.8 × 10⁷ J (= 5 kWh). 9 × 10⁶ J is the energy used by only one device.

Q 58 | phs | Mechanics & Matter
Object with mass (m) is moving with velocity (v). To double the kinetic energy the velocity must change to (keeping the mass constant)
(1) 2V
(2) V/2
(3) √2 V
(4) V/√2
= 3
~ KE = ½mv², so at constant mass KE ∝ v². For KE to become 2 times, v² must become 2 times, i.e. v must become √2 V. Making the velocity 2V would make the KE four times.

(2) Only momentum is conserved
