Q 1 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
According to the chapter, the biodiversity we see today is the result of about how many years of evolution?
(1) 2.5–3.5 million years
(2) 2.5–3.5 billion years
(3) 13.7 billion years
(4) 10,000 years
= 2
~ Present biodiversity is the outcome of 2.5–3.5 billion years of evolution.

Q 2 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Biodiversity is discussed in the chapter at three levels. Which of the following lists them correctly?
(1) Genetic diversity, species diversity and ecosystem diversity
(2) Plant diversity, animal diversity and microbial diversity
(3) Local, national and global diversity
(4) Land, water and air diversity
= 1
~ Biodiversity is understood at the genetic level (variation of genes within species), the species level and the ecosystem level.

Q 3 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Genetic diversity refers to:
(1) The number of different ecosystems in a region
(2) The variation of genes within a species
(3) The number of species in a hotspot
(4) The variety of habitats on the earth
= 2
~ Genes are the basic building blocks of life. Groups of individuals with similar physical characteristics form a species, and human beings genetically belong to Homo sapiens.

Q 4 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Diversity in the crops grown by farmers is referred to in the chapter as:
(1) Agro-biodiversity
(2) Endemism
(3) Ecosystem diversity
(4) Exotic diversity
= 1
~ The variety of crops is called agro-biodiversity; it is part of the economic role of biodiversity.

Q 6 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements:
1. Tropical regions occupy about one-fourth of the world's area but contain about three-fourths of the world's human population.
2. Tropical rain forests contain about 50 per cent of the species on the earth.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 3
~ Because so many people depend on tropical resources, biodiversity loss is especially severe in tropical regions.

Q 7 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which of the following roles of biodiversity is described as: "species capture and store energy, produce and decompose organic materials, help cycle water and nutrients, and fix atmospheric gases"?
(1) Economic role
(2) Cultural role
(3) Scientific role
(4) Ecological role
= 4
~ The ecological role is about how species keep ecosystems working. The economic role covers food crops, livestock, forestry, fish and medicines, and the scientific role helps us understand how life evolved.

Q 8 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Species that are not natural inhabitants of a local habitat but are introduced into it are called:
(1) Keystone species
(2) Endemic species
(3) Exotic species
(4) Rare species
= 3
~ Exotic species can disturb local ecosystems. The chapter gives Hawaii as an example where introduced species threaten unique plants and animals.

Q 9 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which one of the following is NOT listed in the chapter as a cause of loss of biodiversity?
(1) Natural calamities such as earthquakes, floods and forest fires
(2) Pesticides and pollutants such as hydrocarbons and toxic heavy metals
(3) Introduction of exotic species
(4) Establishment of biosphere reserves
= 4
~ Biosphere reserves are a conservation measure. Poaching of tigers, elephants, rhinoceros and other animals is another cause listed.

Q 15 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Biodiversity hotspots are identified by IUCN on the basis of:
(1) Their vegetation, because plants determine the primary productivity of an ecosystem
(2) The number of tourists they attract
(3) Their mineral wealth
(4) Their population density
= 1
~ Hotspots are areas rich in species diversity that are most vulnerable. Plants are used to define them because they determine primary productivity.

Q 16 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
According to the chapter, about what share of Madagascar's plants and animals are found nowhere else in the world?
(1) 15 per cent
(2) 40 per cent
(3) 85 per cent
(4) 100 per cent
= 3
~ Madagascar is a mega diversity country with very high endemism; about 85 per cent of its plants and animals are found nowhere else.

Q 17 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements:
1. The average half-life of a species is estimated at 1 to 4 million years.
2. Only about 10 per cent of the species that ever lived on the earth are extinct.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 1
~ About 99 per cent of all species that ever lived are extinct. Extinction is natural, but human activities have greatly increased its pace.

Q 18 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
How does biodiversity generally change from the tropics towards the poles?
(1) The number of species increases towards the poles
(2) Biodiversity is richer in the tropics; towards the poles there are fewer species but larger populations
(3) It remains the same everywhere
(4) There are no species near the poles
= 2
~ As we move towards the poles, there are fewer and fewer species but more individuals of each species.

Q 19 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which of the following steps for conservation are suggested in the chapter?
1. Efforts should be made to preserve species that are endangered.
2. Habitats where species feed, breed, rest and nurse their young should be safeguarded.
3. International trade in wild plants and animals should be regulated.
Select the correct answer:
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ The chapter also suggests preserving varieties of food crops, forage plants, timber trees and livestock, and protecting the habitats of their wild relatives.
