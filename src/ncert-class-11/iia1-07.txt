# NCERT Class 11 Fine Arts (Art & Culture), An Introduction to Indian Art Part I, Chapter 7: Indian Bronze Sculpture
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The 'lost-wax' process of casting metal images is also known as:
(1) Repoussé
(2) Champlevé
(3) Sand casting
(4) Cire-perdu
= 4
~ The cire-perdu or lost-wax process was learnt as long ago as the Indus Valley Culture and is still used, especially in Himachal Pradesh, Odisha, Bihar, Madhya Pradesh and West Bengal.

Q 2 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
According to the chapter, bronze is an alloy made by mixing:
(1) Copper, zinc and tin
(2) Copper and iron
(3) Gold and silver
(4) Tin and lead only
= 1
~ The chapter says the Indus people discovered how to make an alloy of copper, zinc and tin, called bronze, along with the lost-wax process.

Q 3 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
In the lost-wax process described in the chapter, the wax model is coated with a thick paste made of:
(1) Lime and sand
(2) Clay, sand and cow-dung in equal parts
(3) Gypsum and water
(4) Clay alone
= 2
~ After the wax wires are wound into the shape of the image, the model is covered with a thick paste of equal parts of clay, sand and cow-dung; a clay pot is then fixed at one opening to receive the molten metal.

Q 4 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
In the lost-wax process, how much metal is used compared with the weight of the wax?
(1) The same weight
(2) Five times the weight
(3) Ten times the weight
(4) Twenty times the weight
= 3
~ The wax is weighed first, and the metal used weighs ten times as much. The metal is mostly scrap from broken pots and pans.

Q 5 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Some images are cast in an alloy of five metals. According to the chapter, these are:
(1) Gold, silver, copper, tin and zinc
(2) Gold, silver, iron, lead and tin
(3) Gold, silver, copper, brass and lead
(4) Silver, copper, zinc, iron and tin
= 3
~ The chapter lists gold, silver, copper, brass and lead as the five metals used for some images.

Q 6 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The bronze 'Chariot', with simple circular wheels, an elongated driver and sturdy bulls, excavated at Daimabad in Maharashtra, is datable to about:
(1) 1500 BCE
(2) 2500 BCE
(3) 500 BCE
(4) 200 CE
= 1
~ The Daimabad bronzes are datable to 1500 BCE. The 'Dancing Girl' from Mohenjodaro, perhaps the earliest bronze sculpture, is datable to 2500 BCE.

Q 7 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Interesting bronze images of Jain tirthankaras of the Kushana period (second century CE) have been found at:
(1) Akota, Gujarat
(2) Kurkihar, Bihar
(3) Phophnar, Maharashtra
(4) Chausa, Bihar
= 4
~ The Chausa (Bihar) tirthankara bronzes belong to the Kushana period, second century CE; many are in the Patna Museum.

Q 8 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Among the Jain tirthankara images, Adinath (Vrishabhnath) is identified by:
(1) A seven-hooded snake over his head
(2) Long locks of hair falling on his shoulders
(3) A lion on his pedestal
(4) A conch in his hand
= 2
~ Adinath is shown with long hairlocks falling to his shoulders; the other tirthankaras have short curly hair.

Q 9 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The Akota hoard of Jain bronzes was found near which city?
(1) Ahmedabad
(2) Vadodara (Baroda)
(3) Surat
(4) Mount Abu
= 2
~ The Akota hoard near Baroda (Vadodara) dates from the end of the fifth to the end of the seventh century CE. Its bronzes were often inlaid with silver and copper for the eyes, crowns and textile details.

Q 10 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Ambika is the Yakshini or Shasanadevi of which tirthankara?
(1) Adinath
(2) Mahavira
(3) Parshvanath
(4) Neminath
= 4
~ Ambika is the Shasanadevi of Neminath, and Chakreshvari is the Shasanadevi of Adinath.

Q 11 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The Sultanganj Buddha, a monumental bronze and an outstanding example of the refined classical style, is from which present-day state?
(1) Bihar
(2) Uttar Pradesh
(3) Maharashtra
(4) West Bengal
= 1
~ Sultanganj is in Bihar. The Dhanesar Khera bronze from Uttar Pradesh follows the Mathura style of drapery, while Sarnath-style bronzes have foldless drapery.

Q 12 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The Vakataka bronze images from Phophnar in Maharashtra show the influence of which style?
(1) The Gandhara style
(2) The Mathura style
(3) The Amaravati style of Andhra Pradesh of the third century CE
(4) The Pala style
= 3
~ The Phophnar bronzes, contemporary with the Gupta bronzes, show the influence of the third-century CE Amaravati style; the right hand is free, so the drapery clings to the right side of the body.

Q 13 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
In the four-headed Vishnu (Chaturanana or Vaikuntha Vishnu) bronzes of Kashmir, the central face is of Vasudeva. Which faces are shown on either side?
(1) Narasimha and Varaha
(2) Vamana and Rama
(3) Balarama and Parashurama
(4) Matsya and Kurma
= 1
~ The central face is Vasudeva, flanked by Narasimha and Varaha. The bronzes of Kashmir and Himachal Pradesh are mostly of the eighth to tenth centuries.

Q 14 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
A school of bronze casting emerged at Nalanda around the ninth century under which dynasty?
(1) The Senas
(2) The Palas
(3) The Guptas
(4) The Pratiharas
= 2
~ Under the Palas of Bihar and Bengal a bronze school emerged at Nalanda; sculptors at nearby Kurkihar revived the classical Gupta style. Tara became popular with the growth of Vajrayana Buddhism.

Q 15 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
A Pallava bronze of Shiva seated in ardhaparyanka asana shows the achamana mudra. What does this gesture suggest?
(1) That he is blessing a devotee
(2) That he is about to begin a dance
(3) That he is teaching sages
(4) That he is about to drink poison
= 4
~ The achamana mudra suggests Shiva is about to drink the poison. Pallava bronzes of this kind date to the eighth and ninth centuries.

Q 16 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The finest Chola bronzes were made in Tamil Nadu during which period?
(1) Seventh to ninth century
(2) Eighth to tenth century
(3) Tenth to twelfth century
(4) Thirteenth to fifteenth century
= 3
~ The finest Chola bronzes date from the tenth to the twelfth century. The widowed Chola queen Sembiyan Mahadevi was a patron in the tenth century, and the technique is still practised at Kumbakonam.

Q 17 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
In the Chola Nataraja bronze, the demon crushed under Shiva's right foot is:
(1) Mahishasura
(2) Apasmara, the demon of ignorance or forgetfulness
(3) Andhakasura
(4) Tripurasura
= 2
~ Shiva balances on his right leg and crushes Apasmara, the demon of ignorance or forgetfulness, with the same foot.

Q 18 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Consider the following statements about the Chola Nataraja:
1. The upper right hand holds the damaru, which keeps the beat (tala).
2. The upper left hand carries a flame.
3. The main left hand is in abhaya hasta.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1
~ Statement 3 is wrong: the main right hand is in abhaya hasta, and the main left hand is in dola hasta, linked to it. The flying hair touches the ring of flames (jwala mala) around the figure.

Q 19 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The raised left leg of the Nataraja in bhujangatrasita stance represents:
(1) Srishti, creation
(2) Samhara, destruction
(3) Tirobhava, kicking away the veil of maya from the devotee's mind
(4) Anugraha, grace
= 3
~ The raised left leg stands for tirobhava, removing the veil of maya from the devotee's mind. The Nataraja form was evolved and fully developed in the Chola period.

Q 20 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The sixteenth-century life-size bronze portraits at Tirupati show Krishnadevaraya with which two queens?
(1) Mohanangi and Alamelu
(2) Rudramadevi and Ganapamba
(3) Ganga Devi and Varadambika
(4) Tirumalamba and Chinnadevi
= 4
~ The Vijayanagara portraits show Krishnadevaraya with Tirumalamba and Chinnadevi, standing with hands joined in namaskara mudra; the faces are true to life while the bodies are idealised.
