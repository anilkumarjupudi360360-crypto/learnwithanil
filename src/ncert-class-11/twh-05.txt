Q 1 | his | Themes in World History
The Swiss historian who wrote "The Civilisation of the Renaissance in Italy" (1860) and stressed the culture of humanism was:
(1) Marc Bloch
(2) Leopold von Ranke
(3) Jacob Burckhardt
(4) Erasmus
= 3
~ Burckhardt taught at the University of Basle. Unlike his teacher Ranke, who focused on politics and diplomacy, he looked at culture.

Q 2 | his | Themes in World History
The word "Renaissance" means:
(1) Revolution
(2) Reformation
(3) Discovery
(4) Rebirth
= 4
~ Nineteenth-century historians used it for the cultural changes of the fourteenth to seventeenth centuries, which they saw as a rebirth of Greek and Roman learning.

Q 3 | his | Themes in World History
The "humanities" (studia humanitatis) of the Italian scholars included which subjects?
(1) Medicine, law and theology only
(2) Grammar, rhetoric, poetry, history and moral philosophy
(3) Mathematics and astronomy only
(4) Farming and trade
= 2
~ These subjects were not drawn from religion. They stressed the skills and values of human beings.

Q 4 | his | Themes in World History
Who among the following is often called the "father of humanism", for his study of ancient Roman authors?
(1) Dante Alighieri
(2) Niccolo Machiavelli
(3) Francesco Petrarch
(4) Martin Luther
= 3
~ Petrarch (1304-78) urged people to read the ancient writers closely. Universities such as Padua and Bologna became centres of the new learning.

Q 5 | his | Themes in World History
Which family dominated Florence and patronised artists and scholars during the Renaissance?
(1) The Medici
(2) The Habsburgs
(3) The Tudors
(4) The Borgia
= 1
~ Florence was a city of wealthy merchants and bankers. Venice and Genoa were the other great Italian city-states of the time.

Q 6 | his | Themes in World History
Greek learning reached the Italian scholars partly through Arab translations and commentaries. Which of the following was an Arab philosopher whose work on Aristotle was widely read in Europe?
(1) Al-Biruni
(2) Ibn Battuta
(3) Ibn Rushd (Averroes)
(4) Abdur Razzaq
= 3
~ Ibn Sina (Avicenna), a physician, and Ibn Rushd, a philosopher, were well known in Europe. Arab scholars had preserved and translated many Greek works.

Q 7 | his | Themes in World History

(1) Michelangelo Buonarroti
(2) Leonardo da Vinci
(3) Raphael
(4) Giotto

~ Leonardo da Vinci (1452-1519) is the best example of the "universal man" of the Renaissance, interested in many fields.

Q 8 | his | Themes in World History
Michelangelo Buonarroti is known for:
(1) The dome of Florence cathedral
(2) The ceiling paintings of the Sistine Chapel, the sculpture "The Pieta" and the design of the dome of St Peter's Church in Rome
(3) Printing the first Bible
(4) Writing "The Prince"
= 2
~ Michelangelo (1475-1564) was a painter, sculptor and architect. The dome of Florence cathedral was designed by Filippo Brunelleschi.

Q 9 | his | Themes in World History
Which of the following features of Renaissance art made paintings look realistic?
1. Linear perspective, to show depth
2. Study of anatomy and the human body
3. Oil paint, which gave richer colours
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3

~ Artists studied light, geometry and the body. Andreas Vesalius, professor of medicine at Padua, did dissections that advanced anatomy.

Q 10 | his | Themes in World History
The first printing press with movable type in Europe was built by:
(1) William Caxton
(2) Galileo Galilei
(3) Erasmus
(4) Johannes Gutenberg
= 4
~ Gutenberg built his press in Germany in the 1450s. Printed books spread new ideas quickly, and many more people could now read.

Q 11 | his | Themes in World History
Niccolo Machiavelli wrote "The Prince" (1513), in which he argued that:
(1) Human beings are driven by self-interest, so a ruler should do what is needed to keep power
(2) Rulers should leave politics to the Church
(3) Human beings are naturally good
(4) Republics are always better than monarchies
= 1
~ Machiavelli was a Florentine. His frank view of politics was very different from earlier writing on good kingship.

Q 12 | his | Themes in World History

Q 13 | his | Themes in World History
The Dutch humanist who criticised the Church in "The Praise of Folly" was:
(1) Thomas More
(2) Martin Luther
(3) Jean Calvin
(4) Erasmus
= 4
~ Desiderius Erasmus (1466-1536) attacked superstition and the Church's wealth. The English humanist Thomas More wrote "Utopia" (1516).

Q 14 | his | Themes in World History
Martin Luther began the Protestant Reformation in 1517 by:
(1) Translating the Quran
(2) Founding the Jesuit order
(3) Becoming Pope
(4) Protesting against the Church's sale of "indulgences", which claimed to free people from sins
= 4
~ Luther, a German monk, said a person needed only faith to be saved. He translated the Bible into German.

Q 15 | his | Themes in World History
The Society of Jesus (Jesuits), founded in 1540, was part of:
(1) The Catholic Church's own reform, or Counter-Reformation
(2) The Protestant Reformation
(3) The scientific revolution
(4) The Renaissance art movement
= 1
~ Ignatius Loyola founded the Society of Jesus to fight Protestantism. The Jesuits served the poor and spread Christianity to other lands.

Q 16 | his | Themes in World History
Who among the following argued that the earth and the planets move around the sun?
(1) Ptolemy
(2) Nicolaus Copernicus
(3) Aristotle
(4) Thomas Aquinas
= 2
~ Copernicus (1473-1543), a Polish scientist, set out the idea in "De revolutionibus". Earlier, Christians believed the earth was the centre of the universe.

Q 17 | his | Themes in World History
Which of the following is correctly matched?
(1) Galileo Galilei - confirmed the idea of a dynamic universe through observation and wrote "The Motion"
(2) Johannes Kepler - wrote that the earth was the centre of the universe
(3) Isaac Newton - founded the Jesuit order
(4) Copernicus - invented the printing press
= 1
~ Kepler showed that planets move around the sun in elliptical orbits. Newton's theory of gravitation completed this new view of the universe. Together these changes are called the scientific revolution.

Q 18 | his | Themes in World History
The German painter whose detailed works such as "Praying Hands" are mentioned in the chapter was:
(1) Leonardo da Vinci
(2) Michelangelo
(3) Albrecht Durer
(4) Raphael
= 3
~ Albrecht Durer (1471-1528) shows how Renaissance ideas spread beyond Italy to other parts of Europe.

Q 19 | his | Themes in World History
Venice in the Renaissance period was:
(1) A republic, ruled by wealthy merchants, where the Church was less dominant
(2) Ruled by the Pope
(3) A part of the Ottoman Empire
(4) A small fishing village
= 1
~ Venice and Genoa were trading cities, unlike most of Europe where feudal lords and the Church were powerful.

Q 20 | his | Themes in World History
Which of the following best describes "humanism" as discussed in the chapter?
(1) Belief that only the Church could explain the world
(2) The study of ancient Indian texts
(3) A culture that stressed human abilities, individual achievement and learning about this world, drawing on Greek and Roman texts
(4) A plan to end all trade

~ Humanists did not reject religion, but they believed people could shape their lives through their own efforts.
