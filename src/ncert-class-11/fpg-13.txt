# NCERT Class 11 Geography, Fundamentals of Physical Geography, Chapter 13: Movements of Ocean Water
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). November 2026.

Q 1 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
In ocean waves, what actually moves across the ocean surface?
(1) The water itself, from one ocean to another
(2) Energy, while the water particles move only in small circles
(3) Salt particles
(4) Sediments from the sea floor
= 2
~ Waves are energy moving across the surface. Water particles only travel in small circles as a wave passes; the energy comes mostly from the wind.

Q 2 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
One-half of the wave height is called the wave's:
(1) Wavelength
(2) Amplitude
(3) Period
(4) Frequency
= 2
~ Wave height is the vertical distance from the bottom of a trough to the top of a crest; amplitude is half of it.

Q 3 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The horizontal distance between two successive crests of a wave is called the:
(1) Wave period
(2) Wave height
(3) Wavelength
(4) Wave speed
= 3
~ Wave period is the time between two successive crests passing a point, wave speed is measured in knots, and wave frequency is the number of waves passing a point in one second.

Q 4 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
According to the chapter, a wave breaks when:
(1) The depth of water is less than half the wavelength of the wave
(2) The wind stops completely
(3) The water becomes more saline
(4) It reaches the middle of the ocean
= 1
~ As a wave enters shallow water, friction with the bottom slows it until it breaks. The chapter gives the condition as depth less than half the wavelength.

Q 5 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The periodical rise and fall of the sea level, once or twice a day, caused mainly by the attraction of the sun and the moon, is called a:
(1) Surge
(2) Current
(3) Wave
(4) Tide
= 4
~ Tides are caused mainly by the gravitational pull of the moon and, to a lesser extent, the sun. Surges, caused by winds and pressure changes, are not regular like tides.

Q 6 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Where do the highest tides in the world occur, according to the chapter?
(1) Bay of Fundy, Nova Scotia, Canada
(2) Gulf of Khambhat, India
(3) English Channel
(4) Hooghly estuary
= 1
~ The tidal bulge in the Bay of Fundy is about 15–16 m, and the water rises at about 240 cm an hour.

Q 7 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Tides having two high tides and two low tides each day, with successive high (or low) tides of about the same height, are called:
(1) Diurnal tides
(2) Mixed tides
(3) Semi-diurnal tides
(4) Neap tides
= 3
~ The semi-diurnal tide is the most common type. Diurnal tides have one high and one low tide a day, and mixed tides have unequal heights.

Q 8 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Mixed tides, which have variations in height, generally occur along:
(1) The Mediterranean coast
(2) The east coast of India
(3) The west coast of North America and many islands of the Pacific Ocean
(4) The Antarctic coast
= 3
~ The chapter gives the west coast of North America and many Pacific islands as areas of mixed tides.

Q 9 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements:
1. Spring tides occur when the sun, the moon and the earth are in a straight line, at full moon and new moon.
2. Neap tides occur at full moon and new moon, when the tidal range is greatest.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 1
~ Spring tides occur at full and new moon. Neap tides come about seven days later, when the sun and moon are at right angles and the tidal range is smaller.

Q 10 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
When the moon is closest to the earth, unusually high and low tides occur. This position of the moon is called:
(1) Apogee
(2) Perihelion
(3) Aphelion
(4) Perigee
= 4
~ At perigee the moon is nearest and tidal ranges are greater than normal; at apogee it is farthest and ranges are smaller.

Q 11 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
When the earth is closest to the sun, around 3 January, tidal ranges are:
(1) Much less than average
(2) Zero
(3) Unusually great
(4) The same as during neap tides
= 3
~ At perihelion (about 3 January) the tidal ranges are unusually great; at aphelion (about 4 July) they are much less than average.

Q 13 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements about the importance of tides:
1. High tides help ships enter harbours near rivers and estuaries that have shallow bars at the entrance.
2. Tides help in desilting sediments and removing polluted water from river estuaries.
3. Tides can be used to generate electricity.

(4) 1, 2 and 3

Q 14 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which of the following are primary forces that influence the movement of ocean currents?
1. Heating by solar energy
2. Wind
3. Gravity
4. Coriolis force
Select the correct answer:
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1, 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2, 3 and 4
= 4
~ Heating makes water expand (equatorial water is about 8 cm higher than in the middle latitudes), wind pushes the surface water, gravity pulls it down the slope and the Coriolis force deflects it.

Q 15 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Large accumulations of water moving in circular currents in all the ocean basins, produced by the Coriolis force, are called:
(1) Gyres
(2) Eddies
(3) Rips
(4) Tsunamis
= 1
~ The Coriolis force deflects water to the right in the Northern Hemisphere and to the left in the Southern Hemisphere, creating large circular gyres.

Q 16 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements about types of ocean currents by depth:
1. Surface currents make up about 10 per cent of all the water in the ocean and occupy the upper 400 m.
2. Deep water currents are driven mainly by the wind blowing over the ocean surface.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 1
~ Deep water currents make up about 90 per cent of ocean water and move because of differences in density and gravity; deep water sinks at high latitudes where it is cold and dense.

Q 17 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Cold ocean currents are usually found:
(1) On the east coasts of continents in low and middle latitudes
(2) On the west coasts of continents in low and middle latitudes, and on the east coasts in higher latitudes of the Northern Hemisphere
(3) Only around the equator
(4) Only in the Indian Ocean
= 2
~ Warm currents, in contrast, flow along the east coasts in low and middle latitudes and along the west coasts in high latitudes of the Northern Hemisphere.

Q 18 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The speed of an ocean current, known as its "drift", is measured in:
(1) Metres per second only
(2) Knots
(3) Millibars
(4) Decibels
= 2
~ Currents are usually strongest near the surface, sometimes exceeding five knots; at depth they are generally slower than 0.5 knots.

Q 19 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
West coasts of continents in tropical and subtropical latitudes (except close to the equator) are bordered by cool waters. What climatic effect does the chapter describe for them?
(1) Very heavy rainfall throughout the year
(2) Relatively low average temperatures, narrow ranges, frequent fog, but generally arid conditions
(3) Extremely cold winters with heavy snow
(4) Frequent tropical cyclones
= 2
~ Cool currents chill the air above them, causing fog but little rain. Warm currents along east coasts in these latitudes give warm and rainy climates.

Q 20 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Why do the best fishing grounds of the world lie in zones where warm and cold currents mix?
(1) Because the water there is free of salt
(2) Because there are no waves in these zones
(3) Because the water there is always frozen
(4) Because mixing replenishes oxygen and favours the growth of plankton, the primary food of fish
= 4
~ Mixing of warm and cold water brings oxygen and nutrients, so plankton thrives and fish populations are large.
