Q 1 | his | Themes in World History
The "three orders" of medieval western European society were:
(1) Kings, merchants and slaves
(2) Clergy (those who prayed), nobility (those who fought) and peasantry (those who worked)
(3) Monks, nuns and friars
(4) Townspeople, guilds and bankers
= 2
~ This idea was put forward by Christian thinkers. A French bishop of the early eleventh century wrote that society was divided into those who pray, those who fight and those who work.

Q 2 | his | Themes in World History
The word "feudalism" comes from the German word "feud", which means:
(1) A piece of land
(2) A quarrel between families
(3) A castle
(4) A tax
= 1
~ Feudalism describes the economic, legal, political and social relations in Europe in the medieval period, centred on land held in return for service.

Q 3 | his | Themes in World History
The French historian who wrote "Feudal Society" and studied the lives of ordinary people in medieval France was:
(1) Jacob Burckhardt
(2) Leopold von Ranke
(3) Fernand Braudel
(4) Marc Bloch
= 4
~ Marc Bloch looked at geography and social relations to understand feudalism. He was killed by the Nazis in 1944.

Q 4 | his | Themes in World History
The country now called France takes its name from:
(1) The Franks, a Germanic people who made Gaul their kingdom
(2) A Roman general
(3) A river in Gaul
(4) A Christian saint
= 1
~ Gaul was a province of the Roman Empire. The Franks, a Germanic tribe, gave their name to France, and their kings had close ties with the Church.

Q 5 | his | Themes in World History
In 800 CE, the Pope crowned which Frankish king as Holy Roman Emperor?
(1) Charlemagne
(2) Clovis
(3) William the Conqueror
(4) Louis XI
= 1
~ The crowning sealed the alliance between the Frankish kings and the Church in Rome.

Q 6 | his | Themes in World History
In 1066, England was conquered by:
(1) The Vikings of Norway
(2) Charlemagne
(3) The Romans
(4) Duke William of Normandy
= 4
~ William divided English land among his followers, who became the landed nobility of England.

Q 7 | his | Themes in World History
In the feudal system, a "fief" was:
(1) A tax paid to the Church
(2) Land given by a lord to a vassal, often a knight, in return for military service
(3) A guild of artisans
(4) A type of plough
= 2
~ The knight gave the lord a regular fee and promised to fight for him. The lord in turn protected the vassal.

Q 8 | his | Themes in World History
"Tithe" was:
(1) A royal tax on trade
(2) A tax of one-tenth of the produce, collected by the Church from peasants
(3) A payment to the lord for using the mill
(4) A fine for leaving the manor
= 2
~ The Church was a major landowner as well. Bishops and abbots held estates like great lords.

Q 9 | his | Themes in World History
Which of the following was true of serfs in medieval Europe?
(1) They were free to move to any town
(2) They paid no rent or labour
(3) They owned large estates
(4) They cultivated plots of land belonging to the lord, gave him most of the produce, worked on his land without pay, and could not leave the estate without permission
= 4
~ Serfs also had to use the lord's mill, oven and wine-press, paying a fee. Their lives were under the lord's control.

Q 10 | his | Themes in World History
The "taille" was:
(1) A Church tax
(2) A tax on bread
(3) A direct tax that kings sometimes imposed on peasants; the clergy and nobles were exempt
(4) A land grant to monasteries
= 3
~ The burden of the taille fell on the peasantry. This privilege of the first two orders lasted until the French Revolution.

Q 11 | his | Themes in World History
Which of the following monasteries is correctly matched with what it is known for?
(1) Monte Cassino - founded by St Francis of Assisi
(2) Cluny - founded in 910 in Burgundy; it led a reform of monastic life
(3) Bingen - founded by Charlemagne
(4) Cluny - founded by St Benedict in Italy in 529
= 2
~ St Benedict founded his monastery in Italy in 529. The abbey of Cluny, founded in 910, became a leading centre of reform. Hildegard of Bingen was a gifted abbess.

Q 12 | his | Themes in World History
The friars of the thirteenth century, such as the Franciscans and Dominicans, differed from monks in that they:
(1) Lived in castles
(2) Fought wars
(3) Moved from place to place, preaching to the people and living on charity
(4) Owned huge estates
= 3
~ The friars chose not to live in monasteries. St Francis of Assisi and St Dominic founded the two orders.

Q 13 | his | Themes in World History
Which of the following technological changes helped European agriculture grow from the eleventh century?
1. Heavy iron-tipped ploughs and mouldboards
2. Horseshoes and a better harness, which allowed horses to pull ploughs
3. The three-field system of rotating crops
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ Under the three-field system, land was divided into three fields: one for a winter crop, one for a spring crop and one left fallow, in rotation. Food output and population rose.

Q 14 | his | Themes in World History
Between about 1000 and 1300, the population of Europe:
(1) Rose from about 42 million to about 73 million
(2) Fell sharply because of wars
(3) Stayed the same
(4) Doubled every 50 years
= 1
~ Better food supply and fewer famines helped. Towns also began to grow in this period.

Q 15 | his | Themes in World History
"Town air makes free" referred to the idea that:
(1) A serf who stayed in a town for a year and a day without being found by the lord became free
(2) Townspeople paid no taxes
(3) Towns had cleaner air
(4) Kings lived in towns
= 1
~ Many serfs ran away to towns. Towns were organised around guilds of craftspeople and merchants.

Q 16 | his | Themes in World History
The great Gothic cathedrals of twelfth-century France, such as Notre Dame in Paris and Chartres, are known for:
(1) Domes in the Roman style
(2) Being built of wood
(3) Having no windows
(4) Tall spires, pointed arches and large stained-glass windows
= 4
~ Cathedrals took generations to build and drew in craftspeople and pilgrims. Their stained glass told Bible stories to people who could not read.

Q 17 | his | Themes in World History
The crisis of the fourteenth century in Europe included which of the following?
1. A great famine in the early fourteenth century
2. The Black Death (1347-50), which killed a large part of the population
3. A shortage of silver that affected trade
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 1, 2 and 3
(4) 3 only
= 3
~ The plague, carried by rats on ships, spread across Europe. With fewer workers, wages rose and lords tried to bring back old controls.

Q 18 | his | Themes in World History
Which of the following was a peasant revolt of the fourteenth century in Europe?
(1) The Taiping Rebellion
(2) The Boxer Rebellion
(3) The Jacquerie in France (1358)
(4) The Santhal Hul
= 3
~ There were also revolts in Flanders (1323) and England (1381). Peasants resisted the attempts of lords to impose old services again.

Q 19 | his | Themes in World History
The "new monarchs" of the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries, such as Louis XI of France and Henry VII of England, strengthened royal power by:
(1) Giving more power to the nobles
(2) Closing all towns
(3) Organising standing armies, a permanent bureaucracy and national taxation
(4) Abolishing armies
= 3
~ The new monarchs reduced the power of feudal lords. Kings now ruled more directly.

Q 20 | his | Themes in World History
In France, the assembly representing the three orders, the Estates-General, did not meet between 1614 and:
(1) 1689
(2) 1789
(3) 1815
(4) 1848
= 2
~ It was called again in 1789. The meeting led to the French Revolution.
