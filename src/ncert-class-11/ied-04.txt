# NCERT Class 11 Economics, Indian Economic Development, Chapter 4: Human Capital Formation in India
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | eco | Indian Economic Development
In the chapter, 'human capital' refers to:
(1) The stock of skill and expertise of a nation at a point of time
(2) The total population of a country
(3) The money invested by people in shares
(4) The number of schools and hospitals in a country
= 1
~ Human capital is the stock of skill and expertise of a nation at a point of time. Human capital formation is the process of adding to this stock over time.

Q 2 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which of the following is NOT a source of human capital formation discussed in the chapter?
(1) On-the-job training
(2) Migration
(3) Purchase of machinery by firms
(4) Expenditure on acquiring information
= 3
~ The sources are investment in education, health, on-the-job training, migration and information. Buying machinery adds to physical capital, not human capital.

Q 3 | eco | Indian Economic Development
In health expenditure, 'social medicine' refers to:
(1) Vaccination against diseases
(2) The spread of health literacy
(3) Medical treatment during illness
(4) Building new hospitals
= 2
~ The chapter divides health expenditure into preventive medicine (vaccination), curative medicine (medical intervention during illness), social medicine (spread of health literacy), and the provision of clean drinking water and good sanitation.

Q 4 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Firms often insist that workers who get on-the-job training at the firm's cost work for the firm for a specific period afterwards. Why?
(1) Because the law requires it
(2) Because trained workers cannot find jobs elsewhere
(3) Because training lowers wages
(4) Because the firm wants to recover the benefits of the higher productivity it has paid for
= 4
~ On-the-job training is an expense for the firm. By binding the worker for a period, the firm makes sure it gains from the extra productivity the training creates.

Q 5 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Why is expenditure on migration treated as a source of human capital formation?
(1) Because migrants always get government jobs
(2) Because migration reduces the population of villages
(3) Because migration has no costs
(4) Because the higher earnings in the new place outweigh the costs of migration
= 4
~ Migration involves costs of transport, a higher cost of living and the psychic cost of living in a strange setting. People migrate because the enhanced earnings exceed these costs, so the expenditure is an investment in human capital.

Q 6 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which statement correctly distinguishes physical capital from human capital?
(1) Both can be sold in the market in the same way
(2) Physical capital is separable from its owner, whereas human capital is inseparable from its owner
(3) Human capital is tangible, physical capital is intangible
(4) Only physical capital benefits society
= 2
~ A machine can be separated from its owner and sold. Human capital is built into a person's body and mind; only its services can be sold.

Q 7 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Consider the following statements:
1. Physical capital is tangible and can be easily sold in the market.
2. Human capital is intangible, and only its services are sold in the market.
3. Unlike physical capital, human capital does not depreciate at all.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1
~ Statement 3 is wrong. Both forms of capital depreciate: machines through use and human capital through ageing. The depreciation of human capital can be reduced by continuous investment in education and health.

Q 8 | eco | Indian Economic Development
What does the chapter say about the mobility of physical and human capital between countries?
(1) Both are completely immobile
(2) Human capital is fully mobile while physical capital cannot cross borders
(3) Physical capital is completely mobile except for artificial trade restrictions, while human capital is not perfectly mobile, being restricted by nationality and culture
(4) Both move freely without any restriction
= 3
~ Machines can be shipped across borders unless trade rules prevent it. People face restrictions of nationality and culture, so human capital is not perfectly mobile between countries.

Q 9 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which statement about the benefits of human capital is correct according to the chapter?
(1) Human capital benefits only the person who owns it
(2) Human capital has no effect on economic growth
(3) Human capital benefits not only its owner but also society, so it creates external benefits
(4) Human capital benefits only the government
= 3
~ An educated person can contribute to democratic participation and spread knowledge; a healthy person helps stop the spread of disease. These spillover effects mean human capital creates benefits for society as well.

Q 10 | eco | Indian Economic Development
How does the chapter distinguish human capital from human development?
(1) Human capital treats education and health as means to raise labour productivity, while human development treats them as integral to human well-being
(2) Both concepts mean exactly the same thing
(3) Human development is only about income, while human capital is only about health
(4) Human capital is concerned with physical capital, human development with natural resources
= 1
~ In the human capital view, education and health matter because they raise productivity. In the human development view, they matter in themselves, because being educated and healthy is part of a good life.

Q 11 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which of the following bodies is concerned with the health sector, unlike the other three?
(1) National Council of Educational Research and Training (NCERT)
(2) Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR)
(3) University Grants Commission (UGC)
(4) All India Council for Technical Education (AICTE)
= 2
~ NCERT, UGC and AICTE regulate the education sector. ICMR is an institution in the health sector.

Q 12 | eco | Indian Economic Development
The Education Commission (1964–66) recommended that what share of GDP be spent on education?
(1) 2 per cent
(2) 3 per cent
(3) 4 per cent
(4) 6 per cent
= 4
~ The Education Commission recommended spending at least 6 per cent of GDP on education to achieve a noticeable rate of growth in educational achievement.

Q 13 | eco | Indian Economic Development
The Tapas Majumdar Committee, appointed in 1998, estimated:
(1) The expenditure needed to bring all children aged 6–14 years under school education
(2) The number of doctors needed in rural areas
(3) The cost of building new universities
(4) The loss caused by brain drain
= 1
~ The committee estimated the spending needed over about ten years to bring every child aged 6–14 years into school education.

Q 14 | eco | Indian Economic Development
The Right to Education Act, 2009:
(1) Makes higher education free for all
(2) Gives every adult the right to a job
(3) Applies only to government schools in cities
(4) Makes free and compulsory education a right for all children aged 6–14 years
= 4
~ The Act, passed in 2009, made free and compulsory education a right of every child in the age group of 6–14 years.

Q 15 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Why does the chapter argue that the government must intervene in education and health?
(1) Because private schools and hospitals are illegal
(2) Because these services create both private and social benefits, and individuals may lack full information about their quality and cost, so markets alone cannot provide them well
(3) Because education and health have no market value
(4) Because only the government can build buildings
= 2
~ Education and health have long-term effects on society, and consumers often lack information about the nature and cost of services. Left entirely to private providers they could be costly and unequally available, so government intervention is needed.

Q 16 | eco | Indian Economic Development
The 'education cess' levied by the Union government, as described in the chapter, is:
(1) A tax on private schools
(2) A fee charged for higher education
(3) A levy on Union taxes whose revenue is earmarked for spending on elementary education
(4) A tax on foreign students
= 3
~ The chapter mentions the education cess on all Union taxes as a way of raising money for education, with its revenue earmarked for elementary education.

Q 17 | eco | Indian Economic Development
What does the chapter say about the link between human capital and economic growth in India?
(1) Both have grown, but it is difficult to establish that growth of human capital caused economic growth
(2) Human capital has fallen while the economy has grown
(3) Economic growth has no relation at all to education and health
(4) It is proven that human capital alone caused all of India's growth
= 1
~ Education and health indicators and GDP have both risen over time. But empirical evidence that more human capital causes growth is unclear, partly because of measurement problems.

Q 18 | eco | Indian Economic Development
According to the chapter, why do individuals invest in education?
(1) Only to get social status
(2) Because it is compulsory for adults
(3) To increase their future income
(4) Because education has no cost
= 3
~ Like firms that invest in capital goods to earn future profits, individuals invest in education to raise their future earnings.

Q 19 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Vaccination against diseases is an example of:
(1) Curative medicine
(2) Social medicine
(3) On-the-job training
(4) Preventive medicine
= 4
~ Vaccination prevents disease before it happens, so it is preventive medicine. Treatment during illness is curative medicine and spreading health literacy is social medicine.

Q 20 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Under 'future prospects', how does the chapter describe the goal of 'education for all' in India?
(1) Fully achieved since 2009
(2) Still a distant dream, though literacy has improved
(3) Achieved only in urban areas
(4) No longer a goal of the government
= 2
~ Literacy rates for adults and youth have improved, but universal education has not yet been achieved. The chapter calls education for all still a distant dream.
