# NCERT Class 11 Political Science, Political Theory, Chapter 3: Equality
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | pol | Political Theory
The slogan "liberty, equality and fraternity" is associated with which historic event?
(1) The American Civil War
(2) The French Revolution
(3) The Russian Revolution
(4) The Industrial Revolution
= 2
~ French revolutionaries raised this slogan against the privileges of the aristocracy and monarchy in 1789. The chapter uses it to show how equality became a powerful political ideal.

Q 2 | pol | Political Theory
Which statement best expresses the idea of equality discussed in the chapter?
(1) Everyone must be treated identically in every situation
(2) Everyone must earn exactly the same income
(3) There should be no differences of any kind between people
(4) All human beings deserve equal consideration and respect because of their common humanity, and no one should be denied rights or opportunities because of birth or social position
= 4
~ Equality does not mean uniformity. It means equal respect and equal rights, and removing differences that come from unjust social arrangements rather than from people's own choices or efforts.

Q 3 | pol | Political Theory
Inequalities that arise because people have different abilities and talents are called natural inequalities. Inequalities created by society, such as those based on caste or gender, are called:
(1) Socially produced inequalities
(2) Natural inequalities
(3) Biological inequalities
(4) Accidental inequalities
= 1
~ Socially produced inequalities come from unequal opportunities or from exploitation and discrimination. The chapter notes that many inequalities once called "natural" were in fact produced by society.

Q 4 | pol | Political Theory
Equality of opportunity means that:
(1) Everyone must end up with the same results
(2) Only the talented should get education
(3) All people should have the same chances to develop their talents and pursue their goals, and differences in outcome should not come from unequal chances
(4) Only men should compete for jobs
= 3
~ The concept focuses on fair starting points: access to education, health care and jobs. People may then reach different results, but not because some were denied the chance.

Q 5 | pol | Political Theory
Granting equal citizenship, with equal rights to vote, express views and form associations, is an example of:
(1) Economic equality
(2) Social equality
(3) Political equality
(4) Natural equality
= 3
~ Political equality means equal civil and political rights for all citizens. The chapter notes it is necessary but not enough, since social and economic inequality may remain.

Q 6 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following is an aspect of social equality, as discussed in the chapter?
(1) Ensuring that all have adequate access to basic goods like health care, education and food, and removing discrimination based on caste or gender
(2) Every citizen having one vote
(3) All industries being owned by the state
(4) Fixing equal salaries for all jobs
= 1
~ Social equality means equal opportunities for all groups, which needs basic goods for everyone and an end to discrimination in social life, such as unequal treatment of women.

Q 7 | pol | Political Theory
Economic equality, according to the chapter, is mainly about:
(1) Abolishing all private property immediately
(2) Everyone having exactly the same wealth
(3) Equal voting rights
(4) Reducing the gap between the rich and the poor, and ensuring that no one lacks basic needs
= 4
~ Complete economic equality is rarely sought. Democracies try to reduce extreme inequality through taxation, welfare and equal opportunities, so that wealth gaps do not become permanent.

Q 8 | pol | Political Theory
Which ideology sees private ownership of important economic resources, such as land and industry, as the main cause of inequality?
(1) Liberalism
(2) Marxism
(3) Feminism
(4) Anarchism
= 2
~ Marx argued that those who own the means of production also gain social and political power. Marxists therefore favour public ownership of key resources.

Q 9 | pol | Political Theory
Which ideology, according to the chapter, accepts competition as the fairest and most efficient way of distributing resources, provided there is equality of opportunity?
(1) Marxism
(2) Socialism
(3) Feminism
(4) Liberalism
= 4
~ Liberals accept inequality of outcome if it results from fair competition. They also want political, social and economic inequalities kept separate so that wealth does not buy political power.

Q 10 | pol | Political Theory
Feminism, as described in the chapter, stands for:
(1) The superiority of women over men
(2) Equal rights for men and women, and the end of patriarchy
(3) Separate electorates for women
(4) Abolishing the family
= 2
~ Feminists challenge patriarchy, a social system that values men more than women and gives them power over women. They distinguish between sex (biological) and gender (social roles).

Q 11 | pol | Political Theory
Feminists distinguish between "sex" and "gender". What does "gender" refer to?
(1) Socially defined roles and expectations for men and women
(2) Biological differences between men and women
(3) The number of women in Parliament
(4) Inherited wealth
= 1
~ Sex refers to biological differences. Gender refers to roles that society assigns, such as assuming that only women should do housework, which feminists argue can and should change.

Q 12 | pol | Political Theory
Ram Manohar Lohia identified five kinds of inequality that must be fought together. Which of the following was NOT one of them?
(1) Inequality between men and women
(2) Inequality based on caste
(3) Inequality between people with different blood groups
(4) Inequality based on skin colour
= 3
~ Lohia named inequality between men and women, based on skin colour, caste, the colonial rule of some countries over others, and economic inequality. Blood groups were not part of his list.

Q 13 | pol | Political Theory
Lohia added two more revolutions to the fight against five inequalities, calling the whole struggle "Sapta Kranti" (seven revolutions). The two were:
(1) Civil liberties against interference in private life, and non-violence
(2) Industrialisation and urbanisation
(3) Free trade and globalisation
(4) Monarchy and religion
= 1
~ The two added revolutions were for civil liberties, against unjust intrusion into private life, and for non-violence as the means of struggle. Lohia argued that all seven revolutions should go on together.

Q 14 | pol | Political Theory
Removing laws that discriminate on the basis of caste, religion or gender, so that everyone is equal before the law, is called:
(1) Affirmative action
(2) Differential treatment
(3) Reservations
(4) Establishing formal equality
= 4
~ Formal equality removes legal barriers and privileges. The Indian Constitution, for example, prohibits discrimination and abolishes untouchability, but formal equality alone does not end social inequality.

Q 15 | pol | Political Theory
Providing ramps for people with disabilities so that they can enter public buildings is an example of:
(1) Formal equality only
(2) Equality through differential treatment
(3) Natural inequality
(4) Economic equality
= 2
~ Treating people differently can be needed to make them truly equal. Ramps, or special arrangements for women's safety at night, help people with different needs enjoy equal access.

Q 16 | pol | Political Theory
Policies such as reservations in education and jobs for disadvantaged groups are known as:
(1) Laissez-faire
(2) Formal equality
(3) Affirmative action
(4) Natural selection
= 3
~ Affirmative action aims to correct the effects of past discrimination by giving special opportunities to disadvantaged groups. The chapter notes the debate about whether such policies should be permanent.

Q 17 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following is an argument against reservations that the chapter discusses?
(1) Reservations make the Constitution unwritten
(2) Reservations abolish elections
(3) Reservations are given only to the rich
(4) Reservations, critics say, may be unfair to others and should be temporary, and some argue they should be based on economic need
= 4
~ The chapter presents arguments on both sides: supporters see reservations as needed to correct deep social inequality, while critics raise concerns about fairness and argue for limits. Students are asked to weigh both.

Q 18 | pol | Political Theory
Why does the chapter say that political equality alone is not enough?
(1) Because people with equal votes may still be very unequal in wealth, education and social status, which affects their real power
(2) Because elections are not held regularly
(3) Because political equality is not part of the Constitution
(4) Because voting is optional
= 1
~ Formal political rights mean little if some people lack education, resources or freedom from social domination. That is why social and economic equality are also needed.

Q 19 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following statements reflects the socialist view of equality as described in the chapter?
(1) The market alone will remove all inequality
(2) Inequality should be reduced through government action, such as public provision of education and health care and control of key resources
(3) Inequality is natural and should not be touched
(4) Only political equality matters
= 2
~ Socialists believe the market produces unfair inequality and that the state must act to reduce it. In India, socialists also linked economic inequality with caste and other social hierarchies.

Q 20 | pol | Political Theory
A society where some people cannot enter certain public places because of their birth violates which kind of equality most directly?
(1) Economic equality
(2) Natural equality
(3) Social equality
(4) International equality
= 3
~ Denying access to public places because of birth, as under untouchability, is a denial of social equality and equal dignity. The Indian Constitution forbids it.
