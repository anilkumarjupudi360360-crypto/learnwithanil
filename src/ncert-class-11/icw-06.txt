Q 1 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
"Independence of the judiciary" means that:
(1) Judges can act in any way they like, without being accountable to anyone
(2) The judiciary can make laws on any subject
(3) Other organs of government must not interfere in the judiciary's work, so that judges can decide without fear or favour
(4) Judges are elected by the people
= 3
~ Independence does not mean arbitrariness or lack of accountability. It means the legislature and executive cannot obstruct the judiciary, so judges can do justice impartially.

Q 2 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which of the following provisions helps keep the Indian judiciary independent?
1. Judges have security of tenure and can be removed only by a difficult procedure
2. The salaries and allowances of judges are not subject to a vote of the legislature
3. The conduct of judges cannot be discussed in the legislature except during a removal motion
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ All three protect independence. Judges' salaries are charged on the Consolidated Fund, their removal is very difficult, and the judiciary can punish contempt of court.

Q 3 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
In the appointment of judges of the Supreme Court, the practice that has emerged from court judgments is that recommendations are made by:
(1) The Prime Minister alone
(2) The Law Minister and the Attorney General
(3) A collegium of the Chief Justice of India and the four senior-most judges of the Supreme Court
(4) The Union Public Service Commission
= 3
~ Since the 1990s, the Supreme Court's judgments have given the senior judges the main say. A group of the Chief Justice and four senior-most judges, the collegium, recommends names; the President makes the appointment.

Q 4 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
By convention, the Chief Justice of India is usually:
(1) The senior-most judge of the Supreme Court
(2) The youngest judge of the Supreme Court
(3) A judge chosen by the Lok Sabha
(4) The Attorney General of India
= 1
~ The convention is to appoint the senior-most judge as Chief Justice. It was broken twice in the 1970s, when senior judges were superseded, and both decisions were controversial.

Q 5 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
On what grounds can a judge of the Supreme Court or a High Court be removed?
(1) Political disagreement with the government
(2) A no-confidence motion in the Lok Sabha
(3) A decision of the President alone
(4) Proven misbehaviour or incapacity
= 4
~ A judge can be removed only for proven misbehaviour or incapacity, through a motion passed by a special majority in both Houses of Parliament. No judge has been removed so far.

Q 6 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The chapter mentions a 1991 removal motion against a Supreme Court judge that failed in Parliament. Who was the judge?
(1) Justice P.N. Bhagwati
(2) Justice V.R. Krishna Iyer
(3) Justice V. Ramaswami
(4) Justice H.R. Khanna
= 3
~ The motion against Justice V. Ramaswami got two-thirds of the members present but not the support of a majority of the total strength of the House, because the ruling party did not vote. It shows how hard removal is.

Q 7 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Disputes between the Union and one or more states, or between states, come directly to the Supreme Court under its:
(1) Original jurisdiction
(2) Appellate jurisdiction
(3) Advisory jurisdiction
(4) Writ jurisdiction
= 1
~ In its original jurisdiction the Supreme Court hears certain cases first, without them going to a lower court. Federal disputes between the Centre and the states are the main example.

Q 8 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
A person whose Fundamental Right has been violated can go directly to the Supreme Court. This is the Court's:
(1) Advisory jurisdiction
(2) Appellate jurisdiction
(3) Special leave jurisdiction
(4) Writ jurisdiction
= 4
~ Under Article 32 the Supreme Court can issue writs such as habeas corpus and mandamus to enforce Fundamental Rights. High Courts can also issue writs, under Article 226.

Q 9 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Under its advisory jurisdiction, the Supreme Court:
(1) Can be asked by the President for its opinion on a question of public importance or law, but its advice is not binding on the President
(2) Must give advice whenever the Prime Minister asks for it
(3) Advises state governments on all their laws
(4) Hears appeals from High Courts
= 1
~ Under Article 143 the President can refer a matter to the Supreme Court for advice. The Court may give its opinion, and the President is not bound to follow it; it helps the government seek legal clarity before acting.

Q 10 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which of the following statements about the High Courts is correct?
(1) Their decisions cannot be appealed against in any court
(2) They are appointed by the state legislatures
(3) They can hear disputes between two states
(4) They can hear appeals from lower courts and issue writs to enforce rights
= 4
~ High Courts hear appeals from subordinate courts, issue writs and supervise the courts below them. Their decisions can be appealed in the Supreme Court; disputes between states go to the Supreme Court.

Q 11 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
"Public Interest Litigation" (PIL) means that:
(1) A person or group can go to court on behalf of those whose rights are affected, for an issue of public interest
(2) Only the person whose rights are violated can go to court
(3) Cases are decided by a public vote
(4) Only the government can file cases
= 1
~ From the late 1970s the Supreme Court allowed any public-spirited person to approach it on behalf of the poor or the public. Even letters and newspaper reports were treated as petitions.

Q 12 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Judicial activism through PIL in India began around:
(1) 1950–1955
(2) 1965–1970
(3) 1979–1980
(4) 2000–2005
= 3
~ The Hussainara Khatoon case (1979), about undertrial prisoners, is seen as the beginning. Judges such as P.N. Bhagwati and V.R. Krishna Iyer took the lead in letting the poor reach the court.

Q 13 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which of the following is a criticism of judicial activism, as discussed in the chapter?
(1) It has reduced the number of cases in the courts
(2) It has abolished Fundamental Rights
(3) It has stopped the courts from hearing cases of the poor
(4) It has blurred the line between the executive and the judiciary, and has added to the burden on the courts
= 4
~ Activism made courts more accessible and democratic, but critics say courts have sometimes entered areas belonging to the executive and legislature, and the extra cases add to their burden.

Q 14 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The power of the judiciary to examine whether a law is consistent with the Constitution, and to declare it invalid if it is not, is called:
(1) Judicial review
(2) Judicial activism
(3) Advisory jurisdiction
(4) Original jurisdiction
= 1
~ Judicial review lets the Supreme Court and High Courts strike down laws that violate Fundamental Rights or the division of powers between the Centre and the states. The term is not in the Constitution, but the power is.

Q 15 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
In which case did the Supreme Court hold that Parliament cannot amend the "basic structure" of the Constitution?
(1) Golaknath case, 1967
(2) Kesavananda Bharati case, 1973
(3) Hussainara Khatoon case, 1979
(4) S.R. Bommai case, 1994
= 2
~ In Kesavananda Bharati (1973) the Court ruled that Parliament can amend any part of the Constitution, but not its basic structure. In Golaknath (1967) it had held that Fundamental Rights could not be amended at all.

Q 16 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The conflict between the judiciary and Parliament in the 1960s and 1970s was mainly about:
(1) The salaries of judges
(2) Parliament's power to restrict the right to property and amend Fundamental Rights, to carry out land reforms and other policies
(3) The location of the Supreme Court
(4) The language of court proceedings
= 2
~ Laws on land reforms and nationalisation clashed with the right to property. Parliament passed amendments, the courts struck some down, and the dispute was settled by the basic structure doctrine and later the removal of property from the Fundamental Rights.

Q 17 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which of the following is the correct order of the court system in India, from the highest to the lowest?
(1) High Court, Supreme Court, District Court, Subordinate Courts
(2) Supreme Court, District Court, High Court, Subordinate Courts
(3) Supreme Court, High Court, District Court, Subordinate Courts
(4) District Court, High Court, Supreme Court, Subordinate Courts
= 3
~ India has an integrated judiciary: the Supreme Court at the top, then the High Courts, then district courts, and subordinate courts below them. The decisions of a higher court bind the courts below.

Q 18 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The Supreme Court can hear an appeal against the judgment of any court or tribunal in India, at its discretion, through:
(1) A writ of habeas corpus
(2) Special leave to appeal
(3) Advisory jurisdiction
(4) A joint sitting of Parliament
= 2
~ Under Article 136 the Supreme Court may grant special leave to appeal from any judgment of any court or tribunal, except military tribunals. It is used when a serious question of law or injustice is involved.

Q 19 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Why is the Indian judiciary called "integrated"?
(1) Because judges are elected together with legislators
(2) Because it is part of the executive
(3) Because every court can hear every kind of case
(4) Because there is a single hierarchy of courts with the Supreme Court at the top, unlike some federations that have separate federal and state courts
= 4
~ In the USA there are separate federal and state courts. India has one unified system: the Supreme Court's decisions bind all courts in the country.

Q 20 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The decisions of the Supreme Court are binding on all other courts in India. Which article ensures this?
(1) Article 32
(2) Article 136
(3) Article 141
(4) Article 368
= 3
~ Article 141 says that the law declared by the Supreme Court is binding on all courts within the territory of India. Article 32 is about writs, 136 about special leave and 368 about amendments.
