# NCERT Class 11 Economics, Indian Economic Development, Chapter 8: Comparative Development Experiences of India and its Neighbours
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | eco | Indian Economic Development
In which year did China announce its first five-year plan?
(1) 1953
(2) 1951
(3) 1956
(4) 1958
= 1
~ India announced its first five-year plan for 1951–56, China in 1953 and Pakistan in 1956. All three started on similar development paths.

Q 2 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Pakistan announced its first five-year plan, called the Medium Term Plan, in:
(1) 1949
(2) 1951
(3) 1956
(4) 1961
= 3
~ Pakistan's first five-year plan, the Medium Term Plan, was announced in 1956, a few years after India (1951) and China (1953).

Q 3 | eco | Indian Economic Development
The People's Republic of China was established in:
(1) 1947
(2) 1953
(3) 1958
(4) 1949
= 4
~ After the People's Republic of China was set up in 1949 under one-party rule, all critical sectors of the economy, enterprises and lands owned by individuals were brought under government ownership and control.

Q 4 | eco | Indian Economic Development
The Great Leap Forward campaign launched in China in 1958 aimed at:
(1) Opening China to foreign trade
(2) Industrialising the country on a massive scale, with people encouraged to set up industries in their backyards
(3) Introducing the one-child norm
(4) Setting up Special Economic Zones
= 2
~ The Great Leap Forward aimed at rapid, massive industrialisation. People were encouraged to set up industries in their backyards, and in rural areas communes were started.

Q 5 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Under the commune system in China:
(1) Land was sold to foreign companies
(2) Each family farmed its own private land
(3) Industries were owned by private entrepreneurs
(4) People collectively cultivated the land
= 4
~ In the commune system people collectively cultivated lands. By 1958 there were about 26,000 communes covering almost the entire farm population.

Q 6 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which problems did the Great Leap Forward campaign face, according to the chapter?
(1) A severe drought that killed about 30 million people, and the withdrawal of Russian professionals after conflicts between Russia and China
(2) A war with India and a stock market crash
(3) An oil crisis and the collapse of the commune system
(4) Floods in Pakistan and a fall in exports
= 1
~ A severe drought caused havoc, killing about 30 million people. When Russia had conflicts with China, it withdrew the professionals it had sent to help China industrialise.

Q 7 | eco | Indian Economic Development
During the Great Proletarian Cultural Revolution (1966–76) in China:
(1) China joined the WTO
(2) Private property was fully restored
(3) Students and professionals were sent to work and learn from the countryside
(4) Communes were broken up
= 3
~ Mao introduced the Great Proletarian Cultural Revolution in 1965; during 1966–76 students and professionals were sent to the countryside to work and learn.

Q 8 | eco | Indian Economic Development
China introduced its economic reforms in:
(1) 1949
(2) 1978
(3) 1988
(4) 1991
= 2
~ China began reforms in phases from 1978. Pakistan began in 1988 and India in 1991.

Q 9 | eco | Indian Economic Development
In the first phase of China's reforms, what happened in agriculture?
(1) All farms were turned into communes
(2) Farming was banned near cities
(3) Land was given to foreign firms
(4) Commune lands were divided into small plots allocated to individual households, which could keep the income after paying taxes
= 4
~ The first phase covered agriculture, foreign trade and investment. Commune lands were split into small plots for individual households, who could keep all income from the land after paying stipulated taxes.

Q 10 | eco | Indian Economic Development
In the later phase of China's reforms, township and village enterprises (TVEs) were allowed to produce goods. What were they?
(1) Enterprises owned by foreign companies
(2) Branches of the central government's ministries
(3) Enterprises owned and operated by local collectives
(4) Farms run by the army
= 3
~ In the later phase, reforms reached industry: private firms and township and village enterprises, owned and run by local collectives, were allowed to produce goods and faced competition with state-owned enterprises.

Q 11 | eco | Indian Economic Development
'Dual pricing' in China's reform process meant:
(1) Farmers and industrial units bought and sold fixed quantities of inputs and outputs at prices set by the government, and the rest at market prices
(2) Two currencies were used in China
(3) Export prices were double the domestic prices
(4) Prices were fixed for rural and urban areas separately
= 1
~ Under dual pricing, a fixed quantity was traded at government prices and the rest at market prices. As production increased over the years, the share of goods traded in the market rose.

Q 12 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Why did China set up Special Economic Zones (SEZs)?
(1) To protect forests
(2) To attract foreign investors
(3) To house rural migrants
(4) To produce food grains
= 2
~ SEZs were set up to attract foreign investors, which helped China expand its industry and exports.

Q 13 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which describes Pakistan's economic policy in the late 1950s and 1960s?
(1) Complete free trade with no tariffs
(2) Collective farming in communes
(3) A regulated framework for import substitution industrialisation, with tariff protection for consumer goods and direct controls on competing imports
(4) Nationalisation of all banks and farms
= 3
~ Pakistan followed a mixed economy and protected its consumer goods industries through tariffs and direct import controls. The Green Revolution then led to mechanisation and more public investment in infrastructure.

Q 14 | eco | Indian Economic Development
What happened to Pakistan's capital goods industries in the 1970s?
(1) They were nationalised
(2) They were sold to China
(3) They were closed down
(4) They were handed to communes
= 1
~ Capital goods industries were nationalised in the 1970s. From the late 1970s and the 1980s Pakistan shifted to denationalisation and encouraged the private sector.

Q 15 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Pakistan initiated its economic reforms in:
(1) 1978
(2) 1991
(3) 1965
(4) 1988
= 4
~ Pakistan's reforms began in 1988, ten years after China (1978) and three years before India (1991).

Q 16 | eco | Indian Economic Development
In the 1980s, Pakistan received financial support mainly from:
(1) The Soviet Union and China
(2) Western nations, along with remittances from Pakistanis who emigrated to the Middle East
(3) Its own oil exports
(4) India
= 2
~ During this period Pakistan received financial support from Western nations and an ever-increasing flow of remittances from emigrants to the Middle East, which helped stimulate growth.

Q 17 | eco | Indian Economic Development
China introduced the one-child norm in the late 1970s. Which consequences does the chapter mention?
(1) A rapid rise in population growth
(2) A rise in the sex ratio above that of India and Pakistan
(3) No change in the population
(4) A low population growth rate, a decline in the sex ratio and, in the coming decades, a larger share of elderly people
= 4
~ The norm brought down population growth but also led to a decline in the sex ratio. The chapter notes that in a few decades China will have more elderly people in proportion to young people.

Q 18 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which statement about the structure of output fits the chapter's comparison?
(1) In all three countries agriculture remains the largest contributor to GDP
(2) In China the services sector shrank after the reforms
(3) In China manufacturing became a major contributor to GDP, while in India and Pakistan the services sector became the largest
(4) Pakistan's growth came mainly from heavy industry
= 3
~ China's growth came mainly from manufacturing and services. India and Pakistan moved from agriculture directly towards services, which now contribute the largest share of GDP.

Q 19 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which reasons does the chapter give for the slowdown of growth in Pakistan?
(1) Political instability, over-dependence on remittances and foreign aid, and agricultural growth that depended on good harvests rather than steady technical change
(2) Too much investment in education
(3) The one-child norm
(4) The commune system
= 1
~ Pakistan's agricultural growth rested on good harvests, its foreign exchange came largely from remittances and volatile farm exports, and it depended increasingly on foreign loans. Political instability added to these problems.

Q 20 | eco | Indian Economic Development
According to the chapter, why could China's reforms produce rapid growth?
(1) Because China had no population problem
(2) Because China had laid the groundwork earlier, in education and health, land reforms, decentralised planning and small enterprises
(3) Because China depended on foreign aid
(4) Because China copied India's reforms
= 2
~ Before 1978 China had built a base in education, health, land reforms and small enterprises, which made the post-reform growth possible. Each reform was first tried on a small scale and then extended.
