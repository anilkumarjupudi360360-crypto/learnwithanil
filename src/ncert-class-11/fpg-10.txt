# NCERT Class 11 Geography, Fundamentals of Physical Geography, Chapter 10: Water in the Atmosphere
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). November 2026.

Q 1 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Water vapour in the atmosphere varies from zero to about what share of its volume?
(1) 1 per cent
(2) 4 per cent
(3) 10 per cent
(4) 21 per cent
= 2
~ The chapter gives a range of zero to four per cent by volume. Even this small amount plays a big role in weather.

Q 2 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The actual amount of water vapour present in the atmosphere, expressed as weight per unit volume of air (grams per cubic metre), is called:
(1) Relative humidity
(2) Absolute humidity
(3) Dew point
(4) Saturation
= 2
~ Absolute humidity is the actual weight of water vapour in a given volume of air. Relative humidity compares this with what the air can hold at that temperature.

Q 3 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements about relative humidity:
1. It is the percentage of moisture present in the air compared with its full capacity at a given temperature.
2. It is greater over the oceans and least over the continents.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 3
~ Relative humidity changes with temperature because warmer air can hold more moisture. It is high over oceans and low over continents.

Q 4 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The temperature at which saturation occurs in a given sample of air is called the:
(1) Boiling point
(2) Dew point
(3) Freezing point
(4) Lapse rate
= 2
~ When air cools to its dew point it becomes saturated and cannot hold more moisture, so condensation begins.

Q 5 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Condensation of water vapour is caused mainly by:
(1) Loss of heat from the air
(2) Increase in the air temperature
(3) Decrease in relative humidity
(4) Increase in wind speed alone
= 1
~ Cooling brings air to saturation. Condensation depends on how much and how fast the air cools, and on its relative humidity.

Q 6 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which set of conditions is ideal for the formation of dew?
(1) Cloudy sky, strong winds and low humidity
(2) Heavy rainfall and high temperature
(3) Hot afternoons with dust storms
(4) Clear sky, calm air, high relative humidity and cold, long nights
= 4
~ Dew forms when moisture is deposited as droplets on cooler surfaces of solid objects such as stones, grass blades and leaves. For dew, the dew point must be above freezing point.

Q 7 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Frost forms on cold surfaces when:
(1) Condensation takes place below the freezing point, with the dew point at or below 0°C
(2) Condensation takes place above the freezing point
(3) Rain falls on a hot surface
(4) Air is completely dry
= 1
~ When the dew point is at or below freezing, the excess moisture is deposited as minute ice crystals instead of water droplets.

Q 8 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
A fog mixed with smoke, common in urban and industrial centres, is called:
(1) Mist
(2) Frost
(3) Haze
(4) Smog
= 4
~ Fog is a cloud with its base at or very near the ground. In cities and industrial areas smoke mixes with fog to form smog.

Q 9 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
How does mist differ from fog, according to the chapter?
(1) Mist contains more moisture; each nucleus has a thicker layer of moisture
(2) Mist forms only over deserts
(3) Mist is drier than fog
(4) Mist forms only above 5,000 m
= 1
~ In mist each nucleus carries a thicker layer of moisture. Mists are frequent over mountains, where rising warm air meets cold surfaces; fogs are drier.

Q 10 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which clouds form at high altitudes of 8,000–12,000 m, are thin and detached with a feathery appearance, and are always white?
(1) Cumulus
(2) Stratus
(3) Cirrus
(4) Nimbus
= 3
~ Cirrus clouds are the high, wispy clouds. Cumulus clouds look like cotton wool and generally form at 4,000–7,000 m.

Q 11 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Clouds that look like cotton wool, exist in patches with a flat base and generally form at a height of 4,000–7,000 m are:
(1) Cumulus
(2) Cirrus
(3) Stratus
(4) Nimbostratus
= 1
~ Cumulus clouds are scattered here and there with flat bases.

Q 12 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Layered clouds covering large portions of the sky, formed due to loss of heat or the mixing of air masses with different temperatures, are:
(1) Cirrus
(2) Cirrocumulus
(3) Cumulus
(4) Stratus
= 4
~ "Stratus" means layered. Such clouds spread as sheets across the sky.

Q 13 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which clouds are black or dark grey, extremely dense and opaque to the sun's rays, and form at middle levels or very near the surface?
(1) Cirrus
(2) Cumulus
(3) Nimbus
(4) Altocumulus
= 3
~ Nimbus clouds are shapeless masses of thick vapour; they sometimes reach so low that they seem to touch the ground.

Q 14 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which of the following clouds are classified in the chapter as clouds with extensive vertical development?
(1) Cirrostratus and cirrocumulus
(2) Altostratus and altocumulus
(3) Stratocumulus and nimbostratus
(4) Cumulus and cumulonimbus
= 4
~ Cirrostratus and cirrocumulus are high clouds, altostratus and altocumulus are middle clouds, stratocumulus and nimbostratus are low clouds, and cumulus and cumulonimbus show vertical development.

Q 15 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Precipitation in the form of frozen raindrops and refrozen melted snow-water, which occurs when a layer of air above freezing point overlies a sub-freezing layer near the ground, is called:
(1) Hail
(2) Dew
(3) Snow
(4) Sleet
= 4
~ Sleet forms when snow melts in a warm layer and refreezes as it falls through cold air near the ground.

Q 16 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Hailstones are recognised by:
(1) Fine flakes of ice
(2) Water droplets on grass
(3) Several concentric layers of ice, one over another
(4) Ice crystals on windows
= 3
~ Raindrops are carried up and down in clouds and freeze repeatedly, so hailstones show concentric layers of ice.

Q 17 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements about convectional rainfall:
1. It is common in summer or in the hotter part of the day.
2. It is very common in the equatorial regions and in the interior parts of continents, particularly in the Northern Hemisphere.
3. It is usually light but of long duration.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1
~ Convectional rain comes as heavy showers of short duration with thunder and lightning, so statement 3 is wrong.

Q 18 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
When saturated air meets a mountain, it is forced to rise and gives rain on the windward slope, while the leeward slope gets little rain. The leeward area is called a:
(1) Doldrum
(2) Rain-shadow area
(3) Front
(4) Convergence zone
= 2
~ This is orographic or relief rain. The air descending on the leeward side warms up and gives little rain, creating a rain-shadow area.

Q 19 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Between 45° and 65° latitude North and South, rainfall is first received on the western margins of the continents and decreases eastwards. This is because of the:
(1) Trade winds
(2) Westerlies
(3) Polar easterlies
(4) Monsoons
= 2
~ The westerlies bring moisture from the oceans onto the western margins. Between 35° and 40° latitude, by contrast, rain is heavier on the eastern coasts.

Q 20 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which of the following areas receive less than 50 cm of rainfall a year, according to the chapter's world distribution of rainfall?
(1) The equatorial belt
(2) Coastal areas of monsoon lands
(3) Rain-shadow areas in the interior of continents and high latitudes
(4) Windward slopes of mountains on western coasts of the cool temperate zone
= 3
~ The equatorial belt, windward western slopes in the cool temperate zone and monsoon coasts receive over 200 cm. Interior rain-shadow areas and high latitudes get below 50 cm.
