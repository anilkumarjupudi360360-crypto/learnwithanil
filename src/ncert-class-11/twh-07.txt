# NCERT Class 11 History, Themes in World History, Chapter 7: Paths to Modernisation
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | his | Themes in World History
From 1603, real power in Japan was held by the shoguns of which family, ruling from Edo (present-day Tokyo)?
(1) The Meiji
(2) The Qing
(3) The Ming
(4) The Tokugawa
= 4
~ The emperor stayed in Kyoto with little power. The country was divided among about 250 domains, each under a lord called a daimyo.

Q 2 | his | Themes in World History
In Tokugawa Japan, the "samurai" were:
(1) The warrior class, who served the shogun and the daimyo
(2) Rich merchants
(3) Buddhist priests
(4) Peasant farmers
= 1
~ The samurai were the ruling elite. By the nineteenth century, many of them had become administrators rather than fighting men.

Q 3 | his | Themes in World History
In 1853, the American Commodore Matthew Perry arrived in Japan and demanded that Japan:
(1) Give up its emperor
(2) Open its ports for trade and diplomatic relations
(3) Become a US colony
(4) Adopt Christianity
= 2
~ Japan signed a treaty with the USA in 1854. The opening of the country set off a political crisis that led to the end of the shogunate.

Q 4 | his | Themes in World History
The Meiji Restoration of 1868:
(1) Restored the shogun to full power
(2) Removed the shogun and brought the emperor to power in Edo, renamed Tokyo
(3) Made Japan a colony
(4) Ended Japan's monarchy
= 2
~ The new emperor's reign was called Meiji, meaning "enlightened government". The new government set out to build a modern state.

Q 5 | his | Themes in World History
The Meiji slogan "fukoku kyohei" meant:
(1) Asia for the Asians
(2) Expel the barbarians
(3) Equality for all
(4) Rich country, strong army
= 4
~ The government wanted to develop the economy and build a strong army, so that Japan would not be colonised by the West.

Q 6 | his | Themes in World History
Which of the following was a Meiji reform?
1. Compulsory schooling for all children from 1870s
2. A conscription law, under which every young man had to serve in the army
3. Abolition of all taxes
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 2
~ The Meiji state also introduced a new land tax and built railways. Textbooks taught loyalty to the emperor and the nation.

Q 7 | his | Themes in World History
The large business houses that dominated the Japanese economy, such as Mitsubishi and Sumitomo, were called:
(1) Zaibatsu
(2) Daimyo
(3) Shogunates
(4) Communes
= 1
~ The zaibatsu were family-controlled business groups. They grew with state support during Japan's industrialisation.

Q 8 | his | Themes in World History
Fukuzawa Yukichi, a leading Meiji intellectual, argued that Japan should:
(1) Stay closed to the world
(2) Follow China's example
(3) "Expel Asia" and learn from the West
(4) Give up industry
= 3
~ He founded what became Keio University. Others, like Ueki Emori, pressed for freedom and people's rights.

Q 9 | his | Themes in World History
Japan colonised Taiwan in 1895 and Korea in 1910. Which wars before these showed Japan's new military strength?
(1) Victory over China in 1894-95 and over Russia in 1904-05
(2) Defeat by China
(3) Victory over Britain
(4) War with the USA in 1900
= 1
~ The victory over Russia was the first time an Asian power defeated a European power in modern times.

Q 10 | his | Themes in World History
After its defeat in the Second World War, Japan was occupied by the USA (1945-52). Japan's new constitution of 1947 is known for:
(1) Restoring the shogun
(2) Abolishing the emperor completely
(3) A "no war" clause, under which Japan renounced war
(4) Making Japan a part of the USA
= 3
~ Article 9 renounces war as a means of settling disputes. Women also got the right to vote, and the zaibatsu were broken up.

Q 11 | his | Themes in World History
Which of the following events of 1964 signalled Japan's recovery after the war?
(1) The first moon landing
(2) Japan's victory over Russia
(3) The Meiji Constitution
(4) The Tokyo Olympics and the start of the high-speed bullet train (Shinkansen)
= 4
~ Japan's rapid economic growth after the war is often called a "miracle". It also brought problems like pollution, such as Minamata disease caused by mercury poisoning.

Q 12 | his | Themes in World History
Which dynasty ruled China from the seventeenth century until 1911?
(1) The Qing (Manchu)
(2) The Ming
(3) The Song
(4) The Han
= 1
~ The Qing rulers faced great challenges in the nineteenth century, including wars with western powers and big rebellions.

Q 13 | his | Themes in World History
The Opium War (1839-42) between Britain and China ended with:
(1) The defeat of Britain
(2) A ban on opium in Britain
(3) The Meiji Restoration
(4) The Treaty of Nanjing, under which China opened ports to trade and gave Hong Kong to Britain
= 4
~ The British had been selling opium from India in China. The war began a period of humiliation for China at the hands of foreign powers.

Q 14 | his | Themes in World History
Kang Youwei and Liang Qichao are known for:
(1) Leading the Boxer Rebellion
(2) Founding the Communist Party
(3) Proposing modern reforms in the Qing administration in 1898, which were cut short
(4) Leading the Long March
= 3
~ The reforms lasted about a hundred days. Liang Qichao also stressed that the Chinese needed to develop a sense of being a nation.

Q 15 | his | Themes in World History
Sun Yat-sen, the founder of modern China, put forward the "Three Principles". These were:
(1) Peace, land and bread
(2) Liberty, equality and fraternity
(3) Rich country, strong army and loyalty
(4) Nationalism, democracy and socialism
= 4
~ After the 1911 revolution, the Qing dynasty was overthrown and the Republic of China was set up in 1912, with Sun Yat-sen as a leading figure.

Q 16 | his | Themes in World History
The May Fourth Movement of 1919 in China began as a protest against:
(1) The Opium War
(2) The death of Mao Zedong
(3) The decision at the post-war peace conference to give Shandong (former German territory) to Japan
(4) The Long March
= 3
~ Students in Beijing led the protests. The movement spread new ideas of nationalism, science and democracy.

Q 17 | his | Themes in World History
Which of the following is correctly matched?
(1) Guomindang - led by Mao Zedong
(2) Long March - the Communists' retreat of about 6,000 miles (1934-35) to Yanan
(3) Communist Party of China - founded in 1949
(4) Long March - the Guomindang's journey to Taiwan
= 2
~ The Guomindang (Nationalist Party) was led by Chiang Kai-shek after Sun Yat-sen. The Communist Party was founded in 1921.

Q 18 | his | Themes in World History
The People's Republic of China was proclaimed on 1 October 1949. Its leader was:
(1) Chiang Kai-shek
(2) Sun Yat-sen
(3) Mao Zedong
(4) Deng Xiaoping
= 3
~ The Guomindang government fled to Taiwan. The new government took over land and industry in stages.

Q 19 | his | Themes in World History
Which leader of China, from 1978, introduced market reforms while keeping the Communist Party's control?
(1) Deng Xiaoping
(2) Mao Zedong
(3) Liu Shaoqi
(4) Chiang Kai-shek
= 1
~ The reforms are often called the "Four Modernisations" in agriculture, industry, science and defence. China's economy grew very fast after them.

Q 20 | his | Themes in World History
Which of the following is the main contrast the chapter draws between Japan and China?
(1) Japan was colonised, China was not
(2) Japan modernised and industrialised quickly while keeping its independence, whereas China faced foreign domination and revolutions before taking a socialist path
(3) China industrialised before Japan
(4) Both followed exactly the same path
= 2
~ The chapter shows that there is no single path to becoming modern. Each country's history shaped the choices it made.
