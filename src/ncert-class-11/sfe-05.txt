Q 1 | eco | Statistics for Economics
What is the arithmetic mean of 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12?
(1) 6
(2) 7
(3) 8
(4) 10
= 3
~ Mean = sum of observations ÷ number of observations = (4 + 6 + 8 + 10 + 12) ÷ 5 = 40 ÷ 5 = 8.

Q 2 | eco | Statistics for Economics
The sum of the deviations of all observations from their arithmetic mean is always:
(1) Zero
(2) Positive
(3) Negative
(4) Equal to the mean
= 1
~ The positive and negative deviations from the mean cancel out exactly, so their sum is always zero. This is an important property of the arithmetic mean.

Q 3 | eco | Statistics for Economics
Which measure of central tendency is most affected by extreme values?
(1) Median
(2) Mode
(3) First quartile
(4) Arithmetic mean
= 4
~ The mean uses every value, so one very large or very small value pulls it up or down. The median and mode are not affected in the same way.

Q 4 | eco | Statistics for Economics
What is the median of 7, 3, 9, 5 and 11?
(1) 9
(2) 7
(3) 5
(4) 6
= 2
~ Arranged in order: 3, 5, 7, 9, 11. With 5 observations, the median is the (5 + 1) ÷ 2 = 3rd value, which is 7.

Q 5 | eco | Statistics for Economics
What is the median of 2, 8, 4, 10, 6 and 12?
(1) 6
(2) 7
(3) 8
(4) 6.5
= 2
~ Arranged: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12. With 6 observations, the median is the average of the 3rd and 4th values: (6 + 8) ÷ 2 = 7.

Q 6 | eco | Statistics for Economics
What is the mode of 3, 5, 5, 7, 8, 5, 9, 7?
(1) 7
(2) 8
(3) 5
(4) 3
= 3
~ The mode is the value that occurs most often. 5 occurs three times, 7 twice and the others once, so the mode is 5.

Q 7 | eco | Statistics for Economics
A student scores 60 in Economics, which carries a weight of 3, and 80 in Statistics, which carries a weight of 1. What is the weighted mean of the marks?
(1) 65
(2) 70
(3) 75
(4) 60
= 1
~ Weighted mean = (60 × 3 + 80 × 1) ÷ (3 + 1) = (180 + 80) ÷ 4 = 260 ÷ 4 = 65. The simple mean would be 70, but the heavier weight on Economics pulls it down.

Q 8 | eco | Statistics for Economics
Using an assumed mean of 50, the sum of the deviations of 10 observations from 50 is −20. What is the arithmetic mean?
(1) 52
(2) 30
(3) 50
(4) 48
= 4
~ By the assumed mean method, mean = A + (Σd ÷ N) = 50 + (−20 ÷ 10) = 50 − 2 = 48.

Q 10 | eco | Statistics for Economics
The median is best described as:
(1) The value that occurs most often
(2) The sum of values divided by their number
(3) The difference between the highest and lowest values
(4) A positional average: the value that divides the arranged data into two equal parts
= 4
~ The median is the middle value when data are arranged in order; half the observations are below it and half above. Since it depends on position, extreme values do not affect it.

Q 11 | eco | Statistics for Economics
For ungrouped data with N observations arranged in ascending order, the median is the value of the:
(1) N ÷ 2 th item
(2) (N + 1) ÷ 2 th item
(3) (N − 1) ÷ 2 th item
(4) N th item
= 2
~ The median is the size of the (N + 1) ÷ 2 th item. If this is not a whole number, the median is the average of the two middle items.

Q 12 | eco | Statistics for Economics
Quartiles divide arranged data into:
(1) Two equal parts
(2) Ten equal parts
(3) Four equal parts
(4) Hundred equal parts
= 3
~ The three quartiles Q1, Q2 and Q3 divide the data into four equal parts. The second quartile is the median.

Q 13 | eco | Statistics for Economics
For the arranged data 3, 5, 8, 10, 12, 15, 18, what is the first quartile (Q1)?
(1) 3
(2) 8
(3) 10
(4) 5
= 4

Q 14 | eco | Statistics for Economics
The 50th percentile of a distribution is the same as its:
(1) Median
(2) Mean
(3) Mode
(4) First quartile
= 1
~ Percentiles divide data into 100 equal parts. The 50th percentile has half the observations below it, which is the median.

Q 15 | eco | Statistics for Economics
Which measure of central tendency can be used for qualitative data, such as the most preferred colour of a product?
(1) Arithmetic mean
(2) Median
(3) Mode
(4) Weighted mean
= 3
~ Colours cannot be added or ranked numerically, but we can find which one occurs most often. That is the mode.

Q 16 | eco | Statistics for Economics
Which statement about the mode is correct?
(1) A distribution can have only one mode
(2) A distribution may have more than one mode
(3) The mode is always equal to the mean
(4) The mode cannot be found for grouped data
= 2
~ A distribution may be bimodal or multimodal if two or more values share the highest frequency. The mode can also be found for grouped data, using a formula or a histogram.

Q 17 | eco | Statistics for Economics
For the frequency distribution 0–10 (5), 10–20 (8), 20–30 (12), 30–40 (10), 40–50 (5), what is the median?
(1) 25
(2) 24.17
(3) 26.5
(4) 25.83
= 4
~ N = 40, so N ÷ 2 = 20. Cumulative frequencies are 5, 13, 25, 35, 40, so the median class is 20–30. Median = L + [(N/2 − c.f.) ÷ f] × h = 20 + [(20 − 13) ÷ 12] × 10 = 20 + 5.83 = 25.83.

Q 18 | eco | Statistics for Economics
For the same distribution, 0–10 (5), 10–20 (8), 20–30 (12), 30–40 (10), 40–50 (5), what is the mode?
(1) 26.67
(2) 25.83
(3) 28
(4) 24
= 1
~ The modal class is 20–30 (highest frequency, 12). D1 = 12 − 8 = 4 and D2 = 12 − 10 = 2. Mode = L + [D1 ÷ (D1 + D2)] × h = 20 + (4 ÷ 6) × 10 = 20 + 6.67 = 26.67.

Q 19 | eco | Statistics for Economics
For the frequency distribution 0–10 (2), 10–20 (3), 20–30 (5), what is the arithmetic mean?
(1) 15
(2) 20
(3) 18
(4) 16
= 3
~ Class marks are 5, 15 and 25. Σfm = 2 × 5 + 3 × 15 + 5 × 25 = 10 + 45 + 125 = 180, and N = 10. Mean = 180 ÷ 10 = 18.

Q 20 | eco | Statistics for Economics
When is the median preferred to the arithmetic mean as an average?
(1) When all values are equal
(2) When the data contain extreme values, such as incomes in a locality with a few very rich families
(3) When the data are qualitative
(4) When weights are given
= 2
~ A few very high incomes would pull the mean far above the typical income. The median, being a positional average, is not affected by such extremes and better shows the typical value.
