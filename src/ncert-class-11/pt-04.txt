# NCERT Class 11 Political Science, Political Theory, Chapter 4: Social Justice

Q 1 | pol | Political Theory
In Plato's Republic, discussed in the chapter, Socrates argues with young friends about whether it is better to be just or unjust. What is Socrates' view?
(1) Unjust people are always happier
(2) Justice is only what benefits the strong
(3) Justice is good for everyone in the long run, because it involves the well-being of all people, not only one's own
(4) Justice does not matter in politics
= 3
~ Glaucon and others argued that the unjust seem to prosper. Socrates replied that justice benefits everyone in the long run, and that a just state serves the good of all its members.

Q 2 | pol | Political Theory
The German philosopher Immanuel Kant, mentioned in the chapter, argued that:
(1) Only rulers have dignity
(2) Every human being possesses dignity and deserves to be treated as an end, with the opportunity to develop his or her talents
(3) Justice means obeying the strongest
(4) Inequality is natural and good
= 2
~ For Kant, human dignity means each person deserves equal respect. If all persons have dignity, justice requires that each gets the chance to pursue his or her goals.

Q 3 | pol | Political Theory
The ancient Chinese philosopher who argued that rulers should punish wrong-doers and reward the virtuous is:
(1) Plato
(2) Kant
(3) Lao Tzu
(4) Confucius
= 4
~ The chapter notes that Confucius linked justice to good government that punishes wrongs and rewards virtue. In ancient India, justice was linked to dharma and maintaining a just social order.

Q 4 | pol | Political Theory
The principle that people should be rewarded according to the effort and skill they put into their work is called:
(1) Proportionate justice
(2) Equal treatment for equals
(3) Recognition of special needs
(4) Natural inequality
= 1
~ Proportionate justice means rewards proportional to effort and skill, for example a surgeon earning more than a helper. It works alongside equal treatment and special care for those with special needs.

Q 5 | pol | Political Theory
"People with the same characteristics should be treated in the same way; they should not be discriminated against because of caste, religion or gender." This principle is called:
(1) Proportionate justice
(2) Equal treatment for equals
(3) Veil of ignorance
(4) Laissez-faire
= 2
~ Equal treatment for equals means equal rights and equal respect for people who are equal in relevant ways, such as men and women doing the same work getting the same pay.

Q 6 | pol | Political Theory
Giving special facilities to people with disabilities, or to those who have been deprived of opportunities, reflects which principle of justice?
(1) Equal treatment for equals only
(2) The harm principle
(3) Free market justice
(4) Recognition of special needs
= 4
~ People with special needs or who have suffered disadvantage may need extra help to have a fair chance. Reservations for SCs and STs are justified partly on this principle.

Q 7 | pol | Political Theory
John Rawls, discussed in the chapter, proposed thinking about a just society from behind a:
(1) Veil of ignorance
(2) Curtain of wealth
(3) Wall of tradition
(4) Shield of power
= 1
~ Behind a veil of ignorance, people decide the rules of society without knowing what position they will occupy in it: rich or poor, healthy or sick. Rawls argued they would then choose fair rules.

Q 8 | pol | Political Theory
According to Rawls, people behind the veil of ignorance would choose rules that:
(1) Benefit the richest
(2) Abolish all private property
(3) Improve the position of the worst-off members of society, since they themselves might be among them
(4) Favour their own caste or religion
= 3
~ Not knowing whether they will be well-off or badly off, rational people would protect themselves by making sure the worst-off are as well placed as possible. Rawls saw this as both rational and fair.

Q 9 | pol | Political Theory
Rawls argued that choosing just rules behind the veil of ignorance does not require people to be unusually generous. Why?
(1) Because people are naturally selfless
(2) Because the state forces them to be generous
(3) Because it is rational: anyone could end up among the disadvantaged, so protecting them is in everyone's interest
(4) Because religion requires it
= 3
~ Rawls's point is that fairness follows from rational self-interest under uncertainty. That is what makes his theory persuasive to people who do not share the same moral views.

Q 10 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following is an example of a measure taken in India to promote social justice?
(1) Abolishing elections
(2) Removing the Fundamental Rights
(3) Banning all private schools
(4) Land reforms and reservations for SCs, STs and backward classes
= 4
~ Land reforms tried to give land to the tiller and break the power of big landlords, and reservations aim to give fair opportunities to historically disadvantaged groups.

Q 11 | pol | Political Theory
Social justice, as discussed in the chapter, is mainly about:
(1) Punishing criminals
(2) A fair distribution of goods, opportunities and rights in society, so that people can live with dignity
(3) Equal marks in examinations
(4) Following ancient customs
= 2
~ Social justice concerns the just distribution of resources and opportunities among people, which may require reducing inequality and ensuring basic needs for all.

Q 12 | pol | Political Theory
Supporters of the free market, as described in the chapter, argue that:
(1) Markets reward talent and effort, allow choice and are efficient, and the state should only ensure a basic minimum
(2) The state should own all industries
(3) Markets always produce equal outcomes
(4) Free markets need no laws at all
= 1
~ Free-market supporters say open competition rewards ability and gives consumers choice. Many accept that the state should provide a basic minimum, such as primary health care, for those in need.

Q 13 | pol | Political Theory
Critics of an unregulated free market argue that:
(1) Markets never produce goods
(2) Markets lower all prices to zero
(3) Markets are always fair to everyone
(4) Markets tend to favour those who already have resources, and may not provide basic services like health and education to the poor
= 4
~ Those who start with wealth, education and contacts have an advantage in the market. Without state action, the poor may be left without access to basic goods, which is why critics favour intervention.

Q 14 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following is generally accepted, in the chapter's discussion, as part of ensuring social justice?
(1) That every person gets exactly the same income
(2) That everyone's basic needs, such as adequate food, housing, health care, safe drinking water and education, are met
(3) That the state controls all private property
(4) That the market alone decides who gets education
= 2
~ There is wide agreement that a just society must ensure a minimum standard of living for all, even if people disagree on how far equality should go beyond that.

Q 15 | pol | Political Theory
Why does the chapter say that all three principles of justice (equal treatment, proportionate reward and special needs) have to be balanced?
(1) Because only one of them is ever valid
(2) Because courts refuse to apply them
(3) Because pushing any one too far can conflict with the others, so a fair society must weigh them together
(4) Because they apply only in Western countries
= 3
~ Equal treatment alone might ignore people's different efforts or needs; proportionate reward alone might ignore deprivation. A just society combines them sensibly.

Q 16 | pol | Political Theory
In the context of social justice, the idea that the state should intervene to make sure that people's basic needs are met is associated with:
(1) Those who favour state intervention to reduce inequality
(2) Those who favour an unregulated market with no state role
(3) Those who oppose all government
(4) Those who believe inequality is natural and good
= 1
~ Supporters of state intervention argue that without it the poor would remain deprived. They support public provision of education, health care and food security.

Q 17 | pol | Political Theory
The idea that justice involves giving each person what is due to him or her has been understood differently over time. In ancient Indian society, justice was associated with:
(1) The free market
(2) Universal adult franchise
(3) Rawls's veil of ignorance
(4) Dharma and maintaining a just social order
= 4
~ The chapter notes that in ancient India justice was linked to dharma, and that rulers were expected to uphold a just social order. Modern ideas of justice emphasise equality and dignity.

Q 18 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following situations would be considered unjust from the point of view of "equal treatment for equals"?
(1) Paying more to a person who works longer hours
(2) Building ramps for wheelchair users
(3) Paying a woman less than a man for doing the same work
(4) Giving scholarships to poor students
= 3
~ Equal work deserves equal pay. Paying women less for the same work treats equals unequally because of gender.

Q 19 | pol | Political Theory
Rawls's book that put forward the theory of justice as fairness is:
(1) On Liberty
(2) A Theory of Justice
(3) The Republic
(4) Das Kapital
= 2
~ A Theory of Justice (1971) is Rawls's major work. On Liberty is by J.S. Mill, The Republic by Plato and Das Kapital by Karl Marx.

Q 20 | pol | Political Theory
Why do the claims of social justice often require some redistribution of resources?
(1) Because existing inequalities, often the result of past injustice, prevent some people from having a fair chance to live with dignity
(2) Because all property is illegal
(3) Because markets do not exist
(4) Because justice means everyone has the same income
= 1
~ Inequalities passed down through generations, like those caused by caste or landlessness, mean that formal equality is not enough. Redistribution, through measures like land reforms, helps level the field.
