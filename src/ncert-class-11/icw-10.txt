Q 1 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
What does the chapter mean by the "political philosophy" of the Constitution?
(1) The personal religious beliefs of its framers
(2) The procedure for amending it
(3) The moral and political values the Constitution stands for, such as freedom, equality, justice and respect for diversity
(4) The list of union territories
= 3
~ Studying the philosophy of the Constitution means asking which ideals it is committed to and how its provisions express them, so that we can judge and interpret it.

Q 2 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Why does the chapter say we should go back to the Constituent Assembly Debates?
(1) Because they are legally binding on courts in every case
(2) Because they replace the text of the Constitution
(3) Because the Constitution has no Preamble
(4) Because they show the reasons behind the provisions and help us understand and defend the values of the Constitution
= 4
~ The debates record why particular choices were made. Knowing the reasons helps us interpret provisions and see the Constitution as a moral vision, not just a legal text.

Q 3 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The chapter describes the Constitution as a means of democratic transformation. This means that it:
(1) Prevents any change in society
(2) Allows only violent revolution
(3) Limits the power of the government and also empowers it and the people to change unjust social structures peacefully
(4) Gives all power to the judiciary
= 3
~ The Constitution restrains power so that it is not misused, and at the same time gives a peaceful, democratic way to end caste discrimination, inequality and other injustices.

Q 4 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The Indian Constitution protected freedom of expression and freedom from arbitrary arrest as fundamental values. This shows its commitment to:
(1) Social justice
(2) Federalism
(3) Universal franchise
(4) Individual freedom
= 4
~ Individual freedom was a demand of the national movement, which had fought censorship and arbitrary arrests under colonial rule. The Constitution made these freedoms fundamental.

Q 5 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Reservations in legislatures and government jobs for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes reflect the Constitution's commitment to:
(1) Individual freedom alone
(2) Secularism
(3) Social justice
(4) National identity
= 3
~ The framers recognised that historical injustice would not end through equal rights alone. Special measures for the most disadvantaged were adopted as part of a commitment to social justice.

Q 6 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The chapter says the Indian Constitution recognises not only individual rights but also the rights of communities. Which provision illustrates this?
(1) The right of religious and linguistic minorities to set up and run their own educational institutions
(2) The right to property
(3) The power of judicial review
(4) The election of the President
= 1
~ The Constitution gives community-based rights, such as the cultural and educational rights of minorities, because the freedom of individuals in India is closely tied to their communities.

Q 7 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
How does the Indian idea of secularism differ from the dominant Western idea, according to the chapter?
(1) Indian secularism requires the state to have an official religion
(2) Indian secularism bans all religious practices
(3) There is no difference at all
(4) The Western idea emphasises mutual exclusion of state and religion, while the Indian idea allows "principled distance": the state can intervene in religion to promote equality and can support religious communities
= 4
~ In the Western model, state and religion stay out of each other's affairs. In India the state keeps a principled distance: it can, for example, ban untouchability or open temples to all castes, and can aid minority institutions.

Q 8 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which of the following is an example of state intervention in religion for the sake of equality, as discussed in the chapter?
(1) Declaring Hinduism the state religion
(2) Banning all religious festivals
(3) Abolishing untouchability and permitting laws that throw open Hindu religious institutions to all castes
(4) Allowing only one community to run schools
= 3
~ The Constitution itself abolishes untouchability and allows laws opening temples to all castes. Such intervention aims to remove caste discrimination within religion.

Q 9 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
By giving every adult the right to vote from the very first election, the Constitution showed its commitment to:
(1) Universal franchise
(2) Asymmetric federalism
(3) Separate electorates
(4) Indirect democracy only
= 1
~ The framers trusted ordinary people with the vote at a time when many were poor and could not read. Universal adult franchise was seen as essential to democracy and national unity.

Q 10 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
"Asymmetric federalism" in the Indian Constitution means that:
(1) Every state has exactly the same powers and status
(2) The states have more power than the Union
(3) The Rajya Sabha has equal seats for all states
(4) Some states and regions have special provisions suited to their history and circumstances
= 4
~ The Constitution gives special provisions to some states, such as those under Article 371 for the north-eastern states. Treating different regions differently in some respects helped hold diverse regions together.

Q 11 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which of the following is one of the "procedural achievements" of the Constitution mentioned in the chapter?
(1) It was written by a single leader
(2) It reflects faith in political deliberation and a spirit of accommodation and compromise
(3) It was copied entirely from the British Constitution
(4) It was imposed without discussion
= 2
~ The Constitution was made through long debate in which members tried to accommodate different views, and some issues were left open for later. This democratic method is itself an achievement.

Q 12 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which of the following criticisms of the Indian Constitution does the chapter discuss?
1. It is unwieldy, because it is too long
2. It is unrepresentative, because the Constituent Assembly was not elected by universal adult franchise
3. It is an alien document, because many of its provisions were borrowed from Western constitutions
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ The chapter examines all three and argues each is overstated: length reflects India's needs, the Assembly represented diverse views, and borrowed ideas were adapted to Indian conditions.

Q 13 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
How does the chapter respond to the criticism that the Constitution is an "alien document"?
(1) It agrees fully that the Constitution is foreign
(2) It says no idea was borrowed from anywhere
(3) It argues that ideas borrowed from other constitutions were adapted to Indian needs, and that a modern Western influence is part of India's own history of modernity
(4) It says the Constitution should be replaced
= 3
~ Borrowing was creative, not blind copying: borrowed institutions were reshaped to suit India. Modern Western ideas had also become part of Indian thinking through the national movement.

Q 14 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which of the following is one of the limitations of the Constitution identified in the chapter?
(1) It does not provide for any Fundamental Rights
(2) It does not deal adequately with some issues of gender justice, particularly within the family
(3) It does not provide for elections
(4) It has no provision for amendment
= 2
~ The chapter mentions three limitations: a centralised idea of national unity, the neglect of some gender justice issues within the family, and placing some social and economic rights in the Directive Principles instead of the Fundamental Rights.

Q 15 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Why does the chapter say that keeping social and economic rights in the Directive Principles is a limitation?
(1) Because Directive Principles cannot be enforced in courts, so these rights are not guaranteed
(2) Because Directive Principles are not part of the Constitution
(3) Because Directive Principles apply only to minorities
(4) Because Directive Principles were removed in 1976
= 1
~ Rights such as work and livelihood are in the Directive Principles, which are not justiciable. Many other constitutions of the time made such rights enforceable, which is why the chapter treats this as a limitation.

Q 16 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which value of the Constitution is reflected in the fact that a citizen of any religion, caste or region has equal rights and the state does not discriminate among them?
(1) Equality and national unity based on equal citizenship
(2) Asymmetric federalism
(3) Separate electorates
(4) Preventive detention
= 1
~ Equal citizenship, without discrimination on grounds of religion, caste, sex or place of birth, binds a diverse people into one nation. The Constitution builds national identity on this rather than on any one religion or language.

Q 17 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The chapter says that the Indian Constitution does not demand that people give up their cultural identities to be part of the nation. This shows its:
(1) Respect for diversity
(2) Centralised notion of unity
(3) Rejection of minority rights
(4) Preference for one language
= 1
~ Indian national identity is meant to coexist with regional, linguistic and religious identities. The Constitution protects these diverse identities while building a shared citizenship.

Q 18 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
According to the chapter, what does it mean to say that the Constitution was made through a "spirit of accommodation"?
(1) That every decision was taken by a vote of one party
(2) That members tried to reach agreement on difficult questions, sometimes by compromise, rather than by imposing a majority view
(3) That the framers avoided all difficult issues
(4) That the British accommodated the Assembly's demands
= 2
~ Many provisions, such as those on language and minorities, came out of negotiation and compromise. This spirit gave the Constitution wide acceptance.

Q 19 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which of the following statements about the Constituent Assembly and the criticism that it was "unrepresentative" is supported by the chapter?
(1) The Assembly included only members of one religion
(2) Although members were not elected by universal adult franchise, the Assembly represented a wide range of opinions, and its debates reflect concerns of many sections of society
(3) The Assembly had no members from the provinces
(4) The members were appointed by the British Parliament
= 2
~ The chapter accepts that the Assembly was elected on a limited franchise but argues that its members brought in diverse views, and that the Constitution's acceptance over the years strengthens its claim to represent the people.

Q 20 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which pair correctly matches a core feature of the Constitution with an example of it?
(1) Social justice — Reservations for SCs and STs
(2) Individual freedom — Preventive detention
(3) Secularism — An official state religion
(4) Universal franchise — Voting rights only for graduates
= 1
~ Reservations are a means of social justice. Preventive detention limits freedom, an official religion would go against secularism, and limiting votes to graduates would deny universal franchise.
