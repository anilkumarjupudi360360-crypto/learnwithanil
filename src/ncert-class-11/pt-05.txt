# NCERT Class 11 Political Science, Political Theory, Chapter 5: Rights
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | pol | Political Theory
According to the chapter, a right is best described as:
(1) A justified claim or entitlement that society recognises as necessary for a life of dignity and well-being
(2) Anything a person wants
(3) A privilege given only to the rich
(4) An order issued by a ruler
= 1
~ Rights are claims backed by reasons that society accepts as valid, such as the need for dignity, freedom or well-being. Not every wish, like wanting a particular dress, is a right.

Q 2 | pol | Political Theory
In the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, political theorists argued that rights are given to people by nature or God and cannot be taken away. These are called:
(1) Legal rights
(2) Cultural rights
(3) Natural rights
(4) Economic rights
= 3
~ Natural rights were held to exist before any government. Thinkers of that time listed life, liberty and property as natural rights that rulers had no power to violate.

Q 3 | pol | Political Theory
Which three rights were commonly listed as natural rights by these early theorists?
(1) Education, health and work
(2) Life, liberty and property
(3) Vote, contest elections and form parties
(4) Language, culture and religion
= 2
~ Life, liberty and property were seen as inalienable natural rights. Later, the idea of human rights widened the list to include social and economic needs.

Q 4 | pol | Political Theory
The Universal Declaration of Human Rights was adopted by the United Nations in:
(1) 1919
(2) 1935
(3) 1945
(4) 1948
= 4
~ The UN General Assembly adopted the Universal Declaration of Human Rights on 10 December 1948. It recognises rights that belong to all human beings, irrespective of nationality, race or gender.

Q 5 | pol | Political Theory
The idea of "human rights", as discussed in the chapter, is based on the belief that:
(1) Only citizens of rich countries have rights
(2) Rights depend on one's caste or religion
(3) Rights exist only if a king grants them
(4) All human beings, simply by being human, are entitled to certain rights and should be treated with dignity
= 4
~ Human rights are universal. They have been used by oppressed groups around the world to challenge discrimination and demand equal treatment.

Q 6 | pol | Political Theory
Rights that are written in the Constitution or recognised in laws of the state, so that they can be enforced, are called:
(1) Natural rights
(2) Legal rights
(3) Moral claims only
(4) Customary privileges
= 2
~ Legal recognition gives rights a firm basis: courts can enforce them. The Indian Constitution, for example, lists Fundamental Rights that citizens can enforce in the courts.

Q 7 | pol | Political Theory
The right to vote, the right to contest elections and the right to form political parties are examples of:
(1) Cultural rights
(2) Economic rights
(3) Political rights
(4) Natural rights only
= 3
~ Political rights let citizens take part in the political process. They are usually supported by civil liberties such as freedom of speech and the right to a fair trial.

Q 8 | pol | Political Theory
Economic rights, according to the chapter, are rights related to:
(1) Meeting basic needs like food, shelter, clothing and health, and a decent livelihood
(2) Voting in elections
(3) Using one's mother tongue
(4) Practising one's religion
= 1
~ Political rights mean little without basic economic security. Many democracies provide for minimum wages, work schemes, food security or unemployment relief.

Q 9 | pol | Political Theory
The right of minorities to be educated in their own language and to preserve their own culture is an example of:
(1) Political rights
(2) Cultural rights
(3) Economic rights
(4) Natural rights only
= 2
~ Cultural rights recognise that people's identities are tied to their language and culture. Many democracies accept such rights to protect minority groups.

Q 10 | pol | Political Theory
"Rights not only place obligations on the state; they also place obligations on us." Which of the following is an example of such an obligation of the individual?
(1) Respecting the rights of others, even when we disagree with them
(2) Ignoring the law when it is inconvenient
(3) Taking away others' freedom of speech
(4) Using public resources only for oneself
= 1
~ If I claim freedom of speech, I must accept the same freedom for others. Rights come with responsibilities: to respect others' rights and to care for common goods.

Q 11 | pol | Political Theory
Why does the chapter say rights place an obligation on us to think about future generations?
(1) Because future generations vote today
(2) Because the Constitution says so in Article 21 only
(3) Because rights end after one generation
(4) Because using resources like air, water and forests carelessly would harm the rights of people who come after us
= 4
~ The environment we leave behind will shape the rights of future generations to a healthy life. This responsibility is especially clear in debates about pollution and climate change.

Q 12 | pol | Political Theory
Why does the chapter say that rights often need to be balanced against each other?
(1) Because only one right can exist at a time
(2) Because rights are not valuable
(3) Because sometimes rights conflict, for example the right to free expression and the right to privacy, and limits must be worked out
(4) Because courts never deal with rights
= 3
~ A journalist's freedom to report may clash with a person's privacy. Democracies try to balance such rights, often through courts, instead of allowing one to wipe out the other.

Q 13 | pol | Political Theory
Which thinker's idea of human dignity held that people should be treated as ends in themselves, never only as means?
(1) Karl Marx
(2) Thomas Hobbes
(3) Immanuel Kant
(4) Niccolò Machiavelli
= 3
~ Kant argued that every person has dignity and deserves respect. This idea underlies the modern view that rights protect each individual's dignity.

Q 14 | pol | Political Theory
In recent years, new rights have been claimed and recognised, as discussed in the chapter. Which of the following is an example?
(1) The right of kings to rule
(2) The right to own slaves
(3) The right to ignore laws
(4) Rights such as the right to information, or the right to clean air and water
= 4
~ As societies face new problems, people claim new rights. In India, laws on the right to information and court judgments on the environment show how the list of rights grows.

Q 15 | pol | Political Theory
Why is it important that rights are recognised by the state, according to the chapter?
(1) Because legal recognition allows rights to be protected and enforced, while also restraining the state itself
(2) Because the state can then abolish them easily
(3) Because only the state can feel rights
(4) Because rights do not need any protection
= 1
~ When rights are in the constitution and laws, people can approach courts to protect them. Rights also tell the state what it must not do, limiting its power over citizens.

Q 16 | pol | Political Theory
Rights such as freedom of expression, freedom of religion and the right to a fair trial, which protect individuals against the state and others, are often called:
(1) Economic entitlements
(2) Civil liberties
(3) Cultural traditions
(4) Natural resources
= 2
~ Civil liberties protect individuals in their daily lives against arbitrary action. They support political rights by allowing people to discuss, organise and dissent freely.

Q 17 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following claims would the chapter NOT treat as a right?
(1) The claim to be educated
(2) The claim to wear clothes of a particular brand
(3) The claim to be free from torture
(4) The claim to express one's views
= 2
~ A right must be important for human dignity or well-being and be justifiable to others. Wanting a brand of clothes is a preference, not a right.

Q 18 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following is the main source of the idea of human rights today?
(1) A belief that rights depend on a person's wealth
(2) A belief that only governments can define what rights are
(3) A belief that rights come from a ruler's kindness
(4) The belief that every human being, by being human, deserves certain conditions to live with dignity
= 4
~ Modern human rights rest on universal human dignity, not on nationality, caste, class or the goodwill of rulers.

Q 19 | pol | Political Theory
Why does the chapter say rights are important in a democracy?
(1) Because they protect individuals and minorities from the tyranny of the majority and allow citizens to take part as equals
(2) Because they allow the majority to do anything it wants
(3) Because they remove the need for elections
(4) Because they apply only to elected leaders
= 1
~ Democracy is not only majority rule. Rights guarantee that even those who lose an election or belong to small groups keep their freedoms and dignity.

Q 20 | pol | Political Theory
The right to education shows how a claim can become a legal right. In India, free and compulsory education for children aged 6 to 14 became a Fundamental Right through:
(1) The 42nd Amendment, 1976
(2) The 44th Amendment, 1978
(3) The 86th Amendment, 2002
(4) The 73rd Amendment, 1992
= 3
~ The 86th Amendment added Article 21A, and the Right to Education Act (2009) put it into effect. Education had earlier been only a Directive Principle.
