# NCERT Class 11 Political Science, Political Theory, Chapter 1: Political Theory: An Introduction
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | pol | Political Theory
According to the chapter, what does political theory mainly deal with?
(1) The day-to-day working of political parties only
(2) The election results of different countries
(3) The biographies of political leaders
(4) The ideas and principles that shape constitutions, governments and social life, such as freedom, equality and justice
= 4
~ Political theory examines the values that inform public life, such as freedom, equality, justice, democracy and secularism, and clarifies what they mean and why they matter.

Q 2 | pol | Political Theory
The chapter argues that politics is:
(1) Only a dirty game of self-interested leaders
(2) Only about what governments do
(3) An important part of social life, concerned with how a society is organised and how collective decisions are made, in which all of us take part
(4) Something ordinary people should avoid
= 3
~ People often see politics as manipulation, but the chapter treats it as the way societies decide on common goals and settle conflicts, through governments and also through citizens' activities and movements.

Q 3 | pol | Political Theory
Which thinker argued that freedom is a fundamental right of human beings, as mentioned in the chapter?
(1) Jean-Jacques Rousseau
(2) Karl Marx
(3) Mahatma Gandhi
(4) Aristotle
= 1
~ The chapter mentions Rousseau as having argued that freedom is the fundamental right of human beings, and Marx as having argued that equality is as crucial as freedom.

Q 4 | pol | Political Theory
Which thinker is mentioned in the chapter as arguing that equality is as important as freedom?
(1) John Locke
(2) Thomas Hobbes
(3) Karl Marx
(4) Plato
= 3
~ Marx stressed economic equality, arguing that freedom means little without it. Political theory looks at how such thinkers defined key ideas.

Q 5 | pol | Political Theory
Which Indian leader discussed the meaning of true freedom, or swaraj, in his book Hind Swaraj?
(1) B.R. Ambedkar
(2) Mahatma Gandhi
(3) Jawaharlal Nehru
(4) Rabindranath Tagore
= 2
~ In Hind Swaraj (1909) Gandhi explained swaraj as self-rule, including rule over oneself, not just the end of British rule.

Q 6 | pol | Political Theory
The chapter mentions that Dr. B.R. Ambedkar argued that the Scheduled Castes should be treated as:
(1) Part of the general category with no special provisions
(2) A separate nation
(3) Voters only in local elections
(4) Minorities entitled to special protection
= 4
~ Ambedkar argued that the Scheduled Castes were a minority who needed special protection. Several of his ideas found a place in the Constitution.

Q 7 | pol | Political Theory
Why does the chapter say we should study political theory?
1. Because we will all be citizens who vote and take part in public life
2. Because it helps us think clearly about our own ideas and feelings on political matters
3. Because it is useful for future politicians, bureaucrats, lawyers and activists
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ The chapter gives all three reasons. Political theory also encourages us to examine prejudices and to think about issues like justice in a reasoned way.

Q 8 | pol | Political Theory
When political theory examines what "freedom" means in different contexts, it is mainly engaged in:
(1) Clarifying the meaning of concepts and examining arguments for and against them
(2) Collecting election data
(3) Writing new laws
(4) Running political parties
= 1
~ Political theory tests ideas by asking what they mean in different situations, for example what freedom of expression includes, and whether the arguments behind them hold.

Q 9 | pol | Political Theory
Questions such as whether parents should be free to stop a daughter's education or decide her marriage show that:
(1) Political theory has no relation to everyday life
(2) Only courts can decide such questions
(3) Questions of freedom and equality arise in our families and everyday lives, not only in government
(4) These matters are fixed by tradition and cannot be debated
= 3
~ Political ideas are tested in daily situations in families, schools and workplaces, not just in Parliament. This is why political theory is relevant to everyone.

Q 10 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following is NOT one of the concepts the chapter says political theory studies?
(1) Freedom
(2) Equality
(3) Justice
(4) Photosynthesis
= 4
~ Political theory studies concepts like freedom, equality, justice, rights, citizenship, nationalism, democracy and secularism. Photosynthesis is a topic of biology.

Q 11 | pol | Political Theory
According to the chapter, why can't political ideas be studied like the laws of physics?
(1) Because political ideas have no meaning
(2) Because they are decided only by courts
(3) Because they never change
(4) Because they involve human values and judgements, and people may reasonably interpret them differently in different contexts
= 4
~ Concepts like equality or freedom are not measured in a laboratory. Their meaning is debated, which is why political theory examines arguments and reasons.

Q 12 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following is an example of "putting political theory into practice", as discussed in the chapter?
(1) Memorising definitions of freedom from a textbook
(2) Asking what equality requires in a concrete case, such as whether reservations or special help for some groups is fair
(3) Avoiding all discussion of politics
(4) Counting the number of political parties
= 2
~ The chapter asks students to apply concepts to real debates, such as how equality of opportunity can be achieved, and to judge arguments on each side.

Q 13 | pol | Political Theory
In a democracy, the meaning of rights and freedoms keeps evolving. What mainly causes this evolution?
(1) New problems and struggles that lead people to reinterpret ideals such as freedom and equality
(2) Only changes in technology
(3) Orders of the President
(4) Changes in the weather
= 1
~ Movements, court cases and debates keep widening the meaning of ideals. For example, the right to life in India now includes the right to livelihood and a clean environment.

Q 14 | pol | Political Theory
Political theory traces its origins to thinkers of which ancient civilisation, where Socrates and Plato discussed what justice is?
(1) Ancient Egypt
(2) Ancient China
(3) Ancient Greece
(4) Mesopotamia
= 3
~ Socrates and Plato in ancient Athens asked what justice is and what a good society should be like. Their questions are still central to political theory.

Q 15 | pol | Political Theory
Kautilya's Arthashastra, one of the earliest Indian texts on politics, is mainly about:
(1) Astronomy
(2) Classical dance
(3) Medicine
(4) Statecraft and governance
= 4
~ The Arthashastra discusses how a state should be run: administration, law, economy, diplomacy and war.

Q 16 | pol | Political Theory
"Political theory is not just about interpreting the world; it also helps us to examine our own ideas and opinions critically." This view suggests that political theory helps citizens to:
(1) Think about issues logically and test their own beliefs and prejudices
(2) Accept every opinion they hear without question
(3) Avoid voting
(4) Depend entirely on experts
= 1
~ Studying arguments about freedom, equality and justice makes us more reflective, able to see other points of view and to defend our own with reasons.

Q 17 | pol | Political Theory
The Indian Constitution reflects certain ideals examined by political theory. Which of the following is found in the Preamble?
(1) Monarchy and hereditary rule
(2) Justice, liberty, equality and fraternity
(3) A single official religion
(4) Rule by experts only
= 2
~ The Preamble promises justice, liberty, equality and fraternity to all citizens. Political theory helps us understand what these ideals mean and require.

Q 18 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following best describes the relationship between politics and political theory, according to the chapter?
(1) Political theory is about ideas that guide political life, while politics is the activity of making collective decisions; theory helps us judge and improve politics
(2) They are unrelated
(3) Politics is only about theory, with no practice
(4) Political theory replaces the need for politics
= 1
~ Political life is shaped by ideas, and ideas are tested in political life. Theory helps citizens and leaders reflect on what goals society should pursue and how.

Q 19 | pol | Political Theory
Which of these questions is a typical question of political theory?
(1) Which party won the most seats in the last election?
(2) How many states does India have?
(3) What is a just distribution of resources in a society?
(4) When is the next session of Parliament?
= 3
~ Political theory asks normative questions about what ought to be, like what is just or fair. Questions of facts and dates belong to other fields of study.

Q 20 | pol | Political Theory
According to the chapter, why are concepts like equality and freedom not "settled once and for all"?
(1) Because no one has ever written about them
(2) Because new situations keep raising new questions about their meaning, which each generation has to debate
(3) Because courts have banned discussion of them
(4) Because they are written only in foreign languages
= 2
~ Ideas such as freedom and equality are constantly reinterpreted as society changes, for example with new technology or new social movements. Political theory keeps them under examination.
