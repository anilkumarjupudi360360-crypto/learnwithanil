Q 1 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The average atmospheric pressure at mean sea level, as given in the chapter, is about:
(1) 913.2 mb
(2) 1,013.2 mb
(3) 1,113.2 mb
(4) 1,213.2 mb
= 2
~ Mean sea level pressure is about 1,013.2 millibars. Pressure is measured with a mercury barometer or an aneroid barometer.

Q 2 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
In the lower atmosphere, pressure decreases rapidly with height, at about:
(1) 1 mb for every 10 m
(2) 1 mb for every 100 m
(3) 10 mb for every 10 m
(4) 1 mb for every 1 km
= 1
~ Pressure falls by about 1 mb for each 10 m increase in elevation in the lower atmosphere, though the rate is not always the same.

Q 3 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The vertical pressure gradient force is much larger than the horizontal one. Why, then, do we not experience strong upward winds?
(1) Because it is balanced by the nearly equal but opposite gravitational force
(2) Because the Coriolis force cancels it
(3) Because friction stops all vertical movement
(4) Because the air has no weight
= 1
~ Gravity balances the vertical pressure gradient force, so strong upward winds do not develop.

Q 4 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
On a weather map, closely spaced isobars indicate:
(1) Calm conditions
(2) A steep pressure gradient and strong winds
(3) Uniform temperature
(4) Heavy snowfall
= 2
~ Isobars join places of equal pressure. When they are close together, pressure changes quickly over a short distance, which drives strong winds.

Q 5 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following pairs of pressure belts and their approximate latitudes:
1. Subtropical high pressure — around 30° North and South
2. Sub-polar low pressure — around 45° North and South
3. Equatorial low pressure — near the equator
Which of the pairs given above are correctly matched?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 3
~ The sub-polar lows lie around 60° North and South; polar highs lie near the poles. The belts shift with the apparent movement of the sun.

Q 6 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which three forces together determine the velocity and direction of wind, according to the chapter?
(1) Pressure gradient force, frictional force and Coriolis force
(2) Gravitational force, magnetic force and tidal force
(3) Centrifugal force, buoyancy and friction only
(4) Pressure gradient force, magnetic force and gravity
= 1
~ Wind moves from high to low pressure (pressure gradient force), is slowed by friction near the surface and is deflected by the earth's rotation (Coriolis force).

Q 7 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements about the Coriolis force:
1. It deflects winds to the right in the Northern Hemisphere and to the left in the Southern Hemisphere.
2. It is maximum at the equator and absent at the poles.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 1
~ Statement 2 is reversed: the Coriolis force is directly proportional to latitude, maximum at the poles and absent at the equator. It was described by a French scientist in 1844.

Q 8 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Why are tropical cyclones not formed near the equator?
(1) The sea surface is too cold there
(2) The Coriolis force is zero there, so a low pressure gets filled instead of intensifying
(3) There is no water vapour at the equator
(4) Winds at the equator are too strong
= 2
~ Without Coriolis deflection, air flows straight into the low and fills it, so no rotating cyclonic system can develop.

Q 9 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
When isobars are straight and there is no friction, the pressure gradient force is balanced by the Coriolis force and the wind blows parallel to the isobars. Such a wind is called a:
(1) Trade wind
(2) Monsoon wind
(3) Katabatic wind
(4) Geostrophic wind
= 4
~ Geostrophic winds occur in the upper atmosphere, about 2–3 km above the surface, where friction no longer acts.

Q 10 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
In the Northern Hemisphere, the winds around a cyclone (low pressure centre) circulate:
(1) Clockwise
(2) Straight outward
(3) Anticlockwise
(4) Straight inward without turning
= 3
~ In the Northern Hemisphere, cyclones turn anticlockwise and anticyclones clockwise; the directions are reversed in the Southern Hemisphere.

Q 11 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Air rises at the Inter Tropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ) and moves polewards, sinking around 30° latitude. This cell of circulation in the tropics is called the:
(1) Ferrel cell
(2) Walker cell
(3) Polar cell
(4) Hadley cell
= 4
~ Converging air rises at the ITCZ up to about 14 km, moves polewards and sinks near 30°. The Ferrel cell lies in the middle latitudes and the polar cell near the poles.

Q 12 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The appearance of warm water off the coast of Peru, replacing the cool Peruvian current, is known as:
(1) La Niña
(2) El Niño
(3) Southern Oscillation
(4) Westerly jet
= 2
~ El Niño is linked to pressure changes over the central Pacific and Australia (the Southern Oscillation); together they are called ENSO.

Q 13 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
In years of strong ENSO, which of the following weather variations are mentioned in the chapter?
1. Heavy rainfall on the arid west coast of South America
2. Drought in Australia and sometimes in India
3. Floods in China
Select the correct answer:
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ All three are listed. ENSO is therefore closely monitored for long-range weather forecasting.

Q 14 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
During the day, land heats faster than the sea, so the pressure over land is lower. The resulting wind blowing from sea to land is called a:
(1) Land breeze
(2) Sea breeze
(3) Valley breeze
(4) Mountain wind
= 2
~ By day the sea breeze blows onto the land; at night the land cools faster and the land breeze blows towards the sea.

Q 15 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
At night, cool and dense air from high plateaus and ice fields drains down into the valleys. Such a cold wind is called a:
(1) Trade wind
(2) Sea breeze
(3) Katabatic wind
(4) Geostrophic wind
= 3
~ At night mountain slopes cool and the dense air descends as the mountain wind; cool air draining from plateaus and ice fields is called katabatic wind.

Q 16 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Match the air mass types with their codes:
A. Maritime tropical — 1. cP
B. Continental tropical — 2. mT
C. Continental polar — 3. cT
D. Maritime polar — 4. mP
Select the correct answer:
(1) A-2, B-3, C-1, D-4
(2) A-3, B-2, C-4, D-1
(3) A-2, B-1, C-3, D-4
(4) A-4, B-3, C-1, D-2
= 1
~ "m" stands for maritime and "c" for continental; "T" for tropical, "P" for polar and "A" for arctic. The fifth type is continental arctic (cA).

Q 17 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
When a cold air mass overtakes a warm air mass and lifts it completely above the land surface, the front formed is called:
(1) Stationary front
(2) Warm front
(3) Cold front
(4) Occluded front
= 4
~ The four types of fronts are cold, warm, stationary and occluded. Fronts occur in the middle latitudes and bring sharp changes in temperature and pressure.

Q 18 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Match the regions with the local names of tropical cyclones:
A. Indian Ocean — 1. Typhoons
B. Atlantic Ocean — 2. Willy-willies
C. Western Pacific and South China Sea — 3. Hurricanes
D. Western Australia — 4. Cyclones
Select the correct answer:
(1) A-2, B-3, C-1, D-4
(2) A-3, B-4, C-2, D-1
(3) A-4, B-1, C-3, D-2
(4) A-4, B-3, C-1, D-2
= 4
~ The same violent tropical storm is called a cyclone in the Indian Ocean, a hurricane in the Atlantic, a typhoon in the western Pacific and South China Sea, and a willy-willy in western Australia.

Q 19 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which one of the following is NOT a favourable condition for the formation of tropical cyclones?
(1) Large sea surface with temperature higher than 27°C
(2) Presence of the Coriolis force
(3) Large variations in vertical wind speed
(4) A pre-existing weak low-pressure area
= 3
~ Tropical cyclones need small, not large, variations in vertical wind speed. Upper divergence above the system is also needed.

Q 20 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements comparing extra-tropical and tropical cyclones:
1. Extra-tropical cyclones have a clear frontal system, which tropical cyclones lack.
2. Extra-tropical cyclones move from east to west, while tropical cyclones generally move from west to east.
3. Tropical cyclones originate only over seas and dissipate after reaching land.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 3
~ Extra-tropical cyclones move from west to east and tropical cyclones generally from east to west. Extra-tropical cyclones have fronts and cover larger areas; tropical cyclones weaken over land.
