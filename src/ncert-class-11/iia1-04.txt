Q 1 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
From the second century BCE, which dynasties held southern and western India, according to the chapter?
(1) The Satvahanas, Ikshvakus, Abhiras and Vakatakas
(2) The Shungas, Kanvas, Kushanas and Guptas
(3) The Cholas, Cheras and Pandyas
(4) The Palas, Senas and Pratiharas
= 1
~ The Shungas, Kanvas, Kushanas and Guptas controlled the north and parts of central India, while the Satvahanas, Ikshvakus, Abhiras and Vakatakas controlled southern and western India.

Q 2 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The sculptures of the Bharhut stupa are now displayed in:
(1) The National Museum, New Delhi
(2) The Patna Museum
(3) The Indian Museum, Kolkata
(4) The Government Museum, Chennai
= 3
~ The Bharhut sculptures are in the Indian Museum, Kolkata. Bharhut figures are tall like the Mauryan Yaksha images, carved in low relief with neat linear contours.

Q 3 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The Ruru Jataka panel at Bharhut shows:
(1) The elephant Bodhisattva giving away his tusks
(2) The Bodhisattva deer rescuing a drowning man, while the king aims an arrow at the deer
(3) A monkey king making a bridge of his body
(4) King Sibi giving his own flesh to save a dove
= 2
~ In the Ruru Jataka the Bodhisattva deer saves a man; the king, who wants the deer, aims an arrow at it. The panel groups the events by place, a typical Bharhut way of narrating.

Q 4 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
In the early Bharhut sculptures the hands are shown folded and the feet in an awkward position. Why?
(1) A religious rule forbade showing hands open
(2) The artists were untrained
(3) The stone was too brittle to carve
(4) The shallow carving of the picture surface did not allow hands and feet to project
= 4
~ Because the carving was shallow, hands and feet could not project, hence the folded hands and awkward feet. Later sculptors carved deeper, giving more volume and a naturalistic look.

Q 5 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Which site in western India has the biggest rock-cut chaitya hall?
(1) Bhaja
(2) Kondivite

(4) Karla

Q 6 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Which gateway (torana) of Stupa-1 at Sanchi was built first?
(1) The northern gateway
(2) The southern gateway
(3) The eastern gateway
(4) The western gateway
= 2
~ The southern gateway was built first; an Ashokan pillar with a lion capital also stands on the southern side.

Q 7 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Stupa-3 at Sanchi is believed to hold the relics of:
(1) The Buddha himself
(2) Ten lesser arhats
(3) Sariputta and Mahamougalayana
(4) Emperor Ashoka
= 3
~ Stupa-1 is presumed to hold the Buddha's relics, Stupa-2 those of ten lesser arhats, and Stupa-3 those of Sariputta and Mahamougalayana.

Q 8 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
On the toranas of Sanchi Stupa-1, which figures are carved as supports to the lowest horizontal bar?
(1) Shalabhanjikas
(2) Dvarapalas
(3) Nagas
(4) Garudas
= 1
~ Each torana has two vertical pillars and three horizontal bars; shalabhanjika figures support the lowest bar. The gateways are carved with events from the Buddha's life and Jatakas.

Q 9 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The early images of the Buddha made at Mathura were modelled on which earlier figures?
(1) Hellenistic statues of Apollo
(2) Painted Bodhisattvas of Ajanta
(3) Yaksha images
(4) Chinese bronzes
= 3
~ Early Mathura Buddha images are modelled on the earlier Yaksha images, while the Gandhara Buddhas show Hellenistic features.

Q 10 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The seated Buddha from the Katra mound, with Padmapani and Vajrapani as attendants and a large halo, belongs to which school of sculpture?
(1) Gandhara
(2) Sarnath
(3) Amaravati
(4) Mathura
= 4
~ The Katra mound Buddha (second century CE) is a Mathura image: seated in padmasana, right hand in abhayamudra, on a lion throne, with an ushnisha and two attendant Bodhisattvas.

Q 11 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The Gandhara style of sculpture combines which traditions?
(1) Chola and Pallava traditions
(2) Bactrian, Parthian and local Gandhara traditions, with Hellenistic features
(3) Mauryan polish and Gupta drapery only
(4) Persian Safavid and Mughal traditions
= 2
~ Gandhara art blends Bactrian, Parthian and local traditions, which explains its Hellenistic look, seen in the Taxila Buddha head with curly hair and sharp outlines.

Q 12 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The largest rock-cut stupa in India, of the fourth–fifth centuries CE, is at:
(1) Anakapalle, near Visakhapatnam
(2) Guntapalle, Eluru district
(3) Bairat, Rajasthan
(4) Karla, Maharashtra
= 1
~ The huge rock-cut stupa at Anakapalle near Visakhapatnam is the largest in India. Guntapalle is known for its circular chaitya cave.

Q 13 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Consider the following statements about the late fifth-century seated Buddha from Sarnath:
1. The transparent drapery covers both shoulders.
2. The halo is heavily ornamented at the centre.
3. A wheel and deer on the throne panel show the Dhammachakrapravartana.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 3 only
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1
~ Statement 2 is wrong: the halo is plain at the centre, with floral motifs on the throne back. The image is in Chunar sandstone and has a slender body, round face and half-closed eyes.

Q 15 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
How many caves are there at Ajanta?
(1) 34
(2) 12
(3) 108
(4) 29
= 4
~ Ajanta in Aurangabad district, Maharashtra, has 29 caves; four are chaitya caves (9, 10, 19 and 26). Ellora has 34 caves and Kanheri 108.

Q 16 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The Mara Vijaya, the largest sculptural panel at Ajanta, is in which cave?
(1) Cave 1
(2) Cave 26
(3) Cave 10
(4) Cave 16
= 2
~ Cave 26, a chaitya patronised by Buddhabhadra, has the Mahaparinibbana image and, on its right wall, the Mara Vijaya panel.

Q 17 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Varahadeva, who patronised Cave 16 at Ajanta, was a minister of which king?
(1) Chandragupta II
(2) Pulakesin II
(3) Harshavardhana
(4) Harishena of the Vakataka dynasty
= 4
~ Varahadeva was a minister of the Vakataka king Harishena; Upendragupta, patron of Caves 17 to 20, was Harishena's feudatory.

Q 18 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Consider the following statements about Ellora:
1. It has 34 caves with Buddhist, Brahmanical and Jain monuments.
2. Cave 12 is a triple-storeyed Buddhist cave.
3. Cave 16, Kailash Leni, is a monolithic rock-cut temple.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1, 2 and 3
(4) 1 and 3 only
= 3
~ All are correct. Ellora's caves date from the fifth to the eleventh century; Cave 14 is the only double-storeyed Brahmanical cave, and the Jain caves come from the ninth century.

Q 19 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Cave 4 at Bagh, which has the most significant paintings there, is popularly known as:
(1) Chitrashala
(2) Rang Mahal
(3) Sheesh Mahal
(4) Lomas Rishi
= 2
~ Bagh Cave 4 is called Rang Mahal, the Palace of Colours. Most Bagh murals were removed in 1982 and are now in the Archaeological Museum, Gwalior.

Q 20 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The three visible faces of the Maheshamurti in the main cave at Elephanta represent:
(1) Shiva at the centre, Bhairava (angry, in profile) and Uma (feminine)
(2) Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva
(3) Shiva, Parvati and Ganesha
(4) Shiva, Nandi and Kartikeya
= 1
~ The early sixth-century Maheshamurti shows the central face as Shiva, an angry profile as Shiva-Bhairava and a feminine face as Uma. Ardhanarishwara and Gangadhara panels are also in the cave.
