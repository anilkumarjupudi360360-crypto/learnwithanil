# NCERT Class 11 Political Science, Indian Constitution at Work, Chapter 9: Constitution as a Living Document
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Why does the chapter call the Indian Constitution a "living document"?
(1) Because it is rewritten completely after every general election
(2) Because it can be changed by the President alone
(3) Because it has no fixed text
(4) Because it responds to changing situations through amendments and judicial interpretation while keeping its core principles
= 4
~ Like a living being, the Constitution adapts to new needs: through amendments by Parliament and through the way courts read it. Its basic framework has nevertheless stayed the same since 1950.

Q 2 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The procedure for amending the Indian Constitution is laid down in:
(1) Article 32
(2) Article 356
(3) Article 368
(4) Article 370
= 3
~ Article 368 gives Parliament the power to amend the Constitution and sets out the procedure. Some provisions can also be changed by an ordinary law, as the Constitution itself allows.

Q 3 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Most amendments under Article 368 require a "special majority" in each House of Parliament. This means:
(1) A majority of the total membership of the House and at least two-thirds of the members present and voting
(2) A simple majority of members present
(3) Three-fourths of the members present
(4) Unanimous support of the House
= 1
~ The special majority has two parts: more than half of the House's total strength must support the bill, and the supporters must be at least two-thirds of those present and voting.

Q 4 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Amendments relating to which of the following also need ratification by the legislatures of at least half of the states?
(1) The salaries of Members of Parliament
(2) The age of retirement of civil servants
(3) The distribution of powers between the Union and the states, and the representation of states in Parliament
(4) The procedure of Question Hour
= 3
~ Provisions that affect the federal structure, such as the lists of powers, the election of the President, the representation of states in Parliament and the powers of the courts, need approval by half the states as well.

Q 5 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which of the following is NOT part of the amendment procedure in India?
(1) Special majority in both Houses of Parliament
(2) Ratification by half the states for certain provisions
(3) A referendum in which all voters vote on the amendment
(4) The President's assent
= 3
~ Unlike some countries such as Switzerland, India does not use a referendum for amendments. There is also no separate body for amending the Constitution; Parliament does it.

Q 6 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
After an amendment bill is passed by Parliament (and ratified by the states where needed), the President:
(1) Has to give assent; there is no option to withhold it or return it
(2) Can send it back for reconsideration like an ordinary bill
(3) Must refer it to the Supreme Court
(4) Can hold a referendum on it
= 1
~ Since the 24th Amendment (1971) the President must give assent to a constitutional amendment bill. The President cannot return it or keep it pending.

Q 7 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The chapter groups the many amendments to the Constitution into categories. Which category does an amendment raising the retirement age of High Court judges from 60 to 62 fall into?
(1) Amendments due to differing interpretations
(2) Amendments through political consensus
(3) Controversial amendments of the Emergency period
(4) Amendments of a technical or administrative nature
= 4
~ Such amendments (here the 15th Amendment, 1963) only clarify or adjust details, such as salaries and retirement ages, without changing basic provisions.

Q 8 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which of the following amendments are examples of changes made through broad political consensus?
1. The anti-defection law (52nd Amendment)
2. Lowering the voting age to 18 (61st Amendment)
3. The 73rd and 74th Amendments on local governments
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ The chapter treats all three as amendments that had the support of most parties, reflecting a shared view of what the political system needed.

Q 9 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Many amendments between 1970 and 1975 arose from a conflict between Parliament and the judiciary. What was the conflict mainly about?
(1) Differing interpretations of the Constitution, especially on Parliament's power to amend Fundamental Rights such as the right to property
(2) The location of the Supreme Court
(3) The number of judges
(4) The language of the Constitution
= 1
~ Parliament wanted to amend Fundamental Rights to carry out land reforms and nationalisation; the courts resisted. Parliament passed amendments to overcome court judgments, and the courts in turn reviewed them.

Q 10 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which amendment, passed during the Emergency, made very wide changes to the Constitution and is often called a "mini constitution"?
(1) 24th Amendment
(2) 38th Amendment
(3) 42nd Amendment
(4) 44th Amendment
= 3
~ The 42nd Amendment (1976) changed many parts of the Constitution, including the Preamble, the term of the Lok Sabha and the powers of the courts. The chapter treats the 38th, 39th and 42nd Amendments as the most controversial.

Q 11 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
After the Emergency, many changes made by the 42nd Amendment were undone mainly by:
(1) The 43rd and 44th Amendments
(2) The 52nd Amendment
(3) The 61st Amendment
(4) The 73rd Amendment
= 1
~ The Janata government's 43rd (1977) and 44th (1978) Amendments reversed many Emergency-era changes and added safeguards, for example on proclaiming a national emergency.

Q 12 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The words "socialist" and "secular" were added to the Preamble by:
(1) The 24th Amendment
(2) The 42nd Amendment
(3) The 44th Amendment
(4) The 86th Amendment
= 2
~ The 42nd Amendment (1976) added "socialist", "secular" and "integrity" to the Preamble. The chapter notes that the principles behind them were already part of the Constitution.

Q 13 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The "basic structure" doctrine, laid down in the Kesavananda Bharati case (1973), means that:
(1) Parliament cannot amend the Constitution at all
(2) Only the Supreme Court can amend the Constitution
(3) The states can veto any amendment
(4) Parliament can amend any part of the Constitution, but not in a way that destroys its basic structure
= 4
~ The Court balanced the two sides: Parliament has wide power to amend, but features such as the supremacy of the Constitution, democracy, secularism, federalism and judicial review cannot be destroyed.

Q 14 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Who decides what is part of the "basic structure" of the Constitution?
(1) Parliament
(2) The judiciary, case by case
(3) The President
(4) The Election Commission
= 2
~ The Constitution does not list the basic structure. The courts identify its elements while deciding cases, which gives the judiciary an important role in shaping the Constitution.

Q 15 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The chapter says the Constitution has been kept flexible as well as rigid. Which statement best describes this balance?
(1) Every part can be changed by a simple majority
(2) No part can ever be changed
(3) Some parts can be changed by simple majority, most need a special majority, and federal provisions also need state ratification
(4) Only the Supreme Court can change any part
= 3
~ The framers made some provisions easy to change, while protecting the most important ones with a special majority and, for federal features, the consent of the states. This makes the Constitution adaptable but not easy to tamper with.

Q 16 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which of the following can be changed by Parliament through a simple majority, without following the special procedure of Article 368?
(1) The Fundamental Rights
(2) The election of the President
(3) The powers of the Supreme Court
(4) The formation of new states and changes in the boundaries of existing states
= 4
~ Under Articles 2 to 4, laws on admitting or forming states and changing boundaries are passed by a simple majority and are not treated as amendments under Article 368.

Q 17 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The judiciary has contributed to the Constitution being a "living document" mainly by:
(1) Interpreting its provisions, such as the right to life, in new ways to meet changing needs
(2) Refusing to hear constitutional cases
(3) Electing the members of Parliament
(4) Writing new articles into the Constitution itself
= 1
~ Courts have widened the meaning of rights, for example reading the right to life to include livelihood, education and a clean environment. Such interpretation keeps the text relevant without formal amendment.

Q 18 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which body was appointed by the Government of India in 2000 to review the working of the Constitution?
(1) The Sarkaria Commission
(2) The National Commission to Review the Working of the Constitution, headed by Justice M.N. Venkatachaliah
(3) The States Reorganisation Commission
(4) The Law Commission headed by Justice Ranganath Misra
= 2
~ The Venkatachaliah Commission reviewed the Constitution's working and suggested changes within its basic framework. It was not asked to write a new constitution.

Q 19 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Why does the chapter say that frequent amendments do not mean the Constitution is weak?
(1) Because most amendments only changed the names of states
(2) Because all amendments were struck down by courts
(3) Because amendments are needed only in a dictatorship
(4) Because many amendments were technical, many came from political consensus, and the basic framework survived the controversial ones
= 4
~ A large number of amendments reflects a constitution that adapts, not one that is weak. The core values and institutions have stayed intact, including after the Emergency-era changes.

Q 20 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which feature of the Indian Constitution keeps the balance between the authority of Parliament and the limits placed on it?
(1) The absence of judicial review
(2) Parliament can amend the Constitution, but the judiciary can review amendments against the basic structure
(3) The President's power to dissolve the Supreme Court
(4) The supremacy of the state legislatures
= 2
~ Elected representatives can change the Constitution, while the courts ensure the changes do not destroy its foundations. The chapter sees this balance as a mark of political maturity.
