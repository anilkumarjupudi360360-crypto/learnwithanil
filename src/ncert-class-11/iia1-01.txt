# NCERT Class 11 Fine Arts (Art & Culture), An Introduction to Indian Art Part I, Chapter 1: Prehistoric Rock Paintings
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The first discovery of rock paintings in India was made in 1867–68 by which archaeologist?
(1) V.S. Wakankar
(2) Archibold Carlleyle
(3) Alexander Cunningham
(4) John Marshall
= 2
~ The chapter credits Archibold Carlleyle with the first discovery of rock paintings in India, in 1867–68. V.S. Wakankar discovered Bhimbetka much later, in 1957–58.

Q 2 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Consider the following statements about the discovery of rock paintings in India:
1. The first discovery in India came about twelve years before the discovery of Altamira in Spain.
2. Cockburn, Anderson, Mitra and Ghosh were among the early archaeologists who found many sites in the subcontinent.
3. The earliest paintings reported in India belong to the Upper Palaeolithic times.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ All three are from the chapter: Carlleyle's discovery (1867–68) came twelve years before Altamira, Cockburn, Anderson, Mitra and Ghosh found many more sites, and India's earliest paintings are reported from the Upper Palaeolithic.

Q 3 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The prehistoric rock shelters of Lakhudiyar in Uttarakhand lie on the banks of which river?
(1) Suyal
(2) Kosi
(3) Ramganga
(4) Sarju
= 1
~ Lakhudiyar's painted rock shelters are on the banks of the river Suyal, about twenty kilometres along the Almora–Barechina road in the Kumaon hills.

Q 4 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
What does the name 'Lakhudiyar' literally mean?
(1) Cave of the hunters
(2) Hill of paintings
(3) One lakh caves
(4) Shelter of the dancers
= 3
~ Lakhudiyar literally means 'one lakh caves'. Its paintings fall into three groups: man, animal and geometric patterns.

Q 5 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The superimposition of paintings at Lakhudiyar shows which order, from the earliest to the latest?
(1) White, red ochre, black
(2) Red ochre, black, white
(3) Black, red ochre, white
(4) Black, white, red ochre
= 3
~ At Lakhudiyar the earliest paintings are in black, over them come red ochre paintings, and the last group is in white.

Q 6 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Which of the following groups of animal motifs is seen in the Lakhudiyar paintings?
(1) A long-snouted animal, a fox and a multi-legged lizard
(2) A rhinoceros, a giraffe and a bison
(3) A camel, a horse and a peacock
(4) A lion, a crocodile and a tiger
= 1
~ The chapter mentions a long-snouted animal, a fox and a multi-legged lizard at Lakhudiyar, along with stick-like humans, wavy lines, groups of dots and a scene of hand-linked dancers.

Q 7 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Kupgallu, Piklihal and Tekkalkota are known for prehistoric paintings made on which kind of rock?
(1) Sandstone
(2) Basalt
(3) Laterite
(4) Granite
= 4
~ In Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh, the granite rocks offered Neolithic people a canvas for their paintings; Kupgallu, Piklihal and Tekkalkota are the main sites named in the chapter.

Q 8 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Three types of paintings are reported from Kupgallu, Piklihal and Tekkalkota. Which of the following is NOT one of them?
(1) Paintings in white
(2) Paintings in green on a black background
(3) Paintings in red ochre over a white background
(4) Paintings in red ochre
= 2
~ The three types are paintings in white, in red ochre over a white background, and in red ochre. They belong to the late historical, early historical and Neolithic periods.

Q 9 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
In the paintings of Kupgallu, Piklihal and Tekkalkota, which kind of motif is described as rare?
(1) Vegetal (plant) motifs
(2) Bulls
(3) Elephants
(4) Stylised humans
= 1
~ The subjects are bulls, elephants, sambhars, gazelles, sheep, goats, horses, stylised humans and tridents, but vegetal motifs are rare.

Q 10 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
According to the chapter, the richest prehistoric paintings in India are found in:
(1) The Western Ghats of Maharashtra
(2) The Vindhya ranges of Madhya Pradesh and their Kaimur extensions into Uttar Pradesh
(3) The Aravalli hills of Rajasthan
(4) The Eastern Ghats of Odisha
= 2
~ The Vindhya ranges of Madhya Pradesh and their Kaimur extensions into Uttar Pradesh have the richest paintings; the area is full of Palaeolithic and Mesolithic remains, forests, wild plants, fruits and streams.

Q 11 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Consider the following statements about Bhimbetka:
1. It is located about 45 km south of Bhopal in the Vindhya hills.
2. It has about 800 rock shelters, of which about 500 bear paintings.
3. Its paintings were discovered in 1957–58 by V.S. Wakankar.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ Bhimbetka, the largest and most spectacular rock-shelter site, is 45 km south of Bhopal, spread over about ten square kilometres with about 800 shelters, 500 of them painted. V.S. Wakankar discovered it in 1957–58.

Q 12 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The Upper Palaeolithic paintings of Bhimbetka are linear representations mainly in which colours?
(1) White and yellow
(2) Black and white
(3) Green and dark red
(4) Purple and orange
= 3
~ Period I (Upper Palaeolithic) paintings are linear drawings in green and dark red of huge animals such as bisons, elephants, tigers, rhinos and boars, besides stick-like human figures.

Q 13 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
In the Upper Palaeolithic paintings of Bhimbetka, the green paintings are mostly of ______ and the red ones of ______.
(1) hunters; dancers
(2) dancers; hunters
(3) animals; plants
(4) honey collectors; warriors
= 2
~ The chapter notes that the green paintings are of dancers and the red ones of hunters.

Q 14 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
In which period were the largest number of paintings made at Bhimbetka?
(1) Upper Palaeolithic
(2) Chalcolithic
(3) Early historical
(4) Mesolithic
= 4
~ Period II, the Mesolithic, has the largest number of paintings. They are smaller than the Upper Palaeolithic ones, and hunting scenes predominate.

Q 15 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Consider the following statements about the Mesolithic paintings of Bhimbetka:
1. They are smaller in size than the paintings of the Upper Palaeolithic period.
2. Hunters are shown with barbed spears, pointed sticks, arrows and bows, and traps and snares are also seen.
3. Animals are stylised while human figures are painted in a naturalistic style.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1
~ Statement 3 is reversed: in the Mesolithic paintings animals are painted naturalistically, while humans are depicted in a stylised manner. The other two statements are correct.

Q 16 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The red colour of prehistoric rock paintings was obtained from:
(1) Chalcedony
(2) Limestone
(3) Haematite (geru)
(4) Cinnabar
= 3
~ Red came from haematite, known in India as geru. Green came from a green variety of chalcedony, and white probably from limestone.

Q 17 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Match the colour used in the prehistoric paintings with its probable source:
A. Red — 1. Limestone
B. Green — 2. Haematite (geru)
C. White — 3. A green variety of chalcedony
Select the correct answer:
(1) A-1, B-2, C-3
(2) A-3, B-1, C-2
(3) A-2, B-1, C-3
(4) A-2, B-3, C-1
= 4
~ Red from haematite (geru), green from a green variety of the stone chalcedony, and white possibly from limestone.

Q 18 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Consider the following statements about how the prehistoric painters of Bhimbetka worked:
1. Rocks or minerals were ground into powder and mixed with water and a sticky binder such as animal fat, gum or tree resin.
2. Brushes were made of plant fibre.
3. Blue and white were the artists' favourite colours.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1
~ Statement 3 is wrong: white and red were the favourite colours. The painters used white, yellow, orange, red ochre, purple, brown, green and black, ground from rocks and minerals and bound with fat, gum or resin, applied with plant-fibre brushes.

Q 19 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Why have the colours of the prehistoric rock paintings survived for thousands of years, according to the chapter?
(1) They were sealed with a glass-like coat of lime
(2) Probably because of a chemical reaction of the oxides present on the rock surface
(3) Each generation repainted them
(4) The caves were closed off by earthquakes
= 2
~ The chapter suggests the colours have lasted because of the chemical reaction of the oxides present on the surface of the rocks.

Q 20 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
At some places in Bhimbetka, paintings have been made one over another in as many as how many layers?
(1) 5
(2) 10
(3) 20
(4) 50
= 3
~ Some places at Bhimbetka have up to 20 layers of paintings. Possible reasons: the artist disliked the earlier work, the place was sacred, or different generations used the same area.
