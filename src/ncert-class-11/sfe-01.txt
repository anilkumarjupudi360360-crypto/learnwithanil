# NCERT Class 11 Economics, Statistics for Economics, Chapter 1: Introduction
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | eco | Statistics for Economics
A person who works for someone else in a job and is paid for it is called, in the chapter, a:
(1) Consumer
(2) Service holder
(3) Producer
(4) Service provider
= 2
~ The chapter describes us all as consumers; some of us are producers, some service holders (in jobs and paid for them) and some service providers (who provide services to others for payment).

Q 2 | eco | Statistics for Economics
A lawyer or a doctor who offers services to people in return for a fee acts as a:
(1) Consumer
(2) Service holder
(3) Service provider
(4) Producer of goods
= 3
~ Those who provide services to others for payment are service providers. A service holder, by contrast, works in a job for an employer.

Q 3 | eco | Statistics for Economics
What is the basic economic problem from which the study of economics begins?
(1) Scarcity: resources are limited and have alternative uses while human wants are unlimited, so choices must be made
(2) Too many goods and too few buyers
(3) The existence of money
(4) The presence of government
= 1
~ Because resources are scarce compared with wants, people have to choose how to use them. Economics studies these choices.

Q 4 | eco | Statistics for Economics
According to the chapter, economics is the study of:
(1) Only money and banking
(2) Only the stock market
(3) Only the government budget
(4) How people choose to use scarce resources that have alternative uses to satisfy their wants
= 4
~ Economics deals with choice: deciding how to use limited resources, which can be put to many uses, in the best possible way.

Q 5 | eco | Statistics for Economics
The chapter mentions three basic economic activities. Which are they?
(1) Saving, investment and taxation
(2) Buying, selling and lending
(3) Exports, imports and aid
(4) Consumption, production and distribution
= 4
~ Consumption, production and distribution are the three basic economic activities studied in economics.

Q 6 | eco | Statistics for Economics
In economics, the study of distribution deals with:
(1) How the national income from production is shared out as wages, profits, rent and interest
(2) How goods are transported from factories to shops
(3) How the government distributes free food
(4) How shops display goods
= 1
~ Distribution is about how the income generated in production is divided among the factors of production, as wages, profits, rent and interest.

Q 7 | eco | Statistics for Economics
'Statistics' used in the singular sense means:
(1) A collection of numbers
(2) A single number
(3) The methods of collecting, organising, presenting, analysing and interpreting data
(4) Only the census
= 3
~ In the singular sense, statistics is a subject: the body of methods used to collect, organise, present, analyse and interpret data.

Q 8 | eco | Statistics for Economics
'Statistics' used in the plural sense means:
(1) The science of economics
(2) Quantitative facts, that is, numerical data
(3) The methods of analysis
(4) A survey of opinions
= 2
~ In the plural sense, statistics refers to numerical facts, such as statistics of national income, population or prices.

Q 9 | eco | Statistics for Economics
Which of the following is an example of qualitative data?
(1) Monthly income of households in rupees
(2) The level of education or the gender of workers
(3) Height of students in centimetres
(4) Number of children in a family
= 2
~ Qualitative data describe attributes that are expressed in categories rather than measured in numbers, such as gender or educational status. The other options are quantitative data.

Q 10 | eco | Statistics for Economics
Which of the following is quantitative data?
(1) The religion of a respondent
(2) Whether a family owns a house or rents one
(3) The colour of cars sold
(4) The annual production of rice in tonnes
= 4
~ Quantitative data are expressed in numbers that can be measured, such as tonnes of rice produced. The others are attributes.

Q 11 | eco | Statistics for Economics
How does statistics help an economist deal with a mass of data?
(1) It condenses the data into a few numerical measures, such as the mean and the variance, that summarise it
(2) It adds more data to it
(3) It removes all numbers from it
(4) It converts the data into a story without figures
= 1
~ Statistics condenses large amounts of data into a few summary measures. An average, for example, tells us about a whole group of numbers at once.

Q 12 | eco | Statistics for Economics
An economist checks whether a rise in the price of a good is followed by a fall in the quantity demanded. Which use of statistics does this show?
(1) Collecting raw data only
(2) Drawing a pie diagram
(3) Finding relationships between economic factors
(4) Entering data in a register
= 3
~ Statistics helps establish relationships between economic variables, such as price and demand, or income and consumption.

Q 13 | eco | Statistics for Economics
Saying 'India produced so many tonnes of wheat this year, so many tonnes more than last year' instead of 'wheat production increased' illustrates which function of statistics?
(1) Presenting economic facts in a precise and definite form
(2) Collecting primary data
(3) Condensing data into a pie chart
(4) Removing qualitative facts
= 1
~ Statistics enables economic facts to be stated precisely and definitely, which helps in understanding them clearly.

Q 14 | eco | Statistics for Economics
Why do governments need statistics when making economic policies?
(1) To avoid making any policy
(2) Because statistics are required by law in every country
(3) To understand the size and causes of problems such as inflation or unemployment, and to design and check policies
(4) Because statistics can replace economic theory
= 3
~ Data on prices, employment, output and incomes show how serious a problem is and what lies behind it. Policies are then designed and evaluated on this basis.

Q 15 | eco | Statistics for Economics
Which caution about statistical methods does the chapter stress?
(1) They always give the right answer
(2) They can be used without understanding the data
(3) They are useful only for large countries
(4) They are no substitute for basic common sense; results must be interpreted carefully
= 4
~ Statistical methods are tools. Used without common sense, an average or other measure can mislead, so careful interpretation is always needed.

Q 16 | eco | Statistics for Economics
Economists study consumption mainly to understand:
(1) How consumers decide what to buy, given their incomes and the prices of goods
(2) How factories are built
(3) How taxes are collected
(4) How money is printed
= 1
~ Consumption is about how people with limited incomes choose among goods and services, depending on prices and their wants.

Q 17 | eco | Statistics for Economics
An economist who wants to understand production would study:
(1) How families spend their weekends
(2) How the stock market index moves
(3) How goods and services are created using land, labour, capital and other inputs
(4) How elections are held
= 3
~ Production is the creation of goods and services, using inputs such as land, labour and capital.

Q 18 | eco | Statistics for Economics
Which of the following is NOT a reason why economists use statistics?
(1) To replace economic reasoning entirely
(2) To present facts precisely
(3) To condense large amounts of data
(4) To find relationships between variables
= 1
~ Statistics supports economic reasoning; it does not replace it. Precision, condensation and finding relationships are its main uses.

Q 19 | eco | Statistics for Economics
Even a very rich person faces scarcity. Why?
(1) Because the rich cannot buy anything
(2) Because prices are always rising
(3) Because the government restricts spending
(4) Because time and other resources are limited compared with wants, so choices still have to be made
= 4
~ Scarcity is about limited resources relative to wants, not just money. Everyone has limited time and must choose how to use it.

Q 20 | eco | Statistics for Economics
A farmer who grows wheat and sells it in the market is acting as a:
(1) Consumer
(2) Producer
(3) Service holder
(4) Service provider
= 2
~ A person who produces goods, such as a farmer or a manufacturer, is a producer.
