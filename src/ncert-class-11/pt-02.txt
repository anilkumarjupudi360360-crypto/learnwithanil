# NCERT Class 11 Political Science, Political Theory, Chapter 2: Freedom
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | pol | Political Theory
Nelson Mandela's autobiography, discussed in the chapter, is titled:
(1) Freedom from Fear
(2) The Story of My Experiments with Truth
(3) Long Walk to Freedom
(4) Hind Swaraj
= 3
~ In Long Walk to Freedom Mandela describes the struggle against apartheid in South Africa and his 27 years in prison. Freedom from Fear is a collection of Aung San Suu Kyi's writings.

Q 2 | pol | Political Theory
"The only real prison is fear, and the only real freedom is freedom from fear." This idea is associated with:
(1) Aung San Suu Kyi
(2) Nelson Mandela
(3) J.S. Mill
(4) Karl Marx
= 1
~ Aung San Suu Kyi of Myanmar, who spent years under house arrest, wrote that freedom means living without fear of what others may think or do.

Q 3 | pol | Political Theory
According to the chapter, freedom means:
(1) The absence of all rules in society
(2) Doing whatever one likes, whatever the effect on others
(3) Only the right to vote
(4) The absence of external constraints, and also the presence of conditions in which people can develop their talents
= 4
~ Freedom has two sides: freedom from unjust constraints, and the opportunity to develop oneself and make choices. Both are needed for a person to be truly free.

Q 4 | pol | Political Theory
The Indian idea of "swaraj", as explained in the chapter, includes:
(1) Only the end of British rule
(2) Rule over the self as well as freedom from external rule
(3) The rule of the strongest
(4) Rule by a king
= 2
~ Swaraj means self-rule. Gandhi stressed that it includes self-control and freedom from the domination of others, not only political independence from Britain.

Q 5 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following can be a source of constraint on freedom, according to the chapter?
1. Domination and external controls, such as colonial rule
2. Social inequality, such as the caste system
3. Extreme economic inequality
(1) 1, 2 and 3
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1 only
= 1
~ Constraints can come from force, from unjust social arrangements and from poverty. A person without resources may not be able to use the freedoms the law gives.

Q 6 | pol | Political Theory
Why does the chapter say that some constraints are necessary in a society?
(1) Because freedom is not valuable
(2) Because governments must control every action
(3) Because people have different ideas and interests, and some rules are needed so that conflicts do not turn violent and everyone's freedom is protected
(4) Because only the rich should be free
= 3
~ In a society where people differ, some limits are needed for peaceful living together. The question is which constraints are justified and which are not.

Q 7 | pol | Political Theory
The "harm principle", discussed in the chapter, was put forward by:
(1) Karl Marx
(2) Mahatma Gandhi
(3) Jean-Jacques Rousseau
(4) John Stuart Mill
= 4
~ In On Liberty, J.S. Mill argued that the only purpose for which power can rightly be used against an individual against his will is to prevent harm to others.

Q 8 | pol | Political Theory
J.S. Mill distinguished between "self-regarding" and "other-regarding" actions. Which statement correctly reflects his view?
(1) The state should control both kinds of actions equally
(2) Self-regarding actions, which affect only the person concerned, should be free from outside interference; other-regarding actions that harm others can be restricted
(3) Other-regarding actions should never be restricted
(4) Only religious authorities should decide on self-regarding actions
= 2
~ For Mill, the state may step in only where an action causes harm to others. Even then, minor harm may call only for social disapproval, not legal punishment.

Q 9 | pol | Political Theory
According to Mill, for a minor harm caused by an action (not a serious harm), the right response is:
(1) Imprisonment
(2) Death penalty
(3) Social disapproval, not the force of law
(4) Banning the person from society permanently
= 3
~ Mill reserved legal punishment for serious harm. Smaller annoyances should be met by social disapproval, so that the power of the state is not used too readily.

Q 10 | pol | Political Theory
"Negative liberty", as explained in the chapter, refers to:
(1) An area of personal life in which no external authority can interfere
(2) The freedom to harm others
(3) The absence of any rights
(4) The state's duty to provide food and shelter
= 1
~ Negative liberty is "freedom from" interference: a protected zone where the individual can act as he or she wishes. The chapter asks how big this zone should be.

Q 11 | pol | Political Theory
"Positive liberty" is concerned mainly with:
(1) Keeping the state out of all areas of life
(2) Freedom from all laws
(3) The freedom of the rich only
(4) Creating enabling conditions, such as education and health, that allow people to develop and to take part in society
= 4
~ Positive liberty is "freedom to": the ability to realise one's potential. It often needs the state and society to provide opportunities and remove obstacles.

Q 12 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following is an example of a positive conception of liberty?
(1) The state staying out of a person's choice of clothes
(2) The state providing free and compulsory education so that every child can develop
(3) Abolishing all taxes
(4) Banning all newspapers
= 2
~ Providing education creates the conditions for freedom. The first option is about non-interference, which relates to negative liberty.

Q 13 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following works is mentioned in the chapter as an example of a book that was banned in India because some groups were offended by it?
(1) Discovery of India
(2) The Satanic Verses
(3) Hind Swaraj
(4) Long Walk to Freedom
= 2
~ Salman Rushdie's The Satanic Verses was banned in India in 1988 after protests. The chapter uses such bans to discuss the limits of freedom of expression.

Q 14 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following is NOT one of the reasons J.S. Mill gave for protecting freedom of expression, even for ideas that seem false?
(1) No idea is completely false; most have some element of truth
(2) Truth emerges from the conflict of opposing views
(3) The state must decide which ideas are true and silence the rest
(4) What is accepted as true today may later be shown to be false
= 3
~ Mill argued against silencing opinions. His reasons include the truth in every idea, the value of debate and the chance that accepted views are wrong. Option 3 is the opposite of his position.

Q 15 | pol | Political Theory
The chapter says that bans on books or films are often a "short-term solution". Why?
(1) Because the issue that caused offence is usually not resolved by banning, and bans can harm freedom of expression
(2) Because bans are never legal
(3) Because books cannot be banned in a democracy
(4) Because films are not covered by freedom of expression
= 1
~ Banning may calm a protest for a while, but it does not address the underlying disagreement, and it sets a precedent for silencing views. The chapter asks students to weigh this carefully.

Q 16 | pol | Political Theory
Under the Indian Constitution, freedom of speech and expression is:
(1) Absolute, with no restrictions possible
(2) Not a Fundamental Right
(3) Available only to journalists
(4) A Fundamental Right subject to reasonable restrictions, such as for public order, decency or the security of the state
= 4
~ Article 19 guarantees freedom of speech and expression, but the state can impose reasonable restrictions on specified grounds. The debate is about what counts as reasonable.

Q 17 | pol | Political Theory
Which idea is central to liberalism, as described in the chapter?
(1) The group always matters more than the individual
(2) Tradition must never be questioned
(3) Individual liberty and tolerance of different views, even ones we disagree with
(4) Only the state can decide what is good for each person
= 3
~ Liberals value individual freedom and tolerate opinions and lifestyles they may not share. Modern liberals also accept a role for the state in removing obstacles to freedom.

Q 18 | pol | Political Theory
Why does the chapter say that freedom is valuable?
(1) Because it allows people to develop their creativity and capacities, and to make their own choices
(2) Because it lets the powerful rule others
(3) Because it ends the need for laws
(4) Because it makes everyone think alike
= 1
~ Freedom lets individuals develop their talents, judgement and creativity and lead lives they value. A free society also benefits from the variety of ideas it allows.

Q 19 | pol | Political Theory
"Freedom does not mean only the absence of constraints; it also requires the ability to make choices." Which situation best illustrates the second part of this statement?
(1) A person with no legal restriction on travel
(2) A poor person who is legally free to study but cannot afford school fees
(3) A person forbidden to speak by law
(4) A citizen voting in an election
= 2
~ The poor student faces no legal ban but still lacks the means to exercise the freedom. This is why freedom also depends on social and economic conditions.

Q 20 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following constraints would most people consider justified, in the light of the chapter's discussion?
(1) A law that forbids women from owning property
(2) A ban on a peaceful protest because the government dislikes it
(3) A rule that people of certain castes cannot enter public places
(4) A law against driving under the influence of alcohol, because it can seriously harm others
= 4
~ Drunken driving endangers others, so restricting it fits the harm principle. The other options restrict people unfairly or for no good reason and would be unjust constraints.
