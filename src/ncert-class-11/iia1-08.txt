# NCERT Class 11 Fine Arts (Art & Culture), An Introduction to Indian Art Part I, Chapter 8: Some Aspects of Indo-Islamic Architecture
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Large-scale building activity under the Delhi Sultanate began in:
(1) The eighth century CE
(2) The early thirteenth century, after the Turkish conquest of northern India
(3) The sixteenth century
(4) The eleventh century
= 2
~ Building on a large scale began in the early thirteenth century after the Turkish conquest. Muslims had, however, been building in Sind and Gujarat since the eighth century.

Q 2 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The 'arcuate' form of construction refers to:
(1) The post-and-lintel (trabeate) method
(2) Corbelled roofs
(3) Arches built with voussoirs and keystones, able to carry domes
(4) Rock-cut architecture
= 3
~ The trabeate (post-and-lintel) method was already used in India; the arcuate form brought arches made of voussoirs fitted with keystones, which could support domes resting on pendentives and squinches.

Q 3 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Match the style of Indo-Islamic architecture with its centres:
A. Imperial style — 1. Bijapur and Golconda
B. Provincial style — 2. Delhi, Agra and Lahore
C. Mughal style — 3. Mandu, Gujarat, Bengal and Jaunpur
D. Deccani style — 4. Delhi Sultanate
Select the correct answer:
(1) A-2, B-3, C-4, D-1
(2) A-4, B-1, C-2, D-3
(3) A-3, B-4, C-1, D-2
(4) A-4, B-3, C-2, D-1
= 4
~ The chapter divides Indo-Islamic architecture into the Imperial style (Delhi Sultanate), the Provincial style (Mandu, Gujarat, Bengal, Jaunpur), the Mughal style (Delhi, Agra, Lahore) and the Deccani style (Bijapur, Golconda).

Q 4 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Since Islam forbade the replication of living forms, Muslim religious art and architecture developed mainly through:
(1) Arabesque, geometric patterns and calligraphy
(2) Sculptures of saints
(3) Wall paintings of court scenes
(4) Terracotta plaques
= 1
~ Hindus adorned surfaces with sculptures and paintings, while Muslims developed the arts of arabesque, geometric patterns and calligraphy on plaster and stone.

Q 5 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Consider the following statements about the materials of Indo-Islamic buildings:
1. Walls were thick, of rubble masonry cased in chunam (limestone plaster) or dressed stone.
2. Brick came into use from the beginning of the seventeenth century.
3. Quartzite, sandstone, buff stone and marble were among the stones used.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1, 2 and 3
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1 and 3 only
= 1
~ All three are given in the chapter. Polychrome tiles finished the walls, and bricks from the seventeenth century gave builders more flexibility.

Q 6 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The term 'chahar bagh' refers to:
(1) A tall minaret
(2) A stepped water tank
(3) A formal four-part garden layout
(4) A monumental gateway
= 3
~ The chahar bagh is the formal four-part garden. Humayun's tomb and the Taj Mahal are set in such gardens.

Q 7 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
In medieval India, sarais were:
(1) Sufi hermitages
(2) Square or rectangular buildings for the temporary stay of travellers, pilgrims and merchants
(3) Public bath houses
(4) Schools for Islamic learning
= 2
~ Sarais ringed cities and dotted the subcontinent, lodging travellers of every kind and encouraging cultural exchange. Khanqahs were Sufi hermitages, hammams baths and madrasas schools.

Q 8 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Which fort, earlier called Devgiri, had staggered entrances so that elephants could not force open the gates, twin forts, and a labyrinthine path?
(1) Gwalior
(2) Golconda
(3) Chittorgarh
(4) Daulatabad
= 4
~ Daulatabad had staggered entrances, a higher inner fort and a confusing path. Golconda had concentric outer walls the enemy had to breach one after another.

Q 9 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Which fort's steep height, which made it virtually impossible to scale, is said to have overawed Babur?
(1) Chittorgarh
(2) Daulatabad
(3) Gwalior
(4) Golconda
= 3
~ The chapter notes that Gwalior fort was invincible because of its steep height and that Babur was overawed by it.

Q 10 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The Qutub Minar of the thirteenth century in Delhi is how high and how many storeys tall?
(1) 210 feet, four storeys
(2) 234 feet, five storeys
(3) 132 feet, four storeys
(4) 200 feet, seven storeys
= 2
~ The Qutub Minar is 234 feet high with five storeys, mixing polygonal and circular shapes in red and buff sandstone, with marble in the upper storeys. It is associated with the saint Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki.

Q 11 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The fifteenth-century Chand Minar, 210 feet high, is at:
(1) Delhi
(2) Bijapur
(3) Mandu
(4) Daulatabad
= 4
~ The Chand Minar at Daulatabad has four storeys and once had chevron-patterned encaustic tiles and bands of Quranic verses; local, Delhi and Iranian architects worked on it.

Q 12 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Mandu was the capital of which dynasty, founded by Hoshang Shah?
(1) The Ghauri dynasty
(2) The Sharqi dynasty
(3) The Bahmani dynasty
(4) The Adil Shahi dynasty
= 1
~ Mandu, about 60 miles from Indore and over 2000 feet high, was the capital of the Ghauri dynasty (1401–1561). Its buildings are light, arched and airy, suited to the climate.

Q 13 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Which building at Mandu, the Sultan's audience hall, has battered (sloping) walls that give the impression of swinging?
(1) Jahaz Mahal
(2) Asharfi Mahal
(3) Baz Bahadur's palace
(4) Hindola Mahal
= 4
~ The Hindola Mahal ('swinging palace') was the audience hall. The Asharfi Mahal was a madrasa, and Rani Rupmati's double pavilion overlooks the Narmada valley.

Q 14 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The Jahaz Mahal at Mandu, a two-storeyed 'ship palace' between two reservoirs, was built by:
(1) Sultan Ghiyasuddin Khilji
(2) Hoshang Shah
(3) Baz Bahadur
(4) Akbar
= 1
~ Sultan Ghiyasuddin Khilji built the Jahaz Mahal, possibly a harem and pleasure resort with a terrace swimming pool.

Q 16 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Within its chahar bagh, where is the Taj Mahal tomb placed?
(1) At the centre of the garden
(2) At the northern end of the garden, on the river bank
(3) At the southern end, near the gateway
(4) On the western side, beside the mosque
= 2
~ The tomb stands at the northern end of the garden on the river bank. A red sandstone mosque to the west and a matching building to the east give balance.

Q 17 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Which decorative technique of the Taj Mahal involves inlaying stones such as yellow marble, jade and jasper into white marble?
(1) Pietra dura
(2) Stucco
(3) Arabesque in plaster
(4) Fresco
= 1
~ The chapter lists four decorative methods at the Taj: relief carving, marble jalis and volutes, pietra dura with yellow marble, jade and jasper, and tessellation. Quranic verses are inlaid in jasper.

Q 18 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The Gol Gumbaz at Bijapur is the mausoleum of:
(1) Ibrahim Adil Shah II
(2) Yusuf Adil Shah
(3) Quli Qutb Shah
(4) Muhammad Adil Shah
= 4
~ It is the tomb of Muhammad Adil Shah (1626–1656), the seventh sultan of the Adil Shahi dynasty, built by the ruler himself and left unfinished.

Q 19 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Consider the following statements about the Gol Gumbaz:
1. It is built of dark grey basalt with decorated plasterwork.
2. A whispering gallery runs along the drum of the dome.
3. Its four corner towers are five-storeyed and circular.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 2
~ Statement 3 is wrong: the four corner towers are seven-storeyed and octagonal. Its dome, 125 feet in diameter, is described as the second largest in the world.

Q 20 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
A Jama Masjid was needed for the Friday congregational prayer, which required the presence of at least how many adult Muslim men?
(1) Ten
(2) Twenty
(3) Forty
(4) Hundred
= 3
~ The Friday prayer needed at least forty adult Muslim men; the Khutba was read in the ruler's name. The mihrab in the Qibla Liwan on the west shows the direction of the Kaaba.
