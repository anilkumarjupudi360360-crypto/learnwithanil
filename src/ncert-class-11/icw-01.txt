# NCERT Class 11 Political Science, Indian Constitution at Work, Chapter 1: Constitution: Why and How?
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
According to the NCERT chapter, what is the first and most basic function of a constitution?
(1) To list the fundamental duties of citizens
(2) To set up an independent judiciary
(3) To declare the country a republic
(4) To provide a set of basic rules that allow minimal coordination among the members of a society
= 4
~ The chapter begins with coordination and assurance: a constitution gives a society basic rules that everyone knows and trusts, so its members can live together. The other functions build on this.

Q 2 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Consider the following functions of a constitution:
1. Specifying who has the power to make decisions in a society
2. Setting limits on what a government can impose on its citizens
3. Enabling the government to fulfil the aspirations of a society
4. Expressing the fundamental identity of a people
Which of the above are discussed in the NCERT chapter as functions of a constitution?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 1, 2 and 3 only
(3) 2, 3 and 4 only
(4) 1, 2, 3 and 4
= 4
~ The chapter lists all four, along with providing basic rules for coordination: deciding who makes decisions, limiting government power, enabling a just society, and expressing a people's collective identity.

Q 3 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The part of a constitution that decides how the government will be constituted and who can make laws relates to which function?
(1) Specifying the decision-making powers in a society
(2) Limiting the powers of the government
(3) Expressing the identity of a people
(4) Fulfilling the aspirations of a society
= 1
~ Specifying decision-making powers is about who is allowed to make laws and how the government is formed. In India, for example, Parliament makes most laws and its members are chosen by the people.

Q 4 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Fundamental rights that the government can never ordinarily violate are an example of which function of a constitution?
(1) Coordination and assurance
(2) Specification of decision-making powers
(3) Limitations on the powers of government
(4) Expression of national identity
= 3
~ Rights such as freedom of speech and the right not to be arbitrarily arrested mark out an area the government cannot enter. The chapter treats such rights as the main way a constitution limits government.

Q 5 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
A constitution can also enable the government to fulfil the aspirations of a society. Which feature of the Indian Constitution best shows this?
(1) It creates a parliamentary system on the British model
(2) It allows the government to take positive steps, such as Directive Principles and protections for weaker sections, to build a just society
(3) It fixes the number of states in India permanently
(4) It makes the President the real head of the government
= 2
~ A constitution can do more than limit power: it can empower the government to remove inequality and deprivation. The Indian Constitution does this through the Directive Principles and special provisions for disadvantaged groups.

Q 6 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which three factors does the NCERT chapter give for a constitution to be effective and to have real authority?
(1) Length, rigidity and a written form
(2) Federalism, judicial review and secularism
(3) Popular elections, a strong executive and a single party
(4) Mode of promulgation, substantive provisions and balanced institutional design
= 4
~ The chapter says a constitution works when it is made in a way people accept (mode of promulgation), when its provisions are fair to all (substantive provisions) and when power is balanced among institutions (balanced institutional design).

Q 7 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
A constitution drawn up by military rulers or by unpopular leaders, without public participation, is unlikely to be effective. This idea in the chapter relates to:
(1) Mode of promulgation
(2) Substantive provisions
(3) Balanced institutional design
(4) Judicial review
= 1
~ Mode of promulgation is about who made the constitution and how. A constitution made by a popular, credible body with people's participation, like India's Constituent Assembly after the national movement, carries more legitimacy.

Q 8 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
According to the chapter, a well-designed constitution ensures that no single institution or group can destroy it. It does so mainly by:
(1) Making the constitution impossible to amend
(2) Fragmenting power among institutions so that they check and balance one another
(3) Concentrating power in the executive
(4) Giving all power to the judiciary
= 2
~ This is balanced institutional design: power is divided among the legislature, executive, judiciary and independent bodies, so each can check the others, while the constitution stays flexible enough to adapt to changing needs.

Q 9 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The members of the Constituent Assembly of India were chosen by:
(1) Direct election by all adult citizens
(2) Nomination by the British Government
(3) Indirect election by the members of the Provincial Legislative Assemblies set up under the Government of India Act, 1935
(4) Nomination by the Indian National Congress
= 3
~ The Constituent Assembly was elected indirectly: members of the provincial assemblies, themselves elected in 1946 under the 1935 Act, chose the members. There was no direct, adult-franchise election.

Q 10 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The composition of the Constituent Assembly broadly followed the plan proposed by:
(1) The Simon Commission
(2) The Cripps Mission
(3) The Cabinet Mission
(4) The Mountbatten Plan
= 3
~ The Assembly was formed roughly along the lines of the plan of the British Cabinet Mission of 1946, which allotted seats to provinces and princely states according to population.

Q 11 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Under the scheme on which the Constituent Assembly was based, seats were allotted to each province and princely state roughly in proportion to population, in the ratio of about:
(1) One seat for every 1 lakh people
(2) One seat for every 10 lakh people
(3) One seat for every 25 lakh people
(4) One seat for every 1 crore people
= 2
~ Seats were allotted roughly in the ratio of 1:10,00,000, that is, one seat for about every ten lakh people. On this basis the provinces were to elect 292 members and the princely states were allotted at least 93 seats.

Q 12 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
In the provinces, how did the members of each community in the Provincial Legislative Assembly elect their representatives to the Constituent Assembly?
(1) By a simple majority (first past the post)
(2) By a list system
(3) By nomination of party leaders
(4) By proportional representation with single transferable vote
= 4
~ Seats in each province were divided among Muslims, Sikhs and the general category in proportion to population, and each community's members in the assembly elected its representatives by proportional representation with single transferable vote.

Q 13 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Consider the following statements about the Constituent Assembly:
1. It held its first sitting on 9 December 1946.
2. It met again as the Constituent Assembly for divided India on 14 August 1947.
3. The representatives of the princely states were chosen in a way to be decided by consultation.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ All three are correct. After Partition the members from the areas that went to Pakistan left, and the Assembly that reassembled on 14 August 1947 framed the Constitution for India.

Q 14 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The "Objectives Resolution", which set out the philosophy behind the Indian Constitution, was moved in the Constituent Assembly in December 1946 by:
(1) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
(2) Dr. Rajendra Prasad
(3) Jawaharlal Nehru
(4) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
= 3
~ Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution on 13 December 1946. It spoke of an independent sovereign republic, a union with autonomous units, and justice, liberty and equality for all; the Preamble grew out of it.

Q 15 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Who was the chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Constituent Assembly?
(1) Dr. Rajendra Prasad
(2) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
(3) B.N. Rau
(4) K.M. Munshi
= 2
~ Dr. B.R. Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee, which prepared the draft constitution that the Assembly debated clause by clause. Dr. Rajendra Prasad was the President of the Constituent Assembly.

Q 16 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The Indian Constitution was adopted by the Constituent Assembly and came into effect, respectively, on:
(1) 15 August 1947 and 26 January 1950
(2) 26 January 1949 and 26 November 1949
(3) 26 November 1949 and 26 January 1950
(4) 26 November 1949 and 15 August 1950
= 3
~ The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 (now observed as Constitution Day) and came into effect on 26 January 1950, the date chosen to recall the 1930 Purna Swaraj pledge.

Q 17 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Match the source constitution with the feature India borrowed from it:
A. British Constitution — 1. Directive Principles of State Policy
B. Irish Constitution — 2. Charter of fundamental rights and judicial review
C. United States Constitution — 3. Parliamentary form of government and first past the post
D. French Constitution — 4. Principles of liberty, equality and fraternity
(1) A-3, B-1, C-2, D-4
(2) A-1, B-3, C-4, D-2
(3) A-3, B-2, C-1, D-4
(4) A-4, B-1, C-2, D-3
= 1
~ The chapter's list: Britain gave the parliamentary system, first past the post, rule of law, the Speaker and law-making procedure; Ireland the Directive Principles; the USA fundamental rights, judicial review and an independent judiciary; France liberty, equality and fraternity.

Q 18 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
According to the NCERT chapter, which two ideas did the makers of the Indian Constitution take from the Canadian Constitution?
(1) A quasi-federal form of government with a strong centre, and the idea of residual powers
(2) Judicial review and impeachment of the President
(3) Directive Principles and the method of electing the President
(4) Emergency provisions and fundamental duties
= 1
~ Canada gave the model of a federation with a strong central government (quasi-federal) and the idea of residual powers, which in India rest with the Union.

Q 19 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which of the following is NOT one of the reasons the chapter gives for the authority of the Constituent Assembly and of the Constitution it made?
(1) Its members were broadly representative of the different sections and regions of India
(2) It worked by public reason and long deliberation, not by a narrow majority imposing its will
(3) Its members were directly elected by universal adult franchise
(4) It carried forward the consensus built during the national movement
= 3
~ Members were elected indirectly by the provincial assemblies, not by universal adult franchise. The chapter traces the Assembly's authority to its representativeness, its open deliberation and the legacy of the freedom struggle.

Q 20 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Consider the following steps in India's constitutional history, mentioned in the chapter:
1. Motilal Nehru and other Congress leaders drafted a constitution for India.
2. The Congress, at its Karachi session, resolved on what independent India's constitution should contain, including fundamental rights.
3. Elections were held to the provincial legislatures under the Government of India Act, 1935.
What is the correct chronological order?
(1) 1, 2, 3
(2) 2, 1, 3
(3) 1, 3, 2
(4) 3, 1, 2
= 1
~ The Nehru Report came in 1928, the Karachi resolution in 1931 and the provincial elections under the 1935 Act in 1937. This long build-up is why the chapter says the Constitution drew on the national movement's experience.
