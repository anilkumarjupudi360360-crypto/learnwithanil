# NCERT Class 11 Geography, India: Physical Environment, Chapter 6: Natural Hazards and Disasters
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). November 2026.

Q 1 | geo | India: Physical Environment
How does the chapter distinguish a natural hazard from a disaster?
(1) There is no difference between the two
(2) A hazard always causes more damage than a disaster
(3) A disaster is always man-made, while a hazard is always natural
(4) A hazard is an element of the natural environment with the potential to cause harm; a disaster is an actual event that causes large-scale loss of life and property
= 4
~ Hazards are potential threats; when they strike and cause serious disruption beyond the coping capacity of the community, they become disasters.

Q 3 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Why do earthquakes occur frequently along the Himalayan arc?
(1) Because of volcanic activity in the Himalayas
(2) Because the Indian plate, moving about 1 cm a year northwards, is locked against the Eurasian plate, and stress builds up until it is released suddenly
(3) Because of heavy rainfall
(4) Because of mining in the mountains
= 2
~ The accumulated energy is released when the lock between the two plates breaks, causing earthquakes along the Himalayan arc.

Q 4 | geo | India: Physical Environment

Q 5 | geo | India: Physical Environment
On the basis of an analysis of more than 1,200 earthquakes, India has been divided into how many earthquake damage risk zones?
(1) Three
(2) Four
(3) Five
(4) Seven
= 3
~ The five zones are very high, high, moderate, low and very low damage risk zones.

Q 7 | geo | India: Physical Environment

Q 8 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Why is a ship at sea not much affected by a tsunami, while coastal areas suffer huge damage?
(1) Over deep water a tsunami has a very long wavelength and limited wave height; in shallow water its wavelength shortens and its height increases
(2) Tsunamis occur only on land
(3) Ships move faster than tsunamis
(4) Tsunamis lose all energy in the open ocean
= 1
~ The speed of a tsunami depends on water depth. Near the coast its height can reach 15 m or more, which is why tsunamis are also called shallow water waves.

~ Mitigating tsunami damage is beyond the capacity of a single country, so international cooperation is needed.

Q 10 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Tropical cyclones are not formed between 0° and 5° latitude. The main reason is:
(1) The absence of moisture there
(2) The very low sea surface temperature there
(3) The absence of the Coriolis force near the equator
(4) Strong westerly winds there
= 3
~ Cyclones need a strong Coriolis force to prevent the low pressure from filling up, along with warm moist air, unstable conditions and no strong vertical wind wedge.

Q 11 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Consider the following statements about tropical cyclones:
1. They are confined to the area between 30°N and 30°S.
2. Horizontally they may extend 500–1,000 km and vertically from the surface to 12–14 km.
3. The centre of a cyclone, called the eye, has the strongest winds and the heaviest clouds.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1
~ The eye is a warm, low-pressure, cloudless core. Cyclones have very steep pressure gradients, draw energy from latent heat and weaken over land as the moisture supply is cut off.

Q 12 | geo | India: Physical Environment
In the Bay of Bengal, tropical cyclones mostly develop during which months?
(1) January and February
(2) April and May only
(3) October and November
(4) July and August only
= 3
~ Most cyclones originate between 10° and 15°N during the monsoon season, but in the Bay of Bengal they mostly develop in October and November. By July the place of origin shifts to around 18°N, near the Sunderban delta.

Q 14 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Which is cited in the chapter as an example of a positive consequence of floods?
(1) Majuli in Assam, the largest riverine island in the world, where good paddy crops follow the annual Brahmaputra floods
(2) Thar desert getting more sand
(3) Dry rivers in Rajasthan
(4) Glaciers in Ladakh melting faster

Q 15 | geo | India: Physical Environment

B. Agricultural — 2. Prolonged period of inadequate and poorly distributed rainfall
C. Hydrological — 3. Water in aquifers, lakes and reservoirs falls below what precipitation can replenish
D. Ecological — 4. Productivity of natural ecosystems fails due to water shortage

(1) A-1, B-2, C-3, D-4

Q 18 | geo | India: Physical Environment
In the local terms for the consequences of drought, "trikal" refers to:
(1) Scarcity of food grains only
(2) Scarcity of fodder only
(3) Shortage of water only
(4) Shortage of food grains, fodder and water together
= 4
~ Akal is scarcity of food grains, trinakal of fodder and jalakal of water; trikal, the shortage of all three, is the most devastating.
