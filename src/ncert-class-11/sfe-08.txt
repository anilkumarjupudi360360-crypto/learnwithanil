# NCERT Class 11 Economics, Statistics for Economics, Chapter 8: Use of Statistical Tools

Q 1 | eco | Statistics for Economics
What is the first step in making a statistical project?
(1) Drawing graphs
(2) Identifying a problem or an area of study
(3) Writing the bibliography
(4) Calculating the mean
= 2
~ A project begins by identifying the problem or area to be studied. This decides the target group, the data to be collected and the tools to be used.

Q 2 | eco | Statistics for Economics
Which is the correct sequence of steps in a project?
(1) Conclusion → collection of data → choice of target group → identifying the problem
(2) Collection of data → identifying the problem → bibliography → analysis
(3) Analysis → presentation → collection → choice of target group
(4) Identifying the problem → choice of target group → collection of data → organisation and presentation → analysis and interpretation → conclusion → bibliography
= 4
~ The steps follow a logical order: decide what to study and whom to study, collect and organise the data, present and analyse them, draw conclusions, and list the sources used.

Q 3 | eco | Statistics for Economics
In a project, the 'target group' refers to:
(1) The teachers who evaluate the project
(2) The investigators who collect the data
(3) The group of people or units the study focuses on, such as households in a study of consumer awareness
(4) The books used for the study
= 3
~ Once the problem is identified, the investigator decides whom the study is about. For a study of consumer awareness, the target group could be households.

Q 4 | eco | Statistics for Economics
The bibliography of a project is:
(1) The list of sources, such as books, reports and websites, that were consulted
(2) The questionnaire used in the survey
(3) The final conclusion
(4) The table of raw data
= 1
~ The bibliography lists the secondary sources used, so that readers can check them and know where the information came from.

Q 5 | eco | Statistics for Economics
For a project on consumer awareness among households, which instrument is most suitable for collecting primary data?
(1) A questionnaire or interview schedule
(2) A published census report
(3) A stock market index
(4) A price index
= 1
~ To know what households themselves know and do, the investigator must ask them directly, using a carefully designed questionnaire or interview schedule.

Q 6 | eco | Statistics for Economics
In a consumer awareness survey, you want to show the percentage of respondents who know each of several quality marks. A respondent may know more than one mark. Which presentation suits best?
(1) A pie diagram
(2) An ogive
(3) A histogram
(4) A simple bar diagram of the percentages
= 4
~ The percentages do not add up to a whole because respondents can know several marks, so a pie diagram is unsuitable. A bar for each mark compares them clearly.

Q 7 | eco | Statistics for Economics
A household's monthly expenditure is divided among food, rent, education and other items. Which diagram best shows the share of each item in the total?
(1) Histogram
(2) Pie diagram
(3) Ogive
(4) Scatter diagram
= 2
~ A pie diagram shows how a total is divided into its components, each as a sector proportional to its share.

Q 8 | eco | Statistics for Economics
To study how the price of onions changed over twelve months, which presentation is most suitable?
(1) A pie diagram
(2) A frequency polygon
(3) An arithmetic line graph (time series graph)
(4) A bivariate table
= 3
~ A line graph with months on the x-axis and prices on the y-axis shows the trend and fluctuations over time.

Q 9 | eco | Statistics for Economics
To find out whether family income and spending on children's education move together, which statistical tool would you use?
(1) Mode
(2) Pie diagram
(3) Index number
(4) A scatter diagram and the coefficient of correlation
= 4
~ Correlation measures whether and how strongly two variables move together. A scatter diagram gives a first visual check.

Q 10 | eco | Statistics for Economics
You want the typical income of families in a locality where a few families are very rich. Which average should you use?
(1) Median
(2) Arithmetic mean
(3) Weighted mean
(4) Sum of incomes
= 1
~ The few very high incomes would raise the mean far above what most families earn. The median is not affected by these extreme values.

Q 11 | eco | Statistics for Economics
To find the brand of toothpaste bought by the largest number of households in a survey, which measure would you use?
(1) Mean
(2) Median
(3) Mode
(4) Correlation
= 3
~ The brand chosen most often is the modal brand. Brands are qualitative, so the mean or median cannot be used.

Q 12 | eco | Statistics for Economics
To measure how the cost of living of workers' families has changed over the years, which tool is used?
(1) Karl Pearson's coefficient
(2) A consumer price index
(3) A frequency polygon
(4) The Sensex
= 2
~ A consumer price index tracks the change in retail prices of the basket of goods and services consumed by a particular group, and so measures the change in their cost of living.

Q 13 | eco | Statistics for Economics
India's first Consumer Protection Act, often discussed in projects on consumer awareness, was enacted in:
(1) 1956
(2) 1972
(3) 2005
(4) 1986
= 4
~ The Consumer Protection Act was first passed in 1986 to protect consumers' rights and set up consumer courts. Awareness of such laws is a common theme of student projects.

Q 14 | eco | Statistics for Economics
Which mark certifies the quality of agricultural products in India?
(1) ISI
(2) Agmark
(3) Hallmark
(4) ISO
= 2
~ Agmark is the quality certification for agricultural products. ISI is the mark for industrial products, and Hallmark is for gold jewellery.

Q 15 | eco | Statistics for Economics
In a school project, why would a student survey a sample of households instead of every household in the town?
(1) To save time and cost while still getting reasonably reliable results
(2) Because a sample always gives exact answers
(3) Because a census is illegal
(4) Because samples need no planning
= 1
~ A well-chosen sample is far cheaper and quicker than a census and can still represent the population fairly well.

Q 16 | eco | Statistics for Economics
Before the main survey, a student tries out the questionnaire on a few respondents to find problems in it. This is called:
(1) Sampling error
(2) A census
(3) A pilot survey
(4) Tabulation
= 3
~ A pilot survey shows whether questions are clear and how long the survey will take, so the questionnaire can be improved before the main survey.

Q 17 | eco | Statistics for Economics
In a project, 'interpretation' of data means:
(1) Drawing meaning from the analysed data and relating the results to the problem being studied
(2) Collecting the data
(3) Writing the questionnaire
(4) Listing the sources used
= 1
~ After the data are analysed with tables, diagrams and measures, interpretation explains what the results say about the question being studied, leading to the conclusion.

Q 18 | eco | Statistics for Economics
In a survey of 200 respondents, 120 say they know about consumer courts. What percentage of respondents know about them?
(1) 40 per cent
(2) 120 per cent
(3) 80 per cent
(4) 60 per cent
= 4
~ Percentage = (120 ÷ 200) × 100 = 60 per cent.

Q 19 | eco | Statistics for Economics
Which of the following is a secondary source of data for a project on changes in retail prices?
(1) The student's own interviews with shopkeepers
(2) A questionnaire filled in by the student's neighbours
(3) Price data published by the government, such as consumer price index releases
(4) A pilot survey conducted by the student
= 3
~ Data already collected and published by someone else, such as government price indices, are secondary data. The others are the student's own primary data.

Q 20 | eco | Statistics for Economics
Which diagram would you use to compare the male and female literacy rates of five states?
(1) A pie diagram
(2) A multiple bar diagram
(3) An ogive
(4) A frequency curve
= 2
~ A multiple bar diagram places the male and female bars side by side for each state, making both comparisons easy at a glance.
