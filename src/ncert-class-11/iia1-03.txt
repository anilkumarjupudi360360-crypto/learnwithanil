# NCERT Class 11 Fine Arts (Art & Culture), An Introduction to Indian Art Part I, Chapter 3: Arts of the Mauryan Period
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
In the sixth century BCE, Buddhism and Jainism emerged in the Gangetic valley as part of which tradition?
(1) Bhakti
(2) Vedic
(3) Tantric
(4) Shramana
= 4
~ Both arose as part of the shramana tradition and opposed the varna and jati systems. Ashoka later patronised the Buddhist shramana tradition in the third century BCE.

Q 2 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
How do the Mauryan pillars differ from the Achaemenian pillars, according to the chapter?
(1) Mauryan pillars were built in pieces by a mason, while Achaemenian pillars were rock-cut
(2) Mauryan pillars were rock-cut and show the carver's skill, while Achaemenian pillars were constructed in pieces by a mason
(3) Both were built of brick
(4) Mauryan pillars had no capital figures
= 2
~ Pillar-building was an old tradition found in the Achaemenian empire too, but Mauryan pillars are rock-cut, displaying the carver's skill, whereas Achaemenian ones were assembled in pieces by a mason.

Q 3 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Mauryan pillars with capital figures have been found at the following places:
1. Basarah-Bakhira
2. Lauriya-Nandangarh
3. Rampurva
4. Sankisa
Which of the above are in Bihar?
(1) 1, 2 and 3 only
(2) 1 and 4 only
(3) 2, 3 and 4 only
(4) 1, 2, 3 and 4
= 1
~ Basarah-Bakhira, Lauriya-Nandangarh and Rampurva are in Bihar; Sankisa and Sarnath, the other two sites named, are in Uttar Pradesh.

Q 4 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The Lion Capital of Sarnath was built by Ashoka to commemorate which event in the Buddha's life?
(1) His birth
(2) His enlightenment
(3) His first sermon, the Dhammachakrapravartana
(4) His mahaparinibbana
= 3
~ The Sarnath capital commemorates the Buddha's first sermon at Sarnath, the Dhammachakrapravartana, and has become the standard symbol of that event.

Q 5 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Which four animals are carved on the circular abacus of the Sarnath Lion Capital, between the chakras?
(1) Bull, horse, elephant and lion
(2) Bull, horse, elephant and deer
(3) Elephant, lion, tiger and bull
(4) Horse, bull, peacock and lion
= 1
~ A bull, a horse, an elephant and a lion are carved on the abacus, moving clockwise, with a chakra between each pair.

Q 6 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
How many spokes does each chakra on the abacus of the Sarnath Lion Capital have?
(1) 8
(2) 12
(3) 16
(4) 24
= 4
~ The abacus has a 24-spoked chakra in each of the four directions. The chakra later became the standard symbol of the Dhammachakra in Buddhist art.

Q 7 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
When the Sarnath Lion Capital was adopted as the National Emblem of independent India, which parts of the original were left out?
(1) The four lions
(2) The crowning wheel and the lotus base
(3) The animals on the abacus
(4) The chakras on the abacus
= 2
~ The capital was adopted without the crowning Dharmachakra wheel (now broken and displayed in the Sarnath site museum) and without the lotus base.

Q 8 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Consider the following statements about the Sarnath Lion Capital:
1. It is kept in the archaeological museum at Sarnath.
2. Four lions are seated back to back on a circular abacus.
3. It was discovered near Pataliputra.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 2 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 3
~ Statement 3 is wrong: the capital was discovered more than a hundred years ago near Varanasi, at Sarnath. It is heavily polished, typical of the Mauryan period, and stands on an inverted lotus base.

Q 9 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The Didarganj Yakshini is displayed in which museum?
(1) Indian Museum, Kolkata
(2) Patna Museum
(3) National Museum, New Delhi
(4) Sarnath Museum
= 2
~ The Didarganj Yakshini, a tall, life-size, free-standing sandstone figure with a polished surface, is in the Patna Museum.

Q 10 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
What does the Didarganj Yakshini hold in her right hand?
(1) A chauri (flywhisk)
(2) A lotus
(3) A mirror
(4) A water pot
= 1
~ She holds a chauri in her right hand; her left hand is broken. Her hair is tied in a knot at the back and she wears thick bell-ornaments on her feet.

Q 11 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Which of the following is NOT one of the narrative methods named in the chapter for Buddhist pictorial art?
(1) Synoptic narrative
(2) Continuous narrative
(3) Episodic narrative
(4) Abstract narrative
= 4
~ The chapter names synoptic, continuous and episodic narratives. The Buddha's life and Jataka stories such as Chhadanta, Vidurpundita, Ruru, Sibi, Vessantara and Shama were frequently shown.

Q 12 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Monumental images of Yakshas and Yakshinis of this period have been found at many places, such as:
(1) Taxila, Bharhut and Sanchi
(2) Amaravati, Nagarjunakonda and Goli
(3) Patna, Vidisha and Mathura
(4) Ajanta, Ellora and Bagh
= 3
~ Large Yaksha and Yakshini statues, mostly standing, are found at Patna, Vidisha and Mathura. Their polished surfaces and full, round faces with pronounced cheeks are typical.

Q 13 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The monumental rock-cut elephant associated with an Ashokan rock edict is at Dhauli, in which present-day state?
(1) Bihar
(2) Madhya Pradesh
(3) Odisha
(4) Andhra Pradesh
= 3
~ The Dhauli elephant in Odisha is modelled in the round with linear rhythm and is associated with an Ashokan rock edict.

Q 16 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Consider the following statements about the Lomus Rishi cave:
1. Its facade has a semicircular chaitya arch as the entrance.
2. An elephant frieze carved in high relief on the chaitya arch shows considerable movement.
3. Its entrance is on the back wall of the hall.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1, 2 and 3
(4) 1 and 2 only
= 4
~ Statement 3 is wrong: the entrance is on the side wall of the hall. Inside, the hall is rectangular with a circular chamber at the back.

Q 17 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
A stupa structure of the third century BCE is found at:
(1) Bairat, Rajasthan
(2) Devnimori, Gujarat
(3) Sannati, Karnataka
(4) Sanghol, Punjab
= 1
~ The chapter mentions the third-century BCE stupa structure at Bairat in Rajasthan. The Great Stupa at Sanchi was first built of brick under Ashoka and later covered with stone.

Q 18 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
In the structure of a stupa, the circular dome built over the cylindrical drum is called the:
(1) Harmika
(2) Chhatra
(3) Torana
(4) Anda
= 4
~ A stupa has a cylindrical drum and a circular anda, with a harmika and chhatra on top; later came the circumambulatory path, railings and gateways (toranas).

Q 19 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Kanha and his disciple Balaka are artisans named in inscriptions at which caves of Maharashtra?
(1) Ajanta and Ellora
(2) Bhaja and Karla
(3) Pitalkhora and Kondane
(4) Nashik and Junnar
= 3
~ Inscriptions name Kanha at Pitalkhora and his disciple Balaka at the Kondane caves. Inscriptions from the second century BCE also name donors, many of them lay devotees, gahapatis, kings, guilds and traders.

Q 20 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
In early Buddhist art, how was the Buddha shown?
(1) As a human figure in the Gandhara style
(2) Through symbols such as footprints, stupas, lotus thrones and chakras
(3) Only in bronze statues
(4) Only in painted portraits
= 2
~ In early Buddhist art the Buddha is shown symbolically, through footprints, stupas, lotus thrones, chakras and so on. Human images of the Buddha came later.
