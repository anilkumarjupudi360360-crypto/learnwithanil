# NCERT Class 11 Geography, Fundamentals of Physical Geography, Chapter 5: Geomorphic Processes
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). November 2026.

Q 1 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements:
1. Endogenic forces originate within the earth and are mainly land-building forces.
2. Exogenic forces derive their energy from the atmosphere, determined by the sun, and are mainly land-wearing forces.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 3
~ The earth's surface stays uneven because internal (endogenic) forces keep raising parts of the crust while external (exogenic) forces keep wearing them down.

Q 2 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The wearing down of relief and the filling up of basins by exogenic processes, which together level the earth's surface, is called:
(1) Diastrophism
(2) Gradation
(3) Volcanism
(4) Orogeny
= 2
~ Gradation includes degradation (wearing down) and aggradation (filling up), both carried out by exogenic processes.

Q 3 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which one of the following is NOT a part of diastrophism?
(1) Orogeny
(2) Epeirogeny
(3) Earthquakes
(4) Weathering
= 4
~ Diastrophism covers orogenic processes, epeirogenic processes, earthquakes and plate tectonics. Weathering is an exogenic process.

Q 4 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Mountain building through severe folding that affects long and narrow belts of the earth's crust is called:
(1) Epeirogeny
(2) Orogeny
(3) Denudation
(4) Exfoliation
= 2
~ Orogeny is mountain building. Epeirogeny is the uplift or warping of large parts of the crust and is sometimes called continent building.

Q 5 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Weathering, mass wasting, erosion and transportation are together called:
(1) Denudation
(2) Diastrophism
(3) Aggradation
(4) Pedogenesis
= 1
~ "Denude" means to strip off or uncover. These four exogenic processes together strip away the earth's surface and are called denudation.

Q 6 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which statement best describes weathering?
(1) Removal of material by running water over long distances
(2) Mechanical disintegration and chemical decomposition of rocks in place, with little or no movement of material
(3) Downslope movement of debris under gravity
(4) Deposition of sediments by wind
= 2
~ Weathering happens in situ through the action of weather and climate. Movement of the broken material is done by mass movements and erosion.

Q 7 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which one of the following is NOT a chemical weathering process?
(1) Carbonation
(2) Hydration
(3) Oxidation
(4) Frost wedging
= 4
~ Solution, carbonation, hydration, oxidation and reduction are chemical processes. Frost wedging, caused by repeated freezing and thawing, is physical weathering.

Q 8 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
When calcium sulphate takes in water and turns into gypsum, which chemical weathering process is at work?
(1) Hydration
(2) Carbonation
(3) Reduction
(4) Solution
= 1
~ Hydration is the chemical addition of water to minerals. The minerals expand, which also helps rocks to break up.

Q 9 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Carbon dioxide from the air and soil combines with water to form carbonic acid, which dissolves minerals such as calcium carbonate. This process is called:
(1) Oxidation
(2) Salt weathering
(3) Hydration
(4) Carbonation
= 4
~ Carbonation is the reaction of carbonate and bicarbonate with minerals. It is important in breaking down limestone and feldspars.

Q 10 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements:
1. Oxidation is the combination of a mineral with oxygen, common in iron-bearing rocks.
2. Reduction takes place when weathered material is exposed to plenty of free oxygen in the air.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 1
~ Oxidation needs oxygen, usually with water. Reduction is the opposite: it happens below the water table or in stagnant water, where there is no free oxygen.

Q 11 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Large, smooth, rounded dome-shaped landforms produced when the overlying rock load is removed and the rock expands are called:
(1) Tors
(2) Inselbergs
(3) Exfoliation domes
(4) Drumlins
= 3
~ Unloading releases pressure, so the outer layers peel off as curved sheets (exfoliation), forming domes. Tors result mainly from thermal expansion.

Q 12 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
According to the chapter, weathering helps in the enrichment and concentration of certain valuable ores. Which group is mentioned?
(1) Uranium and thorium
(2) Gold, silver and platinum
(3) Iron, manganese, aluminium and copper
(4) Coal and petroleum
= 3
~ When rocks weather, unwanted materials are leached away, leaving ores of iron, manganese, aluminium and copper concentrated.

Q 13 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements about mass movements:
1. They transfer rock debris down slopes under the direct influence of gravity.
2. In mass movements, air, water or ice carry the debris along.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 1
~ In mass movements the debris moves by gravity; it may carry water or ice with it, but is not carried by them. That is why mass movements are not counted as erosion.

Q 14 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which one of the following is NOT an activating cause of mass movements listed in the chapter?
(1) Removal of support from below the materials
(2) Overloading of slopes due to heavy rainfall
(3) Indiscriminate removal of natural vegetation
(4) Planting grass and trees on slopes
= 4
~ The chapter lists nine activating causes, including removal of support, steeper and higher slopes, overloading, earthquakes, explosions and removal of vegetation. Planting vegetation stabilises slopes.

Q 15 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The slow downslope flow of soil saturated with water, common in moist temperate areas where frozen ground melts at the surface, is called:
(1) Creep
(2) Debris avalanche
(3) Rock fall
(4) Solifluction
= 4
~ Creep and solifluction are slow movements. Solifluction is soil flowing slowly downhill when it is soaked with water.

Q 16 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
In which type of landslide does the rock debris slip down with a backward rotation relative to the slope?
(1) Rockslide
(2) Debris slide
(3) Slump
(4) Rock fall
= 3
~ A slump rotates backwards as it slips. A debris slide moves without backward rotation, a rockslide slides along bedding, joint or fault surfaces, and a rock fall drops freely.

Q 17 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements:
1. Erosion involves the acquisition and transportation of rock debris.
2. Deposition is a consequence of erosion, happening when the agents lose velocity and energy.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 3
~ Agents like running water, glaciers, wind and waves erode and carry material, and they drop it when their energy falls.

Q 18 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Among the soil-forming factors, which two are described as active control factors?
(1) Parent material and topography
(2) Climate and biological activity
(3) Topography and time
(4) Parent material and time
= 2
~ Climate and biological activity actively drive soil formation. Parent material, topography and time are passive control factors.

Q 19 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Why do humid tropical and equatorial soils usually contain little humus?
(1) Because there is no vegetation in these regions
(2) Because bacterial activity is intense and dead vegetation is rapidly oxidised
(3) Because the soils are permanently frozen
(4) Because there is no rainfall
= 2
~ High temperature and moisture make bacteria very active, so organic matter decomposes quickly. In cold climates humus accumulates, and in sub-arctic and tundra areas layers of peat form.

Q 20 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Rhizobium, a type of bacteria that lives in the root nodules of leguminous plants, is important for soil because it:
(1) Fixes atmospheric nitrogen
(2) Produces carbonic acid
(3) Forms hardpans
(4) Causes salinisation
= 1
~ The chapter notes that some bacteria and soil organisms take gaseous nitrogen from the air and make it available to plants; Rhizobium in legume roots is the example.
