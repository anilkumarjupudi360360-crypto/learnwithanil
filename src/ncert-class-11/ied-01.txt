Q 3 | eco | Indian Economic Development
According to the chapter, the economic policies of the colonial government in India were mainly concerned with:
(1) Protecting and promoting the economic interests of Britain rather than developing the Indian economy
(2) Building a strong capital goods industry in India
(3) Raising the productivity of Indian agriculture
(4) Making India self-reliant in food and clothing
= 1
~ The chapter says colonial policy was concerned more with the protection and promotion of Britain's economic interests than with developing India's economy. India was turned into a supplier of raw materials and a market for British goods.

Q 4 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Under colonial rule, roughly what share of India's population lived in villages and depended directly or indirectly on agriculture?
(1) About 50 per cent
(2) About 65 per cent
(3) About 85 per cent
(4) About 95 per cent
= 3
~ The Indian economy remained fundamentally agrarian: about 85 per cent of the population lived mostly in villages and drew its livelihood from agriculture.

Q 5 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which statement best describes the zamindari system in the then Bengal Presidency as discussed in the chapter?
(1) Zamindars invested heavily in irrigation and better seeds
(2) Cultivators owned the land and paid revenue directly to the government
(3) Profits from agriculture went to the zamindars, who collected rent but did little to improve agriculture
(4) Revenue was fixed each year as a share of the actual harvest
= 3
~ Under the zamindari system the zamindars' main interest was collecting rent regardless of the cultivators' condition. The terms of the revenue settlement, with fixed dates for depositing revenue on pain of losing the land, added to the cultivators' misery.

Q 6 | eco | Indian Economic Development
The commercialisation of agriculture in colonial India refers to:
(1) A shift towards cash crops, often meant for British industries, which seldom improved the condition of farmers
(2) The use of tractors and machines on a large scale
(3) Government purchase of food grains at fixed prices
(4) The setting up of co-operative markets for farmers
= 1
~ Some regions saw higher yields of cash crops, but the gains went to traders, moneylenders and the colonial economy. Farmers were pushed to grow commercial crops while food production and productivity stagnated.

Q 7 | eco | Indian Economic Development
How did the partition of the country affect India's jute industry?
(1) India lost all its jute mills to Pakistan
(2) The jute industry gained because jute prices rose
(3) Jute growing shifted entirely to Punjab
(4) Much of the jute-producing area went to East Pakistan, leaving India's jute mills short of raw jute
= 4
~ The jute mills were mostly in and around Calcutta, but much of the area that grew raw jute went to East Pakistan (now Bangladesh). India's jute industry suffered as a result.

Q 8 | eco | Indian Economic Development
The colonial policy of de-industrialisation had a twin motive. Which of the following were part of it?
1. To reduce India to an exporter of raw materials for the industries of Britain
2. To turn India into a market for the finished products of British industries
3. To build a capital goods industry in India to supply the empire
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 2
~ The twin motive was to make India a source of cheap raw materials for British factories and a large market for their output. No capital goods industry was built up; its absence was one of the chapter's main criticisms.

Q 9 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which statement about the growth of modern industry in the second half of the nineteenth century is correct?
(1) Cotton textile mills were set up mainly by Indians in the western parts of the country, while jute mills were dominated by foreigners and concentrated in Bengal
(2) Jute mills were set up mainly by Indians in Maharashtra, while cotton mills were foreign-owned in Bengal
(3) Both cotton and jute mills were owned only by the colonial government
(4) Modern industry first came up in the iron and steel sector in Punjab
= 1
~ Cotton textile mills, mostly Indian-owned, came up in the western parts, mainly Maharashtra and Gujarat. Jute mills, dominated by foreigners, were concentrated in Bengal.

Q 12 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Under colonial rule, India became:
(1) An exporter of finished consumer goods and an importer of raw materials
(2) An exporter of primary products such as raw silk, cotton, wool, sugar, indigo and jute, and an importer of finished consumer goods and light machinery
(3) An exporter of machinery and an importer of food grains
(4) A country with almost no foreign trade
= 2
~ Colonial policies made India an exporter of primary products and an importer of finished goods such as cotton, silk and woollen clothes, and capital goods such as light machinery produced in British factories.

Q 13 | eco | Indian Economic Development
More than half of India's foreign trade during colonial rule was restricted to which country?
(1) China
(2) Britain
(3) Persia (Iran)
(4) Ceylon (Sri Lanka)
= 2
~ Britain kept monopoly control over India's exports and imports. More than half of the trade was with Britain; the rest was allowed with a few countries such as China, Ceylon and Persia.

Q 14 | eco | Indian Economic Development
What effect did the opening of the Suez Canal have on India's foreign trade?
(1) It ended Britain's control over Indian trade
(2) It shifted India's trade towards Japan
(3) It made Indian exports more expensive and reduced them
(4) It further intensified British control over India's foreign trade by providing a shorter, cheaper route
= 4
~ The Suez Canal, opened in 1869, cut the cost and time of shipping between Britain and India and served as a direct route for goods. It tightened Britain's grip on India's trade.

Q 15 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Colonial India had a large export surplus. What does the chapter say happened to it?
(1) It was used to build India's gold reserves
(2) It was used to set up heavy industries in India
(3) It was used to pay for the colonial government's office in Britain, its wars and imports of invisible items, leading to a drain of India's wealth
(4) It was distributed to Indian farmers as higher prices
= 3
~ The surplus did not bring gold or silver into India. It went to meet the expenses of an office set up by the colonial government in Britain, war expenses and the import of invisible items, all of which drained Indian wealth.

Q 16 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which year is described as the 'year of great divide' in India's demographic history?
(1) 1921
(2) 1881
(3) 1947
(4) 1951
= 1
~ The first official census of India was held in 1881. Before 1921 India was in the first stage of the demographic transition; the second stage began after 1921, so 1921 is called the year of the great divide.

Q 17 | eco | Indian Economic Development
According to the chapter, the infant mortality rate on the eve of independence was about:
(1) 33 per thousand
(2) 72 per thousand
(3) 146 per thousand
(4) 218 per thousand
= 4
~ Infant mortality was alarmingly high at about 218 per thousand. Life expectancy was also very low, about 32 years; overall literacy was below 16 per cent and female literacy about 7 per cent.

Q 18 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which of the following correctly describes the occupational structure of colonial India?
(1) Agriculture about 70–75 per cent of the workforce, manufacturing about 10 per cent and services about 15–20 per cent
(2) Agriculture about 40 per cent, manufacturing about 30 per cent and services about 30 per cent
(3) Agriculture about 50 per cent, manufacturing about 25 per cent and services about 25 per cent
(4) Agriculture about 90 per cent, manufacturing about 8 per cent and services about 2 per cent
= 1
~ Agriculture was the largest occupation with 70–75 per cent of the workforce, while manufacturing employed about 10 per cent and services 15–20 per cent.

Q 19 | eco | Indian Economic Development
In which regions did the share of the workforce dependent on agriculture fall, with a rise in manufacturing and services, during colonial rule?
(1) Orissa, Rajasthan and Punjab
(2) Parts of the then Madras Presidency, Bombay and Bengal
(3) Only the princely states
(4) Assam and the North-East
= 2
~ Growing regional variation was a feature of the period. Parts of the Madras Presidency, Bombay and Bengal saw agriculture's share decline; in Orissa, Rajasthan and Punjab the share of workers in agriculture actually increased.

Q 20 | eco | Indian Economic Development
According to the chapter, the expensive system of electric telegraph in colonial India mainly served the purpose of:
(1) Helping Indian merchants expand inland trade
(2) Linking villages with urban markets
(3) Maintaining law and order
(4) Spreading education and news
