Q 1 | eco | Indian Economic Development
During the economic crisis of 1991, India's foreign exchange reserves had fallen to a level that was:
(1) Enough to pay for about one year of imports
(2) Enough to pay for about six months of imports
(3) Exactly equal to its foreign debt
(4) Not enough to finance imports for more than about two weeks
= 4
~ By 1991 the reserves could not finance imports for more than two weeks and were not enough to pay the interest due to international lenders. Prices of essential goods also rose sharply.

Q 2 | eco | Indian Economic Development
To deal with the 1991 crisis, India approached which institutions, and how large was the loan it received?
(1) The Asian Development Bank and the WTO, US$ 2 billion
(2) The International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (World Bank) and the International Monetary Fund, US$ 7 billion
(3) The United Nations and the World Bank, US$ 20 billion
(4) The IMF alone, US$ 1 billion
= 2
~ India approached the World Bank (IBRD) and the IMF and received US$ 7 billion. In return they expected India to liberalise, open up to foreign trade and reduce the role of the government.

Q 3 | eco | Indian Economic Development
The New Economic Policy of 1991 had stabilisation measures and structural reform measures. Which description is correct?
(1) Stabilisation measures were short-term, meant to correct weaknesses in the balance of payments and control inflation; structural reforms were long-term, meant to improve efficiency and international competitiveness
(2) Stabilisation measures were long-term and structural reforms were short-term
(3) Both were short-term measures to raise tax revenue
(4) Structural reforms meant only devaluation of the rupee
= 1
~ Stabilisation measures aimed to keep enough foreign exchange and bring rising prices under control. Structural reforms aimed to remove rigidities in various sectors so that the economy became more efficient and competitive.

Q 4 | eco | Indian Economic Development
After the 1991 reforms, industrial licensing was abolished for almost all products. Which of the following was NOT among the categories that still needed a licence?
(1) Alcohol
(2) Cigarettes
(3) Cotton textiles
(4) Industrial explosives
= 3
~ Licensing was kept only for a few categories: alcohol, cigarettes, hazardous chemicals, industrial explosives, electronics, aerospace, and drugs and pharmaceuticals. Cotton textiles were not among them.

Q 5 | eco | Indian Economic Development
According to the chapter, which industries are now reserved for the public sector?
(1) A part of atomic energy generation and some core activities in railway transport
(2) Defence, steel and coal
(3) Banking and insurance
(4) Telecommunications and airlines
= 1
~ The list of industries reserved for the public sector was cut sharply after 1991. Only a part of atomic energy generation and some core railway activities remain reserved.

Q 6 | eco | Indian Economic Development
The financial sector reforms changed the role of the Reserve Bank of India from:
(1) Lender to borrower
(2) Facilitator to regulator
(3) Central bank to commercial bank
(4) Regulator to facilitator of the financial sector
= 4
~ One major aim of the reforms was to reduce the RBI's role from regulator to facilitator, so that the financial sector could take decisions on many matters without consulting the RBI.

Q 7 | eco | Indian Economic Development

1. Private sector banks, Indian as well as foreign, were allowed to enter the banking sector.
2. The foreign investment limit in banks was raised to around 74 per cent.
3. Foreign institutional investors such as merchant bankers, mutual funds and pension funds were allowed to invest in Indian financial markets.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 1, 2 and 3
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1 and 3 only

Q 8 | eco | Indian Economic Development
The Goods and Services Tax (GST) Act was passed in 2016. From when was GST implemented?
(1) April 2016
(2) January 2017
(3) July 2017
(4) April 2018
= 3
~ GST came into force in July 2017. It replaced many indirect taxes with one tax, in the spirit of 'one nation, one tax, one market'.

Q 9 | eco | Indian Economic Development
As an immediate step to resolve the balance of payments crisis in 1991, the rupee was:
(1) Made fully convertible on the capital account
(2) Devalued against foreign currencies
(3) Fixed permanently to the US dollar
(4) Replaced by a new currency
= 2
~ The rupee was devalued, which increased the inflow of foreign exchange. Since then the exchange rate has increasingly been determined by the market, with the government stepping in only when needed.

Q 10 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Quantitative restrictions on imports of manufactured consumer goods and agricultural products were fully removed from:
(1) April 2001
(2) July 1991
(3) January 1995
(4) April 2010
= 1
~ Trade reforms dismantled quantitative restrictions, cut tariff rates and removed import licensing (except for hazardous and environmentally sensitive industries). QRs on these goods were fully removed from April 2001.

Q 11 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Selling a part of the equity of public sector enterprises to the public is called:
(1) Nationalisation
(2) Outsourcing
(3) Liberalisation
(4) Disinvestment
= 4
~ Privatisation means shedding government ownership or management of an enterprise. When it is done by selling part of the equity of public sector enterprises to the public, it is called disinvestment.

Q 12 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Why did the government give some public sector enterprises the status of Maharatnas, Navratnas and Miniratnas?
(1) To sell them to foreign companies quickly
(2) To merge them with private firms
(3) To improve their efficiency and help them compete globally by giving them greater managerial and operational autonomy
(4) To bring them under the direct control of the RBI
= 3
~ The status gave these enterprises more freedom in running the company and taking decisions, so that they could become more efficient and expand. Indian Oil Corporation and Steel Authority of India are examples of Maharatnas.

Q 13 | eco | Indian Economic Development
In the context of globalisation, 'outsourcing' means:
(1) Banning imports from other countries
(2) Moving a company's head office abroad
(3) A company hiring regular services from external sources, mostly from other countries, which it earlier provided itself
(4) Selling shares of a company to foreign investors
= 3
~ Services such as call centres, record keeping, accountancy and banking services are now outsourced. India became a favourite destination because of its large pool of cheap, skilled workers and improved communication technology.

Q 14 | eco | Indian Economic Development
The World Trade Organisation (WTO) was founded in:
(1) 1995, as the successor to GATT
(2) 1948, with 23 countries
(3) 1991, along with India's reforms
(4) 2001, at Doha
= 1
~ The WTO was founded in 1995 as the successor to the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT), which had been set up in 1948 with 23 countries as a global trade organisation.

Q 15 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which of the following describes the aim of the WTO as given in the chapter?
(1) To give loans to countries with balance of payments problems
(2) To establish a rule-based trading regime in which nations cannot place arbitrary restrictions on trade, and to expand production and trade of services
(3) To fix the exchange rates of all currencies
(4) To fund infrastructure in developing countries
= 2
~ The WTO is meant to ensure a rule-based trading system, to make optimum use of world resources while protecting the environment, and to cover trade in services as well as goods. Loans for balance of payments problems are the IMF's job.

Q 16 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which of the following are criticisms of the reforms' effect on agriculture made in the chapter?
1. Public investment in agricultural infrastructure such as irrigation, power, roads and research fell.
2. Removing fertiliser subsidies raised the cost of production.
3. Export-oriented policies shifted production from food crops to cash crops.
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ The chapter says agriculture did not benefit from the reforms: public investment declined, fertiliser subsidy cuts hurt small farmers, and the shift to export crops put pressure on food grain prices.

Q 17 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which reason does the chapter give for the poor performance of industry during the reform period?
(1) Cheaper imports replaced demand for domestic goods, and investment in infrastructure such as power supply remained inadequate
(2) The government banned all foreign investment
(3) Industrial licensing became stricter after 1991
(4) The rupee became too strong
= 1
~ Cheap imports cut demand for Indian goods, and infrastructure, including power, stayed weak. Developing countries also could not access markets in developed countries because of high non-tariff barriers.

Q 18 | eco | Indian Economic Development
What is the main criticism of disinvestment discussed in the chapter?
(1) The government sold too few shares
(2) The assets of public sector enterprises were undervalued, and the money raised was used to meet revenue shortfalls instead of for development
(3) Only foreign companies were allowed to buy the shares
(4) Disinvestment made public enterprises larger
= 2
~ Critics say PSU assets were sold below their value, causing a loss to the government, and that the proceeds were used to offset revenue shortages rather than to develop public enterprises or build social infrastructure.

Q 19 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which statement about growth in the reform period is in line with the chapter's appraisal?
(1) Agriculture became the fastest-growing sector
(2) All three sectors grew at the same rate
(3) The industrial sector grew faster than at any time before 1991, without fluctuations
(4) The services sector grew fast, while the growth of agriculture declined
= 4
~ GDP growth improved, driven mainly by services. Agriculture's growth declined and industry's growth fluctuated, which is why critics say the benefits of reform were uneven.

Q 20 | eco | Indian Economic Development
According to the chapter, the origin of the 1991 financial crisis can be traced to:
(1) A sudden fall in food grain production in 1990
(2) The Green Revolution
(3) Inefficient management of the economy in the 1980s, when government spending exceeded its revenue and was met by borrowing
(4) The setting up of the WTO
= 3
~ In the 1980s the government spent more than it earned and borrowed to make up the gap, while export earnings could not pay for growing imports. This led to the balance of payments crisis of 1991.
