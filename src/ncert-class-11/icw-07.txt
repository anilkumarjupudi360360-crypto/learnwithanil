# NCERT Class 11 Political Science, Indian Constitution at Work, Chapter 7: Federalism
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Federalism is a system in which:
(1) All power is held by the central government
(2) Local governments make all laws
(3) There is no written constitution
(4) Power is divided between two levels of government, each with its own area of authority, usually laid down in a written constitution
= 4
~ In a federation there are at least two sets of government, one for the whole country and one for the regions, each with powers given by the constitution. Disputes between them are usually settled by the judiciary.

Q 2 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The chapter discusses the break-up of which country in 1991 as an example of a federation that failed to hold together?
(1) Canada
(2) Nigeria
(3) Soviet Union
(4) Switzerland
= 3
~ The USSR broke into fifteen independent countries in 1991. Excessive centralisation and the domination of one region over others are seen as reasons.

Q 3 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Article 1 of the Indian Constitution describes India as:
(1) A union of states
(2) A federation of states
(3) A confederation of states
(4) A unitary state with provinces
= 1
~ Article 1 says "India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States." The word "federation" is not used, which reflects that the states did not come together by agreement and cannot leave the Union.

Q 4 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The division of legislative powers between the Union and the states is given in which Schedule of the Constitution?
(1) First Schedule
(2) Fourth Schedule
(3) Seventh Schedule
(4) Tenth Schedule
= 3
~ The Seventh Schedule has three lists: the Union List, the State List and the Concurrent List. The First Schedule lists the states and union territories.

Q 5 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Defence, foreign affairs, banking and atomic energy are subjects in the:
(1) State List
(2) Concurrent List
(3) Residuary powers
(4) Union List
= 4
~ Subjects of national importance such as defence, foreign affairs, currency, banking and atomic energy are in the Union List, on which only Parliament can make laws.

Q 6 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Police, agriculture and public health are subjects in the:
(1) State List
(2) Union List
(3) Concurrent List
(4) Residuary powers
= 1
~ The State List has subjects of local or regional importance, such as police, agriculture, local government and public health, on which the states make laws.

Q 7 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Education, forests and marriage are subjects in the Concurrent List. If a state law on a Concurrent List subject conflicts with a law of Parliament on it, then:
(1) The state law prevails
(2) The law of Parliament prevails
(3) Both laws stand cancelled
(4) The Governor decides which law applies
= 2
~ Both Parliament and the states can legislate on Concurrent List subjects, but where the two conflict, the Union law prevails.

Q 8 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which government has the power to make laws on subjects not mentioned in any of the three lists (residuary powers)?
(1) The state governments
(2) Both together, with the Governor's consent
(3) The Union (Parliament)
(4) The Supreme Court
= 3
~ Residuary powers rest with Parliament (Article 248). Matters like cyber law, which did not exist when the lists were made, fall under it.

Q 9 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which of the following features shows that the Indian Constitution creates a strong central government?
1. Parliament can form new states and change the boundaries of existing ones
2. During an emergency, the federal system can work like a unitary one
3. The Governor of a state is appointed by the President
4. The Centre has more important sources of revenue than the states
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1, 3 and 4 only
(4) 1, 2, 3 and 4
= 4
~ All four point to a strong Centre. The framers wanted a strong Union to keep a large and diverse country together after Partition and to plan its economic development.

Q 10 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Under Article 3, Parliament can create a new state or change the boundaries of a state. Which of the following is correct?
(1) The consent of the state concerned is required
(2) A referendum in the state is compulsory
(3) The Supreme Court must approve the change
(4) The views of the state legislature are sought, but Parliament is not bound by them
= 4
~ The President refers the bill to the state legislature for its views, but Parliament can go ahead whatever the state says. This shows that the states' boundaries are not fixed by agreement.

Q 11 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The role of the Governor has been a source of conflict between the Centre and the states mainly because:
(1) The Governor, appointed by the Centre, has been seen as acting on the Centre's instructions, for example in recommending President's Rule
(2) The Governor is elected by the state legislature
(3) The Governor heads the High Court
(4) The Governor controls the state's police directly
= 1
~ Governors are appointed by the President on the Centre's advice. Their reports recommending President's Rule and their choices when no party has a majority have often been accused of serving the party ruling at the Centre.

Q 12 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
President's Rule in a state is imposed under:
(1) Article 352
(2) Article 356
(3) Article 360
(4) Article 370
= 2
~ Article 356 allows the President to take over a state's administration if its government cannot be carried on according to the Constitution. Article 352 deals with a national emergency and Article 360 with a financial emergency.

Q 13 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The Supreme Court laid down limits on the use of Article 356, holding that the dismissal of a state government can be reviewed by the courts and that majority must be tested on the floor of the House, in:
(1) Kesavananda Bharati case
(2) Golaknath case
(3) S.R. Bommai case
(4) Minerva Mills case
= 3
~ In S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994) the Court ruled that a government's majority must be tested in the assembly and that President's Rule is open to judicial review. Misuse of Article 356 has since declined.

Q 14 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
In 1983 the Central Government appointed a commission to examine Centre–state relations. It was headed by:
(1) Justice R.S. Sarkaria
(2) Justice M.M. Punchhi
(3) K. Santhanam
(4) Balwant Rai Mehta
= 1
~ The Sarkaria Commission (1983–1988) examined Centre–state relations and recommended, among other things, that Governors be impartial and that Article 356 be used only as a last resort.

Q 15 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Demands for more autonomy for the states, discussed in the chapter, have included which of the following?
1. More powers and important subjects for the states
2. Independent sources of revenue and more financial resources
3. Administrative autonomy, without Central control over state services
4. Cultural and linguistic autonomy
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 1, 2 and 3 only
(3) 3 and 4 only
(4) 1, 2, 3 and 4
= 4
~ Parties such as the DMK in Tamil Nadu and the Akali Dal in Punjab, and later several states, have raised demands of all these kinds, including opposition to the imposition of Hindi.

Q 16 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Disputes between states over sharing river waters, such as the Cauvery, have been dealt with mainly through:
(1) The Election Commission
(2) A national referendum
(3) Water disputes tribunals set up by the Centre
(4) The Finance Commission
= 3
~ Inter-state river disputes, like the one between Karnataka and Tamil Nadu over the Cauvery, have been referred to tribunals set up under a law of Parliament.

Q 17 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Why does the chapter describe some features of Indian federalism as "asymmetric"?
(1) Because all states are treated exactly alike
(2) Because some states and regions have special provisions in the Constitution because of their history and circumstances
(3) Because the Rajya Sabha has equal seats for every state
(4) Because the Union has no special powers
= 2
~ Articles 371 and others give special provisions to states such as Nagaland, Mizoram, Assam and Andhra Pradesh, and to tribal areas. Such asymmetry accommodates regional needs within one framework.

Q 18 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The reorganisation of states on the basis of language in 1956 followed the report of:
(1) The States Reorganisation Commission
(2) The Sarkaria Commission
(3) The Finance Commission
(4) The Cabinet Mission
= 1
~ The States Reorganisation Commission's report led to the States Reorganisation Act, 1956, which redrew most state boundaries on linguistic lines. The chapter treats it as a way of accommodating diversity, not a threat to unity.

Q 19 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which three new states were created in the year 2000?
(1) Telangana, Uttarakhand and Jharkhand
(2) Chhattisgarh, Uttarakhand and Jharkhand
(3) Goa, Mizoram and Arunachal Pradesh
(4) Chhattisgarh, Telangana and Goa
= 2
~ Chhattisgarh (from Madhya Pradesh), Uttarakhand (from Uttar Pradesh) and Jharkhand (from Bihar) were formed in 2000. Telangana was formed from Andhra Pradesh in 2014.

Q 20 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
During a national emergency under Article 352, which of the following can happen?
(1) Parliament can make laws on subjects in the State List
(2) The states become independent of the Union
(3) The Supreme Court is dissolved
(4) Elections to the Lok Sabha become compulsory every year
= 1
~ During a national emergency the federal division of powers can be set aside: Parliament can legislate on State List subjects and the Centre can give directions to the states, so the system works in a unitary way.
