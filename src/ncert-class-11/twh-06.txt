# NCERT Class 11 History, Themes in World History, Chapter 6: Displacing Indigenous Peoples
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | his | Themes in World History
The chapter looks at the displacement of indigenous peoples mainly in:
(1) India and China
(2) Africa and Japan
(3) North America (the USA and Canada) and Australia
(4) South-East Asia
= 3
~ In these lands, European settlers took over the land and the native people were pushed aside. The chapter compares the two experiences.

Q 2 | his | Themes in World History
The term "settler" in the chapter refers to:
(1) Europeans, such as the British, French, Dutch and others, who went to live permanently in colonies like North America, Australia and South Africa
(2) Native people who settled in towns
(3) Traders who came only for a season
(4) Missionaries only
= 1
~ In South Africa, the Dutch settlers came to be called Boers. In the Americas and Australia, settlers took over land for farming and mining.

Q 3 | his | Themes in World History
Which of the following was true of most native peoples of North America before Europeans arrived?
(1) They lived in large industrial cities
(2) They farmed, hunted and fished, lived in groups along river valleys, and did not have a sense of land as private property
(3) They used gold coins
(4) They did not trade
= 2
~ Native peoples saw land as something shared by the community. Formal written ownership deeds were a European idea.

Q 4 | his | Themes in World History
European settlers in North America were drawn to the region at first mainly by:
(1) Fish and furs, especially beaver fur for hats
(2) Spices
(3) Silk
(4) Tea
= 1
~ Fur trade with the natives grew quickly. Later, settlers came to farm, cut forests and mine.

Q 5 | his | Themes in World History
Under the US "Indian Removal Act" of 1830, the government:
(1) Gave all natives citizenship
(2) Returned land to the natives
(3) Built schools for natives in their homeland
(4) Forced native peoples living east of the Mississippi to move west, often by force
= 4
~ The natives were pushed into lands thought less useful to settlers, and later into reservations.

Q 6 | his | Themes in World History
In 1832, Chief Justice John Marshall of the US Supreme Court ruled that the Cherokee were a distinct community whose land rights should be respected. What happened next?
(1) The ruling was followed and the Cherokee kept their land
(2) President Andrew Jackson refused to honour the ruling, and the Cherokee were evicted; many died on the long walk known as the "Trail of Tears"
(3) The Cherokee moved to Canada
(4) The Cherokee became the largest landowners
= 2
~ Thousands of Cherokee were driven out of their land, and very many died on the way. The episode shows how settlers' demand for land overrode even the courts.

Q 7 | his | Themes in World History
The USA bought the large territory of Louisiana in 1803 from:
(1) Britain
(2) Spain
(3) France
(4) Russia
= 3
~ Through purchases and wars, the USA expanded westwards from the Atlantic coast to the Pacific. Alaska was bought from Russia later.

Q 8 | his | Themes in World History
The discovery of gold in California in the late 1840s led to:
(1) A rush of settlers to the west and, in time, the building of railways across the continent
(2) The end of railways in the USA
(3) The return of land to the natives
(4) A ban on immigration
= 1
~ The "gold rush" brought thousands of people seeking fortunes. Railways later linked the east and west coasts of the USA and Canada.

Q 9 | his | Themes in World History
In 1934, the US "Indian Reorganisation Act" gave natives in reservations:
(1) Free passage to Europe
(2) The right to buy land and take loans
(3) Seats in the Senate
(4) The right to own slaves
= 2
~ It was a step towards recognising native rights. In 1954, natives prepared a Declaration of Indian Rights, asking for citizenship on their own terms.

Q 10 | his | Themes in World History
In Canada, the native peoples are today often called:
(1) Boers
(2) Aborigines
(3) First Nations
(4) Métis only
= 3
~ Canada's 1982 Constitution accepted the existing aboriginal and treaty rights of the native peoples.

Q 11 | his | Themes in World History
Canada faced the problem of the French-speaking settlers, who lived mainly in:
(1) Quebec
(2) British Columbia
(3) Ontario's south coast
(4) Alaska
= 1
~ Canada's people included both British and French settlers. Making them part of one nation was a long political issue.

Q 12 | his | Themes in World History
Who were the "Métis" of Canada?
(1) Dutch settlers
(2) A group of gold miners
(3) People of mixed native and European (French) descent
(4) Native people who never met Europeans
= 3
~ The Métis rose in revolt in the nineteenth century to protect their land and identity.

Q 13 | his | Themes in World History
The native people of Australia are called:
(1) Aborigines
(2) Maori
(3) Inuit
(4) Cherokee
= 1
~ They had lived on the continent for tens of thousands of years before Europeans arrived. They were made up of many groups with different languages.

Q 14 | his | Themes in World History
In 1770, the British claimed Australia after the voyage of:
(1) Christopher Columbus
(2) Vasco da Gama
(3) Ferdinand Magellan
(4) Captain James Cook
= 4
~ The British later began settling Australia, at first with convicts, in 1788. The land was treated as if no one owned it.

Q 15 | his | Themes in World History
The anthropologist W.E.H. Stanner, in 1968, used the phrase "the Great Australian Silence" to describe:
(1) The empty deserts of Australia
(2) The failure of historians to write about the Aboriginal people
(3) A period of peace
(4) A ban on newspapers
= 2
~ After this, historians and universities began to study Aboriginal cultures. Henry Reynolds' book "Why Weren't We Told?" (1999) challenged the old version of Australian history.

Q 16 | his | Themes in World History
Since 1974, Australia has followed an official policy of:
(1) Assimilation, forcing all to give up native ways
(2) Expelling settlers
(3) Separate states for natives
(4) "Multiculturalism", giving equal respect to native cultures and to the cultures of immigrants
= 4
~ This was a change from the earlier policy of trying to make natives give up their way of life.

Q 17 | his | Themes in World History
The 1992 Mabo case in the High Court of Australia was important because it:
(1) Made Australia a republic
(2) Gave all land to the settlers
(3) Abolished the High Court
(4) Recognised that the Aboriginal people had rights to land before the British came, rejecting the idea of "terra nullius" (land belonging to no one)
= 4
~ The case was brought by Eddie Mabo, a Torres Strait Islander. It changed the legal basis of land claims in Australia.

Q 18 | his | Themes in World History
The name of Australia's capital, Canberra, is believed to come from an Aboriginal word meaning:
(1) Land of gold
(2) Meeting place
(3) Big river
(4) Red earth
= 2
~ The capital was built in the twentieth century. Its name reflects the land's Aboriginal past.

Q 19 | his | Themes in World History
Which of the following was a common view among many European settlers about native peoples, as the chapter shows?
(1) That natives were the rightful owners of all land
(2) That natives should be paid for all land taken
(3) That natives were "uncivilised" and that land was wasted unless it was farmed or mined in the European way
(4) That natives should rule the settlers
= 3
~ Such ideas were used to justify taking land. Only much later did governments begin to recognise native rights.

Q 20 | his | Themes in World History
Which of the following statements is correct?
(1) Native peoples gave up their claims willingly
(2) There was no conflict between settlers and natives
(3) The process ended in the eighteenth century
(4) In the twentieth century, native peoples in the USA, Canada and Australia began to win back some recognition of their rights and cultures
= 4
~ Struggles and court cases slowly changed government policies, though native peoples still face many problems.
