# NCERT Class 11 Geography, Fundamentals of Physical Geography, Chapter 12: Water (Oceans)
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). November 2026.

Q 1 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
According to the chapter, about what share of the water that falls on land returns to the atmosphere through evaporation?
(1) 9 per cent
(2) 29 per cent
(3) 59 per cent
(4) 97 per cent
= 3
~ About 59 per cent returns through evaporation; the rest runs off on the surface, infiltrates the ground or becomes part of glaciers. This is part of the hydrological cycle.

Q 2 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The circulation of water within the earth's hydrosphere in its different forms — liquid, solid and gas — is called the:
(1) Carbon cycle
(2) Hydrological cycle
(3) Rock cycle
(4) Nitrogen cycle
= 2
~ The hydrological (water) cycle links the oceans, atmosphere, land surface, underground and living organisms through evaporation, condensation, precipitation, infiltration and runoff.

Q 3 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which of the following correctly lists the four major divisions of the ocean floor?
(1) Continental shelf, continental slope, deep sea plain and oceanic deeps
(2) Mid-oceanic ridge, seamount, guyot and atoll
(3) Continental margin, coral reef, lagoon and delta
(4) Abyssal plain, canyon, bar and spit
= 1
~ Seamounts, guyots, ridges, canyons and atolls are minor relief features; the four major divisions are the shelf, slope, deep sea plain and deeps.

Q 4 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements about the continental shelf:
1. It is the shallowest part of the ocean, with an average gradient of 1° or even less.
2. Its average width is about 80 km, but it is almost absent along coasts like Chile.
3. The Siberian shelf in the Arctic Ocean, the largest in the world, stretches up to about 1,500 km.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ All three are in the chapter. The shelf ends at a very steep slope called the shelf break, and its depth varies from about 30 m to 600 m.

Q 5 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Continental shelves are important economically because:
(1) They are the deepest parts of the ocean
(2) They are covered with sediments and are a source of fossil fuels
(3) They have no marine life
(4) They lie only in polar regions
= 2
~ Thick sediments brought by rivers, glaciers and wind accumulate on the shelves; over long periods they become a source of fossil fuels.

Q 6 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The continental slope connects the continental shelf and the ocean basins. Its gradient is about:
(1) Less than 1°
(2) 2°–5°
(3) 20°–25°
(4) 45°
= 2
~ The slope begins where the shelf drops sharply; its depth ranges from about 200 m to 3,000 m, and canyons and trenches are seen in this region.

Q 7 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The deep sea plains, the flattest and smoothest regions of the world, lie at depths of about:
(1) 30–600 m
(2) 200–3,000 m
(3) 3,000–6,000 m
(4) More than 11,000 m
= 3
~ Deep sea plains are gently sloping areas of the ocean basins, covered with fine-grained sediments like clay and silt.

Q 8 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Of the 57 oceanic deeps that have been explored, how many are in the Pacific Ocean according to the chapter?
(1) 6
(2) 19
(3) 32
(4) 57
= 3
~ The chapter lists 32 in the Pacific, 19 in the Atlantic and 6 in the Indian Ocean. Deeps occur at the base of continental slopes and along island arcs, often with volcanoes and strong earthquakes.

Q 9 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Iceland is cited in the chapter as an example of:
(1) A guyot
(2) A continental shelf
(3) An atoll
(4) A part of the mid-oceanic ridge rising above the ocean surface
= 4
~ Mid-oceanic ridges are two chains of mountains separated by a depression; some peaks reach about 2,500 m and a few, like Iceland, rise above the sea.

Q 10 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
A mountain with a pointed summit rising from the sea floor but not reaching the surface, usually volcanic in origin, is called a:
(1) Atoll
(2) Guyot
(3) Seamount
(4) Submarine canyon
= 3
~ Seamounts may be 3,000–4,500 m tall; the Emperor seamount, an extension of the Hawaiian Islands, is the example. Flat-topped seamounts are guyots.

Q 11 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which of the following is described in the chapter as the best known submarine canyon?
(1) Grand Canyon
(2) Mariana Canyon
(3) Colorado Canyon
(4) Hudson Canyon
= 4
~ Submarine canyons are deep valleys cut across the continental shelves and slopes, often extending from the mouths of large rivers. The Hudson Canyon is the best known.

Q 12 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Low islands found in tropical oceans, consisting of coral reefs surrounding a central depression, are called:
(1) Guyots
(2) Deltas
(3) Seamounts
(4) Atolls
= 4
~ The central depression of an atoll may be part of the sea, called a lagoon, or may enclose fresh, brackish or highly saline water.

Q 13 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which one of the following is NOT a factor affecting the distribution of ocean water temperature, according to the chapter?
(1) Latitude
(2) Unequal distribution of land and water
(3) Prevailing winds
(4) The tides
= 4
~ The factors listed are latitude, unequal distribution of land and water, prevailing winds and ocean currents.

Q 14 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements about the three-layer temperature structure of the oceans in tropical regions:
1. The top layer of warm water is about 500 m thick with temperatures of 20°–25°C.
2. The middle layer, the thermocline, shows a rapid decrease in temperature with depth.
3. In the Arctic and Antarctic, there are three distinct layers, the same as in the tropics.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1
~ In polar areas surface water is close to 0°C, so temperature changes very little with depth and there is only one layer of cold water.

Q 15 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The average temperature of the surface water of the oceans is about 27°C. It decreases from the equator towards the poles at a rate of about:
(1) 0.5°C per degree of latitude
(2) 5°C per degree of latitude
(3) 0.05°C per degree of latitude
(4) 2°C per degree of latitude
= 1
~ The average is about 22°C at 20° latitude, 14°C at 40° and close to 0°C near the poles.

Q 16 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Why do the oceans of the Northern Hemisphere record higher temperatures than those of the Southern Hemisphere?
(1) They are deeper
(2) They are in contact with a larger extent of land
(3) They receive more rainfall
(4) They have more icebergs
= 2
~ The Northern Hemisphere has much more land, which heats the adjoining oceans more. Average annual surface temperatures are about 19°C in the Northern and 16°C in the Southern Hemisphere.

Q 17 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Salinity is calculated as the amount of salt in grams dissolved in 1,000 grams of sea water. What value is used as the upper limit to demarcate brackish water?
(1) 2.47 ‰
(2) 24.7 ‰
(3) 35 ‰
(4) 41 ‰
= 2
~ Salinity is expressed in parts per thousand (‰). Water with up to 24.7 ‰ is treated as brackish; the normal open ocean ranges from 33 ‰ to 37 ‰.

Q 18 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which of the following water bodies is mentioned in the chapter as having the highest salinity, about 330 ‰?
(1) Dead Sea
(2) Great Salt Lake
(3) Lake Van in Turkey
(4) Red Sea
= 3
~ The chapter gives Lake Van (330 ‰), the Dead Sea (238 ‰) and the Great Salt Lake (220 ‰). The land-locked Red Sea has about 41 ‰.

Q 19 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements about salinity in the Indian Ocean:
1. The average salinity of the Indian Ocean is about 35 ‰.
2. The Bay of Bengal has low salinity because of the influx of river water from the Ganga.
3. The Arabian Sea has lower salinity than the Bay of Bengal.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1
~ The Arabian Sea has higher salinity because of high evaporation and low influx of fresh water.

Q 20 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which of the following correctly explains salinity differences in seas around Europe?
(1) The North Sea has higher salinity because of the saline water brought by the North Atlantic Drift, while the Baltic Sea has low salinity because of large river inflow
(2) The Baltic Sea has the highest salinity because of evaporation
(3) The Black Sea has very high salinity because no rivers flow into it
(4) The Mediterranean Sea has very low salinity because of heavy rainfall
= 1
~ The Mediterranean has high salinity due to high evaporation, and the Black Sea has very low salinity due to enormous freshwater inflow from rivers.
