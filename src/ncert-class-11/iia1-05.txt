# NCERT Class 11 Fine Arts (Art & Culture), An Introduction to Indian Art Part I, Chapter 5: Later Mural Traditions
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Badami in Karnataka was the capital of which dynasty?
(1) The Rashtrakutas
(2) The early Chalukyas
(3) The Pallavas
(4) The Kadambas
= 2
~ Badami was the capital of the early Chalukyan dynasty, which, the chapter says, ruled the region from 543 to 598 CE after the decline of the Vakatakas.

Q 2 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Who patronised the excavation of the Badami caves?
(1) Mangalesha
(2) Pulakesi II
(3) Vikramaditya II
(4) Kirtivarman II
= 1
~ The Chalukya king Mangalesha, younger son of Pulakesi I and brother of Kirtivarman I, patronised the excavation of the Badami caves.

Q 3 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Cave No. 4 at Badami, whose inscription records the dedication of an image of Vishnu, is popularly known as:
(1) The Shiva cave
(2) The Jain cave
(3) The Vishnu cave
(4) Ravana Phadi
= 3
~ The inscription of 578–579 CE in Cave No. 4 shows the patron's Vaishnava affiliation, so the cave is popularly called the Vishnu cave.

Q 4 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The surviving fragment of painting on the vaulted roof of the front mandapa of the Badami Vishnu cave shows:
(1) A battle from the Ramayana
(2) A Jataka story
(3) Shiva as Tripurantaka
(4) A palace scene with Kirtivarman, his wife and feudatories watching a dance
= 4
~ The painting shows Kirtivarman seated in his palace with his wife and feudatories watching a dance; Indra and his retinue appear towards the corner. The style extends the Ajanta tradition.

Q 5 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The Pallava king Mahendravarman I is given which titles in the Mandagapattu inscription?
(1) Vichitrachitta, Chitrakarapuli and Chaityakari
(2) Mamalla and Rajasimha
(3) Gangaikonda and Mummadi
(4) Vikramaditya and Kavirajamarga
= 1
~ The Mandagapattu inscription calls Mahendravarman I Vichitrachitta (curious-minded), Chitrakarapuli (tiger among artists) and Chaityakari (temple builder). He built temples at Panamalai, Mandagapattu and Kanchipuram.

Q 6 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The paintings of Somaskanda in the Kanchipuram temple were patronised by which Pallava king?
(1) Mahendravarman I
(2) Narasimhavarman I
(3) Rajasimha
(4) Nandivarman II
= 3
~ The Kanchipuram paintings were patronised by Rajasimha. Their faces are round and large, the lines rhythmic and the ornamentation richer than before.

Q 7 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The paintings at Sittanavasal belong to which tradition and dynasty?
(1) Buddhist, Pallava
(2) Jaina, Pandya
(3) Shaiva, Chola
(4) Vaishnava, Nayaka
= 2
~ The Pandya period paintings survive at the Tirumalaipuram caves and the Jaina caves of Sittanavasal, on the ceilings of shrines, in verandas and on brackets.

Q 8 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The paintings on the veranda pillars at Sittanavasal show:
(1) Kings in a royal procession
(2) Scenes of Krishna's childhood
(3) Jataka stories
(4) Dancing celestial nymphs
= 4
~ The pillars show dancing celestial nymphs, their contours firmly drawn in vermilion red on a lighter background, with yellow bodies and subtle modelling.

Q 9 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
In the Brihadeswara temple at Thanjavur, the Chola paintings are found:
(1) On the ceiling of the main mandapa
(2) On the outer walls of the gopuram
(3) On the pillars of the Nandi pavilion
(4) On the walls of the narrow passage surrounding the shrine
= 4
~ The Chola paintings are on the walls of the narrow passage around the shrine. Two layers were found; the upper one belongs to the sixteenth-century Nayaka period.

A. Brihadeswara temple, Thanjavur — 1. Rajaraja Chola

Select the correct answer:
(1) A-1, B-2, C-3
(2) A-2, B-1, C-3

Q 11 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Consider the following themes:
1. Shiva in Kailash, Shiva as Tripurantaka and Shiva as Nataraja
2. A portrait of the patron Rajaraja with his mentor Kuruvar
3. Scenes from the life of Vardhamana Mahavira
Which of the above are among the themes of the Chola paintings in the Brihadeswara temple?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 2 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 3
~ Scenes from Mahavira's life are among the early paintings at Thiruparakunram, not at Thanjavur. The Brihadeswara paintings show Shiva's forms, dancing figures and Rajaraja with Kuruvar.

Q 12 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The Chola dynasty, which ruled from the ninth to the thirteenth century, reached its zenith in which century?
(1) Ninth century
(2) Eleventh century
(3) Thirteenth century
(4) Seventh century
= 2
~ The Chola power reached its zenith in the eleventh century, when the great temples at Thanjavur and Gangaikonda Cholapuram were built.

Q 13 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Fourteenth-century paintings representing the early phase of the Vijayanagara style are found at:
(1) Tiruparakunram, near Trichy
(2) Lepakshi
(3) Sittanavasal
(4) Panamalai
= 1
~ The paintings at Tiruparakunram near Trichy, done in the fourteenth century, represent the early phase of Vijayanagara painting.

Q 14 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The ceiling paintings in the mandapa of the Virupaksha temple at Hampi include:
(1) Kathakali dancers
(2) The Mughal court
(3) The twenty-four Jain Tirthankaras
(4) Vidyaranya, the spiritual teacher of Bukkaraya Harsha, carried in a palanquin

Q 16 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Which of the following is NOT a feature of the Vijayanagara style of painting described in the chapter?
(1) Faces mostly in profile
(2) Large frontal eyes
(3) Three-dimensional figures with heavy modelling
(4) Compositions arranged in rectilinear compartments
= 3
~ Vijayanagara figures are two-dimensional, with profile faces, large frontal eyes and narrow waists, arranged in rectilinear compartments.

Q 17 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
A Nayaka painting panel narrating the story of Muchukunda is found at:
(1) Tiruvarur
(2) Chidambaram
(3) Chengam
(4) Srirangam
= 1
~ Tiruvarur has the Muchukunda panel. Chidambaram has panels on Shiva as Bhikshatana Murti and Vishnu as Mohini.

Q 18 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The Sri Krishna temple at Chengam in Arcot district, representing the late Nayaka phase, has 60 panels narrating:
(1) The Mahabharata
(2) The Bhagavata Purana
(3) The Shiva Purana
(4) The Ramayana
= 4
~ The 60 panels at Chengam narrate the Ramayana. Nayaka painting extends the Vijayanagara style with small regional changes.

Q 19 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
The Kerala mural painters drew elements from which local art forms?
(1) Mughal and Rajput miniatures
(2) Pala and Nepalese manuscripts
(3) Kathakali and kalam ezhuthu
(4) Persian carpets
= 3
~ Kerala painters borrowed from Kathakali and kalam ezhuthu (ritual floor painting), while selectively adopting elements of the Nayaka and Vijayanagara schools. Their colours are vibrant and figures three-dimensional.

Q 20 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I
Consider the following statements about Kerala murals:
1. Kerala painters developed their own pictorial language between the sixteenth and eighteenth centuries.
2. More than sixty sites with murals have been found.
3. They were painted mostly on palm-leaf manuscripts.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 2
~ Statement 3 is wrong: Kerala murals are on shrine walls, temple cloister walls and some palace walls, such as the Dutch palace in Kochi, Krishnapuram palace at Kayamkulam and Padmanabhapuram palace.
