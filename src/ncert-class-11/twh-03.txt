# NCERT Class 11 History, Themes in World History, Chapter 3: Nomadic Empires
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | his | Themes in World History
Genghis Khan's original name was:
(1) Temujin
(2) Toghril
(3) Jamuqa
(4) Qubilai
= 1
~ Temujin was born around 1162 near the Onon river in northern Mongolia. His father Yesugei was a chieftain of the Kiyat, a family group of the Borjigid clan.

Q 2 | his | Themes in World History
In 1206, at a great assembly of Mongol chiefs (quriltai), Temujin was proclaimed Genghis Khan, meaning:
(1) The Khan of Khans of China
(2) The Oceanic Khan or Universal Ruler
(3) The Son of Heaven
(4) The Protector of Islam
= 2
~ After defeating rivals such as the Tatars, the Kereyits and his former blood-brother Jamuqa, Temujin became the leader of the Mongol peoples in 1206.

Q 3 | his | Themes in World History
Which of the following was an early ally of Temujin, ruler of the Kereyits, who later fell out with him?
(1) Hulegu
(2) Batu
(3) Ong Khan (Toghril)
(4) Timur
= 3
~ Temujin's anda (blood-brother) Jamuqa was also an early ally who became an enemy. Temujin defeated both.

Q 4 | his | Themes in World History
The Mongols were mainly:
(1) Settled rice farmers
(2) Sea traders
(3) Pastoralists who herded horses, sheep and to a lesser extent other livestock, and also hunter-gatherers in the Siberian forests
(4) Forest dwellers who did not keep animals
= 3
~ The Mongols were a diverse body of people linked by a common language. Their pastoral life on the steppe meant they moved with their herds.

Q 5 | his | Themes in World History
The Great Wall of China was built by Chinese rulers mainly:
(1) To keep out raids by nomadic peoples from the north
(2) To stop floods
(3) To mark the border with India
(4) As a royal palace
= 1
~ Nomads often raided settled lands. The wall is a reminder of how much the agrarian societies of China feared the steppe peoples.

Q 6 | his | Themes in World History
In 1218, the governor of Otrar executed Genghis Khan's envoys. This led to Mongol campaigns in 1219-21 against:
(1) The Khwarazm Shah, Sultan Muhammad, in Central Asia and eastern Iran
(2) The Song dynasty of China
(3) The Mamluks of Egypt
(4) The Delhi Sultanate
= 1
~ The campaigns destroyed cities such as Otrar, Bukhara, Samarqand, Balkh, Gurganj, Merv, Nishapur and Herat.

Q 7 | his | Themes in World History
While chasing Jalaluddin, the son of the Khwarazm Shah, Mongol forces reached:
(1) The Ganga
(2) The Nile
(3) The Volga
(4) The Indus
= 4
~ Genghis Khan pursued Jalaluddin into Afghanistan and the Sind region, then turned back home through Uighur territory.

Q 8 | his | Themes in World History
Genghis Khan organised his army on the decimal system. A unit of 10,000 soldiers was called a:
(1) Arban
(2) Tumen
(3) Jaghun
(4) Minqan
= 2
~ The units were arban (10), jaghun (100), minqan (1,000) and tumen (10,000). Genghis Khan broke up old tribal groups and mixed people from different groups in the new units.

Q 9 | his | Themes in World History
The Mongol "yam" was:
(1) A tax on land
(2) A type of tent
(3) A fast courier system with fresh horses and riders at fixed posts, to carry news and orders across the empire
(4) The Mongol court language
= 3
~ The courier system was paid for by contributions from the pastoral people, through a tax called qubcur. It kept the vast empire connected.

Q 10 | his | Themes in World History
The code of laws said to have been laid down by Genghis Khan, which the later Mongols used to claim their identity, was called the:
(1) Shariat
(2) Ulus
(3) Paiza
(4) Yasa
= 4
~ The Yasa first meant customs and decrees. Over time, the Mongols used it to bind together a confederacy of very different people.

Q 11 | his | Themes in World History
Match the sons of Genghis Khan with what they received:
A. Jochi           1. The Mongol homeland
B. Chaghatai       2. The Russian steppes
C. Ogodei          3. The Transoxanian steppe
D. Toluy           4. Became the Great Khan
(1) A-1, B-3, C-4, D-2
(2) A-3, B-2, C-1, D-4
(3) A-2, B-3, C-4, D-1
(4) A-4, B-1, C-2, D-3
= 3
~ By Mongol custom, the youngest son, Toluy, got the original homeland. Ogodei, chosen by his father, became the Great Khan.

Q 12 | his | Themes in World History
In 1260, a Mongol army was defeated at Ain Jalut in Syria by:
(1) The Mamluks of Egypt
(2) The Song Chinese
(3) The Russians
(4) The Delhi Sultans
= 1
~ The defeat checked the westward expansion of the Mongols into the Mediterranean world.

Q 13 | his | Themes in World History
Who among the following sacked Baghdad in 1258 and founded the line of Il-Khanid rulers in Iran?
(1) Batu
(2) Hulegu
(3) Mongke
(4) Qubilai
= 2
~ Hulegu was a son of Toluy. His descendants ruled Iran as the Il-Khans.

Q 14 | his | Themes in World History
Ghazan Khan (1295-1304), the Il-Khanid ruler of Iran, is remembered for:
(1) Destroying all the cities of Iran
(2) Founding the Yuan dynasty
(3) Defeating the Mamluks at Ain Jalut
(4) Ordering the Mongols to stop harassing peasants and to protect them, after converting to Islam
= 4
~ His speech to his commanders shows the change from nomadic raids to ruling over settled farming people.

Q 15 | his | Themes in World History
Who among the following Mongol rulers founded the Yuan dynasty in China?
(1) Ogodei
(2) Qubilai Khan
(3) Hulegu
(4) Batu
= 2
~ Qubilai was a grandson of Genghis Khan. Under him, the Mongols ruled all of China.

Q 16 | his | Themes in World History
Consider the following statements about sources on the Mongols:
1. Ata-Malik Juwaini wrote a history of the world conqueror (Genghis Khan).
2. Rashiduddin, a minister of the Il-Khanid ruler Ghazan Khan, compiled a history using Mongol sources.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Neither 1 nor 2
(4) Both 1 and 2
= 4
~ Most of what we know about the Mongols comes from chroniclers and travellers in the settled societies they conquered, such as Persian, Chinese and European writers.

Q 17 | his | Themes in World History
The Mongols issued a pass or passport to envoys and merchants travelling across the empire. It was called the:
(1) Quriltai
(2) Anda
(3) Paiza (gerege)
(4) Noyan
= 3
~ The pass allowed safe passage and showed how the Mongols encouraged trade along the Silk Route.

Q 18 | his | Themes in World History
Timur, the ruler of Central Asia in the fourteenth century, could not take the title of "Khan" because he was not a descendant of Genghis Khan. He took instead the title:
(1) Guregen, "royal son-in-law"
(2) Sultan of Hind
(3) Shahinshah
(4) Il-Khan
= 1
~ Timur married a princess of Genghis Khan's family. His claims show the great prestige of Genghis Khan's lineage.

Q 19 | his | Themes in World History
How is Genghis Khan seen in Mongolia today?
(1) As a villain
(2) He is forgotten
(3) Only as a religious teacher
(4) As a great national hero, after Mongolia became independent of Russian control in the early 1990s
= 4
~ For the Mongols, Genghis Khan is a symbol of national identity. Outside Mongolia, writers in conquered lands often described his campaigns as destructive.

Q 20 | his | Themes in World History
Which of the following best explains the Mongols' success in controlling a vast empire, according to the chapter?
(1) They used ships to move troops
(2) A disciplined, mobile cavalry army organised in decimal units, efficient communication, and the use of administrators and experts from conquered peoples
(3) They converted everyone to one religion
(4) They had the largest population in Asia
= 2
~ The Mongols also tolerated many religions and used Chinese, Persian and Uighur administrators. Their empire brought Europe and Asia into closer contact.
