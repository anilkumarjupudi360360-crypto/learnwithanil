# NCERT Class 11 Political Science, Political Theory, Chapter 7: Nationalism
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | pol | Political Theory
In nineteenth-century Europe, nationalism played a part in:
(1) Only the break-up of states
(2) Only the unification of states
(3) Ending all wars
(4) Both the unification of smaller kingdoms into larger states, like Germany and Italy, and the break-up of large empires
= 4
~ Nationalism brought together small states into Germany and Italy, and in the early twentieth century it helped break up the Austro-Hungarian, Russian and Ottoman empires.

Q 2 | pol | Political Theory
How does the chapter describe a nation?
(1) A group of people related by blood, like a family
(2) A community largely based on shared beliefs, history, territory and political ideals, whose members may never meet one another
(3) Any group of people who speak the same language
(4) An association like a club, formed by contract
= 2
~ A nation is unlike a family or a club. Its members are bound by a sense of belonging built on shared beliefs, a common history and aspirations for a shared political future.

Q 3 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following does the chapter NOT treat as an essential basis of nationhood?
(1) Shared beliefs
(2) Shared history
(3) A single common religion
(4) Shared political ideals
= 3
~ The chapter argues that a nation does not need a single religion, race or language. Many nations, like India, include many religions and languages and are united by shared political ideals and history.

Q 4 | pol | Political Theory
The claim of a national group to govern itself and decide its own future is called:
(1) The right to national self-determination
(2) The harm principle
(3) Universal franchise
(4) Proportional representation
= 1
~ National self-determination is the demand that a nation should have its own state or self-government. After the First World War it was used to redraw the map of Europe.

Q 5 | pol | Political Theory
After the First World War, the idea of "one culture, one state" was used to redraw state boundaries in Europe. What problem did this create, according to the chapter?
(1) No state was ever formed
(2) All states became very small islands
(3) It was impossible to give every cultural group its own state, and new minorities were created within the new states
(4) All minorities disappeared
= 3
~ People of different cultures lived mixed together, so new borders left many as minorities in the new states. This caused tensions, migration and conflict.

Q 6 | pol | Political Theory
The chapter suggests that instead of creating a separate state for every cultural group, democracies should:
(1) Recognise and protect the rights and identities of minorities within existing states
(2) Expel minorities
(3) Ban all regional languages
(4) Create one state for every language
= 1
~ The chapter favours pluralism: accommodating diversity through rights for minorities and federal arrangements, so that groups feel secure without breaking up states.

Q 7 | pol | Political Theory
Which Indian poet and thinker criticised narrow nationalism, saying that patriotism cannot be our final spiritual shelter and that his refuge was humanity?
(1) Mahatma Gandhi
(2) Subhas Chandra Bose
(3) Bal Gangadhar Tilak
(4) Rabindranath Tagore
= 4
~ Tagore opposed colonial rule but warned against a nationalism that hates other peoples. He valued the universal ideals of humanity above the nation.

Q 8 | pol | Political Theory
Which statement about Tagore's views on nationalism is correct?
(1) He supported British rule in India
(2) He opposed colonial rule but criticised the kind of nationalism that rejects other cultures and glorifies the nation above humanity
(3) He wanted India to conquer other countries
(4) He was against any contact with Western ideas
= 2
~ Tagore believed India could learn from the West while resisting colonialism. He feared that aggressive nationalism would lead to hatred and violence.

Q 9 | pol | Political Theory
The chapter notes that nationalism has had both positive and negative effects. Which of the following is a negative effect it mentions?
(1) Bitter conflicts and wars, and the oppression of minorities in the name of the nation
(2) The end of colonial rule
(3) Unity among diverse people in a freedom struggle
(4) The creation of democratic constitutions
= 1
~ Nationalism inspired freedom struggles but has also been used to justify war and to suppress groups seen as outsiders.

Q 10 | pol | Political Theory
In Asia and Africa, nationalism was closely associated with:
(1) The growth of monarchies
(2) The spread of colonial empires
(3) Struggles against colonial rule for independence
(4) The rejection of all forms of statehood
= 3
~ Anti-colonial movements in countries like India drew on nationalism to unite people against foreign rule and to claim self-government.

Q 11 | pol | Political Theory
Why does the chapter say a nation is an "imagined" community?
(1) Because nations do not exist at all
(2) Because members of a nation will never know most of their fellow members, yet they feel bound together by shared beliefs and aspirations
(3) Because nations exist only in films
(4) Because only poets belong to nations
= 2
~ Imagined does not mean false. It means the bond among millions of people who never meet is built on shared ideas, stories and symbols.

Q 12 | pol | Political Theory
The chapter says that the territory a nation claims is important because:
(1) It is always very small
(2) It has no value for national identity
(3) It is fixed forever and cannot be disputed
(4) Nations often see a particular territory as their homeland, tied to their history and memories
= 4
~ A homeland carries emotional meaning for a nation. Competing claims on the same territory are one reason for conflicts between nations.

Q 13 | pol | Political Theory
What does the chapter mean by "shared political ideals" as a basis of nationhood?
(1) Everybody must vote for the same party
(2) A common commitment to values such as democracy, secularism and liberalism, which members of a nation accept
(3) Everybody must follow the same religion
(4) Following the orders of one leader
= 2
~ A nation can be bound by a shared vision of its political future. In India, commitment to democracy and the Constitution is part of what holds diverse people together.

Q 14 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following best describes the Indian approach to national identity, as discussed in the chapter?
(1) One religion and one language for all
(2) A nation for only the majority community
(3) Abolition of all regional identities
(4) Unity in diversity: a national identity that accommodates many religions, languages and regional identities
= 4
~ India's national movement brought together people of different backgrounds. The Constitution protects diverse identities while building a common citizenship.

Q 15 | pol | Political Theory
"Nationalism and pluralism" in the chapter refers to the idea that:
(1) There must be one nationality in every state
(2) Every group must form its own country
(3) A nation can contain many cultural groups, whose rights and identities should be respected
(4) Minorities should have no rights
= 3
~ Pluralism means accepting many identities within one nation. Democracies try to accommodate them through rights for minorities and devolution of power.

Q 16 | pol | Political Theory
Why do many national groups that demand self-determination want their own state, according to the chapter?
(1) Because they feel their culture, rights and identity would be better protected under their own government
(2) Because international law requires every group to have a state
(3) Because they want to stop holding elections
(4) Because they want to join an empire
= 1
~ Groups who feel excluded or oppressed often see statehood as the only guarantee of their identity. The chapter argues that democracies can often address such fears through rights and autonomy.

Q 17 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following empires broke up in the early twentieth century, partly due to nationalist movements?
(1) The Austro-Hungarian and Ottoman empires
(2) The Mughal and Vijayanagara empires
(3) The Roman and Persian empires
(4) The Maurya and Gupta empires
= 1
~ After the First World War, the Austro-Hungarian and Ottoman empires broke into several new states based on national identities. The Russian empire also collapsed in 1917.

Q 18 | pol | Political Theory
According to the chapter, national identity in a democracy should be:
(1) Based on loyalty to one religion
(2) Fixed by the government and never questioned
(3) Inclusive, recognising the equal rights of all members including minorities
(4) Defined only by language
= 3
~ An inclusive national identity makes all citizens feel they belong. Excluding minorities from the idea of the nation creates insecurity and conflict.

Q 19 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following is an example of a national symbol that helps create a shared sense of belonging, as mentioned at the start of the chapter?
(1) A private company logo
(2) A local market
(3) A shopping festival
(4) The Republic Day parade and the national anthem
= 4
~ Celebrations, anthems and flags build a shared identity. The chapter begins by noting how such occasions express nationalism.

Q 20 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following statements reflects the chapter's overall view of nationalism?
(1) Nationalism is always harmful
(2) Nationalism has inspired freedom and unity but can also lead to intolerance; a democratic nationalism respects diversity and minority rights
(3) Nationalism has no place in modern politics
(4) Nationalism requires that only one culture survive in a country
= 2
~ The chapter asks students to recognise both sides: nationalism helped end colonial rule and unite people, but in narrow forms it has caused exclusion and violence.
