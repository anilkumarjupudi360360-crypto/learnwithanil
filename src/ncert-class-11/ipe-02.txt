Q 1 | geo | India: Physical Environment
On the basis of geological structure and formations, India is divided into which three geological divisions?
(1) The Peninsular Block, the Himalayas and other Peninsular mountains, and the Indo-Ganga-Brahmaputra Plain
(2) The Thar Desert, the Deccan Plateau and the Islands
(3) The Western Ghats, the Eastern Ghats and the Coastal Plains
(4) The Northern Plains, the Central Highlands and the Andaman Islands
= 1
~ These three geological divisions differ in rock type, age and stability.

Q 2 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Consider the following statements about the Peninsular Block:
1. It is made up mostly of gneisses and granites and has stood like a rigid block since the Cambrian period.
2. The rift valleys of the Narmada, Tapi and Mahanadi and the Satpura block mountains are examples of block faulting in it.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 3
~ The block is part of the Indo-Australian plate. Except for the western coast that sank below the sea, its original basement has stayed largely unaffected.

Q 3 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Which of the following are mentioned as relict and residual mountains of the Peninsular Block?
(1) Patkai Bum, Naga hills and Mizo hills
(2) Shiwalik, Pir Panjal and Zaskar ranges
(3) Aravali hills, Nallamala hills, Javadi hills and Mahendragiri hills
(4) Karakoram and Ladakh ranges
= 3
~ The Peninsula has old, worn-down mountains such as the Aravali, Nallamala, Javadi, Veliconda, Palkonda and Mahendragiri hills.

Q 4 | geo | India: Physical Environment
How do the Himalayas differ geologically from the Peninsular Block?
(1) They are older and more stable
(2) They are young, weak and flexible in structure, and are still subject to endogenic and exogenic forces
(3) They are made entirely of basalt
(4) They have no rivers
= 2
~ The young Himalayas are tectonic in origin and are dissected by fast-flowing rivers in their youthful stage, forming gorges, V-shaped valleys, rapids and waterfalls.

Q 5 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Which of the following correctly lists the three parallel ranges of the Himalayas from north to south?
(1) Shiwalik, Lesser Himalaya, Greater Himalaya
(2) Greater Himalaya, Lesser Himalaya, Shiwalik
(3) Lesser Himalaya, Greater Himalaya, Shiwalik
(4) Karakoram, Zaskar, Pir Panjal
= 2
~ The Greater (Central axial) Himalaya is the northernmost, the Lesser Himalaya lies in the middle and the Shiwalik forms the outermost foothills.

Q 6 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Karewa formations, known for the cultivation of Zafran (a local variety of saffron), are found in:
(1) Darjiling and Sikkim Himalayas
(2) Arunachal Himalayas
(3) Kashmir Himalayas

= 3
~ Karewas are thick deposits of glacial clay and other materials embedded with moraines in the Kashmir valley.

Q 8 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Which of the following pairs of lakes in the Kashmir Himalayas are salt water lakes?
(1) Dal and Wular
(2) Pangong Tso and Tso Moriri
(3) Dal and Pangong Tso

Q 9 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Which is the largest of the "duns" (longitudinal valleys between the Lesser Himalaya and the Shiwalik) mentioned in the chapter?
(1) Kota dun
(2) Nalagarh dun
(3) Dehra Dun
(4) Jammu dun
= 3
~ Dehra Dun is the largest, about 35–45 km long and 22–25 km wide. Other duns include Chandigarh-Kalka, Nalagarh, Harike and Kota.

Q 10 | geo | India: Physical Environment
The Bhotia tribes of the Himachal and Uttarakhand Himalayas migrate in summer to "Bugyals". Bugyals are:
(1) River confluences
(2) Glacial lakes
(3) Hill stations in the Lesser Himalaya
(4) Summer grasslands in the higher reaches
= 4
~ Bhotias are nomadic groups living in the Great Himalayan range; they move to the high summer pastures called bugyals and return to the valleys in winter.

Q 11 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Why did the British introduce tea plantations in the Darjiling and Sikkim Himalayas?
(1) Because of the presence of karewa formations
(2) Because the area had no rainfall
(3) Because it was a cold desert
(4) Because of moderate slopes, thick soil with high organic content, well-distributed rainfall and mild winters
= 4
~ These physical conditions favour tea. The higher reaches are inhabited by the Lepcha tribes, while the southern part has a mixed population.

Q 12 | geo | India: Physical Environment

(1) Satluj and Beas
(2) Tista and Barak

Q 14 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Loktak lake, an important lake in the Eastern Hills, is located in:
(1) Mizoram
(2) Tripura
(3) Nagaland
(4) Manipur
= 4
~ The Eastern Hills are known as Patkai Bum, the Naga hills, the Manipur hills and the Mizo or Lushai hills. Loktak lies in the centre of Manipur.

Q 15 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Consider the following statements about the Northern Plains from north to south:
1. The Bhabar is a narrow belt, about 8–10 km wide, at the foot of the Shiwalik where streams deposit heavy rocks and boulders and disappear.
2. The Tarai lies north of the Bhabar and is a dry, stony belt where streams disappear.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 1
~ The Tarai lies south of the Bhabar; it is marshy and swampy because the streams re-emerge there. It is the Bhabar where streams disappear among boulders.

Q 16 | geo | India: Physical Environment
The Peninsular Plateau is bounded by the Delhi ridge in the north-west, the Rajmahal hills in the east, the Gir range in the west and which hills in the south?
(1) Nilgiri hills
(2) Shevaroy hills
(3) Javadi hills
(4) Cardamom hills

Q 17 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Which is the highest peak of the Peninsular Plateau?
(1) Dodabetta
(2) Saddle Peak
(3) Mahendragiri
(4) Anaimudi
= 4
~ Anaimudi (2,695 m) on the Anaimalai hills of the Western Ghats is the highest peak, followed by Dodabetta (2,637 m) on the Nilgiri hills.

Q 18 | geo | India: Physical Environment
How was the Meghalaya plateau separated from the main Peninsular Block?
(1) By the uplift of the Aravalis
(2) By a huge fault between the Rajmahal hills and the Meghalaya plateau, created by the north-eastward push of the Indian plate and later filled by river deposits
(3) By a volcanic eruption
(4) By glaciers that carved a valley
= 2
~ The Meghalaya plateau, divided into the Garo, Khasi and Jaintia hills, and the Karbi Anglong plateau now stand detached from the Peninsular Block.

Q 19 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Wood fossils at Aakal and marine deposits around Brahmsar near Jaisalmer are cited as evidence that:
(1) The Indian Desert was under the sea during the Mesozoic era
(2) The desert was once covered by glaciers
(3) The desert was formed by volcanic activity
(4) The Aravalis were once higher than the Himalayas
= 1
~ The wood fossils are estimated to be about 180 million years old. Today the desert has longitudinal dunes, barchans, mushroom rocks and oases, and very low rainfall.

Q 20 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Consider the following statements:
1. The western coastal plain is an emergent coast with well-developed deltas.
2. The eastern coastal plain is an emergent coast with well-developed deltas, and its continental shelf extends up to about 500 km into the sea.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 2
~ The western coastal plain is a submerged coast with natural harbours such as Kandla, Mazagaon, JLN port, Marmagao, Mangalore and Cochin. The eastern plain is the emergent one with deltas and a wide continental shelf.
