# NCERT Class 11 Geography, India: Physical Environment, Chapter 3: Drainage System
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). November 2026.

Q 1 | geo | India: Physical Environment
The boundary line separating one drainage basin from another is called a:
(1) Catchment area
(2) Watershed
(3) Water divide plain
(4) Delta
= 2
~ The area drained by a single river is its catchment area or drainage basin; the line separating two basins is the watershed (water divide).

Q 2 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Match the drainage patterns with their examples given in the chapter:
A. Dendritic — 1. Rivers flowing into Loktak lake in Manipur
B. Radial — 2. Rivers of the northern plain
C. Trellis — 3. Rivers originating from the Amarkantak range
D. Centripetal — 4. Rivers in the upper part of the Himalayan region
Select the correct answer:
(1) A-2, B-3, C-4, D-1
(2) A-3, B-2, C-1, D-4
(3) A-2, B-4, C-3, D-1
(4) A-1, B-3, C-4, D-2
= 1
~ Dendritic resembles tree branches, radial flows out from a hill in all directions, trellis has parallel primary tributaries joined at right angles, and centripetal drains into a central lake or depression.

Q 3 | geo | India: Physical Environment
About what share of India's drainage area is oriented towards the Bay of Bengal?
(1) 23 per cent
(2) 50 per cent
(3) 77 per cent
(4) 90 per cent
= 3
~ Nearly 77 per cent drains to the Bay of Bengal (Ganga, Brahmaputra, Mahanadi, Krishna, etc.), while 23 per cent (Indus, Narmada, Tapi, Mahi, Periyar) drains into the Arabian Sea.

Q 4 | geo | India: Physical Environment
On the basis of catchment area, a "major river basin" in India has a catchment area of more than:
(1) 2,000 sq km
(2) 10,000 sq km
(3) 20,000 sq km
(4) 1,00,000 sq km
= 3
~ Major basins exceed 20,000 sq km (14 basins), medium basins have 2,000–20,000 sq km (44 basins) and minor basins have less than 2,000 sq km.

Q 5 | geo | India: Physical Environment
According to one view in the chapter, a mighty river called the "Shiwalik" or "Indo-Brahma" once flowed along the entire length of the Himalaya. Which events are believed to have dismembered it into the present three systems?
(1) Pleistocene upheaval in the western Himalayas including uplift of the Potwar Plateau (Delhi Ridge), and down-thrusting of the Malda gap
(2) Formation of the Deccan Traps
(3) Rise of the Western Ghats
(4) Glaciation of the Thar desert
= 1
~ The Potwar Plateau uplift separated the Indus and Ganga systems, and the down-thrusting of the Malda gap diverted the Ganga and Brahmaputra towards the Bay of Bengal.

Q 6 | geo | India: Physical Environment
The Indus river is known in Tibet as:
(1) Tsangpo
(2) Dihang
(3) Langchen Khambab
(4) Singi Khamban (Lion's mouth)
= 4
~ The Indus rises near Bokhar Chu in the Kailash range of Tibet, where it is called Singi Khamban. Langchen Khambab is the Tibetan name of the Satluj, and Tsangpo of the Brahmaputra.

Q 7 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Under the Indus Water Treaty (1960), India can use about what share of the total discharge of the Indus river system?
(1) 10 per cent
(2) 20 per cent
(3) 50 per cent
(4) 80 per cent
= 2
~ The chapter states that India can use only 20 per cent of the total discharge carried by the Indus system.

Q 10 | geo | India: Physical Environment
The Satluj, an antecedent river that passes through the Shipki La and feeds the Bhakra Nangal project, originates in:
(1) Beas Kund near Rohtang Pass
(2) Yamunotri glacier
(3) Verinag spring
(4) 'Raksas tal' near Mansarovar in Tibet
= 4
~ The Satluj rises in Raksas tal near Mansarovar at about 4,555 m, where it is called Langchen Khambab. Beas Kund is the source of the Beas.

Q 11 | geo | India: Physical Environment
The Bhagirathi and the Alaknanda meet to form the Ganga at:
(1) Haridwar
(2) Allahabad (Prayagraj)
(3) Rudraprayag
(4) Devprayag
= 4
~ The Bhagirathi rises from the Gangotri glacier near Gaumukh; after meeting the Alaknanda at Devprayag, it is called the Ganga. It enters the plains at Haridwar.

1. Its total length is about 2,525 km, shared by Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar and West Bengal.

Q 13 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Which river, the westernmost and longest tributary of the Ganga, rises in the Yamunotri glacier on the Banderpunch range?

Q 15 | geo | India: Physical Environment

Q 16 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Consider the following statements about the Brahmaputra:
1. It originates in the Chemayungdung glacier of the Kailash range near the Mansarovar lake.
2. In Tibet it is known as the Tsangpo, meaning "the purifier".
3. It enters India near Dhubri in Assam, where it is called the Siang.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1

Q 17 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Which events are believed to have shaped the present drainage system of Peninsular India?
1. Subsidence of the western flank of the Peninsula in the early tertiary period
2. Subsidence and trough faulting of the northern flank during the upheaval of the Himalayas
3. Slight tilting of the Peninsular block from south-east to north-west
Select the correct answer:
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1
~ The tilt was from north-west to south-east, which turned most rivers towards the Bay of Bengal. The Narmada and Tapi flow in trough faults, which is why they lack deltas.

Q 18 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Which is the largest Peninsular river system, also called the "Dakshin Ganga"?
(1) Krishna
(2) Godavari
(3) Mahanadi
(4) Kaveri
= 2
~ The Godavari rises in Nashik district of Maharashtra, is about 1,465 km long and drains about 3.13 lakh sq km; its tributaries include the Penganga, Indravati, Pranhita and Manjra.

Q 19 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Why does the Kaveri carry water throughout the year with comparatively less fluctuation than other Peninsular rivers?
(1) It is fed by Himalayan glaciers
(2) Its upper catchment gets rain from the south-west monsoon and its lower catchment from the north-east monsoon
(3) It has no tributaries
(4) It flows only through Kerala
= 2
~ The Kaveri rises in the Brahmagiri hills of Kodagu district, Karnataka. Its basin lies about 3 per cent in Kerala, 41 per cent in Karnataka and 56 per cent in Tamil Nadu.

Q 20 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Consider the following statements about the Narmada:
1. It originates near Mahabaleshwar in the Sahyadri.
2. It flows in a rift valley between the Satpura in the south and the Vindhyan range in the north.
3. It forms the Dhuandhar waterfall near Jabalpur and a broad estuary south of Bharuch.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 2
~ The Narmada rises on the western flank of the Amarkantak plateau; Mahabaleshwar is the source of the Krishna. The Narmada flows about 1,312 km into the Arabian Sea, forming a 27 km long estuary.
