# NCERT Class 11 Political Science, Indian Constitution at Work, Chapter 2: Rights in the Indian Constitution
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
A list of rights mentioned and protected by the constitution is called a:
(1) Charter of Duties
(2) Instrument of Instructions
(3) Directive Principle
(4) Bill of Rights
= 4
~ A Bill of Rights is the list of rights written into and protected by a constitution. It stops the government from acting against them and gives a remedy if they are violated.

Q 2 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
How do Fundamental Rights differ from other rights given by ordinary laws?
(1) Fundamental Rights are guaranteed and protected by the Constitution itself, and ordinary laws cannot take them away
(2) Fundamental Rights can be changed by any ordinary law of Parliament
(3) Fundamental Rights are only for citizens of states, not union territories
(4) Fundamental Rights cannot be enforced in any court
= 1
~ Ordinary legal rights are given and changed by ordinary laws. Fundamental Rights are in the Constitution: Parliament can change them only by amending the Constitution, and courts can strike down laws that violate them.

Q 3 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which of the following is NOT part of the Right to Equality in the Indian Constitution?
(1) Equality before law and equal protection of laws
(2) Abolition of untouchability
(3) Abolition of titles
(4) Freedom to form associations and unions
= 4
~ The freedom to form associations and unions is part of the Right to Freedom (Article 19). The Right to Equality covers equality before law, no discrimination, equal opportunity in public employment, abolition of untouchability and abolition of titles.

Q 4 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Reservations in jobs and education for the Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and backward classes are best explained by which idea in the chapter?
(1) Equality means treating everyone in exactly the same way in all cases
(2) Special measures for disadvantaged groups are needed to achieve real equality of opportunity, and the Constitution allows them
(3) Reservations violate the Right to Equality and are allowed only during an Emergency
(4) Reservations are a Directive Principle and cannot be enforced
= 2
~ The Constitution itself allows the state to make special provisions and reservations for socially and educationally backward classes, SCs and STs. Such measures are seen as a way to make equality of opportunity real, not a violation of it.

Q 5 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Consider the following freedoms:
1. Freedom of speech and expression
2. Freedom to assemble peacefully without arms
3. Freedom to move freely throughout the territory of India
4. Freedom to acquire, hold and dispose of property
Which of the above are guaranteed to citizens under Article 19 today?
(1) 1, 2 and 3 only
(2) 1, 2, 3 and 4
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 2 and 4 only
= 1
~ Article 19 now has six freedoms: speech and expression, assembly, association, movement, residence and settlement, and profession, occupation, trade or business. The freedom relating to property was taken out by the 44th Amendment (1978).

Q 6 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The right to property was removed from the list of Fundamental Rights in 1978. What is its status now?
(1) It has been abolished completely
(2) It is a Directive Principle
(3) It is a legal (constitutional) right under Article 300A, not a Fundamental Right
(4) It is a Fundamental Duty
= 3
~ The 44th Amendment (1978) removed the right to property from Part III. It survives as a constitutional legal right in Article 300A: no one can be deprived of property except by authority of law.

Q 7 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which article of the Constitution states that no person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law?
(1) Article 14
(2) Article 19
(3) Article 21
(4) Article 32
= 3
~ Article 21 protects life and personal liberty. Courts have read it widely to include, among other things, the right to a livelihood, shelter and a clean environment.

Q 8 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
A person arrested under ordinary law must be produced before the nearest magistrate within:
(1) 12 hours of arrest
(2) 24 hours of arrest, excluding travel time
(3) 48 hours of arrest
(4) 7 days of arrest
= 2
~ Article 22 gives an arrested person the right to know the grounds of arrest, to consult a lawyer and to be produced before a magistrate within 24 hours, not counting the time taken to travel there.

Q 9 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Detention of a person on the apprehension that he or she is likely to commit an offence, without a trial, is called:
(1) Judicial custody
(2) Habeas corpus
(3) Contempt of court
(4) Preventive detention
= 4
~ Under preventive detention a person can be held on suspicion of a likely threat to law and order or security, for a limited period. The chapter notes that it is open to misuse, which is why it is a much-debated provision.

Q 10 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The Right against Exploitation prohibits which of the following?
1. Traffic in human beings
2. Forced labour (begar)
3. Employment of children below 14 years in factories, mines and other hazardous work
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ Articles 23 and 24 forbid human trafficking, begar and other forced labour, and the employment of children below fourteen in factories, mines or other hazardous work.

Q 11 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which of the following statements about the Right to Freedom of Religion is correct?
(1) It allows the government to declare an official state religion
(2) It allows religious instruction in every educational institution fully maintained by the state
(3) Every person has the freedom to profess, practise and propagate the religion of his or her choice, subject to public order, morality and health
(4) It applies only to citizens, not to foreigners living in India
= 3
~ Freedom of religion belongs to every person, citizen or not, and is subject to public order, morality and health. India has no state religion, and no religious instruction can be given in institutions wholly maintained out of state funds.

Q 12 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Under the Cultural and Educational Rights, minorities have the right to establish and administer educational institutions of their choice. Minorities here include:
(1) Only religious minorities
(2) Only linguistic minorities
(3) Religious and linguistic minorities
(4) Only Scheduled Tribes
= 3
~ Articles 29 and 30 protect any section of citizens with a distinct language, script or culture, and give religious and linguistic minorities the right to set up and run their own educational institutions.

Q 13 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Dr. B.R. Ambedkar described which Fundamental Right as "the heart and soul" of the Constitution?
(1) Right to Equality
(2) Right to Freedom
(3) Right to Constitutional Remedies
(4) Right to Freedom of Religion
= 3
~ The Right to Constitutional Remedies (Article 32) lets a citizen go straight to the Supreme Court to enforce Fundamental Rights. Without it the other rights would be only promises, which is why Ambedkar gave it this name.

Q 14 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Match the writ with what it does:
A. Habeas corpus — 1. Orders a public official to perform a legal duty that is not being done
B. Mandamus — 2. Orders that an arrested person be produced before the court
C. Quo warranto — 3. Transfers a case from a lower court to a higher court or authority
D. Certiorari — 4. Asks a person by what authority he holds a public office
(1) A-2, B-1, C-4, D-3
(2) A-1, B-2, C-3, D-4
(3) A-2, B-4, C-1, D-3
(4) A-3, B-1, C-4, D-2
= 1
~ Habeas corpus brings an arrested person before the court; mandamus compels a public duty; quo warranto questions a person's right to hold an office; certiorari moves a case up to a higher court or authority. The fifth writ, prohibition, stops a lower court from going beyond its jurisdiction.

Q 15 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which writ is issued by a higher court to a lower court when the lower court goes beyond its jurisdiction, ordering it to stop the proceedings?
(1) Mandamus
(2) Prohibition
(3) Quo warranto
(4) Habeas corpus
= 2
~ Prohibition prevents a lower court from continuing with a case that falls outside its jurisdiction. Certiorari, by contrast, transfers a matter to the higher court or authority.

Q 16 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Consider the following statements about the National Human Rights Commission (NHRC), as described in the chapter:
1. It was set up by a law passed in 1993.
2. It can enquire into complaints of human rights violations on its own or on a petition.
3. Its decisions are final and binding on the government, like a court order.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1
~ The NHRC was created under the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993, and can investigate complaints and visit jails. It cannot itself punish: it recommends action to the government, so statement 3 is wrong.

Q 17 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Directive Principles of State Policy are described as "non-justiciable". This means that:
(1) Citizens cannot go to court to have them enforced
(2) They are not part of the Constitution
(3) Courts can strike down any law that follows them
(4) They apply only to state governments
= 1
~ No court can order the government to implement a Directive Principle. Still, they are fundamental in the governance of the country, and the government is expected to apply them while making laws and policies.

Q 18 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which of the following is a Directive Principle and NOT a Fundamental Right?
(1) Protection against untouchability
(2) Freedom of conscience
(3) Protection against forced labour
(4) Equal pay for equal work for men and women
= 4
~ Equal pay for equal work for men and women is a Directive Principle (Article 39). The other three are Fundamental Rights that can be enforced in court.

Q 19 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The list of Fundamental Duties of citizens was added to the Constitution by:
(1) The 24th Amendment, 1971
(2) The 42nd Amendment, 1976
(3) The 44th Amendment, 1978
(4) The 73rd Amendment, 1992
= 2
~ The 42nd Amendment (1976) inserted Part IVA with the Fundamental Duties, such as respecting the Constitution, the national flag and the national anthem. They are not enforceable in court, and they do not make the Fundamental Rights conditional on them.

Q 20 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
How does the chapter explain the relationship between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles?
(1) Directive Principles always override Fundamental Rights
(2) They have nothing to do with each other
(3) They often complement each other: rights restrain the government, while Directive Principles urge it to act; at times the two have conflicted, for example over the right to property
(4) Fundamental Rights are only for the government and Directive Principles only for citizens
= 3
~ Fundamental Rights protect individuals by limiting the state; Directive Principles push the state to work for social and economic justice. When policies such as land reforms clashed with the right to property, the conflict was resolved through amendments and court judgments.
