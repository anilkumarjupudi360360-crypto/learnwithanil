# NCERT Class 11 Geography, Fundamentals of Physical Geography, Chapter 11: World Climate and Climate Change
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). November 2026.

Q 1 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which three broad approaches to classifying climate are mentioned in the chapter?
(1) Empirical, genetic and applied
(2) Physical, human and regional
(3) Tropical, temperate and polar
(4) Historical, statistical and spatial
= 1
~ Empirical classification is based on observed data such as temperature and precipitation, genetic on the causes of climate, and applied on a specific purpose.

Q 2 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Koeppen's scheme of climatic classification, developed in 1918, is based mainly on:
(1) Wind direction and ocean currents
(2) Mean annual and mean monthly temperature and precipitation
(3) Soil types and natural vegetation alone
(4) Altitude and latitude alone
= 2
~ V. Koeppen related the distribution of vegetation to climate and used temperature and precipitation data to define the climate groups. It is an empirical scheme.

Q 3 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Match Koeppen's climate groups with their criteria:
A. Group A — 1. Potential evaporation exceeds precipitation
B. Group B — 2. Average temperature of the coldest month is 18°C or higher
C. Group D — 3. Average temperature of all months is below 10°C
D. Group E — 4. Average temperature of the coldest month is –3°C or below
Select the correct answer:
(1) A-2, B-1, C-4, D-3
(2) A-1, B-2, C-3, D-4
(3) A-2, B-1, C-3, D-4
(4) A-4, B-1, C-2, D-3
= 1
~ Group A is tropical, B is dry, D is cold snow forest and E is polar. Group C (warm temperate) has its coldest month between –3°C and 18°C, and H marks highland climates.

Q 4 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
In Koeppen's scheme, the small letter "s" in a climate code such as Cs indicates:
(1) No dry season
(2) Monsoon climate
(3) Dry season in summer
(4) Dry season in winter
= 3
~ The small letters show the seasonality of precipitation: f = no dry season, m = monsoon, w = winter dry season and s = summer dry season.

Q 5 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The Tropical Monsoon climate (Am) is found over:
(1) North-western Europe
(2) Central California and central Chile
(3) The Indian subcontinent, north-eastern South America and northern Australia
(4) The Sahara and Arabian deserts
= 3
~ Am climates have heavy rainfall mostly in summer and a dry winter. Central California and Chile are Mediterranean (Cs), and north-western Europe is Cfb.

Q 6 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The Tropical Wet climate (Af), with no dry season, is found in:
(1) Greenland
(2) The North Indian plains
(3) North-eastern Asia
(4) The Amazon Basin, western equatorial Africa and the islands of the East Indies
= 4
~ Af climates lie near the equator and receive rain throughout the year. They support dense tropical evergreen forests.

Q 8 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements about the Mediterranean climate (Cs):
1. It occurs roughly between 30° and 40° latitude, as in central California, central Chile and parts of south-eastern and south-western Australia.
2. It has hot, dry summers and mild, rainy winters.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 3
~ In summer these areas come under subtropical high pressure and stay dry; in winter they get rain from the westerlies. Annual precipitation ranges from about 35 to 90 cm.

Q 9 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
According to the chapter, the Humid Subtropical climate with dry winters (Cwa) is found in:
(1) Antarctica
(2) The Amazon Basin
(3) Western Europe
(4) The North Indian plains and the interior plains of South China
= 4
~ Cwa lies poleward of the Tropic of Cancer and Capricorn, mainly in the North Indian plains and South China's interior plains.

Q 10 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The Marine West Coast climate (Cfb), with mild summers, mild winters and rain throughout the year, is found in:
(1) The Arabian Peninsula
(2) Central Africa
(3) North-eastern Asia
(4) North-western Europe, the west coast of North America and New Zealand
= 4
~ Cfb lies poleward of the Mediterranean climate on the west coasts of continents, where onshore westerlies bring rain all year.

Q 11 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Cold snow forest climates (Group D) are found in a large continental belt between which latitudes?
(1) 10°–30° North
(2) 40°–70° North
(3) 40°–70° South
(4) 70°–90° South
= 2
~ D climates occur only in the Northern Hemisphere, because the Southern Hemisphere has little land at these latitudes. Dw (dry winters) occurs in north-eastern Asia.

Q 12 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Polar climates (Group E) exist poleward of about 70° latitude. The chapter's example of the ice cap climate (EF) is:
(1) Iceland
(2) Siberia
(3) Plateau Station, Antarctica
(4) Northern Canada
= 3
~ The ice cap climate occurs over interior Greenland and Antarctica; Plateau Station in Antarctica (79°S) is the chapter's example.

Q 13 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
According to the chapter, the Harappan civilisation flourished in the Rajasthan region during about:
(1) 8,000 BC
(2) 2,000–1,700 BC
(3) 500–300 million years ago
(4) 1550–1850 AD
= 2
~ Rajasthan was wet and cool around 8,000 BC, had higher rainfall from about 3,000 to 1,700 BC, and the Harappan civilisation flourished there from about 2,000 to 1,700 BC. Dry conditions came later.

Q 14 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The present inter-glacial period began about how long ago?
(1) 18,000 years ago
(2) 10,000 years ago
(3) 1,000 years ago
(4) 1 million years ago
= 2
~ The last major glacial peak of the Pleistocene was about 18,000 years ago, and the present inter-glacial period began about 10,000 years ago.

Q 16 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which of the following are mentioned in the chapter as natural (astronomical or terrestrial) causes of climate change?
1. Changes in sunspot activity
2. Milankovitch oscillations in the earth's orbit, wobble and tilt
3. Volcanic eruptions that throw aerosols into the atmosphere
Select the correct answer:
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ Sunspots change solar output, Milankovitch cycles change how insolation is received, and volcanic aerosols reduce the solar radiation reaching the surface.

Q 17 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which one of the following is NOT listed as a primary greenhouse gas in the chapter?
(1) Carbon dioxide
(2) Methane
(3) Nitrogen
(4) Nitrous oxide
= 3
~ The primary greenhouse gases named are carbon dioxide, chlorofluorocarbons, methane, nitrous oxide and ozone. Nitrogen, the most abundant gas, is not a greenhouse gas.

Q 18 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements about the Kyoto Protocol, as given in the chapter:
1. It was proclaimed in 1997 and came into force in 2005.
2. It required all countries, including developing countries like India, to reduce their emissions to 5 per cent below 1990 levels by 2012.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 1
~ The protocol was proclaimed in 1997, came into force in 2005 and was ratified by 141 nations. The 5 per cent cut below 1990 levels applied to 35 industrialised countries, not to all countries.

Q 19 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The time taken for carbon dioxide to adjust between its sources and sinks, as given in the chapter, is about:
(1) 1–2 years
(2) 20–50 years
(3) 500 years
(4) 10,000 years
= 2
~ CO2 has the largest concentration among greenhouse gases and its concentration has been rising about 0.5 per cent a year; its adjustment time is 20–50 years.

Q 20 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
According to the chapter, by how much was the mean annual temperature at the end of the twentieth century higher than at the end of the nineteenth century?
(1) About 0.6°C
(2) About 2.5°C
(3) About 6°C
(4) No change
= 1
~ The chapter puts the rise at about 0.6°C, with warming phases in 1901–44 and 1977–99. The seven warmest years of 1856–2000 all fell in the last decade, and 1998 was the warmest.
