# NCERT Class 11 Geography, Fundamentals of Physical Geography, Chapter 2: The Origin and Evolution of the Earth
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). November 2026.

Q 1 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The "nebular hypothesis" on the origin of the earth and planets was first put forward by:
(1) Edwin Hubble
(2) Immanuel Kant
(3) Fred Hoyle
(4) Otto Schmidt
= 2
~ The German philosopher Immanuel Kant proposed that the planets formed out of a cloud of material around a slowly rotating young sun.

Q 2 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
In which year did the mathematician Laplace revise the nebular hypothesis?
(1) 1796
(2) 1850
(3) 1900
(4) 1920
= 1
~ Laplace revised Kant's nebular hypothesis in 1796. The hypothesis held that the planets formed from material associated with a young sun.

Q 3 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Around 1950, Otto Schmidt and Carl Weizsäcker revised the nebular hypothesis. According to their view, the planets were formed mainly through:
(1) A collision between the sun and another star
(2) Volcanic eruptions on the sun
(3) Accretion of material from a solar nebula of gas and dust around the sun
(4) Capture of passing asteroids by the sun
= 3
~ They held that the sun was surrounded by a solar nebula made mostly of hydrogen, helium and dust. Friction and collisions formed a disc, and the planets grew by accretion.

Q 4 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The Big Bang theory is also known as the:
(1) Steady state hypothesis
(2) Planetesimal hypothesis
(3) Tidal hypothesis
(4) Expanding universe hypothesis
= 4
~ The most widely accepted modern theory of the universe's origin is the Big Bang theory, also called the expanding universe hypothesis.

Q 5 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Evidence that the universe is expanding was provided in 1920 by:
(1) Edwin Hubble
(2) Alfred Wegener
(3) Arthur Holmes
(4) Laplace
= 1
~ Edwin Hubble showed that galaxies are moving farther apart, so the universe is expanding. This supports the Big Bang theory.

Q 6 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
According to the Big Bang theory, the event that started the expansion of the universe took place about:
(1) 4.6 billion years ago
(2) 6 billion years ago
(3) 13.7 billion years ago
(4) 100 billion years ago
= 3
~ Scientists believe the "bang" happened about 13.7 billion years before the present, and the expansion continues even today.

Q 7 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
According to the Big Bang theory, the first atom began to form:
(1) Within the first three minutes after the Big Bang
(2) About 300,000 years after the Big Bang
(3) About 5 billion years after the Big Bang
(4) Only after the stars were formed
= 1
~ The chapter says that within the first three minutes of the Big Bang, the first atom began to form as energy started turning into matter.

Q 8 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements about the early universe:
1. Within about 300,000 years of the Big Bang, the temperature dropped to about 4,500 K.
2. At that stage atomic matter formed and the universe became transparent.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 3
~ Both are stated in the chapter: as the universe cooled to about 4,500 K within 300,000 years, atomic matter formed and the universe became transparent.

Q 9 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The "steady state" concept, an alternative to the Big Bang, was associated with Hoyle. It held that:
(1) The universe is shrinking over time
(2) The universe has been roughly the same at any point of time
(3) The universe began with a collision of two stars
(4) The universe is only about 4.6 billion years old
= 2
~ Hoyle's steady state idea saw the universe as more or less the same at all times. Growing evidence for expansion has led scientists to favour the expanding universe instead.

Q 10 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The formation of a galaxy begins with the accumulation of hydrogen gas in the form of a very large cloud. Such a cloud is called a:
(1) Nebula
(2) Planetesimal
(3) Asteroid belt
(4) Black hole
= 1
~ A galaxy begins as a nebula, a huge cloud of hydrogen gas. Localised lumps of gas within it later grow into stars.

Q 11 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
A "light year" is a measure of:
(1) Time
(2) Brightness
(3) Speed
(4) Distance
= 4
~ A light year is the distance light travels in one year, about 9.461 × 10^12 km, at a speed of about 300,000 km per second. The chapter uses it to express the size of galaxies.

Q 12 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The formation of stars is believed to have taken place about:
(1) 5–6 billion years ago
(2) 13.7 billion years ago
(3) 3.8 billion years ago
(4) 500 million years ago
= 1
~ The chapter places star formation at around 5–6 billion years ago, long after the Big Bang.

Q 13 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Arrange the following stages in the formation of planets in the correct order:
1. Small planetesimals accrete to form a few large bodies — the planets.
2. Stars are localised lumps of gas within a nebula; gravity forms a core with a rotating disc of gas and dust around it.
3. The gas cloud condenses and matter around the core forms small rounded objects that grow by cohesion into planetesimals.
Select the correct answer:
(1) 1 – 2 – 3
(2) 2 – 1 – 3
(3) 3 – 2 – 1
(4) 2 – 3 – 1
= 4
~ The sequence is: lumps of gas with a core and disc, then condensation into planetesimals, and finally accretion of many planetesimals into a few planets.

Q 14 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
In the formation of planets, "planetesimals" are:
(1) Large gas giants like Jupiter
(2) Moons that orbit the planets
(3) Fragments thrown out by the Big Bang
(4) Small rounded bodies formed by the condensation and cohesion of matter around the core
= 4
~ Planetesimals are small bodies formed by condensation and collisions in the disc around a young star. Many of them later accreted into planets.

Q 15 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The earth is estimated to be about how old?
(1) 13.7 billion years
(2) 4,600 million years
(3) 3,800 million years
(4) 2,000 million years
= 2
~ The earth formed about 4,600 million (4.6 billion) years ago, much later than the universe itself.

Q 16 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
While the earth was forming, heavier materials such as iron sank towards the centre and lighter ones moved towards the surface. This process that produced the earth's layers is called:
(1) Degassing
(2) Accretion
(3) Differentiation
(4) Photosynthesis
= 3
~ Differentiation separated the earth's materials by density, giving the layers from the crust to the core.

Q 17 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The earth's primordial atmosphere, rich in hydrogen and helium, was lost mainly because of:
(1) Photosynthesis by early plants
(2) Formation of the oceans
(3) Volcanic eruptions
(4) The solar winds
= 4
~ The first stage in the evolution of the atmosphere was the stripping away of the original hydrogen–helium atmosphere by the solar winds.

Q 18 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The release of gases and water vapour from the earth's hot interior, which built up the early atmosphere, is called:
(1) Weathering
(2) Condensation
(3) Degassing
(4) Subduction
= 3
~ Degassing released water vapour, nitrogen, carbon dioxide, methane, ammonia and other gases from the solidifying earth. The early atmosphere had very little free oxygen.

Q 19 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Arrange the following events in chronological order (earliest first):
1. Photosynthesis evolved.
2. Life began on the earth.
3. The oceans and atmosphere were flooded with oxygen.
Select the correct answer:
(1) 1 – 2 – 3
(2) 2 – 1 – 3
(3) 2 – 3 – 1
(4) 3 – 2 – 1
= 2
~ Life began about 3,800 million years ago, photosynthesis evolved about 2,500–3,000 million years ago, and oxygen flooded the oceans and atmosphere about 2,000 million years ago.

Q 20 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements:
1. The oceans were formed about 3,000 million years after the earth's formation.
2. Microscopic fossil structures resembling blue-green algae have been found in rocks older than about 3,000 million years.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 2
~ The oceans formed within about 500 million years of the earth's formation and are about 4,000 million years old. The microfossils show that life existed very early.
