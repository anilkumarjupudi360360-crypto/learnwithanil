# NCERT Class 11 Political Science, Indian Constitution at Work, Chapter 4: Executive
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The executive is the organ of government that primarily:
(1) Makes laws
(2) Interprets laws and settles disputes
(3) Implements laws and policies and runs the administration
(4) Conducts elections
= 3
~ The legislature makes laws, the judiciary interprets them, and the executive carries them out. The executive includes the political leaders who head the government and the civil servants who run the administration.

Q 2 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The political executive and the permanent executive differ in that:
(1) The political executive is elected for a fixed period, while civil servants are appointed for long-term careers
(2) The political executive is appointed by the UPSC
(3) The permanent executive changes after every election
(4) Civil servants are responsible to the voters directly
= 1
~ Ministers are the political executive: they come through elections and change with governments. Civil servants are the permanent executive, appointed on merit and continuing in office whichever party is in power.

Q 3 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
In which system is the head of the government also the head of the state, elected for a fixed term and not removable by a vote of the legislature (except by impeachment)?
(1) Parliamentary system
(2) Semi-presidential system
(3) Constitutional monarchy
(4) Presidential system
= 4
~ In a presidential system, as in the USA or Brazil, the President is both head of state and head of government and is elected for a fixed term. The legislature can remove him only by impeachment.

Q 4 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
A system with both a President and a Prime Minister, where the President has important day-to-day powers and may belong to a different party from the Prime Minister, is called:
(1) Presidential
(2) Parliamentary
(3) Semi-presidential
(4) Federal
= 3
~ In a semi-presidential system, as in France or Sri Lanka, the President is directly elected and has real powers, while the Prime Minister and cabinet must also have the legislature's support.

Q 5 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Why did the makers of the Indian Constitution choose the parliamentary executive?
(1) Because it makes the executive answerable to and controlled by the people's representatives
(2) Because it gives the President unlimited powers
(3) Because the President is directly elected by the people
(4) Because it removes the need for a legislature
= 1
~ The framers wanted an executive that is responsible to the legislature and, through it, to the people. They were also familiar with the parliamentary system from the colonial period.

Q 6 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The President of India is elected by:
(1) All adult citizens of India
(2) The members of the Lok Sabha only
(3) The elected members of both Houses of Parliament and of the Legislative Assemblies of the states (and of Delhi and Puducherry)
(4) The members of the Rajya Sabha and the Council of Ministers
= 3
~ The President is elected indirectly by an electoral college of elected MPs and elected MLAs, using proportional representation with single transferable vote. Nominated members do not vote.

Q 7 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
On which ground can the President of India be removed from office by impeachment?
(1) Violation of the Constitution
(2) Loss of majority in the Lok Sabha
(3) Disagreement with the Prime Minister
(4) Refusal to sign a money bill
= 1
~ The only ground for impeachment is violation of the Constitution. Impeachment needs a special majority in both Houses of Parliament.

Q 8 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Article 74(1) of the Constitution provides that the President acts on:
(1) His or her own judgement in all matters
(2) The aid and advice of the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister
(3) The advice of the Chief Justice of India
(4) The advice of the Governors of the states
= 2
~ Under Article 74(1) there is a Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at the head to aid and advise the President, who acts in accordance with that advice. Real executive power therefore lies with the council.

Q 9 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
After the 42nd and 44th Amendments, what can the President do if he or she disagrees with the advice of the Council of Ministers?
(1) Reject the advice permanently
(2) Ask the council to reconsider it once; if the council sends the same advice again, the President must act on it
(3) Dismiss the Council of Ministers at will
(4) Refer the matter to the Supreme Court in every case
= 2
~ The 42nd Amendment (1976) made the advice binding. The 44th Amendment (1978) let the President send it back once for reconsideration, but the President is bound by the advice given after reconsideration.

Q 10 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
When the President neither signs a bill nor returns it to Parliament, keeping it pending without any time limit, this is popularly called:
(1) Absolute veto
(2) Pocket veto
(3) Suspensive veto
(4) Qualified veto
= 2
~ The Constitution sets no time limit for the President to decide on a bill (other than a money bill or a constitutional amendment). Keeping a bill pending indefinitely is called the pocket veto. President Zail Singh used it in 1986 on the Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill.

Q 11 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
In which situation does the President of India have real discretion in appointing a Prime Minister?
(1) When the ruling party has a clear majority in the Lok Sabha
(2) When a money bill is to be introduced
(3) When the Rajya Sabha is dissolved
(4) When no party or coalition has a clear majority after an election
= 4
~ If one party or alliance has a clear majority, the President must invite its leader. Discretion comes in when the result is unclear: the President then judges who is most likely to win the confidence of the Lok Sabha.

Q 12 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Consider the following statements about the Vice-President of India:
1. He or she is elected by the members of both Houses of Parliament.
2. He or she is the ex officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.
3. He or she acts as President when the office of President falls vacant, until a new President is elected.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ All three are correct. The Vice-President holds office for five years and can be removed by a resolution of the Rajya Sabha passed by a majority and agreed to by the Lok Sabha.

Q 13 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The Prime Minister is appointed by the President, but to remain in office the Prime Minister must:
(1) Have the support (confidence) of a majority in the Lok Sabha
(2) Be a member of the Rajya Sabha
(3) Have the support of a majority of state governments
(4) Win a direct national election
= 1
~ The Prime Minister and the Council of Ministers stay in office only as long as they have the confidence of the Lok Sabha. If they lose a vote of confidence or a no-confidence motion succeeds, they must resign.

Q 14 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
A person who is not a Member of Parliament can be appointed a minister, but must become a member of either House within:
(1) Three months
(2) One year
(3) Two years
(4) Six months
= 4
~ A minister who is not an MP has to get elected or nominated to either House within six months, or cease to be a minister.

Q 15 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The 91st Amendment (2003) limited the total number of ministers, including the Prime Minister, to:
(1) 10 percent of the members of the Lok Sabha
(2) 15 percent of the members of the Lok Sabha
(3) 20 percent of the total members of both Houses
(4) 25 percent of the members of the Rajya Sabha
= 2
~ The 91st Amendment capped the size of the Council of Ministers at 15 percent of the total strength of the Lok Sabha (and, in states, of the Legislative Assembly).

Q 16 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The principle of "collective responsibility" of the Council of Ministers means that:
(1) Each minister answers only to the Prime Minister
(2) Ministers are responsible only to the President
(3) If the Lok Sabha passes a no-confidence motion, the whole Council of Ministers has to resign, and ministers stand by cabinet decisions in public
(4) Ministers are responsible to the courts for every decision
= 3
~ The council works as a team. A vote against one ministry's policy is a vote against the whole government, and ministers who disagree with a cabinet decision are expected to accept it or resign.

Q 17 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The chapter describes the Prime Minister's position as central for several reasons. Which of the following is NOT one of them?
(1) The Prime Minister chooses the ministers and allocates portfolios
(2) The Prime Minister heads the Council of Ministers and links it with the President and Parliament
(3) The Prime Minister is directly elected by all the voters of India
(4) The Prime Minister usually leads the majority party or coalition in the Lok Sabha
= 3
~ The Prime Minister is not directly elected by the whole country; he or she is a member of Parliament who leads the majority. Coalition governments since 1989 have made the Prime Minister's position depend more on allies.

Q 18 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Members of the All India Services, such as the IAS and IPS, are:
(1) Recruited by the Union Public Service Commission and serve in the states under the state governments, but can be disciplined only by the Central Government
(2) Recruited by the states and work only for the Centre
(3) Elected by the people of each district
(4) Appointed by the Governor and can be removed by the state government at any time
= 1
~ IAS and IPS officers are recruited through the UPSC and allotted to state cadres. They work under the state government, but only the Centre can take disciplinary action against them.

Q 19 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Why does the Constitution give civil servants security of service?
(1) So that they can join political parties
(2) So that they never have to follow the instructions of ministers
(3) So that they can make laws
(4) So that they can do their work impartially and efficiently, without fear of the party in power
= 4
~ Civil servants must carry out the policies of whichever government is elected, but neutrally. Security of service protects them from political pressure, while ministers remain responsible for policy.

Q 20 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which body recruits civil servants for the central services through competitive examinations?
(1) Election Commission of India
(2) Staff Selection Commission of the states
(3) Union Public Service Commission
(4) Finance Commission
= 3
~ The Union Public Service Commission conducts the examinations for the all-India and central services; each state has a State Public Service Commission for its own services. Their members have security of tenure.
