# NCERT Class 11 Political Science, Political Theory, Chapter 8: Secularism
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | pol | Political Theory
According to the chapter, secularism is first of all a doctrine that opposes:
(1) All religions and religious practice
(2) Science and modern education
(3) All forms of institutionalised religious domination, both between religions and within religions
(4) The freedom to change one's religion
= 3
~ Secularism is not anti-religion. It opposes domination of one religious group by another, and also domination within a religion, such as caste discrimination or the subordination of women.

Q 2 | pol | Political Theory
Discrimination against Dalits within a religious community, or the denial of equal status to women within it, is an example of:
(1) Intra-religious domination
(2) Inter-religious domination
(3) National self-determination
(4) Proportional representation
= 1
~ Intra-religious domination happens inside a religion. Secularism challenges it too, which is why the Indian Constitution abolished untouchability.

Q 3 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following is a feature of a secular state, as described in the chapter?
(1) It is run by religious leaders
(2) It does not establish any official religion and is not run by the heads of any religion
(3) It bans all religious festivals
(4) It supports only the majority religion
= 2
~ A secular state keeps institutional separation from religion: no state religion and no rule by religious heads. Its aim is to promote peace, freedom and equality among and within religious groups.

Q 4 | pol | Political Theory
The dominant Western (European) model of secularism, as described in the chapter, is based on:
(1) State intervention to reform religions
(2) Financial support to all religions equally
(3) Recognition of community rights for religious minorities
(4) Mutual exclusion: the state does not interfere in religion and religion does not interfere in the affairs of the state
= 4
~ The Western model keeps a strict wall between state and religion. It focuses on individual freedom and equality between individuals, and pays little attention to the rights of communities.

Q 5 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following is a feature of Indian secularism that distinguishes it from the Western model?
(1) The state never deals with any religious matter
(2) Only individuals, not communities, have rights
(3) Religious minorities have no special rights
(4) The state can intervene in religion to reform practices like untouchability, and it can give aid to educational institutions run by religious communities
= 4
~ Indian secularism allows the state to engage with religion for equality and reform, and recognises the community rights of minorities, such as setting up their own educational institutions.

Q 6 | pol | Political Theory
The idea that the state keeps a "principled distance" from religion means that:
(1) The state treats all religions with total indifference in every case
(2) The state may keep away from or intervene in religion, depending on which approach better promotes freedom and equality
(3) The state supports only one religion
(4) The state bans all religious practice in public
= 2
~ Principled distance is not fixed separation. The state decides, on principle, when to stay out of religious matters and when to intervene, for example against untouchability or to secure equality for women.

Q 7 | pol | Political Theory
Which provision of the Indian Constitution is an example of state intervention in religion to end intra-religious domination?
(1) Abolition of untouchability
(2) Freedom of the press
(3) The office of the Vice-President
(4) The Seventh Schedule
= 1
~ The Constitution abolished untouchability (Article 17) and allows laws throwing open Hindu religious institutions to all classes. This shows Indian secularism's concern for equality within religions.

Q 8 | pol | Political Theory
In Turkey, Mustafa Kemal Ataturk followed a kind of secularism that:
(1) Encouraged religious leaders to run the state
(2) Gave equal funds to all religions
(3) Involved active state intervention against religion in public life, such as discouraging traditional dress and replacing the Arabic script
(4) Was identical to the Indian model of secularism
= 3
~ Ataturk aggressively pushed religion out of public life, banning the fez and changing the script. The chapter contrasts this with the Indian approach, which respects all religions.

Q 9 | pol | Political Theory
How did Jawaharlal Nehru understand secularism, according to the chapter?
(1) As hostility to all religions
(2) As equal respect for all religions, complete opposition to communalism and a state that does not favour any religion
(3) As support for the majority religion only
(4) As a ban on religious education everywhere
= 2
~ Nehru was not personally religious, but he did not oppose religion. For him secularism meant equal respect for all faiths and firm opposition to communalism of every kind.

Q 10 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following criticisms of Indian secularism does the chapter discuss?
(1) That it is too similar to the American model
(2) That it ignores the Constitution
(3) That it is anti-religious, a Western import, favours minorities, is too interventionist and encourages vote bank politics
(4) That it bans elections
= 3
~ The chapter examines these criticisms one by one and argues that Indian secularism is a distinct model suited to India's diversity, though it can be misused in practice.

Q 11 | pol | Political Theory
Critics say that Indian secularism leads to "vote bank politics". What does this criticism mean?
(1) That banks fund elections
(2) That secularism stops people from voting
(3) That elections are held in banks
(4) That politicians may use religious groups as blocks of voters, making promises to them for votes
= 4
~ The chapter replies that seeking the support of groups is part of democratic politics; the problem arises only when leaders exploit such groups without serving their real interests.

Q 12 | pol | Political Theory
How does the chapter respond to the criticism that Indian secularism is a "Western import"?
(1) It argues that Indian secularism grew out of India's own deep religious diversity and differs from the Western model in important ways
(2) It agrees that it was copied from the West without change
(3) It says secularism does not exist in India
(4) It says secularism is only found in Europe
= 1
~ Indian secularism developed in response to the coexistence of many religions in India. Its concern for community rights and intra-religious equality makes it different from the Western model.

Q 13 | pol | Political Theory
Why does Indian secularism give importance to community-based rights, unlike the Western model?
(1) Because Indian communities have no individual members
(2) Because the Constitution bans individual rights
(3) Because in a diverse society, minority communities need protection of their culture and educational institutions to be truly equal
(4) Because communities elect the President
= 3
~ Equality for individuals in India often depends on the security of their communities. Hence the Constitution gives minorities the right to preserve their culture and run educational institutions.

Q 14 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following is an example of inter-religious domination?
(1) A religious community denying women the right to enter places of worship
(2) Violence or discrimination by members of one religious community against those of another
(3) Different caste groups within one religion
(4) A person changing his or her own religion freely
= 2
~ Inter-religious domination means one religious group dominating or attacking another. Secularism aims to end both this and domination within religions.

Q 15 | pol | Political Theory
Critics argue that Indian secularism is "interventionist", since the state interferes in religious practices. What is the chapter's response?
(1) That intervention for reform and equality is consistent with principled distance and is aimed at freedom and equality, not domination
(2) That the state never intervenes in any religion
(3) That intervention is only allowed in favour of the majority
(4) That intervention is banned by the Constitution
= 1
~ The state intervenes to protect rights, such as ending untouchability or ensuring equality for women. Such intervention is guided by constitutional values, not by hostility to religion.

Q 16 | pol | Political Theory
Which statement about secularism and religion is correct, according to the chapter?
(1) A secular person must not believe in any religion
(2) Secularism requires closing all places of worship
(3) Secularism means the majority religion is the state religion
(4) Secularism does not oppose religion; a person can be deeply religious and secular at the same time
= 4
~ Secularism opposes religious domination and discrimination, not faith itself. Many deeply religious people have defended secularism.

Q 17 | pol | Political Theory
The Indian state's support to educational institutions run by religious minorities is consistent with secularism because:
(1) Secularism means favouring only minorities
(2) Such support is given on a non-discriminatory basis and helps protect the community rights recognised by the Constitution
(3) Secularism bans such institutions
(4) Only the majority community can run schools
= 2
~ Aid given fairly, without favouring any religion, helps communities preserve their culture. This fits Indian secularism's idea of equal respect, not mutual exclusion.

Q 18 | pol | Political Theory
Why does the chapter say that the Western model of mutual exclusion may not suit a society like India?
(1) Because India has no religions
(2) Because India's Constitution is unwritten
(3) Because India has only one religion
(4) Because religious diversity and deep social inequalities within religions in India may require the state to engage with religion to protect equality and freedom
= 4
~ Strict non-interference might leave practices like untouchability untouched. India's model allows the state to act against injustice while respecting all faiths.

Q 19 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following best describes the aim of a secular state?
(1) To promote one religion over others
(2) To ban public discussion of religion
(3) To promote peace, freedom and equality among and within religious communities
(4) To give religious leaders control of the courts
= 3
~ A secular state protects individuals and communities from religious domination and ensures equal citizenship regardless of faith.

Q 20 | pol | Political Theory
The word "secular" was added to the Preamble of the Indian Constitution in 1976. Which statement about this is correct, according to the chapter's view?
(1) The Constitution was secular in its provisions from the start; the amendment made the word explicit
(2) Before 1976 India had a state religion
(3) The word was later removed by the 44th Amendment
(4) The amendment made India a religious state
= 1
~ Fundamental Rights such as freedom of religion and equality before law already made the Constitution secular in content. The 42nd Amendment only added the word to the Preamble.
