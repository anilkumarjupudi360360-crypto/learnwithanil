# NCERT Class 11 Political Science, Indian Constitution at Work, Chapter 5: Legislature
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
A legislature with two Houses is called:
(1) Unicameral
(2) Federal
(3) Plural
(4) Bicameral
= 4
~ A bicameral legislature has two Houses, like India's Parliament (Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha). Some states also have two Houses: a Legislative Assembly and a Legislative Council.

Q 2 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Why does the chapter say a second House is useful in a large and diverse federal country like India?
(1) It allows the representation of the states and gives an opportunity to reconsider decisions taken by the other House
(2) It makes the passing of laws impossible
(3) It replaces the need for elections
(4) It gives the President a vote in Parliament
= 1
~ The Rajya Sabha represents the states and lets every decision be considered twice. A hasty decision of one House can be checked by the other.

Q 3 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
In the United States Senate every state has equal representation. The Indian Rajya Sabha follows a different principle, in which:
(1) Every state has exactly two members
(2) States are represented roughly according to their population, so bigger states get more members
(3) Only union territories are represented
(4) Members are nominated by the President for all states
= 2
~ India follows asymmetrical representation: Uttar Pradesh has 31 Rajya Sabha members while small states such as Sikkim have one. In the US, each state elects two senators whatever its size.

Q 4 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Consider the following statements about the Rajya Sabha:
1. It is a permanent House and is never dissolved.
2. Its members are elected for six years, and one-third of them retire every two years.
3. The President nominates twelve members with distinction in literature, science, art and social service.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ All three are correct. Because one-third of the members retire every second year, the Rajya Sabha never goes out of existence; its composition changes gradually.

Q 5 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Members of the Lok Sabha are elected:
(1) Directly by the people from territorial constituencies, normally for five years
(2) By the state legislative assemblies
(3) By the Rajya Sabha
(4) By the President, on the advice of the Prime Minister
= 1
~ Lok Sabha members are directly elected by the voters of single-member constituencies using first past the post. The House has a five-year term but can be dissolved earlier.

Q 6 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which of the following powers belongs exclusively to the Lok Sabha?
(1) Electing the Vice-President
(2) Taking part in the impeachment of the President
(3) Approving constitutional amendments
(4) Passing a no-confidence motion that can remove the Council of Ministers
= 4
~ The Council of Ministers is responsible only to the Lok Sabha, so only the Lok Sabha can remove it through a no-confidence motion. The other powers are shared with the Rajya Sabha.

Q 7 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
A money bill can be introduced:
(1) In either House of Parliament
(2) Only in the Rajya Sabha
(3) Only in the Lok Sabha
(4) Only in a joint sitting of both Houses
= 3
~ Money bills can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha. The Rajya Sabha can only suggest changes, which the Lok Sabha may accept or reject.

Q 8 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
For how many days at most can the Rajya Sabha delay a money bill?
(1) 14 days
(2) 7 days
(3) 30 days
(4) 6 months
= 1
~ If the Rajya Sabha does not return a money bill within 14 days, it is deemed to have been passed by both Houses in the form in which the Lok Sabha passed it.

Q 9 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Who decides whether a bill is a money bill?
(1) The President
(2) The Chairman of the Rajya Sabha
(3) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha
(4) The Finance Minister
= 3
~ The Speaker certifies whether a bill is a money bill, and that decision is final for the purposes of passing it.

Q 10 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which of the following is a special power of the Rajya Sabha that the Lok Sabha does not have?
(1) Passing a no-confidence motion
(2) Introducing a money bill
(3) Authorising Parliament to make a law on a subject in the State List in the national interest
(4) Approving the Union Budget
= 3
~ Under Article 249 the Rajya Sabha, by a two-thirds majority of members present and voting, can let Parliament legislate on a State List subject. It also has a special role in creating new All India Services.

Q 11 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
If the two Houses of Parliament disagree on an ordinary bill, the deadlock is resolved by:
(1) A decision of the President alone
(2) A joint sitting of both Houses
(3) A reference to the Supreme Court
(4) A national referendum
= 2
~ The President can summon a joint sitting of both Houses, presided over by the Speaker. Since the Lok Sabha has more members, its view usually prevails.

Q 12 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Put the stages of an ordinary bill in the correct order:
1. The bill is sent to the other House
2. The bill is introduced and discussed in one House, often after a committee examines it
3. The bill receives the President's assent and becomes an Act
4. The bill is passed in the House where it was introduced
(1) 2, 4, 1, 3
(2) 4, 2, 1, 3
(3) 2, 1, 4, 3
(4) 1, 2, 4, 3
= 1
~ A bill is introduced and discussed, examined by a committee if needed, passed by the first House, then goes through the same steps in the second House, and becomes law after the President's assent.

Q 13 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The hour at the start of a sitting when members ask ministers questions on government work is called:
(1) Zero Hour
(2) Adjournment motion
(3) Calling attention
(4) Question Hour
= 4
~ Question Hour is one of the main ways Parliament keeps the executive accountable. Zero Hour, which follows it, lets members raise urgent matters, though it is not mentioned in the rules.

Q 14 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which of the following are ways in which Parliament controls the executive?
1. Debates and discussions
2. Approval or refusal of laws and of the budget
3. Financial control through grants
4. No-confidence motion
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2, 3 and 4 only
(3) 1, 3 and 4 only
(4) 1, 2, 3 and 4
= 4
~ The chapter lists all of these. Parliament can debate policies, refuse laws and money the executive needs, examine spending, and finally remove the government through a no-confidence motion in the Lok Sabha.

Q 15 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Why does Parliament work through committees?
(1) Because the Constitution bans debates in the House
(2) Because the House cannot examine every bill and the spending of every ministry in detail in its limited time
(3) Because committees replace the need for the President's assent
(4) Because committee members are directly elected by the people
= 2
~ Standing committees, including those for each group of ministries, and joint committees study bills, budget grants and policies in detail. Their reports help the House decide.

Q 16 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
The anti-defection law was added to the Constitution by:
(1) The 42nd Amendment, 1976
(2) The 44th Amendment, 1978
(3) The 52nd Amendment, 1985
(4) The 61st Amendment, 1988
= 3
~ The 52nd Amendment (1985), with later changes in 2003, created the Tenth Schedule. A member who defects can be disqualified from the House.

Q 17 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Under the anti-defection law, who decides whether a member of the Lok Sabha should be disqualified for defection?
(1) The President
(2) The Election Commission
(3) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha
(4) The Prime Minister
= 3
~ The presiding officer of the House (the Speaker or the Chairman) decides on disqualification for defection. Courts can review these decisions.

Q 18 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Which of the following counts as defection under the anti-defection law?
(1) A member voting against the party's direction in the House, or voluntarily giving up party membership
(2) A member asking a question in Question Hour
(3) A member speaking in a committee
(4) A member contesting from a different constituency in the next election
= 1
~ Defection means voluntarily leaving one's party, or voting or abstaining against its whip in the House without permission. The law was made to stop legislators from changing sides for gain.

Q 19 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Who presides over a joint sitting of the two Houses of Parliament?
(1) The President
(2) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha
(3) The Vice-President
(4) The Prime Minister
= 2
~ The Speaker presides over a joint sitting. The Speaker is also the key officer who keeps order in the Lok Sabha and protects the rights of members.

Q 20 | pol | Indian Constitution at Work
Besides making laws, Parliament has other functions. Which of the following is an electoral or judicial function of Parliament?
(1) Appointing the Chief Justice of India
(2) Recruiting civil servants
(3) Conducting elections to state assemblies
(4) Electing the President and the Vice-President, and removing the President and judges of higher courts through special procedures
= 4
~ MPs take part in electing the President and elect the Vice-President, and Parliament alone can impeach the President and remove judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts. Appointments of judges and the conduct of elections are done by other bodies.
