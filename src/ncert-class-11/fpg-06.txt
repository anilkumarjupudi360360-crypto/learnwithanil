# NCERT Class 11 Geography, Fundamentals of Physical Geography, Chapter 6: Landforms and their Evolution
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). November 2026.

Q 1 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Meanders, natural levees and oxbow lakes are characteristic of which stage in the evolution of a landscape by running water?
(1) Youth
(2) Mature
(3) Old
(4) None of the stages; they form only in deserts
= 3
~ In old age, rivers flow on gentle gradients and work sideways, forming meanders, natural levees and oxbow lakes. Youth has V-shaped valleys, waterfalls and rapids.

Q 2 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
At the old stage of erosion by running water, most of the landscape is reduced to an almost level surface. This surface is called a:
(1) Pediment
(2) Peneplain
(3) Plateau
(4) Playa
= 2
~ A peneplain ("almost plain") forms when divides are worn down; a few resistant rock remnants called monadnocks may stand above it.

Q 3 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements:
1. A gorge has very steep to straight sides and is almost equally wide at its top and bottom.
2. A canyon has steep, step-like side slopes and is wider at its top than at its bottom.
3. Canyons commonly form in horizontally bedded sedimentary rocks, while gorges form in hard rocks.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ Both are deep valleys; a canyon is a variant of a gorge. The chapter gives the Kaveri near Hogenakkal (Tamil Nadu) as a gorge and the Colorado in the USA as a canyon.

Q 4 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Large, deep holes at the base of waterfalls, formed by the impact and swirling of falling water and stones, are called:
(1) Potholes
(2) Plunge pools
(3) Sinkholes
(4) Cirques
= 2
~ Potholes are circular hollows drilled into the rocky river bed; the large, deep ones at the foot of waterfalls are plunge pools, which also help the valley deepen.

Q 5 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
River terraces that occur at the same elevation on both sides of a river are called:
(1) Paired terraces
(2) Unpaired terraces
(3) Incised meanders
(4) Point bars
= 1
~ Terraces mark old valley floors or floodplain levels. When they appear at matching heights on both banks, they are paired terraces.

Q 6 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Alluvial fans form when streams from higher levels break into foot-slope plains of low gradient. In arid and semi-arid climates, these fans are usually:
(1) Low cones with gentle slopes
(2) High cones with steep slopes
(3) Absent altogether
(4) Made only of fine clay
= 2
~ In humid areas alluvial fans are low cones with gentle slopes from head to toe; in arid and semi-arid areas they are high cones with steep slopes.

Q 7 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
How do deltas differ from alluvial fans?
(1) Deltas are made of unsorted boulders
(2) Delta deposits are well sorted with clear stratification, the coarsest settling first
(3) Deltas form only in deserts
(4) Deltas never have distributaries
= 2
~ Rivers carry material to the sea or a lake and drop it in order of size, so delta deposits are sorted and stratified, unlike the coarse, poorly sorted deposits of fans.

Q 8 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Meanders are described in the chapter as:
(1) A type of landform made of hard rock
(2) Deposits left by glaciers
(3) Not a landform but a type of channel pattern
(4) Hollows formed by wind deflation
= 3
~ Meanders are loops in a river's course. They develop where water flows over gentle gradients and works laterally on soft alluvial banks; the Coriolis force is also cited as a cause.

Q 9 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
In a meander of a large river, the concave bank is marked by active undercutting and appears as a steep scarp. This bank is known as the:
(1) Cut-off bank
(2) Slip-off bank
(3) Natural levee
(4) Point bar
= 1
~ The concave bank is the cut-off or undercut bank where erosion is active. The convex bank has a long, gentle slope where deposition takes place.

Q 10 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The chapter's photograph of oxbow lakes is of which river near Muzaffarpur, Bihar?
(1) Kosi
(2) Gandak
(3) Burhi Gandak
(4) Son
= 3
~ The Burhi Gandak near Muzaffarpur shows oxbow lakes — meander loops cut off from the main channel.

Q 11 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The term "karst topography" comes from the Karst region located in:
(1) The Alps, near the North Sea
(2) The Himalayas, near the Bay of Bengal
(3) The Andes, near the Pacific
(4) The Balkans, adjacent to the Adriatic Sea
= 4
~ Landforms made by groundwater in limestone or dolomite are called karst, after the Karst region of the Balkans next to the Adriatic Sea.

Q 12 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
When several sinkholes and dolines join together in limestone areas, the long, narrow to wide trenches formed are called:
(1) Lapies
(2) Stalagmites
(3) Valley sinks or uvalas
(4) Tarns
= 3
~ Sinkholes may be solution sinks or collapse sinks (dolines). When they merge, they form valley sinks or uvalas.

Q 13 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements about cave deposits:
1. Stalactites hang from the roof of a limestone cave like icicles.
2. Stalagmites rise up from the floor of the cave.
3. When a stalactite and a stalagmite fuse, they form a sinkhole.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1
~ When a stalactite and a stalagmite fuse they form a pillar or column. Sinkholes are depressions formed by solution or collapse at the surface of limestone areas.

Q 14 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Deep, long and wide troughs or basins with very steep, concave to vertical walls at the heads of glacial valleys, which may hold "tarn lakes" after the ice melts, are called:
(1) Drumlins
(2) Fjords
(3) Eskers
(4) Cirques
= 4
~ Cirques are commonly found at the heads of glacial valleys. Lakes formed in them after the glacier disappears are called tarns.

Q 15 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which of the following are given in the chapter as examples of horns formed by headward erosion of radiating cirques?
(1) Matterhorn and Everest
(2) Kilimanjaro and Fuji
(3) Kangchenjunga and Aconcagua
(4) Mont Blanc and Denali
= 1
~ When cirques erode back towards each other from several sides, sharp pointed peaks called horns remain. The chapter names the Matterhorn in the Alps and Everest in the Himalayas.

Q 16 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Very deep glacial troughs filled with sea water, which form shorelines in high latitudes, are called:
(1) Lagoons
(2) Estuaries
(3) Spits
(4) Fjords
= 4
~ Glaciers carve U-shaped troughs; where these are drowned by the sea along high-latitude coasts, they form fjords (fiords).

Q 17 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements about glacial deposits:
1. Glacial till is a mix of unsorted coarse and fine debris.
2. Terminal moraines are ridges deposited along the sides of a glacier, parallel to it.
3. Drumlins are smooth, oval ridges whose blunter "stoss end" faces the direction from which the glacier came.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 3
~ Ridges along the sides are lateral moraines; terminal moraines form at the end (toe) of the glacier. Till is unsorted, and drumlins show the direction of ice movement.

Q 18 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
According to the chapter, the west coast of India is largely a high rocky retreating coast, while the east coast is mostly:
(1) A coast of fjords
(2) A coast of active volcanoes
(3) A coral coast without beaches
(4) A low sedimentary coast
= 4
~ High rocky coasts are dominated by erosional features like cliffs and stacks. Low sedimentary coasts, like India's east coast, are dominated by depositional features such as beaches, bars and lagoons.

Q 19 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Gently inclined rocky floors close to mountains at their foot in deserts, which may grow into a low featureless plain when the mountains are worn down, are called:
(1) Playas
(2) Pediments
(3) Barchans
(4) Uvalas
= 2
~ Pediments develop as mountain fronts retreat. They can merge into a pediplain, leaving a few remnant hills called inselbergs.

Q 20 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Crescent-shaped sand dunes whose points or wings are directed away from the wind, forming where the wind direction is constant and moderate, are called:
(1) Barchans
(2) Seifs
(3) Transverse dunes
(4) Longitudinal dunes
= 1
~ Barchans point downwind. Seifs have only one wing, parabolic dunes form where vegetation partly covers the sand, longitudinal dunes form where sand supply is poor, and transverse dunes lie at right angles to the wind.
