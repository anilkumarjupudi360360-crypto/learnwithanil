# NCERT Class 11 Geography, India: Physical Environment, Chapter 5: Natural Vegetation
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). November 2026.

Q 1 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Tropical evergreen forests are found in areas with annual rainfall and mean annual temperature of:
(1) Over 200 cm and above 22°C
(2) 70–100 cm and below 15°C
(3) Less than 50 cm and above 30°C
(4) 100–200 cm and below 10°C
= 1
~ These forests grow on the western slope of the Western Ghats, the hills of the north-east and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands. They are well stratified and trees may reach 60 m or more.

Q 2 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Why do tropical evergreen forests appear green all the year round?
(1) Because the trees never flower
(2) Because there is no definite time for the trees to shed leaves, flower and fruit
(3) Because they receive snowfall in winter
(4) Because they are planted by people
= 2
~ Different trees shed leaves at different times, so the forest never looks bare. Rosewood, mahogany, aini and ebony are typical species.

Q 3 | geo | India: Physical Environment
According to the chapter, why were the oak forests of Garhwal and Kumaon replaced by pine (chir) during British rule?
(1) Pine was needed to lay railway lines
(2) Oak trees died due to a disease
(3) Pine gave better fruits
(4) Oak forests were burnt by tribes
= 1
~ The British exploited forests commercially; forests were also cleared for tea, rubber and coffee plantations. Protective use of forests gave way to commercial use.

Q 4 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Which are the most widespread forests in India, also called monsoon forests?
(1) Tropical evergreen forests
(2) Littoral and swamp forests
(3) Montane forests
(4) Tropical deciduous forests
= 4
~ Tropical deciduous forests grow where rainfall is 70–200 cm. They are divided into moist deciduous (100–200 cm) and dry deciduous (70–100 cm).

Q 5 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Teak, sal, shisham, hurra, mahua, amla, semul, kusum and sandalwood are the main species of which forest type?
(1) Tropical thorn forests
(2) Alpine forests
(3) Moist deciduous forests
(4) Mangrove forests
= 3
~ Moist deciduous forests are found in the north-eastern states along the Himalayan foothills, the eastern slopes of the Western Ghats and Odisha.

Q 6 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Tropical thorn forests occur in areas that receive rainfall of:
(1) Less than 50 cm
(2) 70–100 cm
(3) 100–200 cm
(4) More than 200 cm
= 1
~ They are found in semi-arid parts of south-west Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh. Babool, ber, wild date palm, khair, neem, khejri and palas are important species.

Q 7 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Consider the following statements about the Himalayan montane forests:
1. Wet temperate forests occur between 1,000 and 2,000 m.
2. Chir pine is a useful commercial tree found well developed between 1,500 and 1,750 m.
3. Deodar, a highly valued endemic species, grows mainly in the eastern part of the Himalayan range.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1
~ Deodar grows mainly in the western part of the Himalayas. Its durable wood is mainly used in construction.

Q 8 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Chinar and walnut, which sustain the famous Kashmir handicrafts, belong to which zone of vegetation?
(1) Tropical thorn zone
(2) Tropical evergreen zone
(3) Mangrove zone
(4) Temperate montane zone of the Himalayas
= 4
~ They grow in the temperate zone of the western Himalayas. Blue pine and spruce appear higher, at 2,225–3,048 m.

Q 9 | geo | India: Physical Environment
The alpine pastures between 3,000 and 4,000 m in the Himalayas are used extensively for transhumance by which tribes?
(1) Bhils and Gonds
(2) Todas and Kotas
(3) Santhals and Mundas
(4) Gujjars, Bakarwals, Bhotiyas and Gaddis
= 4
~ Silver fir, junipers, pines, birch and rhododendrons grow in this zone, and its pastures support seasonal migration of herders.

Q 10 | geo | India: Physical Environment
The temperate forests of the Nilgiris, Anaimalai and Palani hills are called:
(1) Kayals
(2) Duars
(3) Sholas
(4) Bugyals
= 3
~ Sholas are southern mountain forests. Magnolia, laurel, cinchona and wattle are some trees of economic significance in them.

Q 11 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Which two Indian sites are mentioned in the chapter as protected waterfowl habitats under the Ramsar Convention?
(1) Loktak Lake and Sambhar Lake

(4) Vembanad Lake and Pulicat Lake

Q 12 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Consider the following statements about mangrove forests in India:
1. They grow in salt marshes, tidal creeks, mud flats and estuaries.
2. They cover about 6,740 sq km, which is about 7 per cent of the world's mangrove forests.
3. They are highly developed in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands and the Sundarbans of West Bengal.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ The Mahanadi, Godavari and Krishna deltas are also significant mangrove areas. These forests are being encroached upon and need conservation.

Q 13 | geo | India: Physical Environment
India's forest policy aims at having what share of its geographical area under forests?
(1) 10 per cent
(2) 23 per cent
(3) 33 per cent
(4) 50 per cent
= 3
~ The National Forest Policy, adopted in 1952 and modified in 1988, aims at 33 per cent of the area under forests.

Q 14 | geo | India: Physical Environment
The National Commission on Agriculture (1976) classified social forestry into three categories. Which of the following is one of them?
(1) Urban forestry
(2) Alpine forestry
(3) Mangrove forestry
(4) Desert forestry
= 1
~ The three categories are urban forestry, rural forestry and farm forestry. Social forestry means managing and protecting forests and afforesting barren land for environmental, social and rural development.

Q 15 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Which of the following statements about farm forestry is correct?
(1) It is done only on government land in cities
(2) Farmers grow trees on their own farm lands for commercial and non-commercial purposes, and forest departments distribute seedlings free to small and medium farmers
(3) It bans the planting of trees near cattle sheds
(4) It is the same as raising trees along roads and railway lines in urban areas
= 2
~ Field margins, grasslands, pastures and land around homes and cow sheds can be used for trees under non-commercial farm forestry.

Q 16 | geo | India: Physical Environment
In which year was the Wildlife Act enacted in India, providing the legal framework for protecting wildlife?
(1) 1952
(2) 1972
(3) 1988
(4) 1991
= 2
~ The Wildlife Act was enacted in 1972 and amended in 1991. India has about 4–5 per cent of all known species on earth.

Q 17 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Project Tiger was launched in:
(1) 1972
(2) 1973
(3) 1986
(4) 1992
= 2
~ Project Tiger began in 1973 with 9 reserves. Project Elephant began in 1992. The Crocodile Breeding Project, Project Hangul and conservation of the Himalayan musk deer are other special schemes.

Q 18 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Biosphere reserves are set up under which international programme?
(1) Ramsar Convention
(2) Montreal Protocol
(3) Kyoto Protocol
(4) UNESCO's Man and Biosphere Programme
= 4
~ A biosphere reserve is a protected area of terrestrial and coastal ecosystems recognised under UNESCO's Man and Biosphere Programme.

Q 19 | geo | India: Physical Environment
According to the chapter, how many biosphere reserves does India have, and how many of them are recognised in UNESCO's World Network of Biosphere Reserves?
(1) 12 and 18
(2) 18 and 12
(3) 18 and 18
(4) 15 and 10
= 2
~ India has 18 biosphere reserves, of which 12 are in UNESCO's World Network.

Q 20 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Which was India's first biosphere reserve, designated in 1986?
(1) Nanda Devi
(2) Sunderban
(3) Nilgiri
(4) Gulf of Mannar
= 3
~ The Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve was set up in 1986. Nanda Devi followed in 1988, and the Gulf of Mannar and Sunderban in 1989.
