# NCERT Class 11 Geography, Fundamentals of Physical Geography, Chapter 1: Geography as a Discipline
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). November 2026.

Q 1 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The term "geography" was first used by which Greek scholar?
(1) Herodotus
(2) Ptolemy
(3) Eratosthenes
(4) Strabo
= 3
~ The chapter credits Eratosthenes (276–194 BC), a Greek scholar, with coining the word. It joins "geo" (earth) and "graphos" (description).

Q 2 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The two Greek roots that make up the word "geography" literally mean:
(1) Earth and description
(2) Land and measurement
(3) World and history
(4) Space and science
= 1
~ "Geo" means earth and "graphos" means description, so geography literally means describing the earth.

Q 3 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following pairs:
1. Systematic approach — Alexander von Humboldt
2. Regional approach — Eratosthenes
Which of the pairs given above is/are correctly matched?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 1
~ Humboldt (1769–1859) introduced the systematic approach, but the regional approach was developed by his contemporary Karl Ritter. Eratosthenes is the Greek scholar who coined the word geography.

Q 4 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Geography is described as the study of "areal differentiation". This means geography mainly explains:
(1) The chemical composition of rocks
(2) How and why phenomena differ from one part of the earth's surface to another
(3) The movement of planets in the solar system
(4) The history of human civilisations
= 2
~ Scholars such as Richard Hartshorne and Hettner described geography as the study of differences of phenomena from place to place, that is, areal differentiation.

Q 5 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
How does the chapter distinguish geography from history?
(1) Geography studies only nature, history studies only people
(2) Geography studies the past, history studies the present
(3) Geography studies variations over space, while history studies changes over time
(4) Both study only political events
= 3
~ The chapter treats geography as concerned with space and history with time. Both are integrating disciplines that draw on many other subjects.

Q 6 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
In explaining how geography deals with change, which element does the chapter call the "fourth dimension"?
(1) Altitude
(2) Time
(3) Population
(4) Distance
= 2
~ Besides the three dimensions of space, time is treated as the fourth dimension, because the features of a place keep changing over time.

Q 7 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which one of the following is NOT a branch of physical geography under the systematic approach?
(1) Geomorphology
(2) Climatology
(3) Soil Geography
(4) Political Geography
= 4
~ Physical geography's branches are geomorphology, climatology, hydrology and soil geography. Political geography is a branch of human geography.

Q 8 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Biogeography has developed at the interface of:
(1) Physical geography and human geography
(2) Geology and astronomy
(3) History and economics
(4) Cartography and statistics
= 1
~ The chapter shows biogeography emerging where physical and human geography meet. Its branches are plant geography, zoo geography, ecology/ecosystem and environmental geography.

Q 9 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which of the following is a branch of geography based on the regional approach?
(1) Climatology
(2) Zoo geography
(3) Hydrology
(4) Regional planning
= 4
~ The regional approach gives regional studies (macro, meso and micro), regional planning, regional development and regional analysis. The other three are systematic branches.

Q 10 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following:
1. Remote sensing
2. Geographical Information System (GIS)
3. Global Positioning System (GPS)
Which of the above are grouped under geo-informatics in the chapter?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ Geo-informatics, one of the methods common to all branches, covers remote sensing, GIS and GPS. Cartography, quantitative techniques and field survey are the other methods.

Q 11 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements:
1. Physical geography has close links with the natural sciences.
2. Human geography has close links with the social sciences.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 3
~ Geography is an integrating discipline: its physical side draws on sciences like geology and meteorology, and its human side on economics, sociology, political science and others.

Q 12 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which branch of physical geography studies the realm of water — oceans, lakes, rivers and other water bodies — and its effect on life?
(1) Geomorphology
(2) Hydrology
(3) Pedology
(4) Climatology
= 2
~ Hydrology deals with water over the earth's surface and its effects on living beings, including humans.

Q 13 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The study of landforms, their evolution and the processes that shape them is called:
(1) Topography
(2) Seismology
(3) Geomorphology
(4) Oceanography
= 3
~ Geomorphology is devoted to landforms, how they evolve and the related processes.

Q 14 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Climatology as a branch of physical geography deals with:
(1) Soil fertility and land use
(2) Distribution of plants and animals
(3) Population movements across regions
(4) The structure of the atmosphere, elements of weather and climate, and climatic types and regions
= 4
~ Climatology covers the atmosphere's structure, weather and climate elements, and the classification of climatic types and regions.

Q 15 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which branch of geography studies the processes of soil formation, soil types, their fertility, distribution and use?
(1) Soil geography
(2) Economic geography
(3) Environmental geography
(4) Hydrology
= 1
~ Soil geography is the fourth branch of physical geography listed in the chapter, along with geomorphology, climatology and hydrology.

Q 16 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Delimitation of electoral constituencies and the study of boundaries between political units fall under which branch of human geography?
(1) Social geography
(2) Historical geography
(3) Economic geography
(4) Political geography
= 4
~ Political geography looks at space from the angle of political events: boundaries, relations between neighbouring political units, delimitation of constituencies and election patterns.

Q 17 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Why does the chapter consider a sharp division between physical geography and human geography not very valid?
(1) Because human geography is older than physical geography
(2) Because physical geography does not study the earth's surface
(3) Because nature and human beings are inseparable and must be studied together
(4) Because both use only maps and no other method
= 3
~ Geography studies the interaction of nature and humans as a whole. Since the two cannot be separated, the dualism is treated as artificial.

Q 18 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
According to the chapter, the basic concern of geography as a discipline is to study:
(1) Only the physical features of the earth
(2) The relationship between the physical environment and human beings, and the spatial patterns they create
(3) Only the economic activities of people
(4) The internal structure of the earth alone
= 2
~ The earth is home to human beings, so geography studies the interaction between the physical environment and humans and the variations this produces over space.

Q 19 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The chapter mentions that India collects manganese nodules from the ocean bed. This example is given to show:
(1) The importance of physical geography in understanding and using natural resources
(2) That oceans have no economic value
(3) That cartography is the most important method in geography
(4) That geography deals only with land
= 1
~ Knowledge of oceans and other physical features helps societies use resources wisely. The manganese nodules example shows the practical value of physical geography.

Q 20 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The chapter's example of a 1,500 km journey taking about 2 hours by plane but about 17 hours by a fast train is used to show that:
(1) Air travel is always cheaper than rail travel
(2) Distance is increasingly understood in terms of the time taken to cover it
(3) Railways are no longer important
(4) Physical distances on the earth keep changing
= 2
~ With modern transport, people think of distance in time (and cost) rather than kilometres. The physical distance stays the same while the time to cover it changes.
