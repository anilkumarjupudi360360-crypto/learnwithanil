# NCERT Class 11 Political Science, Political Theory, Chapter 6: Citizenship
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | pol | Political Theory
The chapter defines citizenship as:
(1) Residence in a country for more than five years
(2) Full and equal membership of a political community, with rights and obligations
(3) Ownership of property in a country
(4) Membership of a political party
= 2
~ Citizens have equal rights and duties in the political community. Citizenship also involves the relationships citizens have with one another and their shared participation in public life.

Q 2 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following struggles is mentioned in the chapter as a struggle for equal citizenship rights?
(1) The Olympic Games
(2) The industrial revolution in Britain
(3) The space race
(4) The struggle against apartheid in South Africa and movements of women and Dalits in India
= 4
~ The chapter mentions the French Revolution, the anti-apartheid struggle and the women's and Dalit movements as struggles for full and equal citizenship.

Q 3 | pol | Political Theory
The British sociologist T.H. Marshall, in Citizenship and Social Class (1950), saw citizenship as involving three kinds of rights. They are:
(1) Civil, political and social rights
(2) Natural, legal and moral rights
(3) Religious, cultural and linguistic rights
(4) Property, inheritance and trade rights
= 1
~ Marshall traced how civil rights (like freedom of speech and property), political rights (like voting) and social rights (like education and welfare) were extended to citizens over time.

Q 4 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following is NOT a way of acquiring Indian citizenship under the Citizenship Act, 1955?
(1) Birth
(2) Descent
(3) Paying a fee to a political party
(4) Registration and naturalisation
= 3
~ Indian citizenship can be acquired by birth, descent, registration, naturalisation or the inclusion of territory into India. Political party membership has nothing to do with it.

Q 5 | pol | Political Theory
The provisions about citizenship in the Indian Constitution are found in:
(1) Part II
(2) Part III
(3) Part IV
(4) Part IXA
= 1
~ Part II (Articles 5 to 11) deals with citizenship at the commencement of the Constitution and gives Parliament the power to make laws on it, which it did through the Citizenship Act, 1955.

Q 6 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following problems related to citizenship in cities is discussed in the chapter?
(1) Too many parks
(2) Excess of public transport
(3) The difficulties of slum-dwellers and the urban poor in getting basic services and being treated as equal citizens
(4) The lack of luxury goods
= 3
~ The chapter points out that the urban poor contribute to the city's economy but often lack housing, water, sanitation and security, which raises the question of what equal citizenship requires for them.

Q 7 | pol | Political Theory
Free movement and the right to settle anywhere in the country are rights of Indian citizens. These rights matter especially to:
(1) Foreign tourists
(2) Diplomats
(3) Only government employees
(4) Migrant workers who move to other states and cities in search of work
= 4
~ Migrants often face hostility from local groups claiming jobs for "sons of the soil". The chapter asks how equal citizenship can be ensured for people who move within the country.

Q 8 | pol | Political Theory
Rights of tribal people and forest dwellers to their lands and forests, discussed in the chapter, raise which question about citizenship?
(1) Whether they should be allowed to vote
(2) How the needs and ways of life of different groups can be protected while treating all as equal citizens
(3) Whether they should leave the country
(4) Whether forests belong to foreign countries
= 2
~ Equal citizenship does not always mean identical policies. Groups with special needs, like forest dwellers whose livelihood depends on forests, may need special protection.

Q 9 | pol | Political Theory
People who are forced to leave their country and have no state willing to accept them as citizens are called:
(1) Diplomats
(2) Stateless people or refugees
(3) Migrant workers
(4) Expatriates
= 2
~ Refugees and stateless people live without the protection that citizenship provides. The chapter uses their plight to show how important citizenship is.

Q 10 | pol | Political Theory
The idea of "universal citizenship" means that:
(1) All countries should merge into one
(2) Every person should be a citizen of every country
(3) Only rich countries should give citizenship
(4) Full and equal membership should be available to all people within the nation-state, and no one should be left out
= 4
~ Universal citizenship holds that every person living in a state should be able to enjoy full membership. In practice, rules about who is a citizen are often exclusive, which creates problems for minorities and migrants.

Q 11 | pol | Political Theory
The idea of "global citizenship", discussed in the chapter, is based on:
(1) The view that we live in an interconnected world and share responsibilities for problems like human rights violations and environmental damage across borders
(2) A single world government that has already been formed
(3) Abolishing all national citizenships
(4) Travelling abroad frequently
= 1
~ Global citizenship is a sense of belonging to a world community. It can make it easier to address problems that cross borders, though it does not replace national citizenship.

Q 12 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following is generally considered an obligation of citizenship?
(1) Avoiding all participation in public life
(2) Disobeying laws whenever one wishes
(3) Respecting the rights of fellow citizens and taking part in the life of the community, such as paying taxes and obeying laws
(4) Supporting only one political party
= 3
~ Citizenship involves duties as well as rights: legal duties like paying taxes and moral obligations to respect others and take part in the community's shared life.

Q 13 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following is an example of citizens claiming rights through peaceful protest, as described in the chapter?
(1) Organised violence against a minority
(2) Bribing officials
(3) Banning newspapers
(4) Struggles like the women's movement and the Dalit movement to claim equal rights
= 4
~ Struggles for citizenship have often taken the form of movements that demand equal rights and dignity for groups that were excluded.

Q 14 | pol | Political Theory
Equal citizenship, according to the chapter, implies that:
(1) Citizens should get the same basic rights and protections from the state, whatever their caste, religion, gender or wealth
(2) All citizens must have the same income
(3) All citizens must speak one language
(4) Citizens must belong to the same religion
= 1
~ Equal citizenship means equal rights and protections. Policies such as special help for the disadvantaged can be part of making that equality real.

Q 15 | pol | Political Theory
In the chapter's discussion of citizenship and nation, which statement is correct?
(1) All countries use exactly the same rules for granting citizenship
(2) Countries define their national identity in different ways, and this affects who is easily accepted as a citizen
(3) Citizenship has nothing to do with national identity
(4) Only rich people can become citizens anywhere
= 2
~ Some countries define the nation by a shared history, religion or ethnicity; others in more inclusive, civic terms. These choices shape the rules for citizenship.

Q 16 | pol | Political Theory
The Indian Constitution, according to the chapter, aimed at an inclusive idea of citizenship. Which provision reflects this?
(1) Citizenship only for people of one religion
(2) A requirement to speak Hindi to be a citizen
(3) Equal rights for all citizens without discrimination on grounds of religion, caste, sex or place of birth
(4) Separate citizenship for each state
= 3
~ India's Constitution gives equal citizenship to all, with no religious or linguistic test. It also provides special protections for groups like SCs, STs and minorities.

Q 17 | pol | Political Theory
Why does the chapter say that the right to free movement and settlement can lead to conflict?
(1) Because people never move
(2) Because the Constitution bans movement
(3) Because local residents sometimes resent newcomers, especially when jobs and resources are scarce, and demand preference for "sons of the soil"
(4) Because migrants cannot vote anywhere
= 3
~ Such tensions show that equal citizenship has to be defended in practice. The state must protect migrants' rights while addressing the concerns of local people.

Q 18 | pol | Political Theory
Which of these is an example of a social right in T.H. Marshall's sense?
(1) Freedom of speech
(2) The right to education and health care
(3) The right to vote
(4) The right to form a political party
= 2
~ Social rights cover economic welfare and security, like education and health care. Freedom of speech is a civil right; voting and forming parties are political rights.

Q 19 | pol | Political Theory
Which of the following best explains why citizenship is not only a legal status, according to the chapter?
(1) Because it can be bought
(2) Because only courts decide it
(3) Because it lasts only for a short time
(4) Because it also involves relationships between citizens and their active participation in shaping the community's life
= 4
~ Citizens do not just hold rights; they take part in debates, movements and civic life, which is how the meaning of citizenship itself has expanded over time.

Q 20 | pol | Political Theory
The French Revolution of 1789 is important in the history of citizenship because:
(1) It asserted the idea that the people, as citizens, are sovereign and equal, ending the privileges of birth
(2) It created a world government
(3) It abolished all governments
(4) It gave citizenship only to the nobility
= 1
~ The Revolution challenged the privileges of the monarchy and aristocracy and proclaimed the rights of man and the citizen, which inspired later struggles for equal citizenship.
