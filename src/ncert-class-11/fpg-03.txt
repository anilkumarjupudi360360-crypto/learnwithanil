# NCERT Class 11 Geography, Fundamentals of Physical Geography, Chapter 3: Interior of the Earth
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). November 2026.

Q 1 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which of the following is a DIRECT source of information about the earth's interior?
(1) Material thrown out by volcanic eruptions
(2) Variation of gravity from place to place
(3) The earth's magnetic field
(4) Seismic waves recorded by seismographs
= 1
~ Surface rocks, mining and drilling, and volcanic material such as magma are direct sources. Gravity, magnetism and seismic waves are indirect sources.

Q 2 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The deepest drilling mentioned in the chapter, at Kola in the Arctic Ocean, has reached a depth of about:
(1) 3 km
(2) 12 km
(3) 40 km
(4) 400 km
= 2
~ The Kola drill reached about 12 km. Even the deep gold mines of South Africa go only 3–4 km down, which is tiny compared with the earth's radius of about 6,378 km.

Q 3 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The force of gravity is greater near the poles and less at the equator. The chapter explains this mainly by:
(1) The presence of ice at the poles
(2) The different distances from the centre of the earth at the poles and at the equator
(3) The rotation of the moon around the earth
(4) Volcanic activity near the equator
= 2
~ The distance from the earth's centre is less at the poles than at the equator. Differences in the gravity actually observed from the expected values are called gravity anomalies, and they reveal how mass is distributed in the crust.

Q 4 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The point on the earth's surface nearest to the focus of an earthquake is called the:
(1) Epicentre
(2) Hypocentre
(3) Shadow zone
(4) Fault plane
= 1
~ The focus or hypocentre is where the energy is released, inside the earth. The epicentre is the point on the surface directly nearest to it, where waves are first felt.

Q 5 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements about P-waves:
1. They are the fastest earthquake waves and are the first to arrive at the surface.
2. They can travel only through solid materials.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 1
~ P-waves (primary waves) move fastest and arrive first. Like sound waves, they pass through gases, liquids and solids; it is S-waves that travel only through solids.

Q 6 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
S-waves cannot pass through liquids. This property has helped scientists conclude that:
(1) The crust is made of liquid rock
(2) The inner core is liquid
(3) The mantle is completely gaseous
(4) The outer core of the earth is in a liquid state
= 4
~ S-waves travel only through solids and disappear at the core. This indicates that the outer core is liquid, while the inner core is solid.

Q 7 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which earthquake waves are the last to be recorded on a seismograph and are the most destructive?
(1) P-waves
(2) S-waves
(3) Surface waves
(4) Body waves
= 3
~ Body waves (P and S) create surface waves when they reach the surface. Surface waves arrive last but cause the most damage, displacing rocks and collapsing structures.

Q 8 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
In which way do the particles of the medium vibrate when a P-wave passes through it?
(1) Perpendicular to the direction of the wave, in a vertical plane
(2) Parallel to the direction of the wave, creating compression and rarefaction
(3) In circles on the surface only
(4) They do not vibrate at all
= 2
~ P-waves vibrate parallel to their direction of travel, pushing and pulling the material. S-waves vibrate at right angles to their direction of travel.

Q 9 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements about shadow zones:
1. The zone between 105° and 145° from the epicentre is a shadow zone for both P- and S-waves.
2. The entire zone beyond 105° from the epicentre does not receive S-waves.
3. The shadow zone of S-waves covers a little more than 40 per cent of the earth's surface.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ All three are given in the chapter. P-waves reappear beyond 145°, but S-waves are absent from the whole area beyond 105°, which makes the S-wave shadow zone much larger.

Q 10 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Match the types of earthquakes with their causes:
A. Tectonic — 1. Explosion of chemical or nuclear devices
B. Collapse — 2. Sliding of rocks along a fault plane
C. Explosion — 3. Roofs of underground mines caving in
D. Reservoir induced — 4. Large dams and reservoirs
Select the correct answer:
(1) A-2, B-3, C-1, D-4
(2) A-3, B-2, C-1, D-4
(3) A-2, B-1, C-3, D-4
(4) A-4, B-3, C-1, D-2
= 1
~ Tectonic earthquakes, the most common, come from sliding along faults. Collapse earthquakes occur in intense mining areas, explosion earthquakes from chemical or nuclear blasts, and reservoir-induced ones near large reservoirs.

Q 11 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements:
1. The Richter scale expresses the magnitude (energy released) of an earthquake, in absolute numbers from 0 to 10.
2. The Mercalli scale expresses the intensity of an earthquake, based on visible damage, from 1 to 12.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 3
~ Magnitude is measured on the Richter scale (0–10) and intensity on the Mercalli scale (1–12), named after an Italian seismologist.

Q 12 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
According to the chapter, a tsunami is generated by an earthquake when:
(1) The epicentre is in a desert
(2) The focus is in the inner core
(3) The earthquake has a magnitude below 2
(4) The epicentre lies under ocean water and the magnitude is high
= 4
~ Tsunamis form when the epicentre is under the sea and the quake is strong enough to displace a large volume of water. They are listed among the effects of earthquakes.

Q 13 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The mean thickness of the oceanic crust is about 5 km. What is the mean thickness of the continental crust, according to the chapter?
(1) About 100 km
(2) About 10 km
(3) About 30 km
(4) About 400 km
= 3
~ Continental crust averages about 30 km and is thicker below major mountains — up to about 70 km in the Himalayan region.

Q 14 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The mantle extends from the Moho discontinuity down to a depth of about:
(1) 400 km
(2) 900 km
(3) 2,900 km
(4) 6,300 km
= 3
~ The mantle stretches from the Moho to about 2,900 km, where the core–mantle boundary lies.

Q 15 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The upper portion of the mantle, extending up to about 400 km and regarded as the main source of magma, is called the:
(1) Asthenosphere
(2) Lithosphere
(3) Moho
(4) Nife
= 1
~ The asthenosphere ("astheno" means weak) is a weak, partly molten zone of the upper mantle from which magma rises during volcanic eruptions.

Q 16 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The lithosphere consists of:
(1) Only the oceanic crust
(2) The crust and the uppermost solid part of the mantle
(3) The outer core and the inner core
(4) The asthenosphere alone
= 2
~ The crust together with the topmost solid part of the mantle forms the lithosphere, whose thickness varies from about 10 to 200 km.

Q 17 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements about the earth's core:
1. The outer core is solid while the inner core is in a liquid state.
2. The core is made of very heavy material, mostly nickel and iron, and is sometimes called the "nife" layer.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 2
~ It is the other way round: the outer core is liquid and the inner core is solid. The core is made largely of nickel (Ni) and iron (Fe), hence "nife".

Q 18 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The Deccan Traps of the Maharashtra plateau are an example of which type of volcanic feature?
(1) Shield volcano
(2) Caldera
(3) Composite volcano
(4) Flood basalt province
= 4
~ Flood basalt provinces form when very fluid lava spreads over vast areas in thick layers; some Deccan Trap flows are more than 50 m thick.

Q 19 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which type of volcano is the largest of all on the earth, made mostly of fluid basaltic lava, and is not very steep — like the Hawaiian volcanoes?
(1) Composite volcano
(2) Caldera
(3) Shield volcano
(4) Cinder cone
= 3
~ Shield volcanoes are built of runny basalt and have gentle slopes. Composite volcanoes erupt cooler, more viscous lava explosively, and calderas are the most explosive of all.

Q 20 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which intrusive volcanic form develops perpendicular to the ground like a wall, is the most common intrusive form in western Maharashtra, and is considered a feeder of the Deccan Traps?
(1) Sill
(2) Lapolith
(3) Batholith
(4) Dyke
= 4
~ Dykes cut across layers vertically. Sills are near-horizontal sheets, lapoliths are saucer-shaped, and batholiths are large bodies of granite cooled deep inside the crust.
