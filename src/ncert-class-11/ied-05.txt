# NCERT Class 11 Economics, Indian Economic Development, Chapter 5: Rural Development
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | eco | Indian Economic Development
How does the chapter describe 'rural development'?
(1) Moving rural people to cities
(2) An action plan focusing on the development of areas that are lagging behind in the overall development of the village economy
(3) Only the building of village roads
(4) Only raising farm prices
= 2
~ Rural development is a comprehensive term: it covers human resources, land reforms, productive resources of each locality, infrastructure and special measures for poverty alleviation in areas lagging behind.

Q 2 | eco | Indian Economic Development
The National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development (NABARD), the apex body that coordinates rural financing, was set up in:
(1) 1969
(2) 1975
(3) 1991
(4) 1982
= 4
~ NABARD was set up in 1982 to coordinate the activities of all institutions involved in the rural financing system.

Q 3 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which event marked the start of the 'social banking' and multi-agency approach to rural credit in India?
(1) The setting up of the Planning Commission in 1950
(2) The Green Revolution in the mid-1960s
(3) The nationalisation of commercial banks in 1969
(4) The economic reforms of 1991
= 3
~ After the nationalisation of commercial banks in 1969, India adopted social banking and a multi-agency approach to meet the needs of rural credit.

Q 4 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which of the following together make up the institutional structure of rural banking described in the chapter?
(1) Commercial banks, regional rural banks, co-operatives and land development banks
(2) Moneylenders, traders and landlords
(3) Only the Reserve Bank of India
(4) Insurance companies and stock exchanges
= 1
~ Rural banking today is a set of multi-agency institutions: commercial banks, regional rural banks (RRBs), co-operatives and land development banks, which provide cheaper credit than moneylenders.

Q 5 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which statement about self-help groups (SHGs) is correct according to the chapter?
(1) Members pool small savings, and credit is given from this pool to needy members, repayable in small instalments at reasonable interest
(2) SHGs are branches of commercial banks
(3) SHGs lend only to big farmers
(4) SHGs collect taxes for the government
= 1
~ SHGs promote thrift through small regular contributions by each member. From the pooled money, members get loans repayable in small instalments at reasonable rates, filling a gap in the formal credit system.

Q 6 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Credit provided through programmes such as self-help groups is generally called:
(1) Priority sector lending
(2) Crop insurance
(3) Microcredit
(4) Venture capital
= 3
~ Such programmes are referred to as microcredit programmes. SHGs have also helped in the empowerment of women.

Q 7 | eco | Indian Economic Development
What limitation of borrowing through self-help groups does the chapter point out?
(1) SHGs charge higher interest than moneylenders
(2) Only men are allowed to borrow
(3) SHG loans cannot be repaid in instalments
(4) The borrowings are mainly confined to consumption purposes
= 4
~ The chapter notes that SHG loans are used mostly for consumption, and borrowing for productive purposes remains limited.

Q 8 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Kudumbashree, discussed in the chapter, is:
(1) A crop insurance scheme in Punjab
(2) A women-oriented, community-based poverty reduction programme in Kerala
(3) A dairy co-operative in Gujarat
(4) A farmers' market in Tamil Nadu

Q 9 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Agricultural marketing, as described in the chapter, includes:
(1) Only the sale of crops in villages
(2) Only the export of food grains
(3) Only the fixing of prices by the government
(4) Assembling, storage, processing, transportation, packaging, grading and distribution of agricultural commodities
= 4
~ Agricultural marketing is the whole process through which farm produce reaches consumers, including assembling, storage, processing, transport, packaging, grading and distribution.

Q 10 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Why did the government regulate agricultural markets?
(1) To create orderly and transparent marketing conditions that benefit both farmers and consumers
(2) To stop farmers from selling their crops
(3) To fix the same price for all crops
(4) To hand over markets to private traders
= 1
~ Private traders and moneylenders often cheated farmers through faulty weighing and manipulated accounts. Regulated markets were meant to create orderly and transparent conditions.

Q 12 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which of the following policy instruments does the chapter mention in agricultural marketing?
1. Minimum support prices for agricultural products
2. Buffer stocks of wheat and rice maintained by the Food Corporation of India
3. Distribution of food grains and sugar through the Public Distribution System
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 1, 2 and 3

Q 14 | eco | Indian Economic Development
According to the chapter, diversification of agricultural activities has two aspects. What are they?
(1) Mechanisation and irrigation
(2) Land reforms and credit
(3) Exports and imports of food grains
(4) A change in cropping pattern, and a shift of workforce from agriculture to allied activities and non-agricultural work
= 4
~ Diversification means growing a wider range of crops and moving workers into allied activities such as livestock, poultry and fisheries, and into non-farm work.

Q 15 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Why is diversification of rural employment needed?
(1) To reduce the risk of depending only on farming and to provide productive, sustainable livelihood options
(2) Because farming is no longer allowed in villages
(3) To raise the number of people in agriculture
(4) Because all farmers have become rich
= 1
~ Agriculture is risky, and in areas without irrigation it is hard to find work in the rabi season. Diversification reduces this risk and gives rural people other sources of income.

Q 16 | eco | Indian Economic Development
In India's mixed crop-livestock farming system, which animals are the most widely held?
(1) Horses, camels and elephants
(2) Cattle, goats and fowl
(3) Sheep and pigs only
(4) Fish and ducks
= 2
~ Farmers combine crops with livestock, and cattle, goats and fowl are the most widely held. Livestock gives families stable income, food security and work, especially for women.

Q 17 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Operation Flood, mentioned in the chapter, is related to:
(1) Dairy: farmers pool their milk, which is processed and marketed through co-operatives

Q 18 | eco | Indian Economic Development
In horticulture, the period 1991–2003 is described as the:
(1) Green Revolution
(2) White Revolution
(3) Blue Revolution
(4) Golden Revolution
= 4
~ Planned investment in horticulture became highly productive in this period, which is called the Golden Revolution. Horticulture covers fruits, vegetables, flowers, spices, plantation crops and medicinal plants.

Q 19 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which of the following is a benefit of organic farming mentioned in the chapter?
(1) It always gives higher yields than modern farming from the first year
(2) It replaces costlier agricultural inputs with locally produced, cheaper organic inputs and can generate good returns
(3) It needs no labour
(4) Its produce lasts longer than other produce
= 2
~ Organic farming uses cheaper local inputs, can earn income through exports, needs more labour and so creates employment, and gives healthier food.

Q 20 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Consider the following statements about the limitations of organic farming:
1. Yields may be lower than in modern farming in the initial years.
2. Organic produce may have more blemishes and a shorter shelf life.
3. It needs less labour than conventional farming.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 2 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 3
~ Statement 3 is wrong: organic farming needs more labour, which is one of its advantages in a country like India. Lower early yields, blemishes and shorter shelf life are its limitations, along with limited choice in the off-season.
