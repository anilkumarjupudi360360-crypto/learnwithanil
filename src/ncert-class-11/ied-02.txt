Q 3 | eco | Indian Economic Development
In the chapter, 'modernisation' as a goal of planning means:
(1) Shifting all workers from villages to cities
(2) Replacing Indian languages with English in administration
(3) Importing all machinery from developed countries
(4) Adopting new technology in production and also changing social outlook, such as accepting that women should have the same rights as men
= 4
~ Modernisation covers new technology, such as a farmer using new seed varieties or a factory using a new machine, and a change in social outlook, such as recognising women's equal rights.

Q 4 | eco | Indian Economic Development
What did the goal of 'self-reliance' mean in India's early five year plans?
(1) Refusing all foreign aid and trade
(2) Avoiding imports of goods that could be produced in India, especially food, to reduce dependence on foreign countries
(3) Each village producing everything it needed
(4) Allowing only private firms to produce consumer goods
= 2
~ The first seven plans stressed self-reliance: avoid importing goods, particularly food, that could be produced at home. Newly independent India feared that dependence on imports would make it vulnerable.

Q 6 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which economic system did India choose after independence?
(1) A capitalist economy in which the market decides everything
(2) A socialist economy on the model of the Soviet Union with no private property
(3) A barter economy
(4) A mixed economy in which both the state and the market play a role, combining features of capitalism and socialism
= 4
~ India's leaders chose a mixed economy: a strong public sector along with private property and markets. The state would plan the economy while the private sector would be encouraged as part of the plan.

Q 7 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Consider the following statements about land reforms after independence:
1. The abolition of intermediaries aimed to make the actual tillers the owners of the land.
2. Land ceiling meant fixing the maximum size of land that an individual could own.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) Both 1 and 2
(2) 1 only
(3) 2 only
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 1
~ Land reforms had two main parts: abolishing intermediaries such as zamindars so that tillers became owners, and land ceilings to reduce the concentration of land ownership.

Q 8 | eco | Indian Economic Development
According to the chapter, which two states had governments committed to the policy of 'land to the tiller' and achieved success in land reforms?
(1) Punjab and Haryana
(2) Gujarat and Maharashtra
(3) Kerala and West Bengal
(4) Bihar and Uttar Pradesh
= 3
~ Land reforms were most successful in Kerala and West Bengal, whose governments were committed to 'land to the tiller'. Most other states did not show the same commitment.

Q 9 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Why did the land ceiling legislation largely fail in many states?
(1) There was no surplus land anywhere in India
(2) Tenants refused to accept the land
(3) The Supreme Court abolished all land ceilings
(4) Big landlords challenged the laws in the courts, delaying them, and meanwhile registered their land in the names of relatives
= 4
~ Big landlords used court cases to delay implementation and in the meantime transferred land to the names of close relatives, so very little surplus land was actually taken over.

Q 10 | eco | Indian Economic Development
The Green Revolution refers to:
(1) A large increase in the production of food grains from the use of high yielding variety (HYV) seeds, especially for wheat and rice
(2) A movement to plant trees across India
(3) The expansion of dairy co-operatives
(4) The spread of organic farming in the 1950s
= 1
~ The Green Revolution was the large rise in food grain output from HYV seeds used with fertilisers, pesticides and regular irrigation. Wheat and rice benefited the most.

Q 11 | eco | Indian Economic Development
The first phase of the Green Revolution (mid-1960s to mid-1970s) was largely restricted to:
(1) Bihar, Odisha and Assam
(2) All states equally
(3) More affluent states such as Punjab, Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu
(4) Rajasthan and Gujarat only
= 3
~ In the first phase HYV seeds were used mainly in the more affluent states, and their benefits went mainly to the wheat-growing regions. In the second phase (mid-1970s to mid-1980s) the technology spread to more states and crops.

Q 12 | eco | Indian Economic Development
What is 'marketed surplus'?
(1) The total production of a farm
(2) The portion of agricultural produce that farmers sell in the market
(3) The food grains kept by the government in buffer stock
(4) The produce lost in storage
= 2
~ Marketed surplus is the part of the produce sold in the market. During the Green Revolution farmers sold a good share of the higher output, food prices fell relative to other goods, and the government could build a buffer stock.

Q 13 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which of the following risks of the HYV technology are discussed in the chapter?
1. It could increase disparities between small and big farmers.
2. HYV crops were more prone to attack by pests.
3. HYV seeds did not need any fertilisers or irrigation.
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1
~ There were fears that only big farmers could afford the inputs and that pest attacks could ruin small farmers. These fears were reduced by government loans at low interest and subsidised fertilisers. Statement 3 is wrong: HYV seeds needed fertilisers and irrigation.

Q 14 | eco | Indian Economic Development
In the debate on agricultural subsidies, which argument is made by those who want subsidies to be gradually removed?
(1) Farmers need subsidies to try out risky new technology
(2) Most farmers are poor and cannot afford inputs without subsidies
(3) Subsidies are needed to keep food grain production high
(4) Subsidies mostly benefit the fertiliser industry and farmers in prosperous regions, and place a huge burden on government finances
= 4
~ Supporters say subsidies are needed for small, poor farmers to adopt new technology. Critics argue that once the technology is profitable, subsidies mainly benefit the fertiliser industry and well-off farmers, so they are a waste of public money.

Q 15 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Despite the Green Revolution, what does the chapter point out as a weakness of Indian agriculture even in 1990?
(1) Food grain output was lower than in 1950
(2) India had to import most of its wheat
(3) About 65 per cent of the population still depended on agriculture, though agriculture's share in GDP had fallen considerably
(4) Land reforms had made all farmers equal
= 3
~ As a country develops, the share of people in agriculture should fall. In India, agriculture's share in GDP fell but as much as 65 per cent of the population continued to be employed in it in 1990.

Q 16 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Into how many categories did the Industrial Policy Resolution of 1956 classify industries?
(1) Two
(2) Three
(3) Four
(4) Five
= 2
~ The 1956 resolution set out three categories: industries owned exclusively by the state; industries where the private sector could supplement the state, which would start new units; and the remaining industries, left to the private sector.

Q 17 | eco | Indian Economic Development
How was industrial licensing used to promote regional equality?
(1) Licences were given only to public sector firms
(2) Licences were banned in big cities
(3) Each state was given an equal number of licences
(4) Licences were given more easily for units in economically backward areas, which also got concessions such as tax benefits and cheaper electricity
= 4
~ No new industry could be set up or expanded without a licence. The government used this to steer industry to backward regions by granting licences more easily there and offering concessions.

Q 18 | eco | Indian Economic Development
The Village and Small-Scale Industries Committee of 1955, which saw small industry as a way to promote rural development, is also known as the:
(1) Karve Committee
(2) Mahalanobis Committee
(3) Narasimham Committee
(4) Tapas Majumdar Committee
= 1
~ The Karve Committee noted that small-scale industries are more labour-intensive and could be used to promote rural development.

Q 19 | eco | Indian Economic Development
In 1950, a small-scale industrial unit was defined as one with a maximum investment of:
(1) ₹50,000
(2) ₹1 lakh
(3) ₹5 lakh
(4) ₹1 crore
= 3
~ Small-scale industry is defined by the maximum investment allowed in a unit's assets. In 1950 the limit was ₹5 lakh; it has been raised several times since.

Q 20 | eco | Indian Economic Development
India's trade policy during 1950–1990 protected domestic industries mainly through:
(1) Export subsidies and free trade agreements
(2) Tariffs and quotas on imports
(3) Repeated devaluation of the rupee
(4) Large inflows of foreign direct investment
= 2
~ The policy of import substitution was inward looking. Tariffs (taxes on imports) made foreign goods costlier and quotas fixed the quantity that could be imported, shielding Indian firms from foreign competition.
