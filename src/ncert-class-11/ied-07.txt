Q 1 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which of the following are functions of the environment described in the chapter?
1. It supplies renewable and non-renewable resources.
2. It assimilates waste.
3. It sustains life by providing genetic and biodiversity.
4. It provides aesthetic services such as scenery.
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 1, 2 and 3 only
(3) 2, 3 and 4 only
(4) 1, 2, 3 and 4
= 4
~ The chapter lists all four functions. The environment can perform them without interruption only as long as the demand on them stays within its carrying capacity.

Q 2 | eco | Indian Economic Development
'Carrying capacity' of the environment implies that:
(1) The environment can carry any amount of waste
(2) Resource extraction is not above the rate of regeneration of the resource, and the wastes generated are within the assimilating capacity of the environment
(3) All resources are non-renewable
(4) The population must be reduced to zero
= 2
~ When extraction exceeds regeneration or waste exceeds what the environment can absorb, the environment fails to perform its life-sustaining function and an environmental crisis results.

Q 3 | eco | Indian Economic Development
'Absorptive capacity' means:
(1) The amount of money a country can borrow
(2) The capacity of factories to use raw materials
(3) The ability of the environment to absorb degradation
(4) The number of people a city can house
= 3
~ Absorptive capacity is the environment's ability to absorb degradation. When degradation goes beyond it, pollution and other problems build up.

Q 4 | eco | Indian Economic Development
The chapter says the 'opportunity cost' of negative environmental impacts is high. What does this mean?
(1) Money that could be used elsewhere has to be spent on dealing with the effects of pollution, such as health problems and clean-up
(2) Environmental protection is free
(3) Polluting industries earn high profits
(4) The government earns revenue from pollution
= 1
~ Pollution leads to diseases and degraded resources. The government has to spend large sums on health and on improving environmental quality, money that could have gone to other uses.

Q 5 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Global warming is:
(1) The warming of oceans during summer
(2) A rise in temperature caused only by volcanoes
(3) A gradual increase in the average temperature of the earth's lower atmosphere because of the increase in greenhouse gases since the Industrial Revolution
(4) The heating of cities by sunlight
= 3
~ Burning of fossil fuels and deforestation add greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide and methane, which trap heat and gradually warm the lower atmosphere.

Q 6 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Under the Kyoto Protocol, agreed at the UN conference on climate change in Kyoto, Japan, in 1997:
(1) All countries agreed to stop using coal immediately
(2) Industrialised nations agreed to reduce their greenhouse gas emissions
(3) India agreed to stop industrial growth
(4) CFCs were banned
= 2
~ The Kyoto Protocol was an international agreement under which industrialised nations were to cut their greenhouse gas emissions. Banning CFCs was the job of the Montreal Protocol.

Q 7 | eco | Indian Economic Development
According to the chapter, ozone depletion is caused mainly by:
(1) Carbon dioxide from vehicles
(2) Methane from paddy fields
(3) Dust storms
(4) Chlorine and bromine compounds such as chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) and halons
= 4
~ High levels of chlorine and bromine compounds in the stratosphere destroy ozone. They come from CFCs used in air conditioners, refrigerators and aerosol propellants, and from halons used in fire extinguishers.

Q 8 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which international agreement banned the use of CFCs and other ozone-depleting chemicals?
(1) Montreal Protocol
(2) Kyoto Protocol
(3) Paris Agreement
(4) Rio Declaration
= 1
~ The Montreal Protocol banned CFC compounds and other ozone-depleting chemicals. Thinning of the ozone layer lets more ultraviolet radiation reach the earth, causing skin cancer and other harm.

Q 9 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which of the following is NOT among the priority environmental issues for India listed in the chapter?
(1) Land degradation
(2) Volcanic eruptions
(3) Biodiversity loss
(4) Solid waste management
= 2
~ The priority issues are land degradation, biodiversity loss, air pollution (especially vehicular pollution in cities), management of fresh water and solid waste management. Volcanic eruptions are not among them.

Q 12 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Sustainable development has been defined as:
(1) Development that gives the highest growth rate today
(2) Development that uses only renewable resources
(3) Development that meets the needs of the present generation without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs
(4) Development that stops all use of natural resources
= 3
~ This is the definition given by the UN Conference on Environment and Development, and the idea is associated with the Brundtland Commission.

Q 13 | eco | Indian Economic Development
According to the chapter, the Brundtland Commission's idea of sustainable development emphasises:
(1) Raising industrial output at any cost
(2) Protecting the future generation, in line with the view that we must hand over the planet earth in good order
(3) Ending all foreign trade
(4) Moving people out of rural areas
= 2
~ The Brundtland Commission emphasises protecting future generations. This matches the environmentalists' argument that we have a moral obligation to hand the earth over to future generations in good order.

Q 14 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which of the following is one of Herman Daly's conditions for sustainable development, as given in the chapter?
(1) The rate of depletion of non-renewable resources should not exceed the rate of creation of renewable substitutes
(2) Population should grow as fast as possible
(3) Technology should use more inputs to produce output
(4) Renewable resources may be extracted faster than they regenerate
= 1
~ Daly's conditions include limiting population within the carrying capacity, input-efficient technological progress, extracting renewable resources no faster than they regenerate, depleting non-renewable resources no faster than renewable substitutes are created, and correcting the inefficiencies caused by pollution.

Q 15 | eco | Indian Economic Development

Q 16 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Gobar gas (biogas) plants promoted in rural areas:
(1) Burn wood to produce electricity
(2) Run on diesel
(3) Use cattle dung to produce gas for fuel and lighting, and the leftover slurry is a good organic fertiliser
(4) Need large dams
= 3
~ Rural households traditionally burn wood, dung cake and other biomass, which causes indoor pollution. Biogas from cattle dung gives cleaner fuel and lighting, and the slurry makes good manure.

Q 17 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which of the following is NOT one of the non-conventional, cleaner sources of energy suggested in the chapter?
(1) Wind power
(2) Solar power through photovoltaic cells
(3) Mini-hydel plants
(4) Coal-based thermal power
= 4
~ Thermal power plants burning coal emit large amounts of carbon dioxide. The chapter suggests wind power, solar power and mini-hydel plants as cleaner alternatives.

Q 18 | eco | Indian Economic Development
How do mini-hydel plants work, as described in the chapter?
(1) They need huge dams that submerge forests
(2) They use the energy of mountain streams to move small turbines and meet local power needs, without changing land use much
(3) They run on imported uranium
(4) They burn agricultural waste
= 2
~ In mountainous regions, streams flow almost everywhere. Mini-hydel plants use them to turn small turbines; they are environment-friendly, do not change land use and generate enough power for local demand.

Q 19 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which of the following is an example of biopest control mentioned in the chapter?
(1) Using neem-based pesticides and protecting pest-eating animals and birds such as snakes, owls and peacocks
(2) Spraying more chemical pesticides
(3) Burning crop residue
(4) Using CFCs in storage
= 1
~ Chemical pesticides contaminate food, soil and water. Biopest control uses plant-based products such as neem and natural predators like snakes, owls and peacocks to keep pests in check.

Q 20 | eco | Indian Economic Development
In vermicomposting, organic waste is converted into compost with the help of:
(1) Bees
(2) Locusts
(3) Earthworms
(4) Termites
= 3
~ Biocomposting returns organic matter to the soil. Earthworms convert organic matter into compost faster than the normal composting process, reducing the need for chemical fertilisers.
