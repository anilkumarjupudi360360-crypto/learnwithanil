Q 1 | geo | India: Physical Environment
The word "monsoon" is derived from the Arabic word "mausim", which means:
(1) Rain
(2) Season
(3) Wind
(4) Storm
= 2
~ Monsoon refers to the seasonal reversal of winds through the year.

Q 2 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Consider the following statements about the diversity of India's climate:
1. In summer, temperatures may touch 55°C in western Rajasthan, while in winter they may drop to around minus 45°C around Leh.
2. Annual precipitation ranges from over 400 cm in Meghalaya to less than 10 cm in Ladakh and western Rajasthan.
3. All parts of India receive most of their rainfall between October and December.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1
~ Most of India receives rain from June to September. Some parts, like the Tamil Nadu coast, get much of their rain in October and November.

Q 3 | geo | India: Physical Environment
How do the Himalayas affect India's climate, according to the chapter?
(1) They block the monsoon winds from entering India
(2) They protect the subcontinent from cold northern winds and trap the monsoon winds, forcing them to shed moisture
(3) They make north India warmer in summer than the desert
(4) They have no climatic effect
= 2
~ The Himalayas act as an effective climatic divide. The high mountain wall shields India from frigid winds and helps confine the monsoon to the subcontinent.

Q 4 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Which of the following is listed in the chapter as a factor related to air pressure and winds that influences India's climate?
(1) Distance from the sea
(2) Altitude
(3) Latitude
(4) Western cyclonic disturbances in winter and tropical depressions during the south-west monsoon
= 4
~ The chapter separates factors related to location and relief (latitude, Himalayas, land–water distribution, distance from sea, altitude, relief) from factors related to air pressure and winds (surface winds, upper air circulation and jet streams, western cyclones and tropical depressions).

Q 5 | geo | India: Physical Environment
The western cyclonic disturbances that bring winter rain to north-western India originate over:
(1) The Bay of Bengal
(2) The Arabian Sea
(3) The Mediterranean Sea
(4) The Pacific Ocean
= 3
~ They are brought into India by the westerly jet stream. A rise in the prevailing night temperature generally signals their approach.

Q 6 | geo | India: Physical Environment
In June, the easterly jet stream blows over the southern part of the Peninsula with a maximum speed of about 90 km per hour. Its role is to:
(1) Bring western disturbances in winter
(2) Cause the retreat of the monsoon in winter
(3) Stop the monsoon from reaching Kerala
(4) Steer tropical depressions into India, which influence the distribution of monsoon rainfall
= 4
~ The tracks of these depressions are the areas of highest rainfall in India; their frequency, direction and intensity shape the rainfall pattern.

(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only

Q 8 | geo | India: Physical Environment
To predict the intensity of the monsoon, the difference in pressure is computed between:
(1) Mumbai and Kolkata
(2) Tokyo and Delhi
(3) Madagascar and Sri Lanka
(4) Tahiti in the Pacific Ocean and Darwin in northern Australia
= 4
~ This periodic change in pressure is the Southern Oscillation. If the pressure differences are negative, it means below-average and late monsoons.

Q 9 | geo | India: Physical Environment
El Niño, connected with the Southern Oscillation, is described in the chapter as:
(1) A warm ocean current that flows past the Peruvian coast, in place of the cold Peruvian current, every 2 to 5 years
(2) A cold current flowing past the coast of India every year
(3) A local storm in Kerala
(4) The jet stream over Tibet
= 1
~ Because pressure changes are linked to El Niño, the combined phenomenon is called ENSO.

Q 10 | geo | India: Physical Environment
By which date does the south-west monsoon usually set in over the Kerala coast?
(1) 1st May
(2) 1st June
(3) 15th July
(4) 1st September
= 2
~ The monsoon reaches Kerala by 1st June, moves swiftly to reach Mumbai and Kolkata between 10th and 13th June, and covers the whole subcontinent by mid-July.

Q 11 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Consider the following statements about breaks in the monsoon:
1. In northern India, rains may fail if rain-bearing storms are not frequent along the monsoon trough.
2. Over the west coast, dry spells are associated with days when winds blow parallel to the coast.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 3
~ A break is a period of one or more weeks without rain during the monsoon season.

Q 12 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Which of the following is NOT given in the chapter as a reason for the excessive cold in north India during winter?
(1) Distance from the moderating influence of the sea
(2) Snowfall in the nearby Himalayan ranges
(3) Cold winds from the Caspian Sea and Turkmenistan around February
(4) Warm ocean currents near the coast
= 4
~ The first three reasons are given in the chapter; together they make north India, especially Punjab, Haryana and Rajasthan, very cold.

Q 13 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Match the local storms of the hot weather season with their descriptions:
A. Mango shower — 1. Helps coffee flowers blossom in Kerala and nearby areas
B. Blossom shower — 2. Pre-monsoon showers in Kerala and coastal Karnataka that help mangoes ripen early
C. Nor Westers (Kalbaisakhi) — 3. Hot, dry, oppressive winds of the northern plains
D. Loo — 4. Dreaded evening thunderstorms in Bengal and Assam
Select the correct answer:
(1) A-2, B-1, C-4, D-3
(2) A-1, B-2, C-3, D-4
(3) A-2, B-1, C-3, D-4
(4) A-4, B-3, C-2, D-1

~ Nor Westers are useful for tea, jute and rice; in Assam they are called "Bardoli Chheerha". The loo blows strongest between Delhi and Patna.

Q 14 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Why does the Tamil Nadu coast remain relatively dry during the south-west monsoon season?
(1) It lies parallel to the Bay of Bengal branch and in the rain-shadow area of the Arabian Sea branch
(2) It is too close to the equator
(3) It is surrounded by deserts
(4) The monsoon never reaches south India
= 1
~ The coast gets most of its rain later, in October and November, from the north-east monsoon and cyclonic depressions.

Q 15 | geo | India: Physical Environment
The Arabian Sea branch of the monsoon strikes the Western Ghats. The windward side receives very heavy rainfall of about:
(1) 25–50 cm
(2) 50–100 cm
(3) 250–400 cm
(4) More than 1,000 cm
= 3
~ After crossing the Western Ghats the winds descend and warm up, so the area east of the Ghats is a rain-shadow area with little rainfall.

Q 16 | geo | India: Physical Environment
Mawsynram, which receives the highest average annual rainfall in the world, is located on the crest of the:
(1) Garo hills
(2) Khasi hills
(3) Western Ghats
(4) Nilgiri hills
= 2
~ A sub-branch of the Bay of Bengal monsoon strikes the Garo and Khasi hills of Meghalaya; Mawsynram on the crest of the Khasi hills gets the highest rainfall.

Q 17 | geo | India: Physical Environment
The hot and humid, oppressive weather during the retreat of the monsoon, when skies are clear and the land is still moist, is commonly known as:
(1) Loo
(2) October heat
(3) Mango shower
(4) Kalbaisakhi
= 2
~ In the second half of October, temperatures begin to fall rapidly, especially in northern India.

Q 18 | geo | India: Physical Environment

1. October and November are the rainiest months on the eastern part of the Peninsula.
2. Cyclonic depressions originating over the Arabian Sea cross the western coast and bring this rain.
3. The densely populated deltas of the Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri are preferred targets of these cyclones.

(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3

Q 19 | geo | India: Physical Environment
In the traditional Indian calendar of six seasons, "Sharada" corresponds to which months?
(1) Chaitra–Vaisakha (March–April)
(2) Sravana–Bhadra (July–August)
(3) Asvina–Kartika (September–October)
(4) Magha–Phalguna (January–February)
= 3
~ The six seasons are Vasanta, Grishma, Varsha, Sharada, Hemanta and Shishira; Sharada falls in Asvina–Kartika.
