# NCERT Class 11 History, Themes in World History, Chapter 2: An Empire Across Three Continents
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | his | Themes in World History
The Roman Empire at its height covered parts of which three continents?
(1) Europe, West Asia and North Africa
(2) Europe, Asia and North America
(3) Asia, Africa and Australia
(4) Europe, South Asia and East Africa
= 1
~ The empire stretched around the Mediterranean, which was the heart of the empire. It included much of Europe, the Fertile Crescent in West Asia and North Africa.

Q 2 | his | Themes in World History
Which rivers formed the northern frontier of the Roman Empire in Europe?
(1) The Rhine and the Danube
(2) The Tigris and the Euphrates
(3) The Nile and the Rhone
(4) The Thames and the Seine
= 1
~ North of the Rhine and Danube lived Germanic peoples outside the empire. To the east, the Euphrates marked the frontier with Iran for much of the period.

Q 3 | his | Themes in World History
The first Roman emperor, who founded the regime known as the "Principate" in 27 BCE, was:
(1) Julius Caesar
(2) Augustus
(3) Constantine
(4) Trajan
= 2
~ Augustus, the adopted son of Julius Caesar, presented himself as the princeps, the "leading citizen", not an absolute ruler, out of respect for the Senate.

Q 4 | his | Themes in World History
The three main players in the political history of the early Roman Empire were:
(1) The emperor, the Pope and the army
(2) The Senate, the Church and the merchants
(3) The emperor, the aristocracy (Senate) and the army
(4) The emperor, the governors and the slaves
= 3
~ The Senate had existed for centuries under the Republic and represented the aristocracy. The army, paid and professional, could make and unmake emperors.

Q 5 | his | Themes in World History
Before the empire, Rome had been a Republic (509 BCE to 27 BCE). Who held real power under the Republic?
(1) An elected president
(2) A hereditary king
(3) A government in which the Senate, controlled by a small group of wealthy families, held power
(4) The Pope
= 3
~ The Republic was in practice an oligarchy of the nobility. It ended when Octavian, later Augustus, took control.

Q 6 | his | Themes in World History
The Roman army was distinctive because:
(1) It was made up of slaves
(2) It was a paid, professional army in which soldiers served for a minimum term of 25 years
(3) It was raised only in times of war
(4) It was commanded by the Senate alone
= 2
~ A paid army was a key feature of the Roman state. Soldiers often agitated for better pay and conditions, sometimes through mutinies.

Q 7 | his | Themes in World History
The chief rival of Rome to the east was:
(1) The Mauryan empire
(2) Egypt of the Pharaohs
(3) The Mongol empire
(4) Iran, ruled first by the Parthians and, from 225 CE, by the Sasanians
= 4
~ Rome and Iran fought often. The Sasanians, who came to power in the third century, were especially aggressive and invaded Roman lands.

Q 8 | his | Themes in World History
The "third-century crisis" of the Roman Empire involved which of the following?
1. Attacks by the Sasanians in the east and by Germanic groups across the Rhine and Danube
2. Rapid change of emperors (about 25 emperors in 47 years, 235-284)
3. A long period of complete peace
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1
~ Rome faced war on several fronts. The frequent change of rulers showed the strains on the empire in this period.

Q 9 | his | Themes in World History
Which language was widely used in the eastern part of the Roman Empire, and which in the west?
(1) Latin in the east and Greek in the west
(2) Aramaic in both
(3) Greek in the east and Latin in the west
(4) Coptic in the east and Celtic in the west
= 3
~ The empire had many local languages, but Greek and Latin were the languages of administration and of educated people.

Q 10 | his | Themes in World History
Which of the following best describes Roman families and women, as given in the chapter?
(1) Joint families were the rule, and women had no property rights
(2) Women could not marry
(3) The nuclear family was widely prevalent, and in the common form of marriage a wife kept full rights to the property of her father
(4) Women ruled the Senate
= 3
~ Women enjoyed considerable legal rights in owning and managing property, although they were under the authority of their fathers or husbands in many other respects, and divorce was relatively easy.

Q 11 | his | Themes in World History
Which of the following was an important commodity carried in amphorae across the Mediterranean, with southern Spain a leading producer?
(1) Olive oil
(2) Rice
(3) Tea
(4) Coffee
= 1
~ The amphora containers found in large numbers show which products were traded. Spanish olive oil was carried in containers called Dressel 20.

Q 12 | his | Themes in World History
Slavery in the Roman Empire:
(1) Did not exist
(2) Was deeply rooted, especially in Italy, though in the early empire slaves became more expensive and employers also used hired labour
(3) Applied only to soldiers
(4) Was abolished by Augustus
= 2
~ Slaves were an investment. Writers on agriculture, such as Columella, discussed how to manage them, and some slaves were later freed.

Q 13 | his | Themes in World History
Which emperor introduced the solidus, a gold coin of about 4.5 grams of pure gold, and founded a second capital?
(1) Augustus
(2) Tiberius
(3) Nero
(4) Constantine
= 4
~ Constantine stabilised the currency with the solidus, which remained in circulation for centuries. He founded Constantinople (now Istanbul) as the new capital.

Q 14 | his | Themes in World History
Which religion became the state religion of the Roman Empire in the fourth century, after Constantine's conversion?
(1) Islam
(2) Judaism
(3) Zoroastrianism
(4) Christianity
= 4
~ Christianisation was a gradual process. Before it, the Romans worshipped many gods, such as Jupiter, Juno, Minerva and Mars.

Q 15 | his | Themes in World History
The term "late antiquity" refers to:
(1) The earliest Roman kings
(2) The last phase of the Roman Empire, broadly the fourth to the seventh centuries
(3) The Roman Republic
(4) The period after the French Revolution
= 2
~ Late antiquity saw big changes in religion, culture and the economy, including Christianisation and, in the seventh century, the rise of Islam.

Q 16 | his | Themes in World History
Which emperor reorganised the empire after the third-century crisis, cutting back territories that had little strategic value and fortifying the frontiers?
(1) Augustus
(2) Trajan
(3) Hadrian
(4) Diocletian
= 4
~ Diocletian (284-305) also separated civil and military functions. Constantine continued many of his changes.

Q 17 | his | Themes in World History
The Roman Empire was culturally diverse. Which of the following was a feature of Roman cities?
(1) They were all in Italy
(2) Urban life had public baths and entertainments, and cities were centres of administration and tax collection
(3) They had no public baths
(4) They had no connection with the countryside
= 2
~ Cities such as Carthage, Alexandria and Antioch were large and important. The government ruled the provinces through these cities.

Q 18 | his | Themes in World History
Egypt was important to the Roman Empire mainly because:
(1) It supplied the Romans with iron
(2) It was the home of the Senate
(3) It was a rich province that produced large quantities of grain
(4) It produced olive oil
= 3
~ Egypt's agriculture along the Nile made it valuable. Papyrus documents from Egypt are also an important source for Roman history.

Q 19 | his | Themes in World History
The Roman emperor Trajan's conquest of territory across the Euphrates in 113-17 CE was:
(1) Held for a thousand years
(2) Given to India
(3) The start of the Republic
(4) Abandoned by his successors
= 4
~ The empire later reached its natural limits. Except for Trajan's short-lived conquest, the Euphrates remained the eastern border for most of the period.

Q 20 | his | Themes in World History
In the seventh century, the eastern and southern parts of the old Roman world came under:
(1) The Arabs, as the expansion of Islam brought them large territories
(2) The Mongols
(3) The Huns
(4) The Mauryas
= 1
~ The Arab conquests, beginning in the 630s, took Syria, Egypt and other provinces. With this, the late antique world was transformed.
