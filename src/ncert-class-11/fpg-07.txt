Q 1 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
After nitrogen and oxygen, which gas has the largest share by volume in the atmosphere, according to the chapter's table?
(1) Carbon dioxide
(2) Helium
(3) Neon
(4) Argon
= 4
~ Nitrogen is about 78 per cent and oxygen about 21 per cent; argon comes next at about 0.93 per cent. Carbon dioxide is only about 0.036 per cent.

Q 2 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
About 99 per cent of the total mass of the atmosphere lies within what height from the earth's surface?
(1) 8 km
(2) 32 km
(3) 80 km
(4) 400 km
= 2
~ The atmosphere is dense near the surface and thins rapidly with height; 99 per cent of its mass lies within 32 km.

Q 3 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements:
1. The proportion of oxygen becomes almost negligible at a height of about 120 km.
2. Carbon dioxide and water vapour are found only up to about 90 km from the surface.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 3
~ The chapter notes that the composition of the higher layers changes; oxygen nearly disappears by 120 km, and CO2 and water vapour do not go beyond about 90 km.

Q 4 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Carbon dioxide is meteorologically important because it is:
(1) Opaque to incoming solar radiation but transparent to outgoing terrestrial radiation
(2) Transparent to incoming solar radiation but opaque to outgoing terrestrial radiation
(3) Opaque to both incoming and outgoing radiation
(4) Transparent to both incoming and outgoing radiation
= 2
~ CO2 lets sunlight in but absorbs part of the heat radiated by the earth and sends some back to the surface. This is the greenhouse effect; its rising volume, mainly from burning fossil fuels, raises air temperature.

Q 5 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The ozone in the atmosphere is mostly found between which heights above the earth's surface?
(1) 0 and 5 km
(2) 10 and 50 km
(3) 80 and 400 km
(4) 400 and 1,000 km
= 2
~ Ozone, found between 10 and 50 km, acts as a filter that absorbs ultraviolet rays from the sun and stops them from reaching the surface.

Q 6 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements about water vapour:
1. It may account for up to four per cent of the air by volume in the warm and wet tropics.
2. It makes up less than one per cent of the air in dry and cold areas of deserts and polar regions.
3. Its amount increases with altitude.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1
~ Water vapour is a variable gas that decreases with altitude and from the equator towards the poles. So statement 3 is wrong.

Q 7 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Water vapour is said to act "like a blanket" around the earth because it:
(1) Reflects all sunlight back to space
(2) Absorbs part of the insolation and preserves the heat radiated by the earth
(3) Removes heat from the earth's surface at night
(4) Blocks the formation of clouds
= 2
~ By absorbing some incoming insolation and holding the earth's radiated heat, water vapour keeps the earth from becoming too hot or too cold. It also affects the stability of the air.

Q 8 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Dust particles are found in higher concentration in subtropical and temperate regions than in equatorial and polar regions. The reason given is:
(1) Greater rainfall in subtropical regions
(2) Volcanic eruptions at the poles
(3) Absence of vegetation at the equator
(4) Dry winds in subtropical and temperate regions
= 4
~ Dry winds lift and carry more dust in subtropical and temperate areas. Dust is mostly concentrated in the lower layers of the atmosphere.

Q 9 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Why are dust and salt particles in the air important for clouds?
(1) They act as hygroscopic nuclei around which water vapour condenses
(2) They raise the temperature of the air
(3) They absorb ozone
(4) They prevent rainfall
= 1
~ Water vapour condenses around these tiny particles, called hygroscopic nuclei, to produce clouds.

Q 10 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The average height of the troposphere is about 13 km. It is thickest at the equator, about 18 km, because:
(1) The equator has the highest air pressure
(2) The earth's gravity is strongest at the equator
(3) There is more ozone at the equator
(4) Heat is carried to great heights by strong convectional currents at the equator
= 4
~ Strong convection lifts heat high over the equator, so the troposphere is about 18 km thick there and only about 8 km near the poles.

Q 11 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
In the troposphere, temperature decreases with height at the rate of about:
(1) 1°C for every 165 m
(2) 1°C for every 1,000 m
(3) 10°C for every 165 m
(4) It does not change with height
= 1
~ The chapter gives a decrease of 1°C for every 165 m of height in the troposphere. Almost all weather changes occur in this layer.

Q 12 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The tropopause, the zone separating the troposphere from the stratosphere, has an air temperature of about:
(1) –80°C over the equator and –45°C over the poles
(2) –45°C over the equator and –80°C over the poles
(3) 0°C everywhere
(4) +15°C over the equator and –15°C over the poles
= 1
~ In the tropopause the temperature stays nearly constant with height; it is about –80°C over the equator and about –45°C over the poles.

Q 13 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which layer of the atmosphere extends up to about 50 km and contains the ozone layer?
(1) Troposphere
(2) Mesosphere
(3) Stratosphere
(4) Exosphere
= 3
~ The stratosphere lies above the tropopause up to about 50 km. Its ozone layer absorbs ultraviolet radiation and shields life on earth.

Q 14 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Consider the following statements about the mesosphere:
1. It lies above the stratosphere and extends up to about 80 km.
2. Temperature in it increases with height, rising to about +100°C at 80 km.
3. Its upper limit is called the mesopause.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 3
~ In the mesosphere temperature decreases with height and falls to about –100°C at 80 km. Above the mesopause lies the ionosphere, part of the thermosphere.

Q 15 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The ionosphere is located between about 80 and 400 km above the mesopause. It is important because:
(1) It contains most of the water vapour
(2) Its electrically charged particles reflect radio waves transmitted from the earth back to the earth
(3) All weather phenomena take place in it
(4) It contains the ozone layer
= 2
~ The ionosphere contains electrically charged particles (ions). It reflects radio waves back to the earth, and in it temperature starts increasing with height.

Q 16 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
The uppermost layer of the atmosphere, about which very little is known and which gradually merges with outer space, is the:
(1) Thermosphere
(2) Mesosphere
(3) Exosphere
(4) Stratosphere
= 3
~ The exosphere lies above the thermosphere. Its contents are extremely rarefied.

Q 17 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Arrange the following layers of the atmosphere from the earth's surface upwards:
1. Mesosphere
2. Troposphere
3. Thermosphere
4. Stratosphere
Select the correct answer:
(1) 2 – 4 – 1 – 3
(2) 2 – 1 – 4 – 3
(3) 4 – 2 – 1 – 3
(4) 2 – 4 – 3 – 1
= 1
~ The order is troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere (with the ionosphere) and finally the exosphere.

Q 18 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Which one of the following is NOT listed in the chapter as an element of weather and climate?
(1) Temperature
(2) Humidity
(3) Precipitation
(4) Latitude
= 4
~ The elements are temperature, pressure, winds, humidity, clouds and precipitation. Latitude is a factor that influences climate, not an element.

Q 19 | geo | Fundamentals of Physical Geography
Almost all weather changes, such as cloud formation and rainfall, take place in the troposphere because:
(1) It is the coldest layer
(2) It has no air movement
(3) It contains the ozone layer
(4) It contains dust particles and water vapour
= 4
~ The troposphere holds almost all the dust and water vapour in the atmosphere, which are needed for clouds, rain and other weather.
