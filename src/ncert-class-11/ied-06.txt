# NCERT Class 11 Economics, Indian Economic Development, Chapter 6: Employment: Growth, Informalisation and Other Issues
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | eco | Indian Economic Development
According to the chapter, who is a 'worker'?
(1) Only a person who has a government job
(2) Only a person who works in a factory
(3) Anyone engaged in economic activities in whatever capacity, high or low, including those temporarily not working because of illness, bad weather or festivals
(4) Anyone above 18 years of age
= 3
~ All those engaged in economic activities are workers, whatever their capacity. People who are temporarily away from work because of illness, injury, bad weather, festivals or social and religious functions are also counted, as are those who help the main workers.

Q 2 | eco | Indian Economic Development
The worker-population ratio is:
(1) The percentage of the population that is actually working: (number of workers ÷ total population) × 100
(2) The number of workers per factory
(3) The ratio of men to women in the workforce
(4) The number of unemployed people per thousand population
= 1
~ The worker-population ratio shows what proportion of the population is actively contributing to the production of goods and services. A higher ratio means more people are engaged in work.

Q 3 | eco | Indian Economic Development
In the chapter, 'economic activities' are activities that:
(1) Are done only for the family
(2) Contribute to the gross national product
(3) Are done only by the government
(4) Do not earn any income
= 2
~ Activities that contribute to the gross national product are called economic activities, and those engaged in them are workers.

Q 4 | eco | Indian Economic Development
The chapter observes that women's participation in work is higher in rural areas than in urban areas. Which explanation fits the chapter?
(1) Urban areas have no jobs for women
(2) Rural women are better educated
(3) The law bars urban women from working
(4) Rural people with limited resources, women included, have to work on farms and elsewhere to earn, while many urban women stay out of paid work and do unpaid household work that is not counted
= 4
~ Poverty and limited resources push rural women into work, often on farms. In urban areas many women do not take up paid jobs, and the household work they do is not counted as economic activity.

Q 5 | eco | Indian Economic Development
By status of employment, the chapter divides workers into which three categories?
(1) Skilled, semi-skilled and unskilled
(2) Self-employed, regular salaried employees and casual wage labourers
(3) Rural, urban and migrant
(4) Public, private and joint sector
= 2
~ Workers are classified as self-employed (owning and running an enterprise), regular salaried employees (hired on a regular basis) and casual wage labourers (hired on a daily basis without regular wages).

Q 6 | eco | Indian Economic Development
A person who owns and runs a cement shop to earn a livelihood is classified as:
(1) A regular salaried employee
(2) A casual wage labourer
(3) A hired worker
(4) Self-employed
= 4
~ Workers who own and operate an enterprise to earn their livelihood are self-employed.

Q 7 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Construction workers hired on a daily basis, who do not get regular wages or social security benefits, are:
(1) Self-employed
(2) Regular salaried employees
(3) Casual wage labourers
(4) Formal sector workers
= 3
~ Casual wage labourers are hired by others and paid the wage for the day's work. They do not get regular wages or social security benefits.

Q 8 | eco | Indian Economic Development
According to the chapter, which category makes up the largest share of India's workforce?
(1) Self-employed
(2) Regular salaried employees
(3) Casual wage labourers
(4) Government employees
= 1
~ Self-employment is the major source of livelihood for both men and women in India; more than half the workforce is self-employed.

Q 9 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which statement is in line with the chapter?
(1) Regular salaried employment is more common in urban areas than in rural areas
(2) There are no casual wage labourers in rural areas
(3) Self-employment is found only in cities
(4) Rural and urban areas have exactly the same pattern of employment
= 1
~ In rural areas self-employment and casual wage labour dominate. In urban areas, where more people have education and specialised skills, regular salaried jobs make up a larger share.

Q 10 | eco | Indian Economic Development
'Jobless growth' means:
(1) Growth of employment without growth of GDP
(2) A fall in GDP along with a fall in employment
(3) The economy producing more goods and services without a proportionate increase in employment
(4) Growth only in the agricultural sector
= 3
~ In some periods GDP grew much faster than employment. The economy could produce more without creating jobs at the same pace; this is called jobless growth.

Q 11 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which establishments are counted as the formal sector in the chapter?
(1) Only factories with more than 100 workers
(2) Only government departments
(3) All establishments in cities
(4) All public sector establishments, and private sector establishments that employ 10 or more hired workers
= 4
~ All public sector establishments and private establishments employing 10 or more hired workers form the formal sector; those working in them are formal sector workers. All others form the informal sector.

Q 12 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which statement about informal sector workers is correct?
(1) They get pension, provident fund and gratuity like formal workers
(2) They do not get regular income or social security benefits and have little protection from the government
(3) They are all employed by the government
(4) They form a very small part of the workforce
= 2
~ Informal sector workers include farmers, agricultural labourers, owners of small enterprises and the self-employed. They lack regular income, social security and legal protection, and they form the large majority of India's workforce.

Q 13 | eco | Indian Economic Development
'Casualisation of the workforce' refers to:
(1) A fall in the number of casual workers
(2) Workers moving from agriculture to industry
(3) Workers getting more holidays
(4) A rising share of casual wage labourers in the workforce
= 4
~ When the proportion of workers hired on a casual, daily basis rises relative to self-employment and regular salaried jobs, the workforce is said to be getting casualised.

Q 14 | eco | Indian Economic Development
'Disguised unemployment' is a situation in which:
(1) A person refuses to work
(2) Workers are unemployed for some months of the year
(3) More people are engaged in a work than are actually needed, so withdrawing some of them would not reduce output
(4) People cannot find work after graduation
= 3
~ A typical example is a family farm on which more family members work than the farm needs. Their marginal contribution to output is zero, so they are disguisedly unemployed.

Q 15 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Seasonal unemployment is found mostly in:
(1) Agriculture, where work is available only in certain months of the year
(2) Software companies
(3) Banks
(4) Government offices
= 1
~ Agricultural work is concentrated in sowing, weeding and harvesting seasons. For the rest of the year many rural workers have no work, and some migrate to cities in search of jobs.

Q 17 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which of the following best describes an unemployed person according to the chapter?
(1) Anyone who is not a student
(2) Anyone who works less than eight hours a day
(3) A person who is not working owing to lack of work, but is seeking work or is available for work under the prevailing conditions of work and pay
(4) A person who has retired from a job
= 3
~ Unemployment is a situation in which people who are not working because there is no work look for it through employment exchanges, friends, relatives or applications, or say they are available for work at prevailing wages.

Q 18 | eco | Indian Economic Development
The Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act, 2005 promises:
(1) A government job to every graduate
(2) Free land to every rural family
(3) Unemployment allowance to every urban youth
(4) 100 days of guaranteed wage employment in a year to every rural household whose adult members volunteer to do unskilled manual work
= 4
~ The Act guarantees 100 days of wage employment a year to rural households willing to do unskilled manual work. It is one of the government's main employment generation programmes.

Q 19 | eco | Indian Economic Development
How does the government generate employment 'indirectly', according to the chapter?
(1) By recruiting people into government departments
(2) When the output of government enterprises rises, private enterprises that get raw materials from them also raise output and employment
(3) By banning machines in factories
(4) By closing public sector units
= 2
~ The government creates jobs directly by employing people in its departments and enterprises. Indirectly, higher output in government enterprises raises output and employment in private enterprises linked to them.

Q 20 | eco | Indian Economic Development
Which statement about the sectoral distribution of employment is in line with the chapter?
(1) The primary sector is the main source of employment for the majority of workers, especially in rural areas, while the tertiary sector employs a larger share in urban areas
(2) The secondary sector employs most workers in rural areas
(3) The tertiary sector employs most rural workers
(4) All three sectors employ equal shares of workers
= 1
~ Agriculture and other primary activities employ most rural workers. In urban areas, services (the tertiary sector) employ the largest share, followed by the secondary sector.
