# NCERT Class 11 Economics, Statistics for Economics, Chapter 7: Index Numbers
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | eco | Statistics for Economics
An index number is:
(1) A statistical device for measuring changes in the magnitude of a group of related variables
(2) The serial number of a page in a book
(3) The average of all prices in one year
(4) A measure of correlation between two variables
= 1
~ An index number measures relative change, for example in prices or quantities, between a base period and a current period, for a group of related items.

Q 2 | eco | Statistics for Economics
Which period should be chosen as the base period of an index number?
(1) The latest year available
(2) A year with very high prices
(3) A normal period, free from extreme fluctuations
(4) A year of war or drought
= 3
~ The base period is the reference with which other periods are compared. It should be a normal year, so that comparisons are not distorted.

Q 3 | eco | Statistics for Economics
The value of an index number for the base period is always taken as:
(1) 0
(2) 100
(3) 1,000
(4) 1
= 2
~ The base period index is 100. An index of 120 for the current period means a 20 per cent rise over the base.

Q 4 | eco | Statistics for Economics
The prices of three goods in the base year are ₹10, ₹20 and ₹30, and in the current year ₹12, ₹25 and ₹35. What is the simple aggregative price index?
(1) 112
(2) 72
(3) 83.3
(4) 120
= 4
~ Simple aggregative index = (Σp1 ÷ Σp0) × 100 = (72 ÷ 60) × 100 = 120.

Q 5 | eco | Statistics for Economics
What is a weakness of the simple aggregative price index?
(1) It cannot be calculated
(2) It always equals 100
(3) It uses current year quantities as weights
(4) It is affected by the units in which prices are quoted and gives no weight to the relative importance of items
= 4
~ In a simple aggregative index, an item with a high price per unit dominates the result, and all items are treated as equally important. Weighted indices correct this.

Q 6 | eco | Statistics for Economics
Laspeyres' price index uses as weights:
(1) Current period quantities
(2) Base period quantities
(3) Equal weights for all goods
(4) Current period prices
= 2
~ Laspeyres' index = (Σp1q0 ÷ Σp0q0) × 100. It measures how much the base period basket would cost at current prices.

Q 7 | eco | Statistics for Economics
Paasche's price index uses as weights:
(1) Current period quantities
(2) Base period quantities
(3) Base period prices only
(4) No weights
= 1
~ Paasche's index = (Σp1q1 ÷ Σp0q1) × 100. It uses the quantities of the current period as weights.

Q 8 | eco | Statistics for Economics
Good A: base price ₹10, current price ₹12, base quantity 5. Good B: base price ₹20, current price ₹25, base quantity 2. What is Laspeyres' price index?
(1) 120
(2) 122
(3) 122.22
(4) 110
= 3
~ Σp1q0 = 12 × 5 + 25 × 2 = 60 + 50 = 110. Σp0q0 = 10 × 5 + 20 × 2 = 50 + 40 = 90. Index = (110 ÷ 90) × 100 = 122.22.

Q 9 | eco | Statistics for Economics
Good A: base price ₹10, current price ₹12, current quantity 6. Good B: base price ₹20, current price ₹25, current quantity 2. What is Paasche's price index?
(1) 122.22
(2) 120
(3) 122
(4) 100
= 3
~ Σp1q1 = 12 × 6 + 25 × 2 = 72 + 50 = 122. Σp0q1 = 10 × 6 + 20 × 2 = 60 + 40 = 100. Index = (122 ÷ 100) × 100 = 122.

Q 10 | eco | Statistics for Economics
The Consumer Price Index (CPI) measures:
(1) The average change in the retail prices of a fixed basket of goods and services consumed by a particular group of people
(2) The change in industrial production
(3) The change in wholesale prices only
(4) The change in share prices
= 1
~ The CPI, also called the cost of living index, shows how the retail prices paid by a group of consumers, such as industrial workers or agricultural labourers, have changed.

Q 11 | eco | Statistics for Economics
How does the Wholesale Price Index (WPI) differ from the CPI, according to the chapter?
(1) The WPI covers only services
(2) The WPI has no reference consumer category and does not include services such as barber charges and repairs
(3) The WPI is calculated only for agricultural labourers
(4) The WPI measures share prices
= 2
~ The WPI tracks prices of goods at the wholesale level for the economy as a whole, so it has no particular consumer group and leaves out services. The CPI is for a specific group and includes services.

Q 12 | eco | Statistics for Economics
The Index of Industrial Production (IIP) measures:
(1) Changes in wholesale prices
(2) Changes in the cost of living
(3) Changes in share prices
(4) Changes in the level of industrial production, such as mining, manufacturing and electricity
= 4
~ The IIP is a quantity index. It shows how the volume of industrial output in a period compares with the base period.

Q 13 | eco | Statistics for Economics
The Sensex is:
(1) An index of wholesale prices
(2) The index of 30 stocks of the Bombay Stock Exchange, with 1978–79 as the base
(3) An index of industrial production
(4) A consumer price index for urban workers
= 2
~ The Sensex is the benchmark index of the Bombay Stock Exchange, based on 30 stocks, with 1978–79 as the base. A rising Sensex shows investors' optimism about the economy.

Q 14 | eco | Statistics for Economics
A worker's money wage is ₹15,000 and the relevant consumer price index is 150. What is the real wage?
(1) ₹22,500
(2) ₹15,000
(3) ₹10,000
(4) ₹150
= 3
~ Real wage = (money wage ÷ CPI) × 100 = (15,000 ÷ 150) × 100 = ₹10,000. This is the wage in terms of base year purchasing power.

Q 15 | eco | Statistics for Economics
If the consumer price index is 125, the purchasing power of a rupee compared with the base year is:
(1) 80 paise
(2) 125 paise
(3) 25 paise
(4) ₹1.25
= 1
~ Purchasing power of money is the reciprocal of the price index: 100 ÷ 125 = 0.80, so a rupee buys what 80 paise bought in the base year.

Q 16 | eco | Statistics for Economics
If the WPI rises from 200 to 210 over a year, the rate of inflation is:
(1) 10 per cent
(2) 2 per cent
(3) 4.76 per cent
(4) 5 per cent
= 4
~ Inflation rate = [(210 − 200) ÷ 200] × 100 = (10 ÷ 200) × 100 = 5 per cent.

Q 17 | eco | Statistics for Economics
For which purpose is the consumer price index widely used?
(1) Measuring industrial output
(2) Measuring share prices
(3) Wage negotiations and fixing dearness allowance
(4) Measuring correlation
= 3
~ Because the CPI shows the change in the cost of living of a group of consumers, it is used in wage contracts and to fix dearness allowance.

Q 18 | eco | Statistics for Economics
Which of the following should be avoided while constructing an index number?
(1) Choosing items that represent the group well
(2) Choosing an abnormal year, such as a year of drought or war, as the base
(3) Using appropriate weights
(4) Using reliable price data
= 2
~ An abnormal base year makes every later comparison misleading. Careful choice of items, weights, formula and data is needed for a reliable index.

Q 19 | eco | Statistics for Economics
The price relatives of three goods are 120, 150 and 90. Using the method of simple average of price relatives, the index is:
(1) 110
(2) 150
(3) 90
(4) 120
= 4
~ Index = average of price relatives = (120 + 150 + 90) ÷ 3 = 360 ÷ 3 = 120.

Q 20 | eco | Statistics for Economics
An index number that measures change in the physical volume of production, rather than prices, is called a:
(1) Quantity index
(2) Price index
(3) Cost of living index
(4) Wholesale price index
= 1
~ A quantity index, such as the Index of Industrial Production, compares physical quantities produced over time. Price indices compare prices.
