Q 1 | qa | Percentage & Profit-Loss
Seema bought a mobile and laptop at a certain price. She sold the mobile at 10% gain and laptop at 25% gain. She found that the cost price of the mobile is equal to the selling price of the laptop. Find her profit percentage.
(1) 13 2/3%
(2) 16 2/3%
(3) 14 2/3%
(4) 15 2/3%
= 2
~ Let the CP of the laptop be ₹100; its SP = ₹125, which equals the CP of the mobile. SP of the mobile = 125 × 1.10 = ₹137.5. Total CP = 100 + 125 = ₹225 and total SP = 125 + 137.5 = ₹262.5, so profit = ₹37.5. Profit % = 37.5/225 × 100 = 16 2/3%.

A number when increased by 50 % gives 2550. The number is:

(2) 5100
(3) 3400
(4) 850

Q 4 | qa | Number System & Simplification
What is the simplest fractional form of 0.625?
(1) 10/20
(2) 25/50
(3) 5/8
(4) 625/1000
= 3
~ 0.625 = 625/1000. Dividing numerator and denominator by their HCF 125 gives 5/8, which cannot be reduced further. 625/1000 has the same value but is not in simplest form, while 10/20 and 25/50 both equal 1/2.

Q 5 | qa | Number System & Simplification
Evaluate: 41 − [36 − {48 ÷ 3 − (4 − 8 ÷ 2) ÷ 2}]
(1) 22
(2) 20
(3) 21
(4) 23
= 3
~ Innermost bracket first: 4 − 8 ÷ 2 = 4 − 4 = 0, and 0 ÷ 2 = 0. Curly bracket: 48 ÷ 3 − 0 = 16. Square bracket: 36 − 16 = 20. Finally 41 − 20 = 21.

Q 6 | qa | Number System & Simplification
What is the greatest number that will divide 446 and 487, leaving remainders 9 and 12, respectively?
(1) 25
(2) 22
(3) 23
(4) 19
= 4
~ The required number divides 446 − 9 = 437 and 487 − 12 = 475 exactly. 437 = 19 × 23 and 475 = 19 × 25, so HCF(437, 475) = 19. Check: 446 = 19 × 23 + 9 and 487 = 19 × 25 + 12.

Q 7 | qa | Simple & Compound Interest
The difference between compound interest and simple interest for 3 years at the rate of 20% per annum is ₹240. What is the principal lent?
(1) ₹1,675
(2) ₹1,875
(3) ₹1,975
(4) ₹1,775
= 2
~ For 3 years, CI − SI = P × (r/100)² × (3 + r/100). With r = 20%: P × 0.04 × 3.2 = 0.128P. So 0.128P = 240, giving P = ₹1,875.

Q 8 | qa | Time, Work, Speed & Distance
Isha can do a certain piece of work in 20 days. Isha and Smriti can together do the same work in 16 days, and Isha, Smriti and Ashlesha can do the same work together in 8 days. In how many days can Isha and Ashlesha do the same work?
(1) 76/11
(2) 89/9
(3) 80/9
(4) 79/10
= 3
~ Ashlesha's one-day work = (Isha + Smriti + Ashlesha) − (Isha + Smriti) = 1/8 − 1/16 = 1/16. Isha + Ashlesha = 1/20 + 1/16 = (4 + 5)/80 = 9/80 of the work per day. So they need 80/9 days (8 8/9 days).

Q 9 | qa | Ratio, Average & Mixtures
The monthly incomes of two friends Chetan and Vipul, are in the ratio 5 : 7 respectively and each of them saves ₹96000 every month. If the ratio of their monthly expenditure is 1 : 3, find the monthly income of Chetan(in ₹).
(1) 120000
(2) 168000
(3) 121000
(4) 119000
= 1
~ Let the incomes be 5x and 7x. Expenditures are 5x − 96000 and 7x − 96000, in the ratio 1 : 3, so 3(5x − 96000) = 7x − 96000, i.e. 15x − 288000 = 7x − 96000, giving 8x = 192000 and x = 24000. Chetan's income = 5 × 24000 = ₹120000 (check: expenditures 24000 and 72000, ratio 1 : 3).

Q 10 | qa | Ratio, Average & Mixtures
In what ratio must a shopkeeper mix two varieties of rice costing ₹75 and ₹80 per kg, respectively, so as to get a mixture worth ₹76.5 per kg?
(1) 7 : 3
(2) 3 : 5
(3) 5 : 3
(4) 3 : 7
= 1
~ By alligation, cheaper : dearer = (80 − 76.5) : (76.5 − 75) = 3.5 : 1.5 = 7 : 3. Check: (7 × 75 + 3 × 80)/10 = (525 + 240)/10 = ₹76.5.

Q 11 | qa | Number System & Simplification
Which of the following numbers is divisible by both 4 and 9?
(1) 2345
(2) 4512
(3) 1728
(4) 3241
= 3
~ A number divisible by 4 must have its last two digits divisible by 4, and one divisible by 9 must have a digit sum divisible by 9. For 1728, 28 is divisible by 4 and 1 + 7 + 2 + 8 = 18 is divisible by 9, so 1728 = 36 × 48. 4512 is divisible by 4 but its digit sum is 12, while 2345 and 3241 are odd.

Q 12 | qa | Time, Work, Speed & Distance
One pipe fills a water tank 3 times faster than the other. If the two pipes together can fill the empty tank in 37 minutes, then how much time will the slower pipe alone take to fill the tank?
(1) 148 minutes
(2) 145 minutes
(3) 150 minutes
(4) 154 minutes
= 1
~ If the slower pipe fills r of the tank per minute, the faster fills 3r, so together 4r = 1/37. Hence r = 1/148, and the slower pipe alone takes 148 minutes (the faster takes 148/3 minutes).

Q 13 | qa | Ratio, Average & Mixtures
How much wheat (in kg, rounded off to the nearest integer) costing ₹36 per kg must be mixed with 55 kg of wheat costing ₹45 per kg so that there may be a gain of 20% by selling the mixture at ₹50 per kg?
(1) 36
(2) 28
(3) 30
(4) 32
= 4
~ For a 20% gain at ₹50 per kg, the cost price of the mixture = 50 ÷ 1.2 = ₹125/3 per kg. By alligation, ₹36 wheat : ₹45 wheat = (45 − 125/3) : (125/3 − 36) = 10/3 : 17/3 = 10 : 17. So the required quantity = 55 × 10/17 ≈ 32.35 kg, i.e. 32 kg.

Q 14 | qa | Simple & Compound Interest
Two banks, A and B, offered loans at 3.5% and 6% per annum, respectively. Chetan borrowed an amount of ₹440000 from each bank. Find the positive difference between the amounts of simple interest paid to the two banks by Chetan after 3 years.
(1) 34000
(2) 34500
(3) 32500
(4) 33000
= 4
~ The principal and time are the same, so the difference in SI = 440000 × (6 − 3.5)% × 3 = 440000 × 2.5 × 3/100 = ₹33000. (SI from A = ₹46200 and from B = ₹79200.)

Q 15 | qa | Time, Work, Speed & Distance
Train A leaves station M at 7:20 AM and reaches station N at 2:20 PM on the same day.
Train B leaves station N at 9:20 AM and reaches station M at 2:20 PM on the same day.
Find the time when Trains A and B meet.
(1) 11:25 AM
(2) 9:38 PM
(3) 10:34 AM
(4) 1:11 PM
= 1
~ Let MN = 35 units. Train A takes 7 hours (speed 5 units/h) and Train B takes 5 hours (speed 7 units/h). By 9:20 AM, A has covered 2 × 5 = 10 units, leaving 25 units. They close this gap at 5 + 7 = 12 units/h, taking 25/12 h = 2 h 5 min, so they meet at 9:20 AM + 2 h 5 min = 11:25 AM.

Q 16 | qa | Mensuration & Geometry
If the radius of the base of a right circular cylinder is decreased by 30% and its height is increased by 224%, then what is the percentage increase (closest integer) in its volume?
(1) 83%
(2) 59%
(3) 58%
(4) 70%
= 2
~ Volume = πr²h. The new radius is 0.7r and the new height is 3.24h, so the new volume = 0.49 × 3.24 = 1.5876 times the original. The increase is 58.76%, which is 59% to the nearest integer.

Q 18 | qa | Mensuration & Geometry
A pencil sharpened at both edges is the combination of _________ and __________.
(1) cylinders and cones
(2) rectangle and cylinder
(3) rectangle and a cone
(4) squares and cones
= 1
~ The unsharpened middle part of a pencil is a cylinder, and each sharpened end is a cone. So a pencil sharpened at both ends is a combination of a cylinder and two cones. Rectangles and squares are plane figures, not solids.

Q 20 | qa | Percentage & Profit-Loss

Q 22 | qa | Time, Work, Speed & Distance

Q 23 | qa | Time, Work, Speed & Distance

Q 24 | qa | Number System & Simplification
Three friends are exercising together. The first friend runs a lap in 12 minutes, the second in 18 minutes, and the third in 24 minutes. If they all start running together, after how many minutes will they all finish a lap together again?
(1) 82 mins
(2) 92 mins
(3) 72 mins
(4) 32 mins
= 3
~ They finish a lap together again after the LCM of 12, 18 and 24 minutes. 12 = 2² × 3, 18 = 2 × 3², 24 = 2³ × 3, so LCM = 2³ × 3² = 72 minutes.

Q 25 | qa | Time, Work, Speed & Distance

(1) 11 km/h
(2) 9 km/h

(4) 4 km/h
= 2

Q 36 | rea | Coding-Decoding
In a certain code language, 'NXCH' is coded as 'QZFJ' and 'EULA' is coded as 'HWOC'. What is the code for 'ZIKR' in the given code language?
(1) DJNS
(2) BINS
(3) BGMR
(4) CKNT
= 4
~ The letters are moved forward alternately by 3 and 2 places: N+3=Q, X+2=Z, C+3=F, H+2=J, and E+3=H, U+2=W, L+3=O, A+2=C. Applying the same to ZIKR: Z+3=C (counting on from A after Z), I+2=K, K+3=N, R+2=T. So ZIKR is coded as CKNT.

Q 37 | rea | Analogy & Classification

Q 42 | rea | Miscellaneous Reasoning

Q 48 | rea | Blood Relations & Directions
Naren starts from Point A and drives 7 km towards the west. He then takes a left turn, drives 5 km, turns left and drives 10 km. He then takes a left turn and drives 9 km. He takes a final left turn, drives 3 km and stops at Point P. How far (shortest distance) and towards which direction should he drive in order to reach Point A again? (All turns are 90° turns only unless specified.)
(1) 4 km to the south
(2) 3 km to the north
(3) 1 km to the south
(4) 2 km to the north
= 1
~ Moving west and then turning left each time, his legs are: 7 km west, 5 km south, 10 km east, 9 km north and 3 km west. East-west: −7 + 10 − 3 = 0; north-south: −5 + 9 = 4 km north. So P is 4 km due north of A, and he must drive 4 km to the south.

Q 71 | sci | Chemistry
Which of the following options best describes the Ideal Gas Law?
(1) The pressure (P), volume (V) and temperature (T) of a fixed quantity of gas are related by the equation PV/T = Constant.
(2) The pressure of a gas is directly proportional to its volume at constant volume.
(3) The volume of a gas is inversely proportional to its temperature at constant pressure.
(4) The total pressure of a mixture of gases is equal to the sum of the partial pressures of the individual gases.
= 1
~ The ideal gas law combines Boyle's, Charles's and Avogadro's laws into PV = nRT; for a fixed quantity of gas (n constant) this gives PV/T = constant. Q 100 | sci | Chemistry
Which principle states that the partial vapour pressure of each volatile component in a solution is directly proportional to its mole fraction?
(1) Raoult's Law
(2) Henry's Law
(3) Dalton's Law
(4) Boyle's Law
= 1
~ Raoult’s law states that for a solution of volatile liquids, the partial vapour pressure of each component equals its pure-component vapour pressure multiplied by its mole fraction in the solution: p₁ = p₁° x₁, so p₁ is directly proportional to x₁. Solutions that obey it over the whole range are called ideal solutions. Henry’s law deals with the solubility of a gas in a liquid, Dalton’s law with the total pressure of a gas mixture, and Boyle’s law with pressure and volume of a gas at constant temperature.

Q 117 | sci | Physics
A negatively charged glass rod attracts a hanging object. The nature of the hanging object is:
(1) may be positively charged or may be neutral
(2) certainly negatively charged
(3) may be negatively charged or may be neutral
(4) certainly positively charged
= 1
~ Unlike charges attract, so a positively charged object will be pulled towards the negatively charged rod. A neutral object is also attracted, because the rod induces a positive charge on the near side of the object and a negative charge on the far side, and the nearer attraction wins. The object cannot be negatively charged, since like charges repel; so attraction alone cannot tell us whether it is positive or neutral, and repulsion is the only sure test of charge.

Q 118 | sci | Chemistry
According to Le Chatelier's Principle, what happens to a system at equilibrium when it is subjected to a change in concentration, pressure or temperature?
(1) The system remains at equilibrium without any changes.
(2) The system will always favour the formation of reactants.
(3) The system will undergo a permanent change in state.
(4) The system shifts in the direction that opposes the change to re-establish equilibrium.
= 4
~ Le Chatelier’s principle states that if a system at equilibrium is disturbed by a change in concentration, pressure or temperature, the equilibrium shifts in the direction that counteracts the change, until a new equilibrium is set up. For example, in N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃, raising the pressure shifts the equilibrium towards ammonia, the side with fewer gas molecules. The shift may favour either products or reactants depending on the change, so it does not always favour reactants.
