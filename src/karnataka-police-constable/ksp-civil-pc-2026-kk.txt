Q 2 | geo | Environment & Ecology
With respect to environmental issues, expand the acronym CPCB.
(1) Central Pollution Controlling Body
(2) Corporate Prevention & Certifying Board
(3) Central Pollution Certifying Body
(4) Central Pollution Control Board
= 4
~ CPCB stands for Central Pollution Control Board, so (4) is correct. Q 3 | sci | Biology
Elephantiasis is caused by the swelling of lymphatic vessels of lower limbs. Name the causative organism of this disease.
(1) Wuchereria
(2) Ascaris
(3) Ancylostoma
(4) Taenia
= 1
~ Elephantiasis (lymphatic filariasis) is caused by the filarial worm Wuchereria, mainly Wuchereria bancrofti, with W. malayi (now called Brugia malayi) causing some cases, so (1) is correct. The adult worms live in the lymphatic vessels and block the flow of lymph, which leads to chronic swelling of the legs, scrotum or arms. The infection is spread by the bite of the female Culex mosquito, which carries the larvae (microfilariae) from person to person. Ascaris is the common intestinal roundworm that causes ascariasis; Ancylostoma is the hookworm, which sucks blood in the intestine and causes anaemia; Taenia is the tapeworm picked up from undercooked pork or beef. None of these three lives in lymph vessels. Exam fact: NCERT Class 12 Biology links filariasis to Wuchereria bancrofti and W. malayi, and India runs Mass Drug Administration (DEC plus albendazole) to eliminate lymphatic filariasis.

Q 4 | sci | Biology
Filtering of blood in kidney occurs in
(1) Henle's loop
(2) Ureter
(3) Collecting duct
(4) Glomerulus
= 4
~ Filtration of blood in the kidney takes place in the glomerulus, so (4) is correct. Each nephron begins with a glomerulus, a tuft of capillaries enclosed by Bowman's capsule; together they form the Malpighian body (renal corpuscle). Blood enters through a wider afferent arteriole and leaves through a narrower efferent arteriole, and the resulting high pressure pushes water, glucose, salts and urea into the capsule. This is called ultrafiltration. An adult forms about 180 litres of filtrate every day, yet only about 1.5 litres leave the body as urine because most of it is reabsorbed. Henle's loop helps concentrate urine by reabsorbing water and salts; the collecting duct reabsorbs water under the control of ADH; the ureter merely carries urine from the kidney to the urinary bladder. None of these filters blood. Memory tip: G for Glomerulus, G for the 'Gate' where blood is first filtered.

Q 5 | sci | Biology
Factors that affect enzyme activity are
(a) Temperature
(b) pH
(c) Light
(d) ATP
(1) (a) and (c) only
(2) (b) and (d) only
(3) (a) and (b) only
(4) (c) and (d) only
= 3
~ Enzymes are protein catalysts, and their activity depends mainly on temperature, pH, substrate concentration, enzyme concentration and the presence of inhibitors or activators. Hence (a) temperature and (b) pH are the correct factors and the answer is (3). Each enzyme works best at an optimum temperature (about 37 °C for most human enzymes); high heat denatures the protein, while low temperature only slows the reaction down. Similarly each enzyme has an optimum pH: pepsin in the stomach works best at about pH 2, while trypsin in the small intestine prefers about pH 8. Light (c) is not a general factor controlling enzyme activity; it is needed for the light reaction of photosynthesis, not for enzymes as such. ATP (d) is the energy currency used in some reactions, not a general regulator of enzyme activity. So options (1), (2) and (4), which include light or ATP, are wrong.

Q 6 | sci | Biology
By counting annual rings of a stem, the age of a tree can be estimated. This study is known as
(1) Dendrology
(2) Pomology
(3) Dendrochronology
(4) Anthology
= 3
~ Estimating the age of a tree by counting its annual (growth) rings is called dendrochronology, so (3) is correct. The word comes from Greek dendron (tree), chronos (time) and logos (study). Every year the vascular cambium produces light-coloured, wide spring wood (early wood) and dark, dense autumn wood (late wood); one light band plus one dark band makes one annual ring, so the number of rings equals the age of the tree in years. The rings are very distinct in temperate trees, where seasons are sharp, and less clear in tropical trees. The width of each ring also records past rainfall and climate. Dendrology (1) is the general study of trees and woody plants (their identification and classification); pomology (2) is the science of fruits and fruit growing; anthology (4) is a collection of poems or writings, not a botanical science at all. Memory tip: chrono means time, so dendro-chrono-logy is telling time from trees.

Q 10 | geo | World Geography
Consider the following statements:
Statement I: The Sun appears to move from east to west.
Statement II: Earth is rotating from west to east in its own axis.
(1) Statement I is correct, but Statement II is incorrect
(2) Statement I is incorrect, but Statement II is correct
(3) Both Statement I and Statement II are correct and Statement II is the correct explanation of Statement I
(4) Both Statement I and Statement II are correct, but Statement II is not the correct explanation of Statement I
= 3
~ Both statements are correct and Statement II explains Statement I, so (3) is the answer. The Earth rotates on its axis from west to east (anticlockwise when seen from above the North Pole) and completes one rotation in about 24 hours. Because we are carried eastward along with the Earth, the Sun, Moon and stars appear to move the opposite way: they rise in the east and set in the west. It is just like trees that seem to run backwards when we look out of a moving train. Options (1) and (2) are wrong because both statements are true, and option (4) is wrong because the Earth's rotation is exactly what causes the Sun's apparent east-to-west motion. Useful facts: rotation causes day and night, while revolution around the Sun, together with the tilt of the axis, causes the seasons. Venus and Uranus rotate in the opposite direction (east to west), so on Venus the Sun would rise in the west.

Q 12 | sci | Chemistry
To detect the leaks, the following odorant is added in LPG cylinders.
(1) Propane
(2) Butane
(3) Ethanethiol
(4) Pentane
= 3
~ LPG is a mixture mainly of propane and butane, which have no smell of their own. To make a leak noticeable, a strong-smelling sulphur compound, ethanethiol (ethyl mercaptan, C₂H₅SH), is added in very small amounts, so (3) is correct. Its rotten-cabbage or garlic-like smell can be detected by the human nose even at extremely low concentrations, so people are warned long before the gas reaches a dangerous level. Propane (1) and butane (2) are the fuel components of LPG itself and are odourless, so they cannot act as warning odorants. Pentane (4) is a higher alkane found in petrol fractions and is not used as an LPG odorant. Safety tip useful on police duty: if you smell LPG, do not operate electrical switches or light a flame, close the regulator, open doors and windows, and call the LPG emergency helpline 1906.

Q 13 | sci | Chemistry
In a soft drink industry, workers fill a gas to the soft drink bottles. Which among the following gases is/are most suitable?
(a) SO₂
(b) NO₂
(c) O₂
(d) CO₂
(1) (a) and (b)
(2) (c) only
(3) (b) and (c)
(4) (d) only
= 4
~ Carbon dioxide (CO₂) is the gas filled in soft drink bottles, so (4) '(d) only' is correct. Under high pressure CO₂ dissolves in the drink and forms weak carbonic acid (H₂CO₃), which gives the tangy taste; when the bottle is opened the pressure falls and the gas escapes as bubbles, producing the fizz. CO₂ is non-toxic, cheap, does not support the growth of most microbes and acts as a mild preservative. Sulphur dioxide (SO₂) is a pungent, poisonous gas responsible for acid rain, and nitrogen dioxide (NO₂) is a toxic reddish-brown gas found in vehicle exhaust, so options (1) and (3) are wrong. Oxygen (O₂) does not dissolve enough to give fizz and would spoil the drink through oxidation and microbial growth, so (2) is also wrong. Memory tip: carbonated (aerated) drinks contain carbon dioxide; solid CO₂ is called 'dry ice'.

Q 14 | sci | Chemistry
Consider the following statements and choose the correct answer from the options given below:
Statement I: Gastric juice contains H₂SO₄
Statement II: Baking soda is used to reduce acidity
(1) Both Statement I and Statement II are correct
(2) Both Statement I and Statement II are incorrect
(3) Statement I is correct but Statement II is incorrect
(4) Statement I is incorrect but Statement II is correct
= 4
~ The answer is (4): Statement I is incorrect and Statement II is correct. Gastric juice secreted by the stomach contains hydrochloric acid (HCl), not sulphuric acid (H₂SO₄). HCl is produced by the parietal (oxyntic) cells of the gastric glands; it makes the stomach strongly acidic (pH about 1.5 to 2), kills germs that come in with food and converts inactive pepsinogen into the active enzyme pepsin. Statement II is correct because baking soda, sodium hydrogen carbonate (NaHCO₃), is a mild base that neutralises excess stomach acid: NaHCO₃ + HCl → NaCl + H₂O + CO₂. That is why it is used as an antacid, along with milk of magnesia (magnesium hydroxide). Therefore options (1), (2) and (3) are wrong. Exam tip: remember stomach – hydrochloric acid, ant sting – formic acid, curd – lactic acid, vinegar – acetic acid.

Q 16 | sci | Chemistry
Corrosion of aluminium can be prevented by
(1) Galvanising
(2) Anodising
(3) Painting
(4) Greasing
= 2
~ Aluminium is protected from corrosion by anodising, so (2) is correct. In anodising, the aluminium article is made the anode in an electrolytic cell containing dilute sulphuric acid. The oxygen released at the anode reacts with the metal and builds up a thicker, uniform layer of aluminium oxide on its surface. This oxide layer is hard, does not flake off and can even be dyed in attractive colours, as seen in pressure cookers, utensils, window frames and mobile phone bodies. Galvanising (1) means coating iron or steel with zinc; it is used to protect iron, not aluminium. Painting (3) and greasing or oiling (4) are general barrier methods used mainly for iron objects such as gates, grills and machine parts. Aluminium naturally forms a thin protective oxide layer in air, and anodising simply makes this layer thicker and stronger. This topic is part of NCERT Class 10 Science, 'Metals and Non-metals'.

Q 17 | sci | Chemistry
These days many countries use desalination plants to meet their potable water requirements. The phenomenon used in this process is
(1) Osmosis
(2) Filtration
(3) Reverse osmosis
(4) Distillation
= 3
~ Most desalination plants work on reverse osmosis, so (3) is correct. In ordinary osmosis (1), water moves through a semi-permeable membrane from a dilute solution to a concentrated one. In reverse osmosis (RO), a pressure greater than the osmotic pressure is applied on the salty (sea water) side, forcing pure water through the membrane in the opposite direction while salts and many impurities are left behind. Countries such as Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Israel, and Indian cities like Chennai (Minjur and Nemmeli plants), obtain drinking water from the sea in this way. Filtration (2) removes only suspended particles, not dissolved salts. Distillation (4) can also remove salt, but it needs a great deal of heat energy, so most modern plants prefer RO. Household 'RO water purifiers' work on the same principle. Memory tip: osmosis is natural flow; reverse osmosis is flow forced backwards by pressure.

Q 18 | sci | Chemistry
An antifreeze used in cars for cooling the engine is
(1) Ethanol
(2) Methanol
(3) Ethanoic acid
(4) Ethylene glycol
= 4
~ Ethylene glycol (ethane-1,2-diol) is the standard antifreeze used in car radiators, so (4) is correct. When mixed with water it lowers the freezing point of the coolant, so the water does not freeze and crack the engine block or radiator in cold weather; it also raises the boiling point, so the coolant does not boil over in summer. It evaporates very little and, with added inhibitors, does not corrode engine parts. Ethanol (1) and methanol (2) were used as antifreeze in the past, but they evaporate easily and catch fire; methanol is also highly poisonous. Ethanoic acid (3), or acetic acid, is the acid in vinegar and would corrode metal parts, so it can never be used as a coolant. Exam tip: ethylene glycol is also a raw material for polyester (PET) fibres and bottles, and the lowering of the freezing point it causes is called depression of freezing point, a colligative property.

Q 22 | geo | Indian Geography
Identify the distribution of an Indian major tribe in the given map:
(1) The Gonds
(2) The Santhals
(3) The Bhils
(4) The Thodas
= 3
~ The shaded area on the map covers western India – southern Rajasthan, eastern Gujarat, western Madhya Pradesh and northern Maharashtra – which is the homeland of the Bhils, so (3) is correct. Q 25 | geo | World Geography
In the oceans, if the ocean currents complete a circle, it is called as
(1) Drift
(2) Gyre
(3) Tides
(4) Waves
= 2
~ When ocean currents join together and move in a large, closed circular loop, the system is called a gyre, so (2) is correct. Gyres are produced by the combined action of the planetary winds (trade winds and westerlies), the Coriolis force and the shape of the continents. Because of the Coriolis effect, gyres rotate clockwise in the Northern Hemisphere and anticlockwise in the Southern Hemisphere. The five major gyres are the North Pacific, South Pacific, North Atlantic, South Atlantic and Indian Ocean gyres. Floating plastic collects in their calm centres, as in the Great Pacific Garbage Patch, and the Sargasso Sea lies inside the North Atlantic gyre. A drift (1) is a slow, broad, wind-driven current such as the North Atlantic Drift; it is only one part of a gyre. Tides (3) are the periodic rise and fall of sea level caused by the pull of the Moon and the Sun. Waves (4) are oscillations of the water surface caused mainly by wind.

= 1

Q 64 | apt | Series & Puzzles
Let the sequence be 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, .... then the sequence is ______.

(2) A geometric sequence
(3) A harmonic sequence

= 1
~ In 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, ... the difference between any two consecutive terms is the same: 3 − 1 = 5 − 3 = 7 − 5 = 9 − 7 = 2. A sequence with a constant common difference is an arithmetic sequence (arithmetic progression, AP), so option (1) is correct. Here the first term a = 1 and the common difference d = 2, so the nth term is aₙ = a + (n − 1)d = 2n − 1. Q 83 | pol | Constitution & Rights

(1) America
(2) Canada
(3) Australia
(4) Russia
= 2
~ The keyed answer is (2) Canada. The Indian federal system follows the Canadian pattern of a 'federation with a strong Centre'. Q 84 | pol | Union & State Government
The posts of Speaker and Deputy Speaker were created in India under the provision of the
(1) Act of 1909
(2) Act of 1919
(3) Act of 1935
(4) Act of 1947
= 2
~ The keyed answer is (2) Act of 1919. The offices of Speaker and Deputy Speaker originated in India in 1921 under the Government of India Act, 1919 (Montagu–Chelmsford Reforms), which set up a bicameral central legislature. At that time they were called the President and Deputy President of the Central Legislative Assembly. In 1921 the Governor-General appointed Frederick Whyte and Sachchidananda Sinha as the first holders, and in 1925 Vithalbhai J. Q 88 | pol | Constitution & Rights
The doctrine of the "Rule of Law" in India implies
(1) Equality before law
(2) Arbitrary exercise of power by the executive
(3) Supremacy of Parliament
(4) Judicial supremacy over the Constitution
= 1
~ The keyed answer is (1) Equality before law. The 'Rule of Law' was explained by the British jurist A.V. Dicey through three elements: supremacy of law, meaning absence of arbitrary power; equality before law, meaning everyone, from the Prime Minister to a common citizen, is subject to the ordinary law of the land; and protection of individual rights. In India it is reflected in Article 14, which guarantees 'equality before the law' (a British idea) and 'equal protection of the laws' (an American idea) to every person, citizen or foreigner. The Supreme Court has held the rule of law to be part of the basic structure of the Constitution (Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain, 1975). Option (2) is the opposite of the rule of law; arbitrary executive power is exactly what it forbids. Option (3) is wrong because India follows constitutional supremacy, not the British model of parliamentary sovereignty; laws made by Parliament can be struck down by courts. Option (4) is wrong because no organ, including the judiciary, is above the Constitution. For police: the rule of law means a constable may act only as the law permits, and no one, however powerful, is above the law.

Q 89 | pol | Union & State Government
Which schedule of the Indian Constitution details the Union-State relations?
(1) Schedule 5
(2) Schedule 8
(3) Schedule 7
(4) Schedule 1
= 3
~ The keyed answer is (3) Schedule 7. Under Article 246, the Seventh Schedule divides legislative subjects between the Union and the States through three lists: the Union List, the State List and the Concurrent List (originally 97, 66 and 47 subjects). Parliament alone can make laws on Union List subjects such as defence, foreign affairs, banking and railways; State Legislatures on State List subjects; and both on Concurrent List subjects, with Union law prevailing in case of conflict. Residuary powers rest with Parliament (Article 248). The 42nd Amendment (1976) shifted five subjects, including education, forests and administration of justice, from the State List to the Concurrent List. Schedule 5 deals with the administration and control of Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes; Schedule 8 lists the 22 recognised languages, including Kannada; Schedule 1 lists the States and Union Territories and their territories. Useful for police aspirants: 'public order' and 'police' are entries 1 and 2 of the State List, which is why the Karnataka State Police works under the State Government. Centre–State relations are dealt with in detail in Parts XI and XII of the Constitution.

Q 90 | pol | Judiciary & Constitutional Bodies
When did the Karnataka State Human Rights Protection Act come into force?
(1) 1992
(2) 1993
(3) 1998
(4) 1999
= 2
~ The keyed answer is (2) 1993. Karnataka has no separate human rights law of its own; human rights protection in the State works under the central Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993, which applies to the whole of India. The Act was first issued as an ordinance and is deemed to have come into force on 28 September 1993; the National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) was set up under it on 12 October 1993, and Section 21 of the Act allows each State to constitute a State Human Rights Commission. Using this Act, the Karnataka Government constituted the Karnataka State Human Rights Commission (KSHRC) in 2005, and the Commission began functioning in Bengaluru in 2007 after its chairperson and members were appointed. Hence 1992, 1998 and 1999 are wrong. Remember: after the 2019 amendment, the NHRC chairperson must be a former Chief Justice of India or a former Supreme Court judge, and a State Commission's chairperson a former Chief Justice or judge of a High Court. Police officers should know that these Commissions can inquire into complaints of custodial violence and other rights violations by public servants.

Q 91 | pol | Union & State Government
Consider the following statements:
(i) The Governor holds office during the pleasure of the President.
(ii) The Governor is appointed by the President.
(iii) The Governor is elected by the people of that state.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) (i) and (ii) only
(2) (i) and (iii) only
(3) (ii) and (iii) only
(4) (i), (ii) and (iii)
= 1
~ The keyed answer is (1), (i) and (ii) only. Statement (ii) is correct: under Article 155, the Governor of a State is appointed by the President by warrant under his hand and seal; in practice the President acts on the advice of the Union Council of Ministers. Statement (i) is correct: under Article 156(1), the Governor holds office during the pleasure of the President. The normal term is five years, but the President can remove a Governor earlier; in B.P. Singhal v. Union of India (2010) the Supreme Court held that this pleasure cannot be exercised arbitrarily. Statement (iii) is wrong: the Governor is not elected by the people. The Constituent Assembly rejected direct election and adopted the Canadian model of appointment, so that the Governor remains a constitutional head while the elected Chief Minister and Council of Ministers form the real executive. Other facts: a Governor must be a citizen of India and at least 35 years old (Article 157), and one person can be Governor of two or more States (7th Amendment, 1956). Karnataka fact: Jayachamarajendra Wadiyar, the last Maharaja of Mysore, was Rajpramukh and then the first Governor of Mysore State (1956–64).

Q 92 | pol | Panchayat Raj & Local Bodies
Which one of the following is the correct sequence of rural local government institutions (lowest level to highest level)?
(1) Zilla Panchayat – Grama Panchayat – Taluk Panchayat
(2) Taluk Panchayat – Grama Panchayat – Zilla Panchayat
(3) Grama Panchayat – Taluk Panchayat – Zilla Panchayat
(4) Grama Panchayat – Zilla Panchayat – Taluk Panchayat
= 3
~ The keyed answer is (3) Grama Panchayat – Taluk Panchayat – Zilla Panchayat. The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 came into force on 24 April 1993, a date now observed as National Panchayati Raj Day. Under Article 243B it provides a three-tier Panchayati Raj system: panchayats at the village, intermediate and district levels, though States with a population below 20 lakh need not have the intermediate tier. In Karnataka, under the Karnataka Gram Swaraj and Panchayat Raj Act, 1993, the lowest tier is the Grama Panchayat for a village or group of villages, the middle tier is the Taluk Panchayat at the taluk level, and the highest tier is the Zilla Panchayat at the district level. Options (1), (2) and (4) jumble this order and are therefore wrong. The three-tier idea was first recommended by the Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957) and was first adopted by Rajasthan at Nagaur on 2 October 1959. Memory tip: go from small to big, village, then taluk, then district (G–T–Z). Panchayat elections are conducted by the State Election Commission, and Karnataka, like many other States, reserves 50% of panchayat seats for women, more than the constitutional minimum of one-third.

Q 94 | pol | Judiciary & Constitutional Bodies
Match the following:
List – I (Writs) / List – II (Jurisdiction)
(a) Habeas Corpus – (i) A correction writ issued to quash an order already passed by an inferior court
(b) Mandamus – (ii) Order issued by a court to compel a public official to perform his/her mandated duty
(c) Certiorari – (iii) Protects individuals from illegal detention
(d) Prohibition – (iv) A preventive order issued by a higher court to a lower court
Codes:
(1) a – i, b – iii, c – iv, d – ii
(2) a – iii, b – ii, c – i, d – iv
(3) a – ii, b – iii, c – iv, d – i
(4) a – iii, b – i, c – iv, d – ii
= 2
~ The keyed answer is (2): a–iii, b–ii, c–i, d–iv. Writs are issued by the Supreme Court under Article 32 and by the High Courts under Article 226. (a) Habeas Corpus, meaning 'to have the body', orders a detaining authority to produce the detained person before the court so that the legality of the detention can be examined; it protects individuals from illegal detention, even by private persons. (b) Mandamus, 'we command', directs a public official or body to perform a public duty it has failed to perform; it cannot be issued against the President, a Governor or a private individual. (c) Certiorari, 'to be certified', is a curative writ by which a higher court quashes an order already passed by a lower court or tribunal that acted without or in excess of jurisdiction. (d) Prohibition, 'to forbid', is a preventive writ that stops a lower court from exceeding its jurisdiction while a case is still pending. The fifth writ, Quo Warranto ('by what authority'), questions a person's right to hold a public office. Police tip: habeas corpus petitions often arise from illegal custody, so remember Article 22(2): an arrested person must be produced before a magistrate within 24 hours.

Q 95 | cur | International & Sports

Q 100 | eco | Indian Economy
Which of the following committees is not related to estimation of poverty in India?
(1) Suresh Tendulkar Committee
(2) C. Rangarajan Committee
(3) M. Narasimham Committee
(4) Lakdawala Committee
= 3
~ The keyed answer is (3) M. Narasimham Committee. The Narasimham Committees of 1991 and 1998, headed by former RBI Governor M. Narasimham, dealt with reform of the financial and banking system: lower SLR and CRR, greater freedom for banks, asset reconstruction companies and stronger capital norms. They had nothing to do with estimating poverty. The other three are poverty-estimation expert groups set up by the Planning Commission. The Lakdawala Expert Group (1993) recommended state-specific poverty lines based on calorie norms and price indices. The Suresh Tendulkar Committee (2009) moved to a consumption basket that also covered health and education; its method gave a poverty ratio of 37.2% for 2004–05 and 21.9% for 2011–12. The C. Rangarajan Committee (2014) revised the method and estimated 29.5% poverty for 2011–12, with a poverty line of about ₹47 a day in urban and ₹32 a day in rural areas. Also remember the Y.K. Alagh Task Force (1979), which first linked poverty lines to calorie requirements. Karnataka angle: coastal Karnataka is called the cradle of Indian banking, because Canara, Syndicate, Vijaya and Corporation banks were founded there.
