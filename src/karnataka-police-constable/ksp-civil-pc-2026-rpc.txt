Q 2 | sci | Biology
Which of the following microbe(algae) is used by astronauts?
(1) Gelidium
(2) Spirulina
(3) Gracilaria
(4) Spirogyra
= 2
~ Spirulina is the correct answer. Spirulina is a spiral-shaped blue-green alga (cyanobacterium) whose dried biomass contains about 60–70 per cent protein, along with vitamins, minerals and antioxidants. Because it is light, grows fast and is highly nutritious, space agencies such as NASA and the European Space Agency have studied it as a compact food supplement for astronauts on long missions, and it is often marketed as a 'superfood'. Gelidium and Gracilaria are red seaweeds valued mainly as sources of agar, the jelly-like substance used in microbial culture media, ice-creams and jellies, not as astronaut food. Spirogyra is a green filamentous freshwater alga seen as slimy 'pond silk' in ponds and tanks; it is a textbook specimen, not a food supplement. Exam tip: NCERT cites Spirulina as single-cell protein (SCP) that can be grown on waste water from potato-processing plants, straw, molasses and even sewage, and it names Chlorella, another protein-rich unicellular alga, as a food supplement for space travellers. Remember 'Spirulina and Chlorella – protein for space' and 'Gelidium and Gracilaria – agar'.

Q 3 | geo | Environment & Ecology
The Minamata Convention is a global UN treaty adopted in 2013 to protect human health and the environment from anthropogenic ______ pollution.
(1) Mercury
(2) Carbon monoxide
(3) Sulphur dioxide
(4) Nitrogen oxides
= 1
~ Mercury is the correct answer. The Minamata Convention on Mercury was adopted in October 2013 at Kumamoto, Japan, and entered into force on 16 August 2017. It is named after Minamata Bay in Japan, where in the 1950s thousands of people suffered severe nerve damage ('Minamata disease') after eating fish contaminated with methylmercury released by a chemical factory. The convention bans new mercury mines, phases out existing ones, restricts mercury in products such as thermometers, batteries and certain lamps, and controls mercury emissions from sources such as coal-fired power plants and small-scale gold mining. India signed it in 2014 and ratified it on 18 June 2018. Carbon monoxide, sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides are important air pollutants, but they are not the subject of this treaty. Memory tip for chemical treaties: Minamata – mercury; Stockholm – persistent organic pollutants; Rotterdam – prior informed consent for hazardous chemicals in trade; Basel – movement of hazardous wastes; Montreal Protocol – ozone-depleting substances.

Q 4 | geo | Environment & Ecology
Identify the reason behind shifting from diesel and petrol to Compressed Natural Gas(CNG).
(a) Burns more efficiently
(b) Cheaper
(c) Laying pipelines to ensure continuous and uninterrupted supply of CNG is easy
(d) Adulterating and siphoning off are difficult
(1) (a), (c) and (d) only
(2) (a), (b) and (d) only
(3) (b), (c) and (d) only
(4) (c), (b) and (a) only
= 2
~ The keyed answer is (a), (b) and (d) only. NCERT's chapter on environmental issues explains why Delhi shifted its public transport from diesel to CNG: CNG burns most efficiently, so very little of it is left unburnt; it is cheaper than petrol or diesel; and, unlike liquid fuels, it cannot be siphoned off by thieves or adulterated. Statement (c) is wrong because it describes the main problem with CNG, not an advantage: laying pipelines to deliver CNG through distribution points and ensuring a continuous, uninterrupted supply is difficult, not easy. Hence options (1), (3) and (4), which all include (c), are wrong. Useful facts: following Supreme Court directions, by the end of 2002 all buses of Delhi were converted to run on CNG, which brought a visible fall in air pollution. CNG is mainly methane stored at high pressure and produces less carbon monoxide, unburnt hydrocarbons and particulate matter than petrol or diesel. In Karnataka, city gas distribution networks now supply CNG and piped natural gas in Bengaluru and several other cities.

Q 5 | sci | Biology
Fish oil is rich in ______
(1) Vitamins
(2) Carbohydrates
(3) Omega 3 fatty acids
(4) Oligosaccharides
= 3
~ Omega-3 fatty acids is the correct answer. Fish oil, obtained from the tissues of oily fish such as sardines, mackerel, salmon and tuna, is especially rich in the long-chain omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids EPA (eicosapentaenoic acid) and DHA (docosahexaenoic acid). These fatty acids help lower blood triglycerides, support heart health and are important for the development of the brain and the retina of the eye. Option (1) is only partly true: fish-liver oils such as cod-liver oil do contain vitamins A and D, but the nutrient for which fish oil is chiefly valued is omega-3 fat, so 'vitamins' is not the best answer. Option (2) is wrong because an oil is a lipid and contains practically no carbohydrates. Option (4) is wrong because oligosaccharides are short-chain carbohydrates (found in pulses, onions and milk), not fats. Memory tip: 'Fish = Omega-3'. Coastal Karnataka's sardine (bhoothai) and mackerel (bangude) are cheap local sources of these healthy fats.

Q 6 | sci | Biology
Which of the following group is an example for modified leaf?
(1) Onion and Cactus
(2) Pea and Ginger
(3) Onion and Garlic
(4) Garlic and Cactus
= 3
~ The keyed answer is Onion and Garlic. In both plants the edible bulb is made up of fleshy, food-storing leaves (scale leaves or leaf bases) attached to a very short, disc-like stem, so both members of this pair are classic textbook examples of leaves modified for storage; NCERT states that the fleshy leaves of onion and garlic store food. Pea and Ginger is wrong as a pair: the tendrils of pea are modified leaflets, but ginger is an underground stem (rhizome) that stores food. Cactus appears in options (1) and (4). Its spines are reduced leaves, but the cactus is more often quoted as an example of a modified stem, because its green, flattened or fleshy stem (the phylloclade of Opuntia) takes over photosynthesis and water storage. So the pair in which both plants are standard examples of leaves modified to store food is onion and garlic. Memory tip: potato, ginger, turmeric – stem; carrot, radish, sweet potato – root; onion, garlic – fleshy leaves. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 7 | sci | Biology
Malfunctioning of kidneys can lead to accumulation of urea in blood. This condition is called
(1) Haemodialysis
(2) Uremia
(3) Renal calculi
(4) Nephritis
= 2
~ Uremia is the correct answer. The kidneys filter urea and other nitrogenous wastes out of the blood and remove them in urine. When the kidneys malfunction, urea accumulates in the blood; this condition is called uremia, and it can cause nausea and fatigue and, if untreated, lead to kidney failure. Option (1), haemodialysis, is the treatment, not the condition: in an artificial kidney, blood mixed with an anticoagulant (heparin) is passed through a dialysing unit with a semi-permeable membrane, urea diffuses out into the dialysing fluid, and the cleared blood is returned to the body after adding anti-heparin. Option (3), renal calculi, means kidney stones, hard masses of crystallised salts such as calcium oxalate. Option (4), nephritis, is inflammation of the kidneys; glomerulonephritis is inflammation of the glomeruli. Exam tip: kidney transplantation is the ultimate treatment for acute renal failure, and a donor who is a close relative is preferred to reduce the chance of rejection. Memory aid: 'Uremia = urea in the blood', since the suffix '-emia' refers to blood.

Q 8 | sci | Biology
In gel electrophoresis, DNA moves towards
(1) Negative electrode
(2) Positive electrode
(3) Neutral
(4) Random
= 2
~ Positive electrode is the correct answer. DNA carries a negative charge because of the phosphate groups in its sugar–phosphate backbone. In gel electrophoresis, DNA fragments are loaded into wells in an agarose gel and an electric field is applied; being negatively charged, they move towards the anode, that is, the positive electrode. The agarose gel acts like a sieve, so smaller fragments move faster and farther than larger ones, and the fragments get separated by size. Option (1) is wrong because a negatively charged molecule is repelled by the negative electrode (cathode). Options (3) and (4) are wrong because the movement of DNA is neither neutral nor random; it is directed by its charge. After separation, the DNA is stained with ethidium bromide and seen as orange bands under UV light; the bands are cut out and extracted from the gel, a step called elution. Exam link: the same technique underlies DNA fingerprinting, which forensic laboratories such as the State Forensic Science Laboratory in Bengaluru use to identify suspects and victims.

Q 9 | geo | World Geography
Match the following:
List-I (Planet) / List-II (Period of revolution)
(a) Jupiter – (i) 84 years
(b) Saturn – (ii) 12 years
(c) Uranus – (iii) 165 years
(d) Neptune – (iv) 29 years
(1) a – iv, b – i, c – iii, d – ii
(2) a – iii, b – ii, c – iv, d – i
(3) a – i, b – iii, c – iv, d – ii
(4) a – ii, b – iv, c – i, d – iii
= 4
~ The correct code is a – ii, b – iv, c – i, d – iii. The period of revolution is the time a planet takes to complete one orbit around the Sun, and by Kepler's third law it increases sharply with distance from the Sun. Jupiter, the largest planet, takes about 11.9 years, roughly 12 years (ii). Saturn, famous for its bright rings, takes about 29.5 years (iv). Uranus, the planet that rotates on its side, takes about 84 years (i). Neptune, the farthest planet, takes about 165 years (iii); discovered in 1846, it completed its first full orbit since discovery only in 2011. Code (1) wrongly gives Jupiter 29 years and Saturn 84 years; code (2) gives Jupiter 165 years; code (3) gives Jupiter 84 years. Quick check: since revolution time increases outward, the values must rise in the order Jupiter < Saturn < Uranus < Neptune, that is 12 < 29 < 84 < 165, and only code (4) follows this order. For comparison, Earth takes about 365.25 days and Mercury only 88 days.

Q 10 | sci | Physics
Consider the following statements:
Statement I: Fuse is a device which protects circuits and appliances by stopping the flow of any unduly high electric current.
Statement II: The fuse is placed in parallel with the device.
(1) Both statements I and II are correct
(2) Both statements I and II are incorrect
(3) Statement I is correct but Statement II is incorrect
(4) Statement I is incorrect but Statement II is correct
= 3
~ Option (3) is correct: Statement I is correct but Statement II is incorrect. A fuse is a safety device that protects electric circuits and appliances by stopping the flow of any unduly high current. It works on the heating effect of current (Joule's law, H = I²Rt): when the current exceeds the rated value, the fuse wire, made of a metal or alloy with a low melting point such as a tin–lead alloy, heats up, melts and breaks the circuit. Statement II is wrong because a fuse is always connected in series with the device. Only in series does the whole current pass through the fuse, so that its melting cuts off the supply. If it were placed in parallel, current would keep flowing through the appliance branch, and the appliance would stay on even after the fuse melted. Domestic fuses are rated 1 A, 2 A, 3 A, 5 A, 10 A and so on; a 1 kW electric iron at 220 V draws 1000/220 = about 4.54 A, so a 5 A fuse is used. Today miniature circuit breakers (MCBs) do the same job and can be reset instead of replaced.

Q 11 | sci | Science & Technology
GPS-Aided GEO Augmented Navigation (GAGAN) is a Satellite-Based Augmentation System (SBAS) developed jointly by
(1) ISRO and DRDO
(2) DRDO and AAI
(3) AAI and HAL
(4) ISRO and AAI
= 4
~ ISRO and AAI is the correct answer. GAGAN (GPS-Aided GEO Augmented Navigation) was jointly developed and implemented by the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) and the Airports Authority of India (AAI). It improves the accuracy and integrity of ordinary GPS signals for civil aviation: ground reference stations compute corrections, and geostationary satellites of the GSAT series broadcast these corrections to aircraft. The DGCA certified GAGAN for en-route navigation (RNP 0.1) on 30 December 2013 and for approach with vertical guidance (APV-1) on 21 April 2015. With this, India joined the USA (WAAS), Europe (EGNOS) and Japan (MSAS) in having its own SBAS, and GAGAN is interoperable with these systems. DRDO is the defence research organisation and HAL is the Bengaluru-based aircraft maker; neither was a GAGAN partner, so options (1), (2) and (3) are wrong. Do not confuse GAGAN with NavIC (formerly IRNSS): NavIC is ISRO's own regional navigation satellite system, designed around a constellation of seven satellites, covering India and about 1,500 km around it, whereas GAGAN only augments GPS.

Q 12 | sci | Physics
An alloy is used in the coil of electric iron box. The reason is
(1) Resistivity of an alloy is higher than that of the constituent metals
(2) Resistance of an alloy is higher than that of the constituent metals
(3) Resistivity of an alloy is less than that of the constituent metals
(4) Alloys oxidise readily at higher temperature
= 1
~ Option (1) is correct. Heating devices such as electric irons, toasters and room heaters use coils of alloys like nichrome (nickel–chromium) instead of pure metals, and NCERT gives two reasons: the resistivity of an alloy is generally higher than that of its constituent metals, and alloys do not oxidise (burn) readily at high temperatures. High resistivity means that a coil of convenient length produces a large amount of heat (H = I²Rt); the resistivity of nichrome is many times that of copper. Option (2) is imprecise: resistance depends on the length and thickness of the wire, so it is not a property of the material; the correct comparison is of resistivity. Option (3) states the opposite of the truth. Option (4) also reverses the fact: alloys are chosen precisely because they do not oxidise readily at high temperature. Memory tip: copper and aluminium (low resistivity) for transmission wires; nichrome (high resistivity) for heating coils; tungsten (melting point about 3380 °C) for bulb filaments.

Q 13 | sci | Chemistry
Suresh uses curd which is kept in copper vessel from last 3 days during dinner. Late night he experiences pain in stomach. One of the reasons for this may be
(1) Water in the curd
(2) Sweetness in curd
(3) Salt in the curd
(4) Reaction of curd with copper
= 4
~ Option (4) is correct. Curd is sour because it contains lactic acid produced by Lactobacillus bacteria during fermentation. When curd is stored in a copper or brass vessel for a long time, as here for three days, the acid slowly reacts with the copper and its surface oxide layer in the presence of air and forms soluble, poisonous copper salts. Eating such contaminated curd can cause stomach pain, nausea, vomiting and diarrhoea. That is why NCERT asks the classic question, 'Why should curd and sour substances not be kept in brass and copper vessels?' The other options are harmless natural components: the water in curd, its slight sweetness from milk sugar (lactose) and the salt in it do not cause such poisoning. Traditional practice: the inside of brass and copper cooking vessels was coated with tin, called 'kalai' (tinning), to prevent such reactions, and sour items like curd, tamarind rasam and pickles are best kept in glass, steel or earthen pots. Fact to remember: acids react with many metals; for example, zinc and dilute acid release hydrogen gas.

Q 14 | sci | Chemistry
Consider the following statements and choose the correct answer from the options given below:
Statement I: Acids are bitter in taste
Statement II: Bases are sour in taste
(1) Both Statement I and Statement II are incorrect
(2) Statement I is correct but Statement II is incorrect
(3) Statement I is incorrect but Statement II is correct
(4) Both Statement I and Statement II are correct
= 1
~ Option (1) is correct: both statements are incorrect, because the tastes have been swapped. Acids are sour, not bitter; lemon (citric acid), tamarind (tartaric acid), vinegar (acetic acid) and curd (lactic acid) taste sour because of the acids in them. Bases are bitter, not sour, and feel soapy to the touch; examples are baking soda (sodium hydrogencarbonate), soap and milk of magnesia. So Statement I is wrong and Statement II is also wrong, which rules out options (2), (3) and (4). Since tasting chemicals is dangerous, laboratories identify acids and bases with indicators: acids turn blue litmus red and bases turn red litmus blue; phenolphthalein turns pink in bases and stays colourless in acids. Litmus is a natural indicator extracted from lichens, and turmeric turns reddish-brown with bases, which is why a turmeric stain on clothes turns red when washed with soap. Memory tip: 'Acid – sour – blue to red; Base – bitter – red to blue.' The word 'acid' itself comes from the Latin 'acidus', meaning sour.

Q 15 | sci | Chemistry
Match the following:
List-I / List-II
(a) Cu + Zn – (i) Solder
(b) Cu + Sn – (ii) Brass
(c) Pb + Sn – (iii) Amalgam
(d) Na + Hg – (iv) Bronze
(1) a – ii, b – iv, c – i, d – iii
(2) a – i, b – iv, c – ii, d – iii
(3) a – ii, b – iii, c – i, d – iv
(4) a – i, b – ii, c – iii, d – iv
= 1
~ The correct code is a – ii, b – iv, c – i, d – iii. Brass is an alloy of copper and zinc (Cu + Zn); it is used for utensils, musical instruments and decorative items. Bronze is an alloy of copper and tin (Cu + Sn); it is harder than copper and is used for statues, medals and bells. Solder is an alloy of lead and tin (Pb + Sn); its low melting point makes it ideal for joining electrical wires and circuit components. Sodium amalgam is an alloy of sodium and mercury (Na + Hg); any alloy in which one of the metals is mercury is called an amalgam, and sodium amalgam is used as a reducing agent in chemistry. Code (2) wrongly pairs Cu + Zn with solder and Pb + Sn with brass; code (3) wrongly pairs Cu + Sn with amalgam and Na + Hg with bronze; code (4) gets every pair wrong. Memory tip: 'Brass has zinc, bronze has tin.' History link: the Harappan 'Dancing Girl' and the Chola Nataraja are famous bronze sculptures, showing how long Indians have worked with this alloy.

Q 16 | sci | Chemistry
The gases used in TV tubes and lighthouses are
(1) Oxygen and Hydrogen
(2) Nitrogen and Carbon dioxide
(3) Krypton and Xenon
(4) Methane and Helium
= 3
~ Krypton and Xenon is the correct answer. Krypton and xenon are noble gases of Group 18; they glow brightly when an electric discharge passes through them, and being chemically inert they do not react with hot filaments or electrodes. Xenon in particular gives an intense, white, daylight-like light, so it is used in the high-intensity lamps of lighthouses, searchlights, vehicle headlamps and photographic flashes, while krypton is used in some fluorescent tubes and flash lamps; general-knowledge books list the two gases together for TV tubes and lighthouses. Option (1) is wrong because oxygen supports combustion and hydrogen is highly inflammable, both dangerous inside a glowing tube. Option (2) is wrong: nitrogen fills some ordinary bulbs and carbon dioxide is used in fire extinguishers, but neither gives the brilliant light a lighthouse needs. Option (4) is wrong: methane is a fuel gas, and helium is used in balloons, airships and the breathing cylinders of deep-sea divers. Memory tip for noble-gas uses: neon – red advertisement signs; argon – filling electric bulbs; krypton and xenon – lighthouses, TV tubes and flash lamps; radon – radiotherapy for cancer.

Q 17 | sci | Physics
Danger signal lights are red in colour because red light is ______ by fog or smoke.
(1) Most scattered
(2) Least scattered
(3) Most diffracted
(4) Least diffracted
= 2
~ Least scattered is the correct answer. When light passes through fog, smoke or dust, it is scattered by the tiny particles. For small particles the amount of scattering is inversely proportional to the fourth power of the wavelength (Rayleigh scattering, intensity ∝ 1/λ⁴). Red light has the longest wavelength in the visible spectrum (about 620–750 nm), so it is scattered the least and can travel the farthest through fog or smoke without losing much intensity. That is why danger signals, traffic 'stop' lights, tail lamps of vehicles and warning lamps on tall towers are red. Option (1) is wrong because blue and violet light, with short wavelengths, are scattered the most; this is why the clear sky looks blue. Options (3) and (4) are wrong because the reason here is scattering by particles, not diffraction, which is the bending of light around the edges of obstacles. Related facts: the Sun looks reddish at sunrise and sunset because blue light is scattered away along the long path through the atmosphere, and to an astronaut above the atmosphere the sky looks dark because there is no scattering there.

Q 18 | sci | Chemistry
Identify the compound from the following options which can be used to clean wells and ponds polluted by organic debris.
(1) Potassium dichromate
(2) Potassium permanganate
(3) Potassium chloride
(4) Potassium sulphate
= 2
~ Potassium permanganate is the correct answer. Potassium permanganate (KMnO₄) is a deep-purple crystalline compound and a strong oxidising agent. When a small amount is added to wells, tanks or ponds polluted by decaying leaves and other organic debris, it oxidises the organic matter and kills many disease-causing bacteria, so it is widely used as a cheap disinfectant for village drinking-water sources, especially after floods. It is also used to wash fruits and vegetables and, in dilute solution, as an antiseptic for wounds. Option (1), potassium dichromate, is also an oxidising agent, but it is toxic and carcinogenic; it is used in laboratory titrations, leather tanning and the old chemical breathalyser test once used by traffic police to detect drunken driving, never for drinking water. Option (3), potassium chloride, is used as a fertiliser (muriate of potash) and as a low-sodium salt substitute. Option (4), potassium sulphate, is also mainly a fertiliser. Exam tip: other water disinfectants are bleaching powder (calcium oxychloride) and chlorine, while boiling and UV treatment are physical methods of disinfection.

Q 19 | sci | Science & Technology
Match List-I with List-II and choose the correct answer from the codes given below:
List-I (Instruments) / List-II (Uses)
(a) Planimeter – (i) To trace, enlarge or reduce maps
(b) Pantograph – (ii) To measure the area
(c) Barometer – (iii) To measure atmospheric pressure
(d) Thermometer – (iv) To measure temperature
(1) a – ii, b – i, c – iii, d – iv
(2) a – i, b – ii, c – iii, d – iv
(3) a – iii, b – i, c – ii, d – iv
(4) a – iii, b – ii, c – i, d – iv
= 1
~ The correct code is a – ii, b – i, c – iii, d – iv. A planimeter is an instrument used to find the area of any plane figure, such as a lake, a forest patch or a plot shown on a map, by tracing its boundary with a pointer. A pantograph is a device of four jointed rods arranged as a parallelogram, used to trace a map or drawing and copy it at an enlarged or reduced scale. A barometer measures atmospheric pressure; the mercury barometer was invented by Torricelli in 1643, and the aneroid barometer works without any liquid. A thermometer measures temperature; a clinical thermometer reads from 35 °C to 42 °C, and normal human body temperature is about 37 °C (98.6 °F). Code (2) swaps the planimeter and the pantograph; codes (3) and (4) wrongly give the planimeter the job of measuring atmospheric pressure. Memory tip: 'plani-meter' measures a plane area, while 'panto-graph' copies drawings. Other useful pairs: hygrometer – humidity, anemometer – wind speed, rain gauge – rainfall, altimeter – altitude, seismograph – earthquakes.

Q 20 | geo | Karnataka Geography
Choose the correct sequence of some important peaks in Karnataka according to their height in descending order.
(1) Chamundi Hills, Biligirirangana Hills, Gopalaswamy Hills, Melukote Hills
(2) Biligirirangana Hills, Gopalaswamy Hills, Melukote Hills, Chamundi Hills
(3) Gopalaswamy Hills, Melukote Hills, Biligirirangana Hills, Chamundi Hills
(4) Melukote Hills, Chamundi Hills, Biligirirangana Hills, Gopalaswamy Hills
= 2
~ The keyed answer is option (2): Biligirirangana Hills, Gopalaswamy Hills, Melukote Hills, Chamundi Hills. All four are hills of the Southern Maidan (plateau) region of Karnataka, and the key arranges them from highest to lowest. Biligirirangana Hills (BR Hills) in Chamarajanagar district, which link the Eastern and Western Ghats and form the BRT Tiger Reserve, are the highest of the four. Himavad Gopalaswamy Betta, about 1,450 m high, lies inside Bandipur National Park in Chamarajanagar district and is known for its Venugopala temple and mist-covered summit. Melukote in Mandya district stands on the Yadavagiri (Yadugiri) hills and is famous for the Cheluvanarayana Swamy temple and its association with Sri Ramanujacharya. Chamundi Hills near Mysuru, about 1,064 m high, carry the Chamundeshwari temple and a huge monolithic Nandi, and come last in the key's order. Options (1) and (4) put Chamundi Hills above both BR Hills and Gopalaswamy Hills, and option (3) puts BR Hills below Gopalaswamy Hills; only option (2) keeps BR Hills above Gopalaswamy Hills and both above Chamundi Hills. Also remember that Mullayanagiri in Chikkamagaluru (about 1,930 m) is the highest peak in Karnataka.

Q 21 | geo | Indian Geography
Identify the correctly matched pairs.
(a) Bhor Ghat – Mumbai and Pune
(b) Thal Ghat – Mumbai and Nasik
(c) Pal Ghat – Rajsamand and Pali
(d) Agumbe Ghat – Udupi and Shivamogga
(1) (a), (b), (c) and (d) are correct
(2) (a) and (b) are correct
(3) (c) and (d) are correct
(4) (a), (b) and (d) are correct
= 4
~ The keyed answer is (a), (b) and (d). Bhor Ghat, crossed by the Mumbai–Pune railway and expressway near Khandala, links Mumbai with Pune. Thal Ghat (also called Kasara Ghat) carries the railway and highway from Mumbai to Nashik. Agumbe Ghat, in the Western Ghats of Shivamogga district, connects the coastal district of Udupi with Shivamogga in the Malnad. Pair (c) is wrong: Pal Ghat (the Palakkad Gap) is a broad gap in the southern Western Ghats between the Nilgiris and the Anaimalai Hills, linking Palakkad in Kerala with Coimbatore in Tamil Nadu, whereas Rajsamand and Pali are districts of Rajasthan in the Aravalli range. Hence options (1) and (3), which include (c), and option (2), which leaves out the correct pair (d), are wrong. Karnataka angle: other important ghat roads of the state are Shiradi Ghat (Mangaluru–Hassan–Bengaluru), Charmadi Ghat (Mangaluru–Chikkamagaluru) and Sampaje Ghat (Mangaluru–Madikeri). Agumbe, called the 'Cherrapunji of the South' for its very heavy rainfall, is also famous for king cobras and its sunset point.

Q 22 | geo | Karnataka Geography
Identify the distribution of soil type in given map:
(1) Alluvial soil
(2) Laterite soil
(3) Black soil
(4) Red soil
= 2
~ Laterite soil is the correct answer. The shaded belt on the map runs from north to south along the Western Ghats (Sahyadri) and the Malnad, just inland of the narrow coastal strip. This high-rainfall zone is where laterite soil is found in Karnataka, in districts such as Belagavi (Khanapur area), Uttara Kannada, Shivamogga, Chikkamagaluru, Kodagu, Hassan and parts of Udupi and Dakshina Kannada. Laterite soil forms under heavy rainfall with alternating wet and dry seasons: leaching washes away silica and lime, leaving behind iron and aluminium oxides that give the soil its reddish colour. It is poor in nitrogen, potash and lime but, with manure, suits plantation crops such as coffee, tea, rubber, cashew, arecanut and pepper; when dried it hardens and can be cut into bricks, hence the Kannada name 'jambittige'. Alluvial soil is confined to river valleys and the coast; black soil covers the northern districts such as Vijayapura, Kalaburagi, Bagalkote and Dharwad; red soil covers most of the southern and eastern plateau. So options (1), (3) and (4) do not match the shaded belt.

Q 23 | geo | World Geography
Consider the following urban classification:
(a) City
(b) Megacity
(c) Megalopolis
(d) Conurbation
Which is the correct sequence from smaller to higher size of population?
(1) (d), (c), (a) and (b)
(2) (b), (a), (c) and (d)
(3) (a), (b), (d) and (c)
(4) (a), (d), (c) and (b)
= 3
~ The keyed answer is option (3): City, Megacity, Conurbation, Megalopolis. A city is a large urban centre, bigger than a town, that serves as a hub of trade, administration and services; in India a city with a population of one lakh or more is called a Class I city. A megacity is a single urban agglomeration with more than 10 million (one crore) people, such as Mumbai, Delhi or Kolkata. A conurbation is a large, continuous built-up area formed when several originally separate towns and cities expand and merge, for example Greater London or the Delhi–NCR belt; because it joins many cities together, the key treats it as larger than a single megacity. A megalopolis, from the Greek for 'great city', is the largest form: a super-metropolitan region formed by a chain of conurbations, the classic example being the Boston–Washington corridor ('BosWash') in the USA. Q 76 | pol | Laws, Police & Society

(4) Disclosure is required by law
= 1
~ A government servant holds official information in trust for the public; it is not personal property. Q 84 | pol | Union & State Government
In a parliamentary government, the Executive is directly responsible to
(1) Judiciary
(2) Legislature
(3) People
(4) Constitution
= 2
~ In a parliamentary system, the real executive (the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers, or the Chief Minister and state ministers) is drawn from the legislature and stays in office only as long as it enjoys the confidence of the popularly elected House. Article 75(3) makes the Union Council of Ministers collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha, and Article 164(2) makes a state Council of Ministers collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly, such as the Karnataka Vidhana Sabha. So the executive is directly responsible to the legislature, and option (2) is correct. The executive is responsible to the people only indirectly, through elections. The judiciary checks the legality of executive action through judicial review, but the executive is not answerable to it in the political sense. The Constitution is the framework that all organs must obey, not a body to which ministers report. India borrowed this parliamentary model from Britain.

Q 85 | pol | Constitution & Rights
Which part of Indian Constitution contains the emergency provision?
(1) Part X
(2) Part XII
(3) Part XVI
(4) Part XVIII
= 4
~ Emergency provisions are contained in Part XVIII of the Constitution, Articles 352 to 360, so option (4) is correct. They provide for three types of emergency: National Emergency under Article 352 (war, external aggression or armed rebellion), President's Rule or State Emergency under Article 356 (failure of constitutional machinery in a state), and Financial Emergency under Article 360. The other parts deal with different subjects: Part X covers Scheduled and Tribal Areas (Articles 244 and 244A); Part XII covers Finance, Property, Contracts and Suits (Articles 264 to 300A); and Part XVI contains special provisions relating to certain classes such as Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Articles 330 to 342A). The other UTs without representation are the Andaman and Nicobar Islands and Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu. Option (1) is wrong because Puducherry and Jammu and Kashmir are represented; option (2) is wrong because Delhi and Puducherry are represented; option (3) is wrong because Delhi is represented. Seats are allotted to the states and UTs in the Fourth Schedule of the Constitution under Article 80, broadly in proportion to population. Karnataka has 12 Rajya Sabha seats. Memory tip: for a Union Territory, no assembly means no Rajya Sabha seat.

Q 92 | pol | Judiciary & Constitutional Bodies
The highest legal advisor to the Government of India is
(1) Solicitor General
(2) Advocate General
(3) Attorney General
(4) Comptroller and Auditor General
= 3
~ The Attorney General of India is the highest law officer and chief legal adviser to the Government of India, so option (3) is correct. The office is created by Article 76: the President appoints a person qualified to be a Supreme Court judge, and the Attorney General holds office during the pleasure of the President. The Attorney General has the right of audience in all courts in India and, under Article 88, may speak in either House of Parliament and its committees but cannot vote. The Solicitor General is the second law officer who assists the Attorney General; this post is not constitutional and exists under the Law Officers (Conditions of Service) Rules, 1987. The Advocate General (Article 165) is the highest law officer of a state, such as the Advocate General of Karnataka, and is appointed by the Governor. The Comptroller and Auditor General (Article 148) audits government accounts and is not a legal adviser. Exam fact: M.C. Setalvad was the first Attorney General of India.

Q 93 | pol | Union & State Government
The Governor submits his/her resignation letter to which of the following?
(1) The Chief Minister of the concerned state
(2) The President
(3) The Prime Minister
(4) The Chief Justice of Supreme Court
= 2
~ Under Article 155, the Governor of a state is appointed by the President by warrant under his hand and seal, and under Article 156(1) the Governor holds office during the pleasure of the President. Article 156(2) says the Governor may resign his office by writing under his hand addressed to the President. So option (2) is correct. A simple rule: one resigns to the authority that appoints. The Chief Minister is appointed by the Governor, so the Governor does not resign to the Chief Minister. The Prime Minister advises the President but is not the appointing authority. The Chief Justice of India administers the oath to the President, while a Governor's oath is administered by the Chief Justice of the state High Court (Article 159); neither of them receives the Governor's resignation. Related pairs to remember: the President resigns to the Vice-President, the Vice-President to the President, and the Speaker to the Deputy Speaker. The Governor's normal term is five years (Article 156(3)).

Q 94 | pol | Union & State Government
As per Article 163 of Indian Constitution, Council of Ministers aid and advise the
(1) Chief Minister
(2) Governor of State
(3) Legislative Council
(4) Legislative Assembly
= 2
~ Article 163(1) says there shall be a Council of Ministers with the Chief Minister at the head to aid and advise the Governor in the exercise of his functions, except where the Constitution requires him to act in his discretion. So option (2), Governor of State, is correct. The Chief Minister is not advised by the Council; he heads it. The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly (Article 164(2)), but it does not 'aid and advise' the Assembly or the Legislative Council; it answers to them. Article 163(3) adds that the question whether any, and if so what, advice was tendered by ministers to the Governor cannot be inquired into in any court. The Union equivalent is Article 74, under which the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister aids and advises the President. Karnataka has a bicameral legislature, the Vidhana Sabha with 224 members and the Vidhana Parishat with 75 members, so options (3) and (4) are familiar distractors. Q 97 | cur | International & Sports
The correct order of Olympic motto is
(1) Faster, Stronger, Higher – Together
(2) Higher, Faster, Stronger – Together
(3) Faster, Higher, Stronger – Together
(4) Higher, Stronger, Faster – Together
= 3
~ The Olympic motto is 'Citius, Altius, Fortius – Communiter', which means 'Faster, Higher, Stronger – Together', so option (3) is correct. The original three-word Latin motto 'Citius, Altius, Fortius' was proposed by Pierre de Coubertin, founder of the modern Olympic Movement, who borrowed it from his friend, the Dominican priest Henri Didon; it was adopted when the International Olympic Committee (IOC) was formed in 1894. In July 2021, just before the Tokyo Games, the IOC Session approved adding the word 'Communiter' (Together) to stress solidarity during the COVID-19 pandemic. The other three options only shuffle the order of the three words; the Latin order Citius, Altius, Fortius gives Faster, Higher, Stronger. Memory tip: picture an athlete who runs faster, jumps higher and lifts stronger, all together as one team. Related facts: the five Olympic rings represent the union of five continents, and Los Angeles will host the 2028 Summer Olympics.

Q 98 | cur | International & Sports
The first Summer Paralympic Games were held in ________ in the year ________.
(1) Atlanta, 1964
(2) Rome, 1960
(3) Toronto, 1960
(4) Tokyo, 1964
= 2
~ The first Summer Paralympic Games were held in Rome in 1960, soon after the Rome Olympics, so option (2) is correct. About 400 athletes from 23 countries took part. The Paralympic movement grew out of the Stoke Mandeville Games, organised in England in 1948 by Dr. Ludwig Guttmann for Second World War veterans with spinal cord injuries. Tokyo hosted the second Summer Paralympics in 1964, Toronto hosted the Games in 1976 and Atlanta in 1996, so the other options mix up host cities and years. India first took part in 1968, and swimmer Murlikant Petkar won India's first Paralympic gold medal at Heidelberg in 1972. Karnataka connection: Karnataka's high jumper H.N. Girisha won a silver medal at the London 2012 Paralympics. At Paris 2024, India achieved its best-ever tally of 29 medals. The Paralympic Games are governed by the International Paralympic Committee (IPC), founded in 1989.

Q 99 | eco | Schemes & Development
Which one of the following pair/s of Human Development Index Indicators is/are correct?
(a) Health, Yoga and Happiness
(b) Health, Reading and Writing
(c) Health, Education and Standard of Living
(d) Health, Communication and Writing
Choose the correct option.
(1) (a) only
(2) (b) and (d) only
(3) (c) only
(4) (b) only
= 3
~ The Human Development Index (HDI), published by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) in its Human Development Report since 1990, measures three basic dimensions: a long and healthy life (health, measured by life expectancy at birth), knowledge (education, measured by mean years of schooling and expected years of schooling) and a decent standard of living (measured by Gross National Income per capita at purchasing power parity). The HDI was developed by the Pakistani economist Mahbub ul Haq, with the Indian economist Amartya Sen contributing to its concept, and its value ranges from 0 to 1. Karnataka has also prepared its own state and district Human Development Reports to track progress across its districts.
