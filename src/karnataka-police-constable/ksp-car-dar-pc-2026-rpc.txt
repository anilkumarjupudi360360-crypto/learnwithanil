Q 1 | apt | Reasoning
The calendar for the year 2026 will be the same as that of the year :
(1) 2030
(2) 2037
(3) 2032
(4) 2029
= 2
~ 2026 is an ordinary year (365 days = 52 weeks + 1 odd day) and it is the second year after the leap year 2024. A calendar repeats only when the odd days in between add up to a multiple of 7 and the new year is of the same type (ordinary or leap). Counting odd days from 2026: 2026 (1), 2027 (1), 2028 (2, leap), 2029 (1), 2030 (1), 2031 (1), 2032 (2, leap), 2033 (1), 2034 (1), 2035 (1), 2036 (2, leap) = 14 odd days, exactly 2 weeks. So 1 January 2037 falls on the same weekday as 1 January 2026 (Thursday), and 2037 is also an ordinary year, so the two calendars are identical: option 2. Before 2029 there are only 4 odd days and before 2030 only 5, so those years begin on different weekdays. 2032 does begin on a Thursday (7 odd days), but it is a leap year of 366 days, so its calendar differs from March onwards. Memory tip: year just after a leap year, add 6 (2025 to 2031); second or third year after a leap year, add 11; a leap year, add 28.

Q 2 | apt | Arithmetic
If a car goes 25 metres in a second, then its speed in km/hr is
(1) 72
(2) 80
(3) 60
(4) 90
= 4
~ To convert metres per second into kilometres per hour, multiply by 18/5. This is because 1 m/s = (1/1000 km) ÷ (1/3600 hr) = 3600/1000 = 18/5 km/hr. So 25 m/s = 25 × 18/5 = 5 × 18 = 90 km/hr, option 4. Check it another way: in one hour (3600 seconds) the car covers 25 × 3600 = 90,000 metres, which is 90 km, so its speed is 90 km/hr. Option 1 (72 km/hr) is the speed for 20 m/s (20 × 18/5 = 72). Option 2 (80 km/hr) corresponds to about 22.2 m/s and option 3 (60 km/hr) to about 16.7 m/s, so neither fits 25 m/s. Exam tip: remember both conversions, km/hr to m/s multiply by 5/18, and m/s to km/hr multiply by 18/5. Handy pairs to memorise: 5 m/s = 18 km/hr, 10 m/s = 36 km/hr, 15 m/s = 54 km/hr, 20 m/s = 72 km/hr and 25 m/s = 90 km/hr. Such unit conversions appear regularly in Karnataka Police constable and PSI papers under speed, time and distance.

Q 3 | apt | Series & Puzzles
Find the missing number of the following series :
25, 26, ____, 77, 128, 205
(1) 51
(2) 179
(3) 15
(4) 1
= 1
~ Rule: from the third term onwards, every term is the sum of the two terms just before it, as in the Fibonacci series. So the missing term is 25 + 26 = 51. Check the rest of the series: 26 + 51 = 77, 51 + 77 = 128 and 77 + 128 = 205. All the given terms fit only when the blank is 51, so option 1 is correct. Another way to see the same pattern: the differences between consecutive terms are 1, 25, 26, 51 and 77, which are the terms of the series itself. Option 2 (179) is far too large, because in this increasing series the missing term must lie between 26 and 77. Options 3 (15) and 4 (1) would make the series fall and then jump, and neither gives 77 when added to 26 (26 + 15 = 41 and 26 + 1 = 27). Exam tip: when a series grows slowly at first and then quickly, test the 'add the previous two terms' rule before trying squares, cubes or multiplication; it cracks many KEA series questions in seconds.

Q 4 | apt | Arithmetic
To complete a piece of work, A takes 50% more time than B. If together they take 18 days to complete the work, how much time shall B take to do it ?
(1) 30 days
(2) 45 days
(3) 40 days
(4) 35 days
= 1
~ Let B take x days to finish the work alone. A takes 50% more time, so A needs 1.5x days. In one day B does 1/x of the work and A does 1/(1.5x) = 2/(3x). Working together, in one day they do 1/x + 2/(3x) = 5/(3x) of the work. Since together they finish in 18 days, 5/(3x) = 1/18, so 3x = 90 and x = 30. Hence B takes 30 days (option 1) and A takes 45 days. Check: 1/30 + 1/45 = 3/90 + 2/90 = 5/90 = 1/18, which is correct. Option 2 (45 days) is A's time, not B's, and is the usual trap in this question. Options 3 (40 days) and 4 (35 days) do not satisfy the equation; for example, if B took 40 days, A would take 60 days and together they would need 24 days, not 18. Shortcut: if A and B alone take a and b days, together they take ab/(a + b) days. Here a = 1.5b, so ab/(a + b) = 1.5b²/2.5b = 0.6b = 18, giving b = 30 days.

Q 5 | apt | Arithmetic
If sum of two numbers is 30 and difference of two numbers is 16, then find the multiplication of these two numbers.
(1) 161
(2) 132
(3) 112
(4) 171
= 1
~ Let the two numbers be x and y, with x + y = 30 and x − y = 16. Adding the two equations gives 2x = 46, so x = 23. Then y = 30 − 23 = 7. Their product (multiplication) is 23 × 7 = 161, which is option 1. Check: 23 + 7 = 30 and 23 − 7 = 16, so both conditions are satisfied. A quick formula gives the product without finding the numbers: xy = [(x + y)² − (x − y)²] ÷ 4 = (900 − 256) ÷ 4 = 644 ÷ 4 = 161. Only one pair of numbers (23 and 7) has a sum of 30 and a difference of 16, so options 2 (132), 3 (112) and 4 (171) cannot be correct; for instance, 171 = 19 × 9, but 19 + 9 is only 28. Exam tip: the identity (a + b)² − (a − b)² = 4ab is very useful for sum, difference and product questions, which are common in the arithmetic section of Karnataka Police constable papers. Always verify by substituting the numbers back.

Q 6 | apt | Arithmetic
Match the following :
List-I (Number) / List-II (Type)
(a) 144 – (i) Non-perfect square
(b) 257 – (ii) Rational
(c) 1/4 – (iii) Irrational
(d) √2 – (iv) Perfect square
(1) a-iii, b-i, c-ii, d-iv
(2) a-ii, b-iii, c-i, d-iv
(3) a-iv, b-i, c-ii, d-iii
(4) a-i, b-iii, c-iv, d-ii
= 3
~ 144 = 12 × 12, so it is a perfect square (a-iv). 257 lies between 16² = 256 and 17² = 289, so it is not the square of any whole number; it is a non-perfect square (b-i). 1/4 is the ratio of two integers with a non-zero denominator, so it is a rational number (c-ii); it also equals the terminating decimal 0.25. √2 cannot be written in the form p/q, and its decimal 1.41421... neither ends nor repeats, so it is irrational (d-iii). The correct code is a-iv, b-i, c-ii, d-iii, which is option 3. Option 1 calls 144 irrational and √2 a perfect square, both wrong. Option 2 also calls √2 a perfect square and 257 irrational. Option 4 calls √2 rational, which is false. Extra fact: 257 is also a prime number (2⁸ + 1, a Fermat prime). Remember for the exam: the square root of any natural number that is not a perfect square is irrational, for example √2, √3 and √5, while every fraction of integers and every terminating or repeating decimal is rational.

Q 7 | apt | Arithmetic
Find the HCF of the numbers 65 and 117.
(1) 14
(2) 13
(3) 16
(4) 12
= 2
~ Prime factorisation: 65 = 5 × 13 and 117 = 3 × 3 × 13. The only common prime factor is 13, so the HCF (Highest Common Factor) of 65 and 117 is 13, option 2. The long-division (Euclid's) method gives the same result: 117 ÷ 65 = 1, remainder 52; 65 ÷ 52 = 1, remainder 13; 52 ÷ 13 = 4, remainder 0. The last divisor, 13, is the HCF. Option 1 (14) is even and cannot divide the odd number 65. Option 3 (16) divides neither number. Option 4 (12) also fails to divide 65; note that 117 = 9 × 13 is divisible by 3, but 65 is not, so no multiple of 3 can be their HCF. Useful fact: HCF × LCM = product of the two numbers, so the LCM of 65 and 117 is (65 × 117) ÷ 13 = 585. Quick check for the exam: the HCF must also divide the difference of the two numbers, 117 − 65 = 52 = 4 × 13, so trying the factors of 52 first leads straight to 13.

Q 8 | apt | Reasoning
If Z = 52 and BAT = 46. Then 'ACT' will be equal to :
(1) 44
(2) 47
(3) 48
(4) 41
= 3
~ Z is the 26th letter of the English alphabet and 26 × 2 = 52. For BAT, the letter positions are B = 2, A = 1 and T = 20; their sum is 23, and 23 × 2 = 46. So the code of a word = 2 × (sum of the alphabet positions of its letters). For ACT: A = 1, C = 3 and T = 20, so the sum is 24 and the code is 24 × 2 = 48, which is option 3. Option 1 (44) would need a letter sum of 22, which ACT does not have. Options 2 (47) and 4 (41) are odd numbers, and doubling a whole number can never give an odd result, so they break the rule. Shortcut: BAT and ACT share the letters A and T and differ only in B (2) and C (3). C is one more than B, so the code of ACT is 2 × 1 = 2 more than that of BAT: 46 + 2 = 48. Tip: remember EJOTY (E = 5, J = 10, O = 15, T = 20, Y = 25) to find letter positions quickly in coding–decoding questions.

Q 9 | apt | Arithmetic
In how many ways can we paint the six faces of a cube with six different colours ?
(1) 60
(2) 180
(3) 90
(4) 30
= 4
~ If the cube were fixed in one position, six different colours could be arranged on its six faces in 6! = 720 ways. But a cube can be turned, and colourings that look identical after a rotation must be counted only once. A cube has 24 rotational positions: any of its 6 faces can be placed on top, and then it can be turned into 4 positions about the vertical axis (6 × 4 = 24). So the number of distinct ways = 720 ÷ 24 = 30, which is option 4. Another method: fix colour 1 on the top face. The bottom face can take any of the remaining 5 colours (5 ways). The other 4 colours go around the four side faces like a ring, giving (4 − 1)! = 3! = 6 circular arrangements. Total = 5 × 6 = 30. Options 1 (60), 2 (180) and 3 (90) are what you get by dividing 720 by 12, 4 or 8, that is, by not accounting for all 24 rotations. Tip: n different objects can be arranged in a circle in (n − 1)! ways; the cube problem combines this with the choice of the opposite face.

Q 10 | apt | Series & Puzzles
Find the missing number :
6 | 9 | 10
4 | 8 | 5
16 | 56 | ?
(1) 72
(2) 45
(3) 54
(4) 40
= 4
~ Work column by column. In each column, the bottom number = (top number − 2) × middle number. Column 1: (6 − 2) × 4 = 4 × 4 = 16. Column 2: (9 − 2) × 8 = 7 × 8 = 56. The rule fits both columns, so for column 3: (10 − 2) × 5 = 8 × 5 = 40, which is option 4. The same rule can be written as bottom = top × middle − 2 × middle: 24 − 8 = 16, 72 − 16 = 56 and 50 − 10 = 40. Option 1 (72) is simply 9 × 8, a product taken from column 2, not column 3. Option 2 (45) would need the rule (top − 1) × middle = 9 × 5, but that rule gives 20 and 64 in the first two columns instead of 16 and 56. Option 3 (54) cannot be produced from 10 and 5 by any simple rule that also works for the other two columns. Exam tip: in number-grid puzzles, always confirm your rule on at least two complete rows or columns before applying it to the one with the question mark.

Q 11 | apt | Arithmetic
Find the value of x in the following equation :
4½ + 3⅙ + x + 2⅓ = 13⅖
(1) 3⅖
(2) 1¾
(3) 7¾
(4) 5½
= 1
~ Add the whole-number parts and the fraction parts separately. Whole numbers: 4 + 3 + 2 = 9. Fractions: 1/2 + 1/6 + 1/3; using the LCM 6 as the common denominator, this becomes 3/6 + 1/6 + 2/6 = 6/6 = 1. So 4½ + 3⅙ + 2⅓ = 9 + 1 = 10, and the equation reduces to 10 + x = 13⅖. Therefore x = 13⅖ − 10 = 3⅖, which is option 1. Check: 10 + 3⅖ = 13⅖. With option 2 (1¾) the left side would be only 11¾, with option 3 (7¾) it would be 17¾, and with option 4 (5½) it would be 15½; none of these equals 13⅖. Notice also that the known fractions add up to a whole number while the right side ends in 2/5, so the fraction part of x must be 2/5; this alone points to option 1 without full calculation. Exam tip: in sums of mixed fractions, adding whole numbers and fractions separately saves time and avoids converting everything into improper fractions, which is where most calculation errors happen.

Directions : (Questions 12 and 13)
In the figure given below, there are three intersecting circles, each representing certain section of people. Different regions are marked a-g. Read the following questions (12 and 13) and choose the correct answer.
Painter who is neither a Singer nor a Dancer :
(1) c
(2) g
(3) b
(4) f
= 4

Directions : (Questions 12 and 13)
In the figure given below, there are three intersecting circles, each representing certain section of people. Different regions are marked a-g. Read the following questions (12 and 13) and choose the correct answer.
Singer who is a Painter but not a Dancer :
(1) d
(2) b
(3) g
(4) c
= 2
~ Circle A stands for Singers, circle B for Painters and circle C for Dancers. A person who is both a singer and a painter must lie in the overlap of circles A and B, which is made up of two regions, b and c. Region c also lies inside the Dancer circle, since it is common to all three circles, so it must be left out. That leaves region b: singers who are painters but not dancers. Option 2 is correct. Option 1 (d) is the overlap of the Singer and Dancer circles outside the Painter circle, so it represents singers who are dancers but not painters. Option 3 (g) is the part of the Dancer circle alone, that is, people who are only dancers. Option 4 (c) is the central region, people who are singers, painters and dancers, so it fails the 'not a Dancer' condition. Likewise, region e here means painters who are dancers but not singers.

Q 14 | sci | Science & Technology
DTH (Direct To Home) receives signals from :
(1) Navigation Satellite
(2) Geostationary Satellite
(3) Astronomical Satellite
(4) Polar Satellite
= 2
~ DTH (Direct To Home) television is beamed to homes by communication satellites placed in the geostationary orbit, about 35,786 km above the equator. A geostationary satellite completes one orbit in about 24 hours, the same time the Earth takes to rotate once, and it moves in the same direction, so it appears fixed at one point in the sky. That is why the small DTH dish on a rooftop can be aligned once and then left fixed. Option 2 is correct. Navigation satellites (option 1), such as India's NavIC (IRNSS) or the American GPS, send position and timing signals, not TV channels. Astronomical satellites (option 3), such as ISRO's AstroSat, observe stars and galaxies. Polar satellites (option 4) orbit a few hundred kilometres up, passing over the poles, and cross any given place only at intervals; they are used for remote sensing, weather and mapping (for example, Cartosat), and a fixed dish could not follow them. Exam fact: ISRO's GSAT series are communication satellites placed in the geostationary orbit, which is also called the Clarke orbit after the writer Arthur C. Clarke, who popularised the idea of using it for communication satellites.

Q 15 | sci | Physics
Match the items in List-I with the items in List-II and choose the correct answer.
List-I / List-II
(a) Uranium 235 – (i) Moderator
(b) Graphite – (ii) Control rod
(c) Cadmium – (iii) Transuranic element
(d) Plutonium – (iv) Fuel
(1) a-iii, b-iv, c-i, d-ii
(2) a-iii, b-iv, c-ii, d-i
(3) a-iv, b-i, c-ii, d-iii
(4) a-ii, b-iii, c-iv, d-i
= 3
~ In a nuclear reactor, Uranium-235 is the fissile material that splits on absorbing a slow neutron and releases energy, so it is the fuel (a-iv). Graphite slows down fast neutrons so that they can cause further fission, so it works as a moderator (b-i); heavy water and ordinary water are other moderators. Cadmium absorbs neutrons strongly, so rods of cadmium (or boron) are moved into and out of the core to control the speed of the chain reaction; these are control rods (c-ii). Plutonium has atomic number 94, higher than uranium's 92, so it is a transuranic element (d-iii); Pu-239 is produced in reactors from U-238 and can itself serve as fuel. The correct code is a-iv, b-i, c-ii, d-iii, which is option 3. Options 1 and 2 wrongly call uranium a transuranic element and graphite a fuel, while option 4 calls uranium a control rod and graphite a transuranic element. Karnataka link: the Kaiga Atomic Power Station in Uttara Kannada district runs pressurised heavy water reactors, in which heavy water is the moderator and natural uranium is the fuel.

Q 16 | sci | Science & Technology
The first Asian country to send space mission (Mars Orbiter Mission) to the planet Mars as a part of its interplanetary project is :
(1) India
(2) Singapore
(3) Japan
(4) China
= 1
~ India's Mars Orbiter Mission (MOM), popularly called Mangalyaan, was launched by ISRO on 5 November 2013 on the PSLV-C25 rocket from Sriharikota and entered orbit around Mars on 24 September 2014. With this, India became the first Asian country to reach Mars orbit and the first country in the world to succeed in its very first attempt; ISRO became the fourth space agency to reach Mars, after the Soviet space programme, NASA and the European Space Agency. The mission cost only about ₹450 crore, making it one of the cheapest Mars missions ever. Option 1 is correct. Japan (option 3) launched Nozomi in 1998, but it failed to enter Mars orbit. China's (option 4) first probe, Yinghuo-1, was lost in 2011 when the Russian Phobos-Grunt mission carrying it failed; China succeeded later with Tianwen-1 in 2021. Singapore (option 2) has never sent a Mars mission. Extra fact: the spacecraft, planned to work for six months, kept working for about eight years before contact was lost in 2022.

Q 17 | sci | Science & Technology
KAMINI is :
(1) A plutonium based fast breeder reactor.
(2) A 3D medium range surveillance radar system.
(3) A mobile surveillance radar system for low level target detection.
(4) A thorium based thermal breeder reactor.
= 4
~ KAMINI (KAlpakkam MINI reactor) is a small 30 kW research reactor at the Indira Gandhi Centre for Atomic Research (IGCAR), Kalpakkam, Tamil Nadu, which attained criticality in 1996. It is fuelled by uranium-233 (U-233), a fissile material that is produced (bred) from thorium-232. Because it works with slow (thermal) neutrons and is part of India's thorium fuel programme, KEA's answer describes it as a thorium-based thermal breeder reactor, option 4. Strictly speaking, KAMINI is a research reactor used for neutron radiography and activation analysis rather than a power-producing breeder, but option 4 is the only description that belongs to it. Option 1 describes the plutonium-based Fast Breeder Test Reactor (FBTR) and the Prototype Fast Breeder Reactor (PFBR), also at Kalpakkam. Options 2 and 3 describe radar systems; for example, Rohini, developed by DRDO's Bengaluru laboratory LRDE, is a 3D medium-range surveillance radar. Exam fact: the third stage of India's three-stage nuclear power programme, planned by Homi J. Bhabha, aims to use the country's huge thorium reserves.

Q 18 | sci | Science & Technology
Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) is used to obtain images of internal organs by using
(1) Electric signals inside human body
(2) Magnetic signals inside human body
(3) LASER
(4) X-Ray
= 2
~ MRI uses a powerful magnet and radio waves. The body is rich in water and fat, and the hydrogen nuclei (protons) in them behave like tiny magnets. In the strong magnetic field of the scanner they line up; a pulse of radio waves tips them out of line, and as they relax back they give out weak signals. These magnetic resonance signals coming from inside the body are picked up and turned by a computer into detailed images, so option 2, 'magnetic signals inside human body', is correct. Option 1 is wrong: the body's electrical signals are recorded in ECG (heart) and EEG (brain), which produce graphs, not pictures of organs. Option 3, LASER, is used in eye surgery and some treatments, not for MRI. Option 4, X-rays, are used in radiography and CT scans; MRI uses no ionising radiation at all, which is its big advantage. Exam fact: MRI is best for soft tissues such as the brain, spinal cord, muscles and ligaments, and patients must remove all metal objects before a scan because of the strong magnet.

Q 19 | sci | Physics
Earth's magnetism is due to :
(1) Doppler Effect
(2) Magnus Effect
(3) Solar Effect
(4) Dynamo Effect
= 4
~ The Earth's magnetic field is explained by the geodynamo, or dynamo effect. The outer core, roughly 2,900 to 5,150 km below the surface, is made of molten iron and nickel, which conduct electricity. Heat escaping from the inner core sets up convection currents in this liquid metal, and the Earth's rotation (through the Coriolis force) twists them into spirals. A moving conducting fluid produces electric currents, and these currents generate a magnetic field, just as a dynamo converts motion into electricity. So option 4 is correct. Option 1, the Doppler effect, is the change in the observed frequency of sound or light when the source and observer move relative to each other; it is used in speed-checking radar guns. Option 2, the Magnus effect, is the sideways force on a spinning ball moving through air, which makes a spinning football curve. Option 3, 'solar effect', is not a recognised cause; the solar wind only distorts the magnetosphere and produces auroras near the poles. Exam fact: the Earth's magnetic poles slowly wander, and the field has reversed its direction many times in geological history.

Q 20 | geo | Environment & Ecology
The device commonly used in thermal power plants and industries to remove particulate matter from exhaust gases :
(1) Catalytic Converter
(2) Electrostatic Precipitator
(3) Exhaust Fans
(4) Absorption Towers
= 2
~ An electrostatic precipitator (ESP) removes fine particles such as fly ash and dust from the flue gases of thermal power plants, cement factories and steel plants. The gas passes between discharge electrodes kept at a very high negative voltage and earthed collecting plates. The particles pick up an electric charge, are attracted to the oppositely charged plates and stick to them; the plates are rapped from time to time so that the collected dust falls into hoppers below. ESPs can remove more than 99% of particulate matter, so option 2 is correct. Option 1, a catalytic converter, is fitted in vehicle exhausts to convert harmful gases such as carbon monoxide, hydrocarbons and nitrogen oxides into less harmful ones; it does not remove particles. Option 3, exhaust fans, simply push air out of a space. Option 4, absorption towers (scrubbers), mainly remove gases such as sulphur dioxide by dissolving them in a liquid. Karnataka link: like all large coal-based plants, the Raichur Thermal Power Station uses ESPs to control fly-ash emissions, and the collected fly ash is used for making cement and bricks.

Expand T.B.G.R.
(1) Temperate Biotechnology Garden and Research
(2) Tropical Botanical Genetics and Research
(3) Tropical Botanical Genera and Research
(4) Tropical Botanical Garden and Research

The abbreviation comes from the Tropical Botanic Garden and Research Institute (TBGRI) at Palode near Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala, now named the Jawaharlal Nehru Tropical Botanic Garden and Research Institute (JNTBGRI). Q 22 | pol | Laws, Police & Society
Identify the core pillars of “Probity in Governance”.
(a) Authority
(b) Integrity
(c) Transparency
(d) Speed
(e) Accountability
(1) (b), (c) and (e)
(2) (a), (b), (c) and (e)
(3) (a), (b) and (c)
(4) (b), (d) and (e)
= 1
~ Probity in governance means honesty, uprightness and strict adherence to ethical principles in public life. Its core pillars are integrity (b), meaning consistency between values and actions and never using public office for private gain; transparency (c), meaning openness in decision-making and free access to information, as promoted by the Right to Information Act, 2005; and accountability (e), meaning that public servants must answer for their decisions and results to citizens, legislatures and courts. So option 1, (b), (c) and (e), is correct. Authority (a) is the legal power to act; probity is about how that power is used, so authority itself is not a pillar of probity. Speed (d) belongs to efficiency, not probity, since a quick decision can still be dishonest. Hence options 2, 3 and 4 are wrong. Exam fact: the Second Administrative Reforms Commission's report 'Ethics in Governance' (2007) and the Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013 aim to strengthen probity; in Karnataka, the Karnataka Lokayukta, created under the Karnataka Lokayukta Act, 1984, investigates corruption by public servants.

Q 23 | sci | Physics
Consider the following statements and choose the correct answer from the options given below :
Assertion (A) : Electrical appliances with metal body have three electrical connections but an electrical bulb has two electrical connections.
Reason (R) : Three pin connections reduce the chance of electric shock.
(1) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).
(2) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
(3) Assertion (A) is true, but Reason (R) is false.
(4) Assertion (A) is false, but the Reason (R) is true.
= 1
~ Both statements are true. Appliances with a metal body, such as an electric iron, refrigerator or washing machine, are connected through a three-pin plug to live, neutral and earth wires, while a bulb needs only live and neutral, so the Assertion is true. Three-pin connections do reduce the chance of electric shock, because the earth wire carries leakage current from a faulty appliance safely to the ground, so the Reason is also true. KEA's key, however, holds that the Reason is not the correct explanation of the Assertion, making option 1 the answer. The Assertion is about the contrast between a metal-bodied appliance and a bulb; the direct cause is that a metal body is a conductor that the user handles and that can become live if the insulation fails, so it must be earthed, whereas a bulb has no such exposed metal body. The Reason states only a general benefit of three-pin connections and does not explain why a bulb needs just two. Options 3 and 4 are wrong because both statements are true. Many would argue that the Reason does explain the Assertion and choose option 2. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 24 | pol | Laws, Police & Society

= 4
~ A halocline (from Greek 'hals' meaning salt and 'klinein' meaning to slope) is a layer in a water body in which salinity changes sharply with depth, so option (4) is correct. It separates the less salty surface water from the saltier, denser water below, and it is prominent where rivers, rainfall or melting ice add fresh water at the surface, as in the Arctic Ocean, the Baltic Sea and the Bay of Bengal. Because salinity affects density, a halocline often coincides with a pycnocline and resists vertical mixing. Option (3) describes an isohaline, the line joining places of equal salinity on a map. Option (1), a line of zero salinity, is not a standard term, since open seawater always contains dissolved salts, and option (2) is not a salinity concept at all (lines of equal ocean depth are called isobaths). So options (1), (2) and (3) are wrong. Memory tip: thermocline = rapid change of temperature with depth, halocline = rapid change of salinity, pycnocline = rapid change of density. The average salinity of the oceans is about 35 parts per thousand.

Q 75 | his | Ancient India
To which one of the following ages ‘microliths’ are associated ?
(1) Mesolithic Age
(2) Bronze Age
(3) Neolithic Age
(4) Palaeolithic Age
= 1
~ Microliths are very small stone tools, usually 1 to 5 cm long, made of stones like chert, chalcedony and jasper and shaped as tiny blades, points, crescents, triangles and trapezes. They were fixed on wooden or bone handles to make arrows, spears and sickles. They are the typical tools of the Mesolithic or Middle Stone Age (roughly 10,000 to 4,000 BCE in India), when people still lived mainly by hunting, fishing and food gathering but had begun to domesticate animals. So option (1) is correct. The Palaeolithic Age is known for larger tools such as hand-axes, cleavers and choppers; the Neolithic Age for polished stone tools (celts) and the beginning of farming; and the Bronze Age for metal tools, as in the Harappan civilisation. Microliths did continue into Neolithic and Chalcolithic times, but they are the defining feature of the Mesolithic. Important Mesolithic sites: Bagor (Rajasthan), Adamgarh and Bhimbetka (Madhya Pradesh), Langhnaj (Gujarat) and Sarai Nahar Rai (Uttar Pradesh). In Karnataka, microlithic tools have been reported from sites such as Sanganakallu in Ballari district.

That is why the Ministry of Women and Child Development now links nutrition with early childhood care and education at anganwadis, for example through its 'Poshan Bhi Padhai Bhi' initiative. Five, seven and three years are wrong figures; 'three' may be confused with the separate emphasis on the first 1,000 days, from pregnancy to the child's second birthday. Campaign tip: September is observed every year as Rashtriya Poshan Maah, and a Poshan Pakhwada (nutrition fortnight) is held around March–April.

Q 89 | eco | Indian Economy

(2) TRIMs
(3) TRIPs
(4) GATT
= 3
~ Geographical Indications (GIs) are a form of intellectual property, and intellectual property is covered by the WTO's Agreement on Trade-Related Aspects of Intellectual Property Rights (TRIPS), which came into force with the WTO on 1 January 1995. Q 90 | his | Art & Culture

Q 91 | sci | Chemistry
__________ is a mixture of about 200 substances.
(1) Coal Tar
(2) Activated Charcoal
(3) Coal Gas
(4) Coke
= 1
~ When coal is processed in industry by heating it in the absence of air, it gives three useful products: coke, coal tar and coal gas. Coal tar is a thick, black liquid with an unpleasant smell, and the NCERT Class 8 science textbook describes it as a mixture of about 200 substances. Products obtained from coal tar are used to make synthetic dyes, drugs, explosives, perfumes, plastics, paints, photographic materials and the naphthalene balls used to repel moths. So option 1 is correct. Coke is a tough, porous, black solid that is an almost pure form of carbon, used in steel making and in extracting metals. Coal gas is a fuel gas, first used for street lighting in London in 1810 and in New York around 1820. Activated charcoal is a porous form of carbon used as an adsorbent in water filters and gas masks; it is not described as a mixture of 200 substances. Note: coal tar was once used to surface roads, but it has largely been replaced by bitumen, a petroleum product.

Q 92 | geo | Environment & Ecology
Which is the correct sequence of the following according to the flow of energy in an ecosystem ?
(a) Carnivores
(b) Herbivores
(c) Sunlight
(d) Producers
(1) (d), (b), (a), (c)
(2) (d), (c), (b), (a)
(3) (c), (d), (b), (a)
(4) (a), (b), (c), (d)
= 3
~ The Sun is the ultimate source of energy for almost every ecosystem. Producers (green plants, algae and phytoplankton) trap sunlight by photosynthesis and store it as chemical energy in food. Herbivores (primary consumers) eat the producers, and carnivores (secondary and higher consumers) eat the herbivores. So the flow is sunlight → producers → herbivores → carnivores, that is (c), (d), (b), (a), option 3. Option 1 puts sunlight at the end, option 2 puts producers before sunlight, and option 4 reverses the whole chain, so all are wrong. Key facts: energy flow in an ecosystem is unidirectional and does not cycle back to the Sun, and according to Lindeman's 10 percent law only about 10 percent of the energy at one trophic level passes to the next, the rest being lost mostly as heat. That is why food chains rarely have more than four or five links and why the pyramid of energy is always upright. Decomposers work at every level, but it is nutrients, not energy, that get recycled.

Q 93 | sci | Chemistry
DDT, an organic insecticide mainly contains
(1) Oxygen
(2) Sulphur
(3) Chlorine
(4) Nitrogen
= 3
~ DDT stands for dichloro-diphenyl-trichloroethane. Its formula is C14H9Cl5, so each molecule has 14 carbon atoms, 9 hydrogen atoms and 5 chlorine atoms; it contains no oxygen, sulphur or nitrogen. Because chlorine is its characteristic element, DDT belongs to the organochlorine group of insecticides, and option 3 is correct. Swiss chemist Paul Müller won the 1948 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for discovering its insecticidal action, and it was widely used against malaria mosquitoes. However, DDT is non-biodegradable and fat-soluble, so it accumulates in living tissues and its concentration rises at each step of a food chain (biomagnification). In birds it causes thin eggshells, a problem highlighted in Rachel Carson's book Silent Spring (1962). It is listed under the Stockholm Convention on persistent organic pollutants; India has banned its agricultural use but allows restricted use for vector control. Endosulfan, another organochlorine, was banned after the tragedy of affected villages in Dakshina Kannada and Kasaragod.

Q 94 | sci | Chemistry
Synthetic indicator among the following is
(1) Litmus
(2) Red Cabbage Leaves
(3) Methyl Orange
(4) Turmeric
= 3
~ Indicators are substances that show whether a solution is acidic or basic by changing colour (or smell). Methyl orange is a synthetic, man-made indicator: it is red in acidic solution and yellow in basic solution, and it is commonly used in acid–base titrations. So option 3 is correct. The other three are natural indicators. Litmus is extracted from lichens; blue litmus turns red in acid and red litmus turns blue in base. Red cabbage leaves contain anthocyanin pigments that change colour across the pH range. Turmeric is yellow but turns reddish-brown in a basic solution, which is why a turmeric stain on clothes turns red when washed with soap. Phenolphthalein is another synthetic indicator, colourless in acid and pink in base. Olfactory indicators such as onion, vanilla essence and clove oil change smell instead of colour. Easy memory tip: if it comes from a plant or a lichen, it is natural; if it carries a chemical name such as methyl orange or phenolphthalein, it is synthetic.

(b) Syphilis
(c) Genital herpes
(d) HIV / AIDS
(1) (b) and (c)
(2) Only (b)
(3) (a), (b) and (c)
(4) (a) and (b)

~ The NCERT Class 12 Biology chapter on reproductive health states that, except for hepatitis-B, genital herpes and HIV infections, other STIs are completely curable if detected early and treated properly. Q 96 | sci | Science & Technology
Bt. Cotton plant is resistant to which of the following ?
(1) Insects
(2) Viruses
(3) Fungus
(4) Bacteria
= 1
~ Bt cotton is a genetically modified cotton that carries cry genes taken from the soil bacterium Bacillus thuringiensis (hence 'Bt'). These genes make the plant produce Cry proteins, which exist as an inactive protoxin; inside the alkaline gut of a feeding insect larva they become active and damage the gut lining, killing the pest. Bt cotton is therefore resistant to insects, especially lepidopteran pests such as the cotton bollworm; genes such as cry1Ac and cry2Ab are used. So option 1 is correct. It gives no special protection against viruses, fungi or bacteria; the bacterium is only the source of the gene, not something the plant resists, and the bacterium itself is unharmed because the toxin stays inactive inside it. In India, Bt cotton was approved for commercial cultivation in 2002 by the GEAC (today called the Genetic Engineering Appraisal Committee), and it is the main GM crop grown commercially in the country. A later problem has been pink bollworm developing resistance. Cotton is an important crop in north Karnataka districts such as Raichur and Haveri.

Q 98 | sci | Biology

Q 99 | geo | Environment & Ecology
Sleeplessness, increased heartbeat and altered breathing pattern are the effects of ________ pollution.
(1) Noise
(2) Soil
(3) Air
(4) Water
= 1
~ Noise pollution is unwanted or excessive sound. Apart from damaging hearing, prolonged exposure to loud noise acts as a stress factor: it causes sleeplessness (insomnia), increased heartbeat, an altered breathing pattern, high blood pressure, headache, irritability and loss of concentration. The NCERT Class 12 Biology chapter on environmental issues (older editions) lists exactly these effects of noise. So option 1 is correct. Q 100 | sci | Chemistry
Which of the following reacts with acid and forms salt and water ?
(1) SO₂
(2) CO
(3) CaO
(4) CO₂
= 3
~ A substance that reacts with an acid to give salt and water behaves as a base, and metal oxides are basic in nature. Calcium oxide (CaO, quicklime) is a basic oxide: CaO + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O, giving the salt calcium chloride and water. So option 3 is correct. Sulphur dioxide (SO₂) and carbon dioxide (CO₂) are non-metallic oxides that are acidic; they react with bases, not acids, to form salt and water, for example CO₂ + Ca(OH)₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O, the reaction that turns lime water milky. Carbon monoxide (CO) is a neutral oxide that does not form salts with either acids or bases. Memory rule: metal oxides are basic, non-metal oxides are acidic, a few such as CO, NO and N₂O are neutral, and aluminium oxide and zinc oxide are amphoteric, reacting with both acids and bases. CaO reacts vigorously with water to form slaked lime, Ca(OH)₂, which is used for whitewashing walls, and quicklime is also used in making cement and in reducing soil acidity.
