Q 1 | rea | Verbal Reasoning
Select the correct option that indicates the arrangement of the given words in the order in which they appear in a telephone directory :
(I) Krishanmurty
(II) Krishnamurthy
(III) Krishnmurthi
(IV) Krishanmurthy
(V) Krishnamurti
(1) (IV), (I), (II), (V), (III)
(2) (IV), (I), (II), (III), (V)
(3) (IV), (V), (II), (III), (I)

Q 2 | rea | Verbal Reasoning
In a certain code language, ‘VIRTUE’ is coded as ‘201’ and ‘TRAGEDY’ is coded as ‘218’. How will ‘PROFANE’ be coded in that language ?
(1) 570
(2) 342
(3) 432
(4) 456
= 2
~ Code = (sum of reverse alphabet positions, A = 26 … Z = 1) × (number of vowels). VIRTUE: 5+18+9+7+6+22 = 67, × 3 vowels = 201. TRAGEDY: 7+9+26+20+22+23+2 = 109, × 2 = 218. PROFANE: 11+9+12+21+26+13+22 = 114, × 3 vowels (O, A, E) = 342.

Q 3 | rea | Non-verbal Reasoning
Directions (Q. Nos. 3 to 5) :
One hundred and twenty five cubes of the same size are arranged in the form of a cube on a table. Then a column of five cubes is removed from each of the four corners. All the exposed faces of the rest of the solid (except the face touching the table) are coloured red. Now, answer these questions based on the above statement :
How many small cubes are there in the solid after the removal of the columns ?
(1) 120
(2) 110
(3) 105
(4) 100
= 3
~ The 125 cubes form a 5 × 5 × 5 cube. Four corner columns of 5 cubes each are removed: 4 × 5 = 20. Cubes left = 125 − 20 = 105.

Q 4 | rea | Non-verbal Reasoning
Directions (Q. Nos. 3 to 5) :
One hundred and twenty five cubes of the same size are arranged in the form of a cube on a table. Then a column of five cubes is removed from each of the four corners. All the exposed faces of the rest of the solid (except the face touching the table) are coloured red. Now, answer these questions based on the above statement :
How many cubes do not have any coloured face ?
(1) 12
(2) 24
(3) 36
(4) 48
= 3
~ A cube is unpainted only if it is not on the top layer and not on any outer side. In each of the lower 4 layers the inner 3 × 3 block (9 cubes) stays hidden — removing the corner columns exposes only edge cubes. So 9 × 4 = 36.

Q 5 | rea | Non-verbal Reasoning
Directions (Q. Nos. 3 to 5) :
One hundred and twenty five cubes of the same size are arranged in the form of a cube on a table. Then a column of five cubes is removed from each of the four corners. All the exposed faces of the rest of the solid (except the face touching the table) are coloured red. Now, answer these questions based on the above statement :
How many cubes have only one red face ?
(1) 40
(2) 25
(3) 20
(4) 15
= 2
~ Top layer: the inner 3 × 3 cubes have only the top face red = 9. Lower 4 layers: only the middle cube of each side (4 per layer) has a single red side face — the others next to a removed corner have two = 4 × 4 = 16. Total = 9 + 16 = 25.

Q 6 | rea | Verbal Reasoning
David gets on the elevator at the 11th floor of a building and rides up at the rate of 57 floors per minute. At the same time, Albert gets on an elevator at the 51st floor of the same building and rides down at the rate of 63 floors per minute. If they continue travelling at these rates, then at which floor will their paths cross ?
(1) 19
(2) 28
(3) 30
(4) 37
= 3
~ Gap = 51 − 11 = 40 floors; they close it at 57 + 63 = 120 floors per minute, so they meet after 40/120 = 1/3 minute. David then has risen 57 × 1/3 = 19 floors: 11 + 19 = 30th floor (check: 51 − 63/3 = 30).

Q 7 | rea | Verbal Reasoning
An egg vendor calls on his first customer and sells half his eggs and half an egg. To the second customer, he sells half of what he has left with and half an egg, and to the third customer, he sells half of what he was then left with and half an egg. However, he did not break any egg. If in the end, the vendor was left with three eggs. How many eggs did he have initially ?
(1) 26
(2) 31
(3) 39
(4) None of these
= 2
~ Selling half plus half an egg leaves (x − 1)/2, so work backwards with x = 2 × (left) + 1: before the third sale 2 × 3 + 1 = 7, before the second 2 × 7 + 1 = 15, initially 2 × 15 + 1 = 31.

Select the option in which the numbers are related in the same way as are the numbers of the following set, (24, 10, 392) :
(1) (29, 18, 242)

Q 11 | rea | Verbal Reasoning
There are deer and peacocks in a zoo. By counting heads they are 80. The number of their legs is 200. How many peacocks are there ?
(1) 20
(2) 30
(3) 50
(4) 60
= 4
~ If all 80 were peacocks there would be 160 legs. The extra 40 legs come from deer (2 extra legs each), so deer = 20 and peacocks = 80 − 20 = 60.

What day of the week was 31st January, 2007 ?
(1) Tuesday
(2) Monday
(3) Thursday
(4) Wednesday
= 4
~ 1 January 2007 was a Monday. 31 January is 30 days later; 30 = 4 weeks + 2 odd days, so it falls 2 days after Monday, i.e. Wednesday.

Q 14 | rea | Verbal Reasoning
Rasik walked 20m towards north. Then he turned right and walks 30m. Then he turns right and walks 35m. Then he turns left and walks 15m. Finally he turns left and walks 15m. In which direction and how many metres is he from the starting position ?
(1) 15m West
(2) 30m East
(3) 30m West
(4) 45m East
= 4
~ Moves: 20 m north, right → 30 m east, right → 35 m south, left → 15 m east, left → 15 m north. North–south: 20 − 35 + 15 = 0. East: 30 + 15 = 45 m. So he is 45 m East of the start.

Q 15 | rea | Verbal Reasoning
How is ‘sure’ written in a code language ?
I. ‘he is sure’ written as ‘ja ha main in that code language
II. ‘is she sure’ written as ‘Ka ja main in that code language
(1) Ja
(2) Ja or ma
(3) Ma
(4) Ha
= 2
~ The codes are ‘ja ha ma’ and ‘ka ja ma’ (the printed 'main' is 'ma' + 'in'). The common words 'is' and 'sure' share the common codes 'ja' and 'ma', so 'sure' can be either 'ja' or 'ma' — it cannot be fixed from these two statements.

Q 16 | rea | Verbal Reasoning
Pointing to a woman, Nirmal said, “She is the daughter of my wife’s grandmother’s only child”. How is the woman related to Nirmal if she is not the wife of Nirmal ?
(1) Wife
(2) Sister-in-law
(3) Sister
(4) None of these
= 2
~ The only child of the wife's grandmother is the wife's parent. That parent's daughter is either the wife herself or the wife's sister. Since she is not his wife, she is his wife's sister, i.e. Nirmal's sister-in-law.

Q 17 | rea | Verbal Reasoning
IF ZIP = 198 and ZAP = 246, then how will you code VIP ?
(1) 174
(2) 222
(3) 888
(4) 990
= 1
~ Code = 6 × (first + last − middle letter), using alphabet positions. ZIP: 6 × (26 + 16 − 9) = 198; ZAP: 6 × (26 + 16 − 1) = 246; VIP: 6 × (22 + 16 − 9) = 174. (The examples also fit 6 × (sum of reverse positions + 3), giving 222 — the answer in many books; OPSC's revised final key is 174.)

Q 28 | rea | Verbal Reasoning
Seven years from now, Anamika will be as old as Malini was 4 years ago. Srinidhi was born 2 years ago. The average age of Anamika, Malini and Srinidhi 10 years from now will be 33 years. What is the present age of Anamika ?
(1) 30 years
(2) 31 years
(3) 29 years
(4) 28 years
= 4
~ Let Anamika be A now. A + 7 = Malini − 4, so Malini = A + 11; Srinidhi = 2. In 10 years the total is 3 × 33 = 99, so present total = 99 − 30 = 69. A + (A + 11) + 2 = 69 → 2A = 56 → A = 28 years.

Q 36 | rea | Verbal Reasoning

Q 37 | rea | Verbal Reasoning

(1) 9 : 45
(2) 9 : 15
(3) 8 : 45

Q 51 | math | Arithmetic
1/(1.3) + 1/(3.5) + 1/(5.7) + 1/(7.9) = ?
(1) 5/11
(2) 6/11
(3) 9/4
(4) 4/9
= 4
~ Here '1.3' means 1 × 3, etc. Each term splits as 1/(a × b) = (1/2)(1/a − 1/b), so the sum telescopes: (1/2)(1 − 1/3 + 1/3 − 1/5 + 1/5 − 1/7 + 1/7 − 1/9) = (1/2)(1 − 1/9) = (1/2)(8/9) = 4/9.

Q 52 | math | Arithmetic
Determine the smallest 3-digit number which is exactly divisible by 6 and 12 ?
(1) 96
(2) 84
(3) 108
(4) 120
= 3
~ A number divisible by both 6 and 12 must be a multiple of their LCM, 12. The smallest 3-digit multiple of 12 is 12 × 9 = 108 (12 × 8 = 96 has only two digits).
