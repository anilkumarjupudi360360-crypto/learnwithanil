# Paper II Reasoning & Mental Ability (Q1-50) and Mathematics (Q51-100), OPSC Assistant Section Officer (Office of the Advocate General, Odisha) written exam of 9 November 2025, Advt. No. 02 of 2025-26, Series A (OPSC's own booklet); answers: OPSC's final answer key of 17 January 2026

Q 1 | rea | Verbal Reasoning
Choose the most appropriate option to complete the following series.
V, R, N, ______, F, B
(1) K
(2) H
(3) J
(4) P
= 3
~ J completes the series. Write each letter's position in the alphabet: V = 22, R = 18, N = 14, F = 6, B = 2. Each step goes back by 4: 22 → 18 → 14 → ? → 6 → 2. So the missing term is 14 − 4 = 10, and the 10th letter is J. Check forwards from the blank: 10 − 4 = 6 = F, and 6 − 4 = 2 = B, so the series holds. The other options break the pattern: K (11) would make a gap of 3, H (8) a gap of 6, and P (16) would make the series go forward instead of backward. Shortcut: remember the EJOTY positions, E = 5, J = 10, O = 15, T = 20, Y = 25; with them you can find any letter's number in a second. Trap: three letters are skipped each time (V, skip U T S, R), so always count the gap, not just the next letter.

Q 2 | rea | Verbal Reasoning
In a certain code language, STRAIGHT is written as TSARGITH. How will THURSDAY be written as in that language?
(1) AYSDURTH
(2) HTRUDSYA
(3) UHTDRSYA
(4) HTRUDSAY
= 2
~ HTRUDSYA follows the same code. Step 1: split STRAIGHT into pairs of letters: ST | RA | IG | HT. Step 2: split the code TSARGITH the same way: TS | AR | GI | TH. Each pair has simply been reversed: ST → TS, RA → AR, IG → GI, HT → TH. Step 3: apply the same rule to THURSDAY: TH | UR | SD | AY becomes HT | RU | DS | YA, which joins up as HTRUDSYA, option (B). Check the others: (A) AYSDURTH keeps each pair as it is but reverses the order of the pairs (AY SD UR TH), a different rule; (C) UHTDRSYA moves the letters in a way that matches nothing in the example; (D) HTRUDSAY swaps the first three pairs but leaves the last pair AY unchanged, so it breaks the rule at the end. Tip: when a word and its code use exactly the same letters, look for a fixed pattern of moving letters, such as swapping pairs, reversing each half, or reversing the whole word. Both words here have eight letters, so splitting them into four pairs is the natural first try.

Q 3 | rea | Verbal Reasoning
A class of boys is standing in only one row. A boy is at thirty third position from both the ends in this sequence. How many boys are there in the class?
(1) 64
(2) 65
(3) 69
(4) 66
= 2
~ There are 65 boys. The boy is 33rd from the left end, so there are 32 boys to his left; he is also 33rd from the right end, so there are 32 boys to his right. Total = boys to the left + the boy himself + boys to the right = 32 + 1 + 32 = 65. In formula form: total = (position from left) + (position from right) − 1 = 33 + 33 − 1 = 65. We subtract 1 because adding the two positions counts the boy twice. The wrong options come from common slips: 66 (D) forgets to subtract 1, 64 (A) subtracts 2, and 69 (C) has no basis. Related uses of the same idea: if there are 65 boys and one is 20th from the left, he is 65 − 20 + 1 = 46th from the right; and a boy standing exactly in the middle of an odd row of n is (n + 1) ÷ 2 from either end, here (65 + 1) ÷ 2 = 33, which confirms the answer. Remember: add the two positions, then remove the one person counted twice.

Q 4 | rea | Verbal Reasoning
Suraj drove his bicycle towards the north. Then he turned to the right and covered a distance of 2 km. Then, he turned to the right and covered a distance of 4 km. He found himself 2 km east of his starting point. How far did he go by bicycle in the north direction?
(1) 2 km
(2) 4 km
(3) 6 km
(4) 8 km
= 2
~ He rode 4 km towards the north. Let the northward distance be x km. Step 1: he rides x km north. Step 2: facing north, a right turn makes him face east; he rides 2 km east. Step 3: facing east, another right turn makes him face south; he rides 4 km south. Now compare with the start. East–west: he has gone 2 km east and nothing west, which matches '2 km east of his starting point'. North–south: he went x km north and 4 km south, and since he ends up exactly east of the start (neither north nor south of it), these must cancel: x − 4 = 0, so x = 4 km. A quick sketch shows the path: up 4, right 2, down 4. Option (A) 2 km would leave him 2 km south of that point, while (C) 6 km and (D) 8 km would leave him north of it. Tip: in direction problems, handle north–south and east–west movements separately, and remember that when facing north, right = east, and when facing east, right = south.

Q 5 | rea | Verbal Reasoning
At present, A is younger than B by 8 years. If 4 years ago, their ages were in the ratio 1 : 2, then what is the present age of B (in years)?
(1) 12
(2) 20
(3) 11
(4) 18
= 2
~ B's present age is 20 years. Let A's present age be x, so B = x + 8, because A is younger by 8 years. Four years ago their ages were x − 4 and x + 4. Given (x − 4) : (x + 4) = 1 : 2, cross-multiply: 2(x − 4) = x + 4, so 2x − 8 = x + 4, which gives x = 12. So A = 12 and B = 12 + 8 = 20 years. Check: four years ago A was 8 and B was 16, and 8 : 16 = 1 : 2. Quicker method: the age difference never changes. In the ratio 1 : 2 the difference is 2 − 1 = 1 part, and this part equals 8 years, so four years ago A was 8 and B was 16; adding 4 gives B = 20 now. Other options: 12 (A) is A's age, not B's, a common slip; 11 (C) and 18 (D) do not give 1 : 2 four years ago. Tip: in age problems, combine the constant difference with the ratio; it saves time.

Q 6 | rea | Non-verbal Reasoning
Find the missing figure in the series from the given answer figures.
(1) 1
(2) 2
(3) 3
(4) 4
= 3
~ Answer figure (3) completes the series. Track the three parts separately, using clock positions. (i) The inner square, with its shaded corner, turns 45° clockwise each time, which is why it appears alternately upright and as a diamond: the shaded corner goes top-right (1:30) → right (3) → bottom-right (4:30) → bottom (6), so next it is at the bottom-left (7:30), with the square upright again. The fifth figure is therefore an upright square with the shaded corner at the bottom-left, the black dot on the right and the white dot on the left, which is figure (3). Q 8 | rea | Non-verbal Reasoning
Select the mirror image of the following figure :
(1) Figure A
(2) Figure B
(3) Figure C
(4) Figure D
= 1
~ Figure (A) is the mirror image. The mirror MN stands vertically to the right of the figure, so in the image left and right swap, while top and bottom stay where they are. Read the zigzag inside the frame from top to bottom: in the given figure the top stroke runs across and bends down on the right side, the middle stroke runs back and bends down on the left side, and then the bottom stroke runs across. In a vertical mirror each bend changes side, so the image must bend first on the left and then on the right, with the strokes still horizontal and the shape the same size; figure (A) shows exactly this. Figure (B) shows the shape tilted at about 45°, which a mirror never does. Figures (C) and (D) show the shape turned through 90°, so that the strokes stand upright; rotation is not reflection. The rectangular frame is symmetric, so it looks the same in the mirror. Rule to remember: vertical mirror → left and right swap, top and bottom stay; horizontal mirror (water image) → top and bottom swap, left and right stay.

Q 9 | rea | Non-verbal Reasoning
Two different positions of the same dice are shown. Which number will be at the top if 6 is at the bottom?
(1) 4
(2) 3
(3) 5
(4) 2
= 4
~ 2 will be at the top. Step 1: find the common face. The number 1 appears in both views; next to it we see 5 and 6 in the first view and 3 and 2 in the second. So 5, 6, 3 and 2 all touch 1, and the only face left, 4, must be opposite 1. Step 2: read each view clockwise, starting from the common face 1 (top → right → left is the clockwise order in these drawings). The first view gives 1 → 5 → 6, and the second gives 1 → 3 → 2. Step 3: numbers in the same place in the two sequences are opposite each other: 5 is opposite 3, and 6 is opposite 2. So the three opposite pairs are 1–4, 5–3 and 6–2. With 6 at the bottom, its opposite face, 2, is at the top. Check the other options: 4 (A) is opposite 1, while 3 (B) and 5 (C) are opposite each other, so none of them can sit across from 6. Tip: two faces seen together in one view are never opposite; use this to cancel options quickly.

Q 10 | rea | Non-verbal Reasoning
Group the given figures into three classes using each figure only once.
(1) 7, 8, 9; 2, 4, 3; 1, 5, 6
(2) 1, 3, 2; 4, 5, 7; 6, 8, 9
(3) 1, 6, 8; 3, 4, 7; 2, 5, 9
(4) 1, 6, 9; 3, 4, 7; 2, 5, 8
= 4
~ The grouping 1, 6, 9; 3, 4, 7; 2, 5, 8 is correct, because the figures are classified by their number of sides. Count the sides of each closed figure. Figure 1 (a right-angled triangle), figure 6 (an upright triangle) and figure 9 (an inverted triangle) have 3 sides. Figure 3 (a kite-like shape), figure 4 (a rectangle) and figure 7 (a slanting quadrilateral) have 4 sides. Figure 2 (an irregular pentagon), figure 5 (a shape with a notch cut into one side) and figure 8 (a square with a V-shaped notch at the top) have 5 sides. Note how a notch adds sides: a square with a V cut into its top keeps its left, bottom and right sides and gains two slanting edges, so it has 5 sides. Why the others fail: (C) swaps figures 8 and 9, putting a pentagon with the triangles and a triangle with the pentagons, while (A) and (B) mix triangles, quadrilaterals and pentagons in every group. Tip: when shapes look irregular, count corners or sides; size, slant and position usually do not matter in classification.

Group the given figures into three classes using each figure only once

(3) 1, 3, 4; 2, 8, 9; 5, 6, 7

Q 14 | rea | Verbal Reasoning
The calendar for the year 2007 will be the same for the year
(1) 2014
(2) 2016
(3) 2017
(4) 2018
= 4
~ 2018 has the same calendar as 2007. Two years share a calendar when (i) both are leap years or both are ordinary years, and (ii) the total number of odd days between them is a multiple of 7. An ordinary year has 365 days = 52 weeks + 1 odd day; a leap year has 2 odd days. Count from 2007: 2007 (1), 2008 (2), 2009 (1), 2010 (1), 2011 (1), 2012 (2), 2013 (1), 2014 (1), 2015 (1), 2016 (2), 2017 (1). The running total reaches 9 by the end of 2013, 11 by 2015, 13 by 2016 and 14 by 2017. Only 14 is a multiple of 7, so 1 January 2018 falls on the same weekday as 1 January 2007 (a Monday), and 2018, like 2007, is not a leap year. 2014 (A) and 2017 (C) fail the odd-day test, and 2016 (B) is a leap year. Shortcut: for an ordinary year that comes 2 or 3 years after a leap year (2007 = 2004 + 3), add 11; for the year just after a leap year, add 6; a leap year repeats after 28 years.

Q 15 | rea | Verbal Reasoning
Choose the appropriate number to complete the series :
1, 4, 27, 16, ? , 36, 343
(1) 125
(2) 25
(3) 87
(4) 120
= 1
~ 125 completes the series. Look at the positions: the terms in odd places (1st, 3rd, 5th, 7th) are cubes, and the terms in even places (2nd, 4th, 6th) are squares, of the natural numbers 1 to 7 in order. So 1 = 1³, 4 = 2², 27 = 3³, 16 = 4², ? = 5³, 36 = 6², 343 = 7³. The missing 5th term must be a cube: 5³ = 5 × 5 × 5 = 125. Why the others fail: 25 (B) is 5², a square, but the 5th place needs a cube; 87 (C) and 120 (D) are neither squares nor cubes. Tip: when the terms jump up and down, as in 4 → 27 → 16, suspect two series mixed together, here alternating cubes and squares. Learn squares up to 30 and cubes up to 20, since they appear in almost every reasoning paper. Quick cube list: 1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 216, 343, 512, 729, 1000. A similar question: 1, 8, 9, 64, 25, ?, 49 has squares in odd places and cubes in even places, so ? = 6³ = 216.

Q 16 | rea | Verbal Reasoning
Arrange the following words in the order in which they appear in an English Dictionary :
(I) Radical
(II) Radiate
(III) Racket
(IV) Radius
(V) Radar
(1) (III), (V), (II), (I), (IV)
(2) (III), (V), (II), (IV), (I)
(3) (III), (V), (IV), (I), (II)
(4) (III), (IV), (II), (I), (V)
= 1
~ The order is Racket, Radar, Radiate, Radical, Radius, i.e. (III), (V), (II), (I), (IV). Dictionary order compares words letter by letter. All five words begin with 'Ra'. At the third letter, Racket has 'c' while the others have 'd', and c comes before d, so Racket (III) is first. The remaining four begin with 'Rad'. At the fourth letter, Radar has 'a' while Radiate, Radical and Radius have 'i', so Radar (V) comes next. The last three begin with 'Radi'. At the fifth letter, Radiate has 'a', Radical 'c' and Radius 'u'; since a < c < u, the order is Radiate (II), Radical (I), Radius (IV). Why the others fail: (B) places Radius before Radical, (C) puts Radiate last, and (D) puts Radar at the end, so each breaks the letter-by-letter rule. Tip: underline the first letter where the words differ and compare only that letter; ignore word length. Also, a shorter word that forms the start of a longer one comes first, so 'Rack' comes before 'Racket'.

Q 17 | rea | Verbal Reasoning
Patro walks 10 km towards North. From there he walks 6 km towards South. Then, he walks 3 km towards East. How far and in which direction is he with reference to his starting point?
(1) 5 km West
(2) 7 km West
(3) 7 km East
(4) 5 km North-east
= 4
~ He is 5 km to the north-east of his starting point. Step 1: he walks 10 km north and then 6 km south, so his net movement is 10 − 6 = 4 km north. Step 2: he then walks 3 km east. He is now 4 km north and 3 km east of the start, which makes a right-angled triangle with the start. Step 3: by Pythagoras' theorem, distance = √(4² + 3²) = √(16 + 9) = √25 = 5 km. Step 4: since he is both north and east of the start, his direction from it is north-east. Why the others fail: (A) and (B) say west, but he never moved west at all; (C) gives 7 km, which simply adds 4 + 3 instead of using the triangle. Shortcut: remember the Pythagorean triples 3-4-5, 6-8-10, 5-12-13 and 8-15-17, so you can read off such distances at once. Note: 'north-east' in these questions means 'between north and east', not exactly 45°; here the exact angle does not matter. Trap: always cancel opposite movements (north and south) first.

Q 18 | rea | Verbal Reasoning
In a certain code language, 'cool drinks available here' is written as 'bhu man juk lop', 'available lost objects here' is written as 'gan bhu nut juk', 'be cool search objects' is written as 'vax der man nut', and 'available drinks desert search' is written as 'but juk der lop'. How will 'lost drinks' be written in that language?
(1) gan lop
(2) nut gan
(3) lop bhu
(4) juk gan
= 1
~ 'Lost drinks' is written as 'gan lop'. Find each word's code by comparing sentences that share a word. 'Available' appears in sentences 1, 2 and 4; the only code common to all three is 'juk', so available = juk. 'Here' appears in sentences 1 and 2; their common codes are bhu and juk, and since juk is already taken, here = bhu. 'Objects' appears in sentences 2 and 3; their common code is 'nut', so objects = nut. Sentence 2, 'available lost objects here' = 'gan bhu nut juk', now has only 'gan' left, so lost = gan. 'Drinks' appears in sentences 1 and 4; their common codes are juk and lop, and juk means 'available', so drinks = lop. Hence 'lost drinks' = 'gan lop', option (A). The others fail: nut (B) means 'objects', bhu (C) means 'here', and juk (D) means 'available'. Bonus decoding: cool = man (sentences 1 and 3) and search = der (sentences 3 and 4). Tip: start with the word that appears in the most sentences, because it narrows the codes fastest.

Q 19 | rea | Verbal Reasoning
There are seven persons A, B, C, D, E, F, and G. Each of them has different heights. C is shorter than only G. The number of persons taller than B is equal to the number of persons shorter than D. Neither A nor E is shortest. Who among the following is shortest?
(1) G
(2) D
(3) B
(4) F
= 4
~ F is the shortest. Step 1: 'C is shorter than only G' means only G is taller than C, so G is the tallest and C is second. Step 2: A and E are not the shortest, and C is second, so the shortest must be B, D or F. Step 3: test B. If B were the shortest, 6 people would be taller than B, so 6 people would have to be shorter than D, making D the tallest; but G is the tallest, so B is not the shortest. Step 4: test D. If D were the shortest, nobody would be shorter than D, so nobody could be taller than B, making B the tallest, which again clashes with G. So D is not the shortest. Step 5: only F remains. A valid order is G > C > B > A > D > E > F: two people (G and C) are taller than B, and two (E and F) are shorter than D, so the condition holds. Tip: in ranking puzzles, fix the sure positions first, then test each option against the conditions.

Find the missing figures in the series from the Answer figures given below :
(1) 1
(2) 4
(3) 3
(4) 2
= 4
~ Answer figure (2) comes next. The two hands always move symmetrically, one clockwise and one anticlockwise, so measure how far each hand is from the 12 o'clock mark: about 22.5° in figure 1 (a narrow V at the top), 45° in figure 2 (pointing to the 1:30 and 10:30 marks, a right angle), 90° in figure 3 (pointing to 3 and 9, a straight line) and 135° in figure 4 (pointing to 4:30 and 7:30, a right angle opening downwards). Q 21 | rea | Verbal Reasoning
A cube of side 18 cm is painted yellow on all the faces and then cut into smaller cubes of sides 3 cm each. Find the number of smaller cubes that have only two faces painted.
(1) 48
(2) 20
(3) 64
(4) 36
= 1
~ 48 small cubes have exactly two faces painted. Step 1: the number of small cubes along each edge is n = 18 ÷ 3 = 6, so there are 6 × 6 × 6 = 216 small cubes in all. Step 2: a small cube has exactly two painted faces only when it lies on an edge of the big cube, not at a corner. Each edge has 6 small cubes, of which 2 are corner cubes, leaving 6 − 2 = 4. Step 3: a cube has 12 edges, so 12 × 4 = 48. Full formula set for an n × n × n cube painted on all faces: three faces painted = 8 (the corners); two faces = 12(n − 2) = 48; one face = 6(n − 2)² = 6 × 16 = 96; no face = (n − 2)³ = 4³ = 64. Check: 8 + 48 + 96 + 64 = 216, the total. Trap: 64 (C) is the number of unpainted cubes, and 36 (D) would be the answer if n were 5. Memory tip: 3 faces = corners, 2 = edges, 1 = faces, 0 = the hidden core.

Q 22 | rea | Non-verbal Reasoning
Group the given figures into three classes using each figure only once.
(1) 1, 4; 2, 3; 5, 6
(2) 1, 5; 2, 6; 4, 3
(3) 1, 6; 2, 3; 4, 5
(4) 1, 2; 3, 6; 4, 5
= 1
~ The grouping 1, 4; 2, 3; 5, 6 is correct, because each pair is made of figures with the same shape. Figures 2 and 3 are identical: a triangle divided into two halves by a vertical line from the top to the base. Figures 1 and 4 are rectangles with a rectangular step cut out of one top corner; in figure 1 the step is at the top-right and in figure 4 at the top-left, so they are mirror images of each other. Figures 5 and 6 are rectangles with a small pointed peak rising from the top edge; the peak is near the right end in figure 5 and near the left end in figure 6, so these too are mirror images. Mirror images count as the same type of figure here. Why the other options fail: (B) pairs 1 with 5 and 4 with 3, mixing a stepped rectangle with a peaked one and with a triangle; (C) pairs 1 with 6 and 4 with 5, again stepped with peaked; (D) pairs 1 with 2. Tip: in classification, first look for identical or mirror-image shapes; their direction rarely matters.

Q 24 | rea | Verbal Reasoning
In the following question, select the related word pair from the given alternatives :
Tuberculosis : Lungs : Typhoid : ?
(1) Intestine
(2) Liver
(3) Lungs
(4) Brain
= 1
~ Typhoid is related to the intestine, just as tuberculosis is to the lungs. The pair links a disease with the main organ it attacks. Tuberculosis is caused by the bacterium Mycobacterium tuberculosis, spreads through droplets in the air and mainly damages the lungs. Q 29 | rea | Verbal Reasoning

(2) North-west
(3) South
(4) South-west
= 4
~ He is now facing south-west. Method 1, step by step: he starts facing west. Turning 45° clockwise from west brings him to north-west (clockwise goes W → NW → N). Fewer men means the food lasts longer, so the answer must be more than 35 days; 29 1/5 days is therefore impossible. 37 1/4 days is too short, and 54 days ignores the 10 days already used (150 × 45 ÷ 125 = 54). Trap: always subtract the days already passed before applying the formula; skipping that step is exactly how the 54-day option is produced.

Q 96 | math | Arithmetic
Two students appeared at an examination. One of them secured 9 marks more than the other and his marks was 56% of the sum of their marks. The marks obtained by them are:
(1) 39, 30
(2) 41, 32
(3) 42, 33
(4) 43, 34
= 3
~ They scored 42 and 33. Let the total of their marks be T. The higher scorer has 56% of T, so the other has 100 − 56 = 44% of T. The difference, 56% − 44% = 12% of T, equals 9 marks. So T = 9 × 100/12 = 75. Higher marks = 56% of 75 = 42; lower = 44% of 75 = 33. Check: 42 − 33 = 9 and 42/75 = 0.56. All four options differ by 9, so only the 56% condition decides: 39/69 ≈ 56.5%, 41/73 ≈ 56.2% and 43/77 ≈ 55.8%, while 42/75 is exactly 56%. Algebra method: x = 0.56(x + x − 9) → x = 1.12x − 5.04 → 0.12x = 5.04 → x = 42. Trap: the 56% is of the sum of both students' marks, not of the maximum marks of the paper. Tip: when a share and a difference are given, convert the difference into a percentage of the total first.

Q 97 | math | Arithmetic
Two pipes A and B can fill a tank in 24 min. and 32 min. respectively. If both the pipes are opened simultaneously, after how much time B should be closed so that the tank is full in 18 minutes?
(1) 9 minutes
(2) 12 minutes
(3) 8 minutes
(4) 15 minutes
= 3
~ Pipe B should be closed after 8 minutes. Pipe A stays open for the full 18 minutes and fills 18/24 = 3/4 of the tank. The remaining 1/4 must come from B, which fills 1/32 per minute: t/32 = 1/4 → t = 8 minutes. Check: in the first 8 minutes both pipes fill 8/24 + 8/32 = 1/3 + 1/4 = 7/12; A alone then fills the rest in 10 minutes: 10/24 = 5/12; total 7/12 + 5/12 = 1. If B were closed after 9, 12 or 15 minutes, the tank would be full before the 18-minute mark. Shortcut: B's running time = (1 − 18/24) × 32 = 8 minutes. Tip: in such questions, first find the work done by the pipe that runs throughout, then give the rest to the other pipe. Trap: B's share is not 1 − 18/32; it is A, not B, that works for the full 18 minutes.

Q 98 | math | Algebra & Geometry
Find a number such that when 15 is subtracted from 7 times the number, the result is 10 more than twice the number:
(1) 5
(2) 7
(3) 8
(4) 6
= 1
~ The number is 5. Translate the words: "15 subtracted from 7 times the number" is 7x − 15; "10 more than twice the number" is 2x + 10. Set them equal: 7x − 15 = 2x + 10. Collect terms: 7x − 2x = 10 + 15, so 5x = 25 and x = 5. Check: 7 × 5 − 15 = 20 and 2 × 5 + 10 = 20. The other options fail: x = 7 gives 34 and 24, x = 8 gives 41 and 26, and x = 6 gives 27 and 22. Word-to-algebra tips: "subtracted from" reverses the order (15 subtracted from 7x is 7x − 15, not 15 − 7x); "more than" means add; "times" means multiply; "is" or "the result is" means equals. Quick option test: since both sides are simple expressions, just substitute each option into 7x − 15 = 2x + 10. Trap: writing 15 − 7x is the most frequent mistake in this type of question.

Q 99 | math | Arithmetic
The smallest number, that must be added to 1780 to make it a perfect square is
(1) 70
(2) 68
(3) 69
(4) 65
= 3
~ 69 must be added. Find the perfect squares on either side of 1780: 42² = 1764 and 43² = 1849. 1780 lies between them, so the next perfect square is 1849. The smallest number to add = 1849 − 1780 = 69. Check: 1780 + 69 = 1849 = 43². Adding 70 gives 1850, 68 gives 1848 and 65 gives 1845; none of these is a perfect square. Related version: the least number to subtract to get a perfect square is 1780 − 1764 = 16. Quick estimate: √1780 is a little over 42 (since 42² = 1764), so the next whole square root is 43. Useful squares: 41² = 1681, 42² = 1764, 43² = 1849, 44² = 1936, 45² = 2025. Trap: don't confuse "added" (go up to 43²) with "subtracted" (go down to 42²), and remember that a perfect square never ends in 2, 3, 7 or 8.

Q 100 | math | Arithmetic
If the number 653 xy is divisible by 90, then (x + y) = ?
(1) 2
(2) 3
(3) 4
(4) 6
= 3
~ x + y = 4. Since 90 = 9 × 10, and 9 and 10 are co-prime, the number 653xy must be divisible by both 10 and 9. Divisibility by 10 needs the last digit to be 0, so y = 0. Divisibility by 9 needs the digit sum to be a multiple of 9: 6 + 5 + 3 + x + 0 = 14 + x. The next multiple of 9 is 18, so x = 4 (x = 13 is not a digit). The number is 65340 = 90 × 726, so x + y = 4 + 0 = 4. The values 2, 3 and 6 would make the digit sum 16, 17 or 20, none a multiple of 9. Rules to remember: by 9 → digit sum divisible by 9; by 10 → last digit 0; by 6 → divisible by both 2 and 3. Trap: split a divisor only into co-prime factors (90 = 9 × 10, not 3 × 30), otherwise the test can mislead.
