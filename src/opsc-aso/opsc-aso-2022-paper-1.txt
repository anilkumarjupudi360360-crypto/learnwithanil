# Paper I General Awareness, OPSC Assistant Section Officer (Odisha Secretariat) written exam of 27 August 2022, Advt. No. 26 of 2021-22, Series A (OPSC's own booklet); answers: OPSC's answer key published with the result (9 January 2024)

Q 1 | eco | Indian Economy
In the guidelines announced by Union Finance Minister in the 2022-2023 Budget, instead of using the term ‘Crypto Currency’, the term ______ is used.
(1) Bitcoins Assets
(2) Ethercoins Assets
(3) Altcoins Assets
(4) Virtual Digital Assets
= 4
~ Virtual Digital Assets (VDAs) is the term Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman used in the Union Budget 2022-23 (presented on 1 February 2022) instead of 'cryptocurrency'. VDA is a wide legal category that covers cryptocurrencies, NFTs (non-fungible tokens) and similar digital tokens. The Budget set up a tax regime for them: income from transferring a VDA is taxed at a flat 30%, with no deduction except the cost of acquisition; losses on VDAs cannot be set off against other income; a VDA received as a gift is taxed in the receiver's hands; and 1% TDS applies on transfers above a threshold (from 1 July 2022). The same Budget announced a Digital Rupee, a central bank digital currency to be issued by the RBI; its pilots began in late 2022. Why the other options are wrong: Bitcoin, Ether and altcoins are only kinds of crypto — Bitcoin is the first cryptocurrency (2009), Ether is the token of the Ethereum network, and 'altcoins' means all coins other than Bitcoin. None was the official term. Memory tip: Budget 2022 → VDA → 30% tax + 1% TDS.

Q 2 | cur | National & International
Which State in India presented ‘Child Budget’ as part of the Annual State Budget ?
(1) Andhra Pradesh
(2) Gujarat
(3) Sikkim
(4) Madhya Pradesh
= 4
~ Madhya Pradesh presented a separate 'Child Budget' for the first time along with its annual budget for 2022-23. Finance Minister Jagdish Devda tabled the budget on 9 March 2022, and the child budget brought together in one statement the money set aside across many departments — such as school education, health, and women and child development — for schemes meant for children below 18. A child budget is not a separate fund; it tags existing spending so that the government and the public can track how much actually reaches children, much as a gender budget does for women. The key picks D because the question is based on the March 2022 news, when Madhya Pradesh's move was widely reported. Gujarat and Sikkim were not part of that news. Note, however, that Andhra Pradesh had already presented a child budget with its 2021-22 budget, so option A is not strictly wrong. Tip: in current-affairs questions, link the fact to the year of the news (here, 2022-23 and Madhya Pradesh). (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 3 | cur | National & International
The Government of India announced it’s decision to convert 150 villages into ‘Villages of Excellence’ with technical assistance from ______.
(1) Japan
(2) Israel
(3) USA
(4) Netherlands
= 2
~ Israel is the country whose technical help India chose to turn 150 villages into 'Villages of Excellence'. The plan belongs to the Indo-Israel Agricultural Project, which the Union Agriculture Ministry runs with Israel's development agency MASHAV; India and Israel have had full diplomatic relations since January 1992. The villages were to be developed around the Indo-Israel Centres of Excellence (CoEs) for agriculture already working in several states. These CoEs demonstrate modern farming such as drip and micro-irrigation, fertigation, protected cultivation and quality nurseries for fruits and vegetables. A 'village of excellence' carries these methods from the centre to farmers' fields nearby, aiming at higher yields, higher incomes and better use of water. Why not the others: Japan, the USA and the Netherlands also cooperate with India in agriculture, but this programme was with Israel. Memory tip: Israel = water-saving farming (drip irrigation), the way a desert country learned to grow food.

Q 4 | cur | National & International
Which one of the following is not the objective of ‘Operation Sajag’ launched by Delhi Police ?
(1) To curb area domination exercise
(2) Keeping tap on criminals
(3) Intensive checking at pickets
(4) Special rewards to traffic-friendly drivers
= 4
~ Giving special rewards to traffic-friendly drivers was not an aim of Operation Sajag, so D is the answer. Operation Sajag (sajag means 'alert') was launched by the North-West district of Delhi Police on 4 October 2021 to curb street crime. Its measures were all policing steps: area-domination exercises (a visible police presence in a locality), intensive checking at pickets, stepped-up patrolling and keeping a tab on known criminals. Option A's wording ('to curb area domination exercise') is clumsy — area domination was a tool of the drive, not something it curbed — but it clearly refers to part of the operation, while B and C are listed measures. Rewarding good drivers is a traffic-management idea with no link to this anti-crime drive. Note that Delhi Police reports to the Union Ministry of Home Affairs, not to the Delhi government. Tip: for 'which is not an objective' questions, look for the option that belongs to a different field — here, traffic.

Q 5 | cur | National & International
The GSI has approved the setting up of country’s first Geological Park at ______.
(1) Lamheta, Jabalpur
(2) Kadagu, Karnataka
(3) Idukki, Kerala
(4) Talala Gir, Gujarat
= 1
~ Lamheta, near Jabalpur in Madhya Pradesh, was approved by the Geological Survey of India (GSI) as the site of the country's first geological park (geo-park). Lamheta lies on the bank of the Narmada, close to the marble rocks of Bhedaghat. The area is famous among geologists: the Lameta Formation, named after Lameta Ghat, is a Cretaceous rock layer that has yielded many dinosaur fossils, and the first dinosaur fossil found in India was collected from the Lameta bed near Jabalpur in 1828 by William Sleeman. The park aims to protect these rock formations and promote geo-tourism and science education. Why not the others: Kodagu (spelt 'Kadagu' in the question) in Karnataka is known for coffee and as the source of the Kaveri; Idukki in Kerala for its arch dam and hill country; and Talala in the Gir area of Gujarat for Gir forest and Kesar mangoes. Don't confuse it with the dinosaur fossil park at Raiyoli near Balasinor in Gujarat. GSI itself was founded in 1851 and has its headquarters in Kolkata.

Q 6 | his | Art & Culture
Match the following :
(I) Meghalaya — (i) Hojagiri
(II) Mizoram — (ii) Wangala Dance
(III) Manipur — (iii) Bamboo Dance
(IV) Tripura — (iv) Thang ta
(1) (I) – (ii), (II) – (iii), (III) – (iv), (IV) – (i)
(2) (I) – (iii), (II) – (i), (III) – (ii), (IV) – (iv)
(3) (I) – (ii), (II) – (i), (III) – (iii), (IV) – (iv)
(4) (I) – (iii), (II) – (iv), (III) – (i), (IV) – (iv)
= 1
~ Option A pairs Meghalaya with Wangala, Mizoram with the Bamboo Dance, Manipur with Thang-ta and Tripura with Hojagiri. Wangala is the post-harvest thanksgiving dance of the Garo people of Meghalaya, held in honour of the sun god Saljong; drums and buffalo-horn trumpets set its rhythm. The Bamboo Dance of Mizoram is called Cheraw: dancers step in and out of pairs of bamboo poles that others, sitting on the ground, clap together. Thang-ta is the armed martial art of Manipur (thang = sword, ta = spear). Hojagiri belongs to the Reang (Bru) community of Tripura, where women balance pitchers, lamps and bottles while moving only the lower body. Option B wrongly gives the Bamboo Dance to Meghalaya and Wangala to Manipur; C sends Hojagiri to Mizoram and Thang-ta to Tripura; D uses (iv) twice, so it cannot be right. Other North-East forms worth remembering: Nongkrem (Khasi, Meghalaya), Lai Haraoba and Ras Lila (Manipur) and Bihu (Assam). Memory tip: Garo = Wangala, Mizo = Cheraw, Manipur = Thang-ta, Reang = Hojagiri.

Q 7 | cur | National & International
The Central Board of Secondary Education has introduced ‘Data Science Subject’ as a subject for it’s students of 8-12 class in collaboration with ______ company.
(1) Inter
(2) Dell
(3) Microsoft
(4) Infosys
= 3
~ Microsoft was the partner when the Central Board of Secondary Education (CBSE) introduced Coding and Data Science as new subjects in 2021. Coding was offered for Classes 6 to 8 and Data Science for Classes 8 to 12 from the 2021-22 academic session, with the course content prepared together with Microsoft. The step followed the National Education Policy 2020, which wants schools to build computational thinking, coding and data skills from an early age. Intel (misprinted here as 'Inter'), Dell and Infosys all run their own education and skilling programmes in India, but none of them was the partner for this CBSE subject. For exam purposes, link each new school subject with its partner and the class range: Microsoft, Coding (6-8) and Data Science (8-12). CBSE is the national board under the Union Ministry of Education, with its headquarters in New Delhi. Trap to avoid: a familiar tech name in the options can pull you away from the partner actually named in the announcement, so learn such tie-ups as pairs. Memory tip: 'MS' for Microsoft and 'MS' for Maths and Science, the base of data science.

Q 8 | sci | Technology
Which of the following Statements is not correct ?
(1) Recently the Ministry of Earth Sciences has launched the Deep Ocean Mission
(2) This Mission mode project is to support the Green Economy initiative of Government of India
(3) This project is aimed to study bio-diversity, the impact of climate change etc
(4) In 2022 India signed a contract with International Sea Board Authority
= 2
~ The statement that the Deep Ocean Mission supports a 'Green Economy' initiative is the incorrect one: the mission is designed to support the Blue Economy initiatives of the Government of India, that is, the sustainable use of ocean resources for growth. The Ministry of Earth Sciences runs it; the Union Cabinet approved it in June 2021 with a budget of about ₹4,077 crore over five years, so statement A is fine. Statement C is also true, since its components cover deep-sea mining technology and a manned submersible, ocean climate-change advisory services, exploration and conservation of deep-sea biodiversity, deep-ocean survey, and energy and fresh water from the sea. Statement D refers to the International Seabed Authority (Kingston, Jamaica), the UN-linked body that gives contracts to explore the deep seabed; India's contract with it to explore polymetallic nodules in the Central Indian Ocean Basin dates from 2002 and has been extended since, and the examiners accept D as correct. The crewed vehicle is Matsya 6000, built by NIOT, Chennai, under the Samudrayaan project. Memory tip: oceans = blue economy; forests and clean energy = green economy.

Q 9 | cur | National & International
Digital India ‘BHASHINI’ is ______.
Which one of the above is not correct ?
(1) India’s Artificial Intelligence-led Language Translation Platform
(2) It gives access to global content in their own languages
(3) It is a step to realize digital photography
(4) It promotes Digital India Vision
= 3
~ Calling BHASHINI 'a step to realise digital photography' is wrong; the platform has nothing to do with cameras or images. BHASHINI (BHASHa INterface for India) is India's Artificial Intelligence-led language translation platform, launched by Prime Minister Narendra Modi on 4 July 2022 during Digital India Week at Gandhinagar. It is the platform of the National Language Translation Mission of the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY). Its aim is to let every citizen use the internet and digital services in his or her own language through speech-to-text, text-to-speech and machine translation tools for Indian languages, including Odia. So A (AI-led translation platform), B (access to global content in one's own language) and D (promotes the Digital India vision) all describe it correctly. Through 'Bhasha Daan', citizens can also contribute voice and text samples to build open language datasets. Trap to avoid: in a 'not correct' question, look for the statement that is off-topic; the word 'digital' appears in both C and D, but only D fits a language mission. Memory tip: Bhasha = language, so BHASHINI = language technology.

Q 10 | cur | Sports, Awards & Misc.
The theme of ‘Earth Day-2022 is ______.
(1) Nurture the Nature
(2) Go Green, Save Green
(3) Save the Earth, Save the Future
(4) Invest in our Planet
= 4
~ 'Invest in Our Planet' was the theme of Earth Day 2022, observed on 22 April. It asked businesses, governments and citizens to put money and effort into climate solutions and a green, prosperous future, instead of treating the environment as a cost; the same theme was kept for Earth Day 2023. Earth Day began in the United States in 1970, inspired by Senator Gaylord Nelson, and is now coordinated worldwide by EARTHDAY.ORG; the UN General Assembly named 22 April International Mother Earth Day in 2009. Options A, B and C are general green slogans that were never the official 2022 theme. Do not confuse Earth Day with World Environment Day (5 June, led by UNEP), whose 2022 theme was 'Only One Earth' with Sweden as host, or with Earth Hour, when lights are switched off for an hour in late March. For 2024 the Earth Day theme was 'Planet vs. Plastics'. Memory tip: Earth Day = 22 April = 'Invest in Our Planet' (2022); Environment Day = 5 June = 'Only One Earth' (2022).

Q 11 | sci | Technology
Which of the following statements is not correct ?
(I) The Chang’e5 Lunar Space Craft Mission is undertaken by China
(II) It found water at its landing site
(III) It is the third Lunar Exploration Mission of Russia
(IV) The Chang’e5 Lunar Space Craft was launched on November 23, 2020
(1) (II) and (IV)
(2) (III) only
(3) (IV) only
(4) (I) only
= 2
~ Statement (III) is the only wrong one: Chang'e-5 is a Chinese mission of the China National Space Administration (CNSA), not Russia's third lunar mission. So statement (I) is correct. Statement (IV) is also correct: the Long March 5 rocket lifted off from Wenchang, Hainan, on 23 November 2020 (UTC; 24 November local time). Statement (II) is correct too, because the lander's spectrometer detected signs of water in the soil and rocks at its landing site in Oceanus Procellarum, a finding published in 2022. Chang'e-5 landed on 1 December 2020, scooped and drilled about 1.7 kg of lunar material and brought it back to Earth in mid-December 2020, making China the third country, after the USA and the USSR, to return Moon samples. The mission is named after Chang'e, the Chinese Moon goddess. Later, Chang'e-6 (2024) brought back the first samples from the Moon's far side. Trap: Russia's recent Moon attempt was Luna-25 (August 2023), which crashed before landing. Memory tip: Chang'e = China; Luna = Russia; Chandrayaan = India.

Q 13 | cur | National & International
Which of the following countries is not a Member of SAARC ?
(1) Bhutan
(2) Bangladesh
(3) Singapore
(4) Sri Lanka
= 3
~ Singapore is not a member of SAARC; it is a South-East Asian nation and a founder member of ASEAN. The South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation was set up at Dhaka on 8 December 1985 by seven countries: Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Maldives, Nepal, Pakistan and Sri Lanka. Afghanistan joined as the eighth member in 2007. Its secretariat is in Kathmandu, Nepal, and 8 December is marked as SAARC Charter Day. Bhutan, Bangladesh and Sri Lanka (options A, B and D) are all founder members. SAARC also has nine observers, including China, Japan, the USA, the European Union, Iran, South Korea, Australia, Mauritius and Myanmar. Its summits have been stalled since the 18th summit at Kathmandu in 2014; the 2016 Islamabad summit was called off after India and others pulled out following the Uri attack. Related group: BIMSTEC (secretariat at Dhaka) links Bay of Bengal countries, including Myanmar and Thailand but not Pakistan. Memory tip for SAARC members: 'MBBS PANI' = Maldives, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Pakistan, Afghanistan, Nepal, India.

Q 14 | cur | National & International
The 15th ‘World Forestry Congress’ was held at ______.
(1) Seoul, Korea
(2) Durban, South Africa
(3) Jakarta, Indonesia
(4) Buenos Aires, Argentina
= 1
~ The XV (15th) World Forestry Congress was held in Seoul, Republic of Korea, from 2 to 6 May 2022, organised by the Korean government with the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO). Its theme was 'Building a Green, Healthy and Resilient Future with Forests', and it closed with the Seoul Forest Declaration. It was the first time the Congress met in Korea. Durban, South Africa (option B) hosted the 14th Congress in 2015, the first held in Africa. Jakarta, Indonesia (option C) hosted the 8th Congress in 1978, and Buenos Aires, Argentina (option D) has hosted it twice, in 1972 (7th) and 2009 (13th). The Congress meets roughly every six years under FAO; the first was held in Rome in 1926. For India, forest data come from the India State of Forest Report, published every two years by the Forest Survey of India, Dehradun, and Odisha is among the states with the largest forest cover. Trap: do not confuse the FAO-linked World Forestry Congress with IUCN's World Conservation Congress. Memory tip: 14th Durban (2015), 15th Seoul (2022).

Q 15 | cur | National & International
‘South Asia Economic Focus’ a bi-annual Economic Report is published by ______.
(1) Asian Development Bank
(2) IMF
(3) World Bank
(4) World Economic Forum
= 3
~ The South Asia Economic Focus is a flagship report of the World Bank, released twice a year, usually in spring (April) and autumn (October) around the Bank-IMF meetings. It reviews growth, inflation, debt and jobs in the eight South Asian countries, gives growth forecasts, and usually carries a special theme chapter. The other options publish different reports: the Asian Development Bank (Manila) brings out the Asian Development Outlook; the IMF publishes the World Economic Outlook, the Global Financial Stability Report and the Fiscal Monitor; and the World Economic Forum (Geneva) releases the Global Gender Gap Report and the Global Risks Report. Other World Bank reports often asked about are the World Development Report, Global Economic Prospects, the India Development Update and the Logistics Performance Index; its Doing Business report was discontinued in 2021. Memory tip: 'Focus' on South Asia comes from the Bank, the 'Outlook' for Asia from ADB, and the 'World Economic Outlook' from the Fund. Trap: the World Economic Forum and the World Economic Outlook sound alike but come from completely different bodies.

Q 16 | geo | Environment & Ecology
‘Minamata Convention’ is an international platform to discuss about ______.
(1) Mercury Pollution
(2) Air Pollution
(3) Soil Pollution
(4) Silicon Pollution
= 1
~ The Minamata Convention on Mercury is the global treaty on mercury pollution. It was adopted in October 2013 at Kumamoto, Japan, and came into force on 16 August 2017; India ratified it in 2018. It is named after Minamata Bay in Japan, where a chemical factory released methylmercury in its waste water in the 1950s; people who ate the contaminated fish suffered severe nerve damage, now called Minamata disease. The Convention bans new mercury mines and phases out existing ones, cuts mercury use in products such as some thermometers, batteries and lamps, and controls emissions from coal-fired power plants and small-scale gold mining. Air, soil and 'silicon' pollution are not its subject. Related treaties: the Basel Convention (cross-border movement of hazardous waste), Rotterdam (prior informed consent for hazardous chemicals) and Stockholm (persistent organic pollutants); the Montreal Protocol deals with ozone-depleting substances. Memory tip: Minamata and Mercury both begin with 'M'. Trap: Itai-itai disease, also from Japan, is caused by cadmium, not mercury.

Q 17 | geo | Indian & World Geography
Which of the following statements is not correct ?
(1) Ukraine is the fourth largest country in Europe after Russia
(2) Kyiv is the largest city in Ukraine
(3) It shares it’s borders with Russia to East and Northest borders
(4) It has coastline along the sea of Azov
= 1
~ The incorrect statement is that Ukraine is the fourth-largest country in Europe after Russia; with an area of about 6.03 lakh sq km it is the second-largest country in Europe, after Russia. The other statements are true. Kyiv (Kiev), on the Dnieper river, is its capital and largest city (B). Ukraine borders Russia to the east and north-east (C); its other neighbours are Belarus to the north, Poland, Slovakia and Hungary to the west, and Romania and Moldova to the south-west. In the south it has coastline on the Black Sea and the Sea of Azov (D), which are joined by the Kerch Strait; the Crimean peninsula lies between them and was annexed by Russia in 2014. Ukraine is called the 'breadbasket of Europe' for its wheat, maize and sunflower oil grown on fertile black soil (chernozem). Russia's full-scale invasion began on 24 February 2022, which is why Ukraine was topical in this 2022 exam. Memory tip: the largest countries in Europe, in order, are Russia, Ukraine, France and Spain.

Q 18 | cur | Sports, Awards & Misc.
According to ‘Charitable Aid Foundation’ which country topped ‘World Giving Index, 2021’ ?
(1) Kenya
(2) India
(3) Indonesia
(4) New Zeeland
= 3
~ Indonesia topped the World Giving Index 2021, published by the UK-based Charities Aid Foundation (CAF; the question calls it 'Charitable Aid Foundation'). The Index ranks countries on three acts of giving, measured through Gallup's World Poll: helping a stranger, donating money to a charity and volunteering time. The 2021 edition, based on the pandemic year 2020, placed Indonesia first, Kenya (option A) second and Nigeria third; New Zealand (option D) was seventh and India (option B) came 14th, a big climb from its earlier ten-year ranking. Indonesia's strong showing is often linked with religious giving such as zakat and a culture of community help, and it has topped the Index more than once. The Index was first published in 2010. Trap: the question asks for the topper, not the most improved country; India's rise was widely reported in Indian news, but it was not first. Memory tip: in giving indices, think of 'I for Indonesia' at the top and 'K for Kenya' close behind; the publisher is CAF, a British charity, not a UN body or a bank.

Q 19 | cur | Sports, Awards & Misc.
With which American Scientist Professor Deepak Dhar shared the prestigious Boltzmann medal in Statistical Physics for the Year 2022 ?
(1) John Hopfield
(2) Kenneth G. Wilson
(3) Sam F. Edwards
(4) Dan Frenkal
= 1
~ John Hopfield of Princeton University shared the 2022 Boltzmann Medal with Professor Deepak Dhar of IISER Pune, who became the first Indian to receive it. The medal is the highest award in statistical physics, given every three years by the Commission on Statistical Physics (C3) of the International Union of Pure and Applied Physics (IUPAP) at the STATPHYS conference. Dhar was honoured for exactly solved models, including self-organised criticality (the 'sandpile' model); Hopfield for applying statistical physics to biology, including neural network memory. All the other options are earlier winners: Kenneth G. Wilson received the first medal in 1975, Sam F. Edwards won in 1995 and Daan Frenkel in 2016. Hopfield later shared the 2024 Nobel Prize in Physics with Geoffrey Hinton for foundational work on machine learning with artificial neural networks. Trap: Wilson is famous for the renormalisation group and a 1982 Nobel, but he was not the 2022 co-winner. Memory tip: 2022 Boltzmann Medal = Dhar (India) + Hopfield (Princeton), and Hopfield's 'network' links both his Boltzmann and Nobel honours.

Q 20 | cur | National & International
‘Dharma Guardian Military Exercise’ is conducted between which of the following countries ?
(1) India and Bhutan
(2) India and USA
(3) India and Japan
(4) India and France
= 3
~ Dharma Guardian is the joint military exercise between the Indian Army and the Japan Ground Self-Defense Force. It began in 2018 at Vairengte, Mizoram, home of the Counter Insurgency and Jungle Warfare School, and its 2022 edition (27 February to 10 March) was held at the Foreign Training Node in Belagavi, Karnataka, focusing on operations in jungle and semi-urban terrain. The other pairs have different exercises: India and the USA hold Yudh Abhyas (army) and Cope India (air force), and both join Japan and Australia in the naval exercise Malabar; India and France hold Shakti (army), Varuna (navy) and Garuda (air force); with Bhutan, India's defence ties run mainly through the Indian Military Training Team (IMTRAT). Other India-Japan exercises include JIMEX (navy) and Veer Guardian (air force). Trap: Japan's forces are called 'Self-Defense Forces' under its pacifist constitution, so the army is the 'Ground' Self-Defense Force. Memory tip: Dharma Guardian = India-Japan armies; Veer Guardian = India-Japan air forces; Shakti = India-France; Yudh Abhyas = India-USA.

Q 21 | his | Modern India & National Movement
Which of the following is not a matched pair ?
(1) Gulab Singh / Jammu
(2) Duleep Singh / Punjab
(3) Shah Shuja / Rohilkhand
(4) Wajid Ali Shah / Awadh
= 3
~ Shah Shuja / Rohilkhand is the wrong pair. Shah Shuja (Shuja Shah Durrani) was the deposed king of Afghanistan whom the British put back on the throne of Kabul in 1839 during the First Anglo-Afghan War, in place of Dost Mohammad; he was killed in 1842. Rohilkhand, the area around Bareilly and Rampur in western Uttar Pradesh, was ruled by Rohilla Afghan chiefs such as Ali Muhammad Khan and Hafiz Rahmat Khan, and was taken by Awadh with Company troops in the Rohilla War of 1774. The other pairs are correct: Gulab Singh, the Dogra ruler of Jammu, got Kashmir from the British by the Treaty of Amritsar (1846) and founded the state of Jammu and Kashmir; Duleep Singh, Ranjit Singh's youngest son, was the last Maharaja of the Sikh kingdom of Punjab, annexed by Dalhousie in 1849; Wajid Ali Shah was the last Nawab of Awadh, deposed in 1856 on the charge of misgovernment. Trap: do not confuse this Afghan Shah Shuja with Shah Shuja, Shah Jahan's son and governor of Bengal.

Q 22 | his | Modern India & National Movement
Which is not the matched pair ?
(1) Krishnaraja Wodeyar / Mysore
(2) Nizamulmulk Asaf Jah / Hyderabad
(3) Govinda Chandra / Cachar
(4) Zaman Shah / Peshawar
= 4
~ Zaman Shah / Peshawar is the mismatched pair, because Zaman Shah Durrani (ruled 1793-1800) was the king of Afghanistan with his capital at Kabul, not the ruler of a Peshawar state; Peshawar was only one of the cities in his empire, and it later passed to the Sikhs. A grandson of Ahmad Shah Abdali, Zaman Shah threatened to invade north India in the 1790s and even occupied Lahore, which alarmed the British under Wellesley. The other pairs are correct: Krishnaraja Wodeyar III was placed on the throne of Mysore in 1799 after Tipu Sultan's death, under a Subsidiary Alliance; Nizam-ul-Mulk Asaf Jah (Qamar-ud-din Khan) founded the independent state of Hyderabad in 1724, starting the Asaf Jahi line of Nizams; Govinda Chandra was the last Kachari raja of Cachar in southern Assam, after whose death in 1830 the British annexed Cachar in 1832. Memory tip: the Durrani rulers in such lists (Ahmad Shah Abdali, Zaman Shah, Shah Shuja) were kings of Afghanistan, ruling first from Kandahar and later from Kabul. Trap: Asaf Jah founded Hyderabad, while Mysore belonged to the Wodeyars, apart from the Haidar-Tipu interlude.

Q 23 | his | Modern India & National Movement
Clause 43, of the Charter Act of 1813 of the British Parliament concerned :
(1) Law and Order situation in India
(2) Education in India
(3) Foreign policy and trade relations in India
(4) Initiation of Indian Renaissance
= 2
~ Clause 43 of the Charter Act of 1813 dealt with education in India. It directed the East India Company to set aside at least one lakh rupees every year for reviving and improving literature, encouraging the learned natives of India, and introducing and promoting knowledge of the sciences among the people of British India. It was the first time the British Parliament accepted that the state had a duty towards Indian education. Because the clause did not say whether the money should go to Oriental learning or Western learning, it led to the Orientalist-Anglicist debate, which Macaulay's Minute and Bentinck's English Education Act (1835) settled in favour of English education. The same Act ended the Company's trade monopoly in India (keeping only tea and the China trade) and allowed Christian missionaries into India. Law and order, foreign policy and the 'Indian Renaissance' (options A, C and D) were not the subject of Clause 43. Memory tip: 1813 = ₹1 lakh for education; 1833 = Company's trading role ended; 1854 = Wood's Despatch, the 'Magna Carta' of English education.

Q 24 | his | Modern India & National Movement
For colonial times in India, which of the following sequence is in proper order – from the lowest to the highest ?
(1) Munsif’s Court, Sadr Amin’s Court, Zilla Court, Sadr Diwani Adalat
(2) Sadr Diwani Adalat, Munsif’s Court, Zilla Court, Sadr Amin’s Court
(3) Munsif’s Court, Zilla Court, Sadr Amin’s Court, Sadr Diwani Adalat
(4) Sadr Diwani Adalat, Sadr Amin’s Court, Zilla Court, Munsif’s Court
= 1
~ From the lowest to the highest, the civil courts of the Company period ran: Munsif's Court → Sadr Amin's Court → Zilla (District) Court → Sadr Diwani Adalat. The Munsif, an Indian officer, heard small money suits at the bottom; the Sadr Amin, also Indian, handled larger suits; the Zilla or District Court, headed by a European district judge, heard bigger cases and appeals from the courts below; and the Sadr Diwani Adalat at Calcutta, set up under Warren Hastings in 1772, was the highest civil court of appeal, with appeals beyond it to the King-in-Council in England. Cornwallis's reforms of 1793 (the Cornwallis Code) separated revenue from judicial work and built this graded system, and Bentinck gave Indians more judicial posts in the 1830s. Options B and D put the Sadr Diwani Adalat at the wrong end, and C puts the Zilla Court below the Sadr Amin, so they break the order. On the criminal side the top court was the Sadr Nizamat Adalat. Both were merged into the new High Courts under the Indian High Courts Act, 1861.

Q 25 | his | Modern India & National Movement
Bhangi was a clan that ruled over a part of :
(1) Dakhan
(2) Rohilkhand
(3) Kaveri Delta
(4) Punjab
= 4
~ The Bhangi misl was one of the twelve Sikh misls (confederacies of chiefs) that ruled parts of Punjab in the second half of the 18th century. Q 26 | his | Ancient & Medieval India
“ekam sat, vipraha bahudha vadanti” comes from the :

(2) Ramayana
(3) Vedas
(4) Brahmanas
= 3
~ 'Ekam sat vipra bahudha vadanti' comes from the Vedas, specifically the Rig Veda (Mandala 1, Sukta 164, verse 46). Q 95 | sci | General Science
What are parasitic, photosynthetic plants called ?
(1) Semiparasites
(2) Hemiparasites
(3) Microparasites
(4) Holoparasites
= 2
~ Hemiparasites are plants that are parasitic yet photosynthetic: they have green leaves with chlorophyll and make their own sugars, but they tap the host's xylem through special roots called haustoria to take water and minerals. Examples are mistletoe (Viscum and Loranthus, often seen on mango trees), Striga or witchweed (a root parasite of sorghum, maize and sugarcane) and sandalwood (Santalum album), whose roots attach to those of nearby host trees. Holoparasites are the opposite: they have no chlorophyll and take everything, including food, from the host, for example Cuscuta (dodder, amarbel), Orobanche (broomrape) and Rafflesia, which has the largest single flower. Microparasites is a term from disease biology for tiny pathogens such as viruses, bacteria and protozoa that multiply inside a host; it does not describe plants. Some older books use 'semi-parasite' loosely for the same idea, but the standard term, and the one the key accepts, is hemiparasite. Memory tip: 'hemi' means half, so a half-parasite still cooks its own food; 'holo' means whole, so it is a complete parasite.

Q 97 | geo | Environment & Ecology
What is the total species deversity across all communities within a geographic area called ?
(1) Alpha diversity
(2) Beta diversity
(3) Gamma diversity
(4) Delta diversity
= 3
~ Gamma diversity is the total species diversity of all the communities taken together in a large geographic area, such as a whole landscape or region. The three standard levels were described by the ecologist R.H. Whittaker. Alpha diversity is the number of species within one community or habitat, for example a single grassland patch or one pond. Beta diversity measures how species change from one community to the next along a gradient, such as from the foot to the top of a hill or from a wet to a dry forest; high beta diversity means neighbouring habitats share few species. Delta diversity is rarely used (a few authors apply it to change between whole landscapes) and it does not mean the total for an area. Example: count the species in one sacred grove (alpha), compare groves in the plains and the hills (beta), and list all species of the whole Eastern Ghats region of Odisha (gamma). Memory tip: alpha = within one, beta = between, gamma = grand total. Don't mix these up with the three levels of biodiversity: genetic, species and ecosystem.

Update for 2026: bigger plants have since come up in China, such as the 3.5 GW Midong project in Xinjiang (2024), and India's Khavda Renewable Energy Park in Kutch, Gujarat, planned at 30 GW of solar and wind, will be the world's largest renewable park when complete. Q 99 | geo | Environment & Ecology
What is the modeling approach for evaluating the response of individual species to climate change called ?
(1) Zero dimension model
(2) Box model
(3) General circulation model
(4) Bioclimatic envelope model
= 4
~ A bioclimatic envelope model (also called a climate envelope or species distribution model) is the standard way to study how a single species may respond to climate change. It takes the places where the species lives today, links them with climate data such as temperature and rainfall to define the 'envelope' of conditions it can tolerate, and then projects where that envelope will lie under future climate scenarios. The output shows likely range shifts, such as species moving uphill or towards the poles, and helps in planning protected areas; software such as MaxEnt is widely used for it. The other options model the climate system, not species: a zero-dimensional model treats the Earth as a single point and balances incoming and outgoing energy; a box model splits the system into a few linked reservoirs (atmosphere, ocean, land) and tracks the flows between them; and a general circulation model (GCM) simulates the three-dimensional movement of air and ocean water and underlies IPCC projections. Limitation: envelope models ignore dispersal, competition and adaptation. Tip: 'bio' in the name points to living species.

Q 100 | geo | Environment & Ecology
Which of the following have high assimilation efficiencies of over 90 percent ?
(1) Secondary carnivores
(2) Herbivores
(3) Predatory spiders
(4) Social insects
= 3
~ Predatory spiders show assimilation efficiencies of over 90 percent, as per the official key. Assimilation efficiency is the share of the food eaten (ingested) that is actually absorbed into the body; the rest is passed out as faeces. A spider injects digestive enzymes into its prey and sucks in only the pre-digested liquid, so it swallows almost nothing indigestible and absorbs nearly all of it. Herbivores score low, about 20–50 percent, because plant food is full of cellulose and lignin that are hard to digest. Carnivores, including secondary carnivores, usually reach about 80 percent, since animal tissue digests easily but bones, hair and feathers are wasted. Social insects are not the group cited for this 90 percent figure. Related terms: production efficiency = production ÷ assimilation (high in cold-blooded animals, low in birds and mammals, which burn energy to keep warm), and ecological efficiency between trophic levels is about 10 percent (Lindeman's ten percent law). Tip: the more liquid and pre-digested the food, the higher the assimilation efficiency.
