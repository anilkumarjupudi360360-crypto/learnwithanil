# Paper I General Awareness, OPSC Assistant Section Officer (Office of the Advocate General, Odisha) written exam of 9 November 2025, Advt. No. 02 of 2025-26, Series A (OPSC's own booklet); answers: OPSC's final answer key of 17 January 2026

Q 1 | his | Modern India & National Movement
What is the correct sequence of the following ?
i. The Hartog Committee
ii. The Hunter Education Commission
iii. The Sadler University Commission
iv. Kothari Education Commission
(1) i, ii, iii, iv
(2) iii, iv, ii, i
(3) ii, iii, iv, i
(4) ii, iii, i, iv
= 4
~ The correct order is ii, iii, i, iv: Hunter Education Commission (1882) → Sadler University Commission (1917–19) → Hartog Committee (1929) → Kothari Education Commission (1964–66). The Hunter Commission, appointed under Lord Ripon, reviewed the working of Wood's Despatch (1854) and stressed primary education, leaving more of secondary and higher education to private effort. The Sadler Commission (Calcutta University Commission) under Michael Sadler studied Calcutta University and recommended a 12-year school course, with an intermediate stage before a three-year degree. The Hartog Committee was an auxiliary committee of the Simon Commission; it pointed out the 'wastage and stagnation' in primary schools and favoured consolidation rather than rapid expansion. The Kothari Commission, chaired by D.S. Kothari after independence, proposed the 10+2+3 pattern and spending 6% of national income on education, and led to the first National Policy on Education in 1968. Option A puts Hartog first, while B and C place the post-1947 Kothari Commission before older colonial bodies. Memory tip: Hunter, Sadler, Hartog, Kothari runs 1882, 1917, 1929, 1964.

Q 2 | pol | Constitution & Polity
Which of the following Articles are related to the Right to Religion ?
(1) Articles 14 to 18
(2) Articles 19 to 22
(3) Articles 25 to 28
(4) Articles 29 and 30
= 3
~ Articles 25 to 28 together form the Right to Freedom of Religion in Part III of the Constitution. Article 25 gives every person freedom of conscience and the right to freely profess, practise and propagate religion, subject to public order, morality and health. Article 26 lets religious denominations manage their own affairs and institutions. Article 27 bars compulsory taxes whose proceeds are used to promote a particular religion. Article 28 forbids religious instruction in educational institutions wholly maintained out of State funds. The other groups belong to different rights: Articles 14–18 are the Right to Equality, Articles 19–22 the Right to Freedom (including life and personal liberty in Article 21), and Articles 29–30 the Cultural and Educational Rights, such as the right of minorities to set up and run their own educational institutions. Note that Article 25 protects every person, citizens and foreigners alike. Memory tip for the Part III blocks: Equality 14, Freedom 19, Against Exploitation 23, Religion 25, Culture and Education 29, Remedies 32.

Q 3 | pol | Constitution & Polity
Which election method is used for Lok Sabha elections in India ?
(1) Proportional Representation
(2) First Past The Post (FPTP)
(3) Single Transferable Vote (STV)
(4) Mixed Electoral System
= 2
~ Lok Sabha elections in India use the First Past The Post (FPTP) system, also called the simple plurality system. The country is divided into single-member territorial constituencies, and the candidate who gets the highest number of votes wins, even if that is less than half of the votes cast. The method is simple for voters and tends to give stable majorities, but a party's share of seats can differ sharply from its share of votes. Proportional Representation, which tries to match seats to vote share, is not used for the Lok Sabha; India uses its Single Transferable Vote (STV) form only for indirect elections, such as those of the President, the Vice-President, Rajya Sabha members and members of State Legislative Councils elected by MLAs. A Mixed Electoral System (part FPTP, part list-based PR, as in Germany) has been suggested by reform bodies but never adopted. The same FPTP method is used for elections to the Odisha Legislative Assembly. Memory tip: direct elections in India = FPTP; indirect elections = PR by STV.

Q 4 | geo | Environment & Ecology
What is edge species ?
(1) The species which occur abundantly in the ecotone
(2) Species on the verge of extinction
(3) Rare species
(4) Endemic species
= 1
~ Edge species are the species that occur mostly or most abundantly in an ecotone, the transition zone where two ecosystems meet, such as a forest and a grassland, or land and sea at an estuary. Because an ecotone mixes conditions and organisms from both neighbouring communities, it often holds more species and a higher density of individuals than either community alone; this is called the edge effect. Ecotones may be narrow or broad, and a typical example is a mangrove belt like Bhitarkanika in Odisha, where life adapted to both salt water and land thrives. The other options describe different ideas: species on the verge of extinction are called endangered or critically endangered (IUCN Red List categories); rare species are those with small populations or a very limited spread; and endemic species are those found naturally in only one particular region, like the Nilgiri tahr of the Western Ghats. Memory tip: edge species live on the 'edge' between two ecosystems, and the edge effect means more variety at the meeting point.

Q 5 | geo | Indian & World Geography
Match the following : Composition of Atmosphere.
a. Oxygen — 1. Is one of the most variable gaseous substance present in atmosphere, constituting between 0.2 to 4 percent.
b. Nitrogen — 2. Constitutes only 21 percent of the total volume of atmosphere.
c. Ozone — 3. Among gases accounts for 78 percent of total atmospheric volume.
d. Water vapour — 4. It forms less than 0.00005 percent by volume of the atmosphere and is unevenly distributed.
(1) a-1, b-2, c-3, d-4
(2) a-2, b-3, c-1, d-4
(3) a-2, b-3, c-4, d-1
(4) a-4, b-1, c-3, d-2
= 3
~ The correct matching is a-2, b-3, c-4, d-1. Oxygen makes up about 21% of the atmosphere by volume (a-2), and nitrogen about 78% (b-3); together the two account for about 99% of dry air. Ozone is only a trace gas, less than 0.00005% by volume, and is unevenly spread, being concentrated mainly in the stratosphere, where it absorbs harmful ultraviolet rays from the Sun (c-4). Water vapour is the most variable gas: it can reach about 4% of the air in warm, wet tropical regions but falls below 1% in cold and dry desert and polar areas, and it decreases with height (d-1). The other options fail: option A makes oxygen the variable gas, option B swaps ozone and water vapour, and option D gives ozone the 78% figure. Argon (about 0.93%) is the third most common gas, and carbon dioxide, though only about 0.04%, is, along with water vapour, a key greenhouse gas. Memory tip: '78-21-1' for nitrogen, oxygen and everything else.

Q 6 | pol | Constitution & Polity
Which of the following Committee is NOT associated with Civil Services in any manner ?
(1) B. R. Mehta Committee
(2) Yugandhar Committee
(3) Yoginder K. Alagh Committee
(4) Hota Committee
= 1
~ The Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957) had nothing to do with the civil services. It was set up to review the Community Development Programme (1952) and the National Extension Service, and it recommended 'democratic decentralisation' through a three-tier Panchayati Raj system: gram panchayat, panchayat samiti and zila parishad. Rajasthan was the first to adopt it, at Nagaur on 2 October 1959, followed by Andhra Pradesh. The other three committees all dealt with the civil services: the Y.K. Alagh Committee (2001) reviewed the Civil Services Examination conducted by the UPSC; the Yugandhar Committee (2003) looked at the training of IAS officers; and the P.C. Hota Committee (2004) suggested reforms in the civil services. Other civil-service bodies worth remembering are the D.S. Kothari Committee (1976) on recruitment and selection methods and the Satish Chandra Committee (1989), which reviewed the examination scheme. Trap: 'Mehta' also appears in the Ashok Mehta Committee (1977), which suggested a two-tier Panchayati Raj; both Mehtas belong to local government, not civil services.

Q 7 | cur | National & International
Which Union Ministry hosted the Purple Fest, 2025 at Rashtrapati Bhavan ?
(1) Ministry of Health and Family Welfare
(2) Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment
(3) Ministry of Women and Child Development
(4) Ministry of Home Affairs
= 2
~ Purple Fest 2025 at Rashtrapati Bhavan was organised by the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment through its Department of Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities (Divyangjan). Held in March 2025 in the presence of President Droupadi Murmu, it celebrated the talents of persons with disabilities through cultural performances, games, stalls and awareness activities, and promoted inclusion and accessibility. Purple is the colour linked worldwide with the disability-rights movement, which is why the festival carries that name. The other ministries are not the nodal agency for disability: Health and Family Welfare looks after public health and hospitals, Women and Child Development runs schemes such as Mission Shakti and Poshan, and Home Affairs deals with internal security and police. Related facts worth remembering: the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 recognises 21 types of disability, and the Accessible India Campaign (Sugamya Bharat Abhiyan) was launched in 2015 to make buildings, transport and websites barrier-free. The disability department was carved out as a separate Department in 2012. Memory tip: disability matters = Social Justice Ministry.

Q 8 | cur | National & International
Mount Lewotobi Laki-Laki, an active volcano, is located in which country ?
(1) Philippines
(2) Indonesia
(3) Japan
(4) Malaysia
= 2
~ Mount Lewotobi Laki-Laki is an active volcano on Flores Island in East Nusa Tenggara province, Indonesia. 'Laki-laki' means 'man' in Indonesian; it stands beside its twin peak, Lewotobi Perempuan ('woman'), so the pair is known locally as a husband-and-wife volcano. A deadly eruption in November 2024 killed several villagers and forced thousands to evacuate, and further powerful eruptions in 2025 threw ash columns kilometres high and disrupted flights to Bali. Indonesia has more active volcanoes than any other country because it lies on the Pacific 'Ring of Fire', where the Indo-Australian plate sinks beneath the Eurasian plate along the Sunda Arc. The other countries are wrong here even though some have famous volcanoes: the Philippines has Mayon and Taal, Japan has Mount Fuji and Sakurajima, and Malaysia has no active volcano at all. Trap: don't confuse Lewotobi with Merapi (Java) or Krakatoa (Sunda Strait), Indonesia's other well-known volcanoes. India's only active volcano is Barren Island in the Andaman Sea.

Q 9 | cur | National & International
BIMSTEC Youth Summit, 2025 was held in
(1) Myanmar
(2) Bangladesh
(3) Thailand
(4) India
= 4
~ India hosted the BIMSTEC Youth Summit 2025, the first such summit of the grouping, at Gandhinagar, Gujarat, in February 2025. Organised by the Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports with the Ministry of External Affairs, it brought together youth delegates from all member countries under the theme 'Youth as a Bridge for Intra-BIMSTEC Exchange', with talks on entrepreneurship, digital connectivity and sustainable development. BIMSTEC (Bay of Bengal Initiative for Multi-Sectoral Technical and Economic Cooperation) was formed by the Bangkok Declaration of 1997 and has seven members: five from South Asia (Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Nepal, Sri Lanka) and two from Southeast Asia (Myanmar, Thailand). Its permanent secretariat is in Dhaka. Thailand hosted the 6th BIMSTEC leaders' Summit in Bangkok in April 2025, where the Bangkok Vision 2030 was adopted and Bangladesh took over the chair, so don't confuse that summit with the youth summit held in India. Myanmar and Bangladesh are members but did not host this event. For Odisha, BIMSTEC matters because the State lies on the Bay of Bengal and has old sea links with Southeast Asia, celebrated every year in Bali Jatra.

Q 10 | his | Modern India & National Movement
Which one of the following is NOT correct ?
(1) First Governor-General of Fort William was Warren Hastings
(2) First Chief Justice of the Supreme Court in British India was Elijah Impey
(3) First Law Member of the Governor-General’s Council was Lord Amherst
(4) First Viceroy as well as the Governor-General of India was Lord Canning
= 3
~ The false statement is that Lord Amherst was the first Law Member of the Governor-General's Council. The Charter Act of 1833 added a fourth, law member to the Council, and Thomas Babington Macaulay was the first to hold the post (1834); he drafted the Indian Penal Code and wrote the famous 1835 Minute on English education. Lord Amherst was Governor-General from 1823 to 1828, remembered for the First Anglo-Burmese War and the Treaty of Yandabo (1826). The other statements are correct: Warren Hastings became the first Governor-General of the Presidency of Fort William (Bengal) under the Regulating Act, 1773; Sir Elijah Impey was the first Chief Justice of the Supreme Court set up at Calcutta in 1774; and Lord Canning, after the Government of India Act, 1858, became the first Viceroy as well as Governor-General. Remember the chain: first Governor-General of Bengal = Warren Hastings (1773); first Governor-General of India = Lord William Bentinck (1833); first Viceroy = Lord Canning (1858).

Q 11 | odk | Geography & Economy of Odisha
In Odisha State, ‘Hexadeltaic region’ is named after
(1) Six deltaic districts of the State
(2) Six coastal districts of the State
(3) Six rivers of the State
(4) Hexagonal shape of the region in the State
= 3
~ Odisha's coastal plain is called the 'Hexadeltaic region' because it is built by the deltas of six rivers. From north to south these are the Subarnarekha, Budhabalanga, Baitarani, Brahmani, Mahanadi and Rushikulya. Over thousands of years these rivers have dropped huge loads of silt where they meet the Bay of Bengal, joining their deltas into one fertile alluvial strip that is the State's rice bowl and its most densely populated belt. The name comes from 'hexa' (six) and 'deltaic', and it refers to rivers, not to districts, a stretch of coast or any hexagonal shape, which is why the other options are wrong. The Mahanadi delta is the largest, and Cuttack, Jagatsinghpur, Kendrapara and Puri lie on it. The plain is widest in the central Mahanadi delta and narrows towards the south near Chilika and Ganjam. It is also very cyclone-prone, as Phailin (2013) and Fani (2019) showed. Memory tip for the six rivers, north to south: 'Subarna, Budha, Baita, Brahma, Maha, Rushi'.

Q 12 | pol | Constitution & Polity
From which of the following countries, the provision of the nomination of Rajya Sabha members was borrowed ?
(1) Australia
(2) United Kingdom
(3) USA
(4) Ireland
= 4
~ The provision for nominating members to the Rajya Sabha was borrowed from the Constitution of Ireland. Under Article 80, the President nominates 12 members who have special knowledge or practical experience in literature, science, art and social service; the Irish Senate likewise has members nominated by the Prime Minister (Taoiseach). From Ireland India also took the Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV) and the method of electing the President through an electoral college. The other countries gave different features: the United Kingdom gave parliamentary government, the rule of law, single citizenship and the office of Speaker; the USA gave Fundamental Rights, judicial review, independence of the judiciary and the impeachment of the President; and Australia gave the Concurrent List, freedom of trade and commerce, and the joint sitting of the two Houses. A related point often asked: nominated Rajya Sabha members can vote in the election of the Vice-President but not in that of the President. Memory tip: Ireland = DPSP, nomination to the Rajya Sabha and the Presidential election method.

Q 13 | pol | Constitution & Polity
Consider the following statements about the Prime Minister and Union Council of Ministers in India :
1. The Prime Minister must always be a member of the Lok Sabha.
2. The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.
3. The Prime Minister can allocate portfolios to Ministers.
4. If someone becomes a Minister or Prime Minister without being an MP, such a person has to get elected to the Parliament within six months.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct ?
(1) Only 2, 3 and 4
(2) Only 1, 2 and 3
(3) Only 1, 3 and 4
(4) Only 1, 2 and 4
= 1
~ Statements 2, 3 and 4 are correct, but statement 1 is wrong. The Constitution does not require the Prime Minister to be a member of the Lok Sabha; he or she can belong to either House. Indira Gandhi was a Rajya Sabha member when she first became PM in 1966, and Manmohan Singh was in the Rajya Sabha throughout his tenure (2004–14). Under Article 75(3), the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha, so a no-confidence motion passed there brings down the whole ministry, including ministers who sit in the Rajya Sabha. The President appoints ministers on the PM's advice, and allocating and reshuffling portfolios is the PM's prerogative. Under Article 75(5), a person who is not an MP may become a minister or even PM, but must become a member of either House within six months or cease to hold office. Options B, C and D are wrong because each includes the false statement 1. The same rules apply in the States under Article 164 for the Chief Minister and State ministers.

Q 14 | pol | Constitution & Polity
What happens if the Rajya Sabha suggests Amendments to a money bill ?
(1) The Lok Sabha is bound to accept them
(2) The Lok Sabha may or may not accept them
(3) The bill is sent to a Joint Session of both the Houses
(4) The bill is automatically rejected
= 2
~ When the Rajya Sabha suggests amendments to a money bill, the Lok Sabha may or may not accept them. Under Article 109, a money bill can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha, and only on the President's recommendation. After the Lok Sabha passes it, the Rajya Sabha can only make recommendations and must return the bill within 14 days. The Lok Sabha is free to accept or reject all or any of these recommendations, and in either case the bill is deemed passed by both Houses in the form the Lok Sabha decides. If the Rajya Sabha does not return it within 14 days, it is deemed passed anyway. Option A is wrong because the Lok Sabha is not bound by the suggestions; option C is wrong because a joint sitting under Article 108 is possible only for ordinary bills, never for money bills or Constitution amendment bills; and option D is wrong because the Rajya Sabha has no power to reject a money bill. Remember too that Article 110 defines a money bill and the Speaker's decision on whether a bill is a money bill is final.

Q 15 | cur | National & International
What is “operation zero lines” ?
(1) Border between Bhutan and India
(2) Border between Bangladesh and India
(3) Border between Nepal and India
(4) Border between Myanmar and India
= 2
~ As per OPSC's key, 'operation zero lines' relates to the India–Bangladesh border. The 'zero line' is the actual international boundary line marked on the ground between the two countries. India and Bangladesh share about 4,096 km of land border, India's longest with any neighbour, running through West Bengal, Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram. It is guarded by the Border Security Force (BSF) on the Indian side and the Border Guard Bangladesh (BGB) on the other. Under the 1975 border guidelines agreed by the two countries, no defence structure is to be built within 150 yards of the zero line, so fencing close to the line often leads to disputes, as it did in early 2025. The Land Boundary Agreement of 2015 settled old enclave problems on this border through the exchange of 162 enclaves. Options A, C and D name India's borders with Bhutan, Nepal and Myanmar, which OPSC's key does not link to this term. Memory tip: BSF guards the Pakistan and Bangladesh borders, SSB the Nepal and Bhutan borders, Assam Rifles the Myanmar border and ITBP the China border.

Q 16 | geo | Environment & Ecology
Match the following : Significance evidence of Global Warming.
a. The edge of the West Antarctic ice sheet is shrinking — 1. At 400 feet every year
b. Glacial retreat increased seven fold in the — 2. Andes Mountain of Peru from 1978 to 1995
c. Glacial ice has shrunk — 3. Nearly 25 percent over the last 60 years in the Tien Shan Mountain, China
d. Almost half of all glacial ice melted away in the — 4. Russian Caucasus Mountain in the past four decades
(1) a-1, b-2, c-3, d-4
(2) a-1, b-2, c-4, d-3
(3) a-1, b-3, c-2, d-4
(4) a-2, b-1, c-3, d-4
= 1
~ The matching is a-1, b-2, c-3, d-4, because each phrase on the left is completed by the item with the same number. The edge of the West Antarctic ice sheet has been shrinking by about 400 feet every year (a-1); glacial retreat in the Andes of Peru increased sevenfold between 1978 and 1995 (b-2); glacial ice in the Tien Shan mountains of China shrank by nearly 25% over the last 60 years (c-3); and almost half of all glacial ice in the Russian Caucasus melted away in four decades (d-4). These figures are classic examples quoted in environment textbooks as evidence of global warming. Retreating glaciers threaten the water supply of millions of people downstream, and melting land ice raises sea level, a serious risk for low-lying coasts such as Odisha's. In India, the Gangotri glacier, the source of the Bhagirathi, is also retreating. The other options mix up the pairs; for example, option B would read 'almost half of all glacial ice melted away in the nearly 25 percent…', which makes no sense. Tip: in statement-completion matches, read each pair as one sentence.

Q 17 | geo | Indian & World Geography
Match the following Indian States on the International Boundaries.
a. Gujarat — 1. Bhutan and Bangladesh
b. Himachal Pradesh — 2. China and Nepal
c. Uttarakhand — 3. China
d. West Bengal — 4. Pakistan
(1) a-1, b-2, c-3, d-4
(2) a-2, b-1, c-4, d-3
(3) a-3, b-4, c-1, d-2
(4) a-4, b-3, c-2, d-1
= 4
~ The correct matching is a-4, b-3, c-2, d-1. Gujarat shares its international border with Pakistan along the Rann of Kutch, including the disputed Sir Creek (a-4). Himachal Pradesh touches only China (the Tibet region), in Kinnaur and Lahaul-Spiti districts (b-3). Uttarakhand borders China (Tibet) in the north and Nepal in the east, where the Kali (Mahakali) river forms the boundary (c-2). West Bengal borders Bangladesh and Bhutan, and also Nepal, which the option does not mention (d-1). The other options mismatch these; for instance, option A pairs Gujarat with Bhutan and Bangladesh. Useful related facts: Rajasthan has the longest border with Pakistan among the States, West Bengal has the longest border with Bangladesh, and West Bengal, Sikkim and Arunachal Pradesh each border three countries. Odisha has no international land border, though its coast faces the Bay of Bengal. Memory tip: west coast State → Pakistan (Gujarat); hill State with only Tibet → China (Himachal); Tibet plus Nepal → Uttarakhand; eastern plains → Bangladesh and Bhutan (West Bengal).

Q 18 | eco | Indian Economy
Which of the following is NOT a function of the Reserve Bank of India ?
(1) Monetary Policy Regulation
(2) Currency Issuance
(3) Regulation of Stock Exchanges
(4) Lender of the Last Resort
= 3
~ Regulation of stock exchanges is not a function of the RBI; it belongs to the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI), which got statutory powers under the SEBI Act, 1992 and oversees stock exchanges such as the BSE and NSE, brokers and mutual funds. The RBI, set up on 1 April 1935 under the RBI Act, 1934 and nationalised in 1949, performs the other three functions. It frames monetary policy through the six-member Monetary Policy Committee, which sets the repo rate to keep inflation within the 4% ± 2% target. It has the sole right to issue bank notes under Section 22 of the RBI Act (the ₹1 note and coins are issued by the Government of India). It acts as lender of last resort, lending to banks in a crisis so that the system does not collapse. The RBI is also banker to the government, the bankers' bank, regulator of banks and NBFCs, and manager of foreign exchange under FEMA, 1999. Trap: insurance is regulated by IRDAI and pensions by PFRDA, not by the RBI.

Q 19 | geo | Environment & Ecology
The Kyoto Protocol was adopted on
(1) 11th December 1997
(2) 21st December 2004
(3) 10th December 1987
(4) 09th December 2003
= 1
~ The Kyoto Protocol was adopted on 11 December 1997 at the third Conference of the Parties (COP-3) to the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change, held in Kyoto, Japan. It entered into force on 16 February 2005, once Russia's ratification brought in enough parties. It set legally binding targets for developed (Annex I) countries to cut six greenhouse gases by an average of about 5% below 1990 levels in the first commitment period, 2008–2012; the Doha Amendment (2012) added a second period, 2013–2020. It also created market-based tools: emissions trading, Joint Implementation and the Clean Development Mechanism (CDM), under which many Indian projects earned carbon credits. India, as a developing country, had no binding target, and the USA signed but never ratified the protocol. The other dates are distractors: 1987 recalls the Montreal Protocol on ozone-depleting substances (adopted that September), while 2003 and 2004 have no such link. The Paris Agreement (2015) later took over as the main climate treaty. Memory tip: Kyoto = 1997, COP-3; Paris = 2015, COP-21.

Q 20 | geo | Indian & World Geography
Which is the deepest part of the ocean in the world ?
(1) The Challenger Deep in the Mariana Trench
(2) The Horizon Deep in the Tonga Trench
(3) South Sandwich Trench in the South Atlantic Ocean
(4) Oceanic Trench in the Indian Ocean
= 1
~ The Challenger Deep, at the southern end of the Mariana Trench in the western Pacific Ocean near Guam, is the deepest known point of the world's oceans, nearly 11 km (about 10,900 m) below sea level. The trench forms where the Pacific plate sinks beneath the smaller Mariana plate, a classic ocean-to-ocean subduction zone. It is so deep that Mount Everest (8,849 m) would fit inside it with about 2 km of water to spare. Jacques Piccard and Don Walsh first reached its bottom in 1960 in the bathyscaphe Trieste, and James Cameron made a solo dive there in 2012. The other options are shallower: the Horizon Deep in the Tonga Trench (about 10,800 m) is the second-deepest point; the South Sandwich Trench is the deepest in the far South Atlantic, at a little over 8,000 m; and the deepest part of the Indian Ocean, the Java (Sunda) Trench, is just over 7,000 m deep. Memory tip: 'Mariana = Maximum'; the deepest trenches lie in the Pacific, along the Ring of Fire.

Q 21 | pol | Constitution & Polity
Which of the following statements regarding maternity leave in India as interpreted by the Supreme Court recently is/are INCORRECT ?
1. The Supreme Court held that maternity leave is a constitutional right under Article 21 – Right to life.
2. Maternity leave benefits are applicable only up to two children, beyond which no benefits will be provided.
3. Maternity leave is a form of paid leave guaranteed under Article 19 of the Constitution.
(1) 2 and 3
(2) 1 and 2
(3) 1 and 3
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1
~ Statements 2 and 3 are the incorrect ones. In K. Umadevi v. State of Tamil Nadu (May 2025), the Supreme Court held that maternity benefits are part of a woman's reproductive rights, which flow from the right to life and personal liberty under Article 21, so statement 1 is correct. The case involved a Tamil Nadu government employee who was refused maternity leave for her third child because State rules limited the benefit to two children. The Court allowed her the leave, noting that her first two children were born from an earlier marriage and she had not availed maternity benefits for them. So statement 2, which says benefits stop completely after two children, is wrong. Statement 3 is also wrong because the Court did not base the right on Article 19, which deals with freedoms such as speech, movement and profession. Background worth knowing: the Maternity Benefit Act, 1961, as amended in 2017, gives 26 weeks of paid leave for the first two children and 12 weeks for later ones. Tip: 'life and dignity' rights are almost always traced to Article 21.

Q 22 | cur | National & International
Which of the following countries NOT participated in the anti-submarine warfare exercise Sea Dragon, 2025, held off the Coast of Guam, USA ?
(1) India
(2) Japan
(3) South Korea
(4) France
= 4
~ France did not take part in Exercise Sea Dragon 2025. Sea Dragon is a US-led multinational anti-submarine warfare (ASW) exercise flown from Andersen Air Force Base in Guam, in the western Pacific. The 2025 edition, held over two weeks in March 2025, brought together five Indo-Pacific nations: Australia, India, Japan, South Korea and the United States. Their maritime patrol aircraft crews practised tracking a real US submarine and simulated targets and were scored on their performance; Australia won the top 'Dragon Belt' award. India took part with the Indian Navy's P-8I long-range maritime reconnaissance aircraft, Japan with the Kawasaki P-1 and South Korea with the P-3 Orion, so options A, B and C are all wrong. Sea Dragon reflects India's growing defence ties in the Indo-Pacific, along with the Malabar naval exercise of the four Quad navies (India, US, Japan, Australia). France and India hold their own bilateral naval exercise, Varuna. Trap: don't confuse Sea Dragon with RIMPAC, the much larger exercise hosted by the US around Hawaii every two years.

Q 23 | cur | Sports, Awards & Misc.
Where is SpaceX’s headquarters located ?
(1) Seattle, Washington
(2) Palo Alto, California
(3) Hawthorne, California
(4) Austin, Texas
= 3,4
~ OPSC accepted both Hawthorne, California and Austin, Texas for this question, because SpaceX's headquarters changed around this time. SpaceX, founded by Elon Musk in 2002, had its headquarters for many years in Hawthorne, near Los Angeles, where it builds its Falcon rockets and Dragon capsules. SpaceX re-incorporated in Texas in February 2024, and in July 2024 Musk announced that its headquarters would move there; its new headquarters is at Starbase, its Starship building and launch site near Brownsville in south Texas, which became an official city in May 2025. Austin itself is not SpaceX's headquarters (Tesla moved its headquarters there in 2021), but since the move was to Texas, OPSC gave credit for option D as well. The other options are wrong: Seattle, Washington is home to Amazon, and Jeff Bezos's rocket company Blue Origin is based nearby in Kent; Palo Alto, California is where Tesla had its headquarters before moving to Austin. Trap: Tesla = Austin, SpaceX = Hawthorne earlier and Starbase now.

Q 24 | cur | National & International
Which country announced the ‘Golden Dome’ missile defence system ?
(1) Russia
(2) Israel
(3) United States
(4) China
= 3

Q 60 | geo | Indian & World Geography
Which river crosses the equator twice ?
(1) Amazon
(2) Nile
(3) Congo
(4) Niger
= 3
~ The Congo River is the only major river in the world that crosses the equator twice. (2) Only 1 and 3
(3) Only 2 and 3
(4) Only 3
= 4
~ Only statement 3 is correct. A scramjet (supersonic combustion ramjet) is an air-breathing engine in which the air stays supersonic while the fuel burns, which lets it work efficiently at hypersonic speeds, above Mach 5. Statement 1 is wrong: a scramjet has no compressor or turbine, that is, no moving parts; the vehicle's own high speed rams the air in, and the shape of the inlet compresses it. Don't mix up: in a ramjet the air is slowed to subsonic speed before burning (best near Mach 3), while in a scramjet it burns at supersonic speed.

Statement 1 is wrong because Pralay is neither air-launched nor a cruise missile. Statement 2 is wrong because its range is 150–500 km, not 700–1,500 km, and it is meant for the Army and the Air Force to strike battlefield targets, not for naval warfare. Odisha angle: Pralay was first tested in December 2021, and in July 2025 DRDO carried out two back-to-back flight tests, all from Dr APJ Abdul Kalam Island off the Odisha coast. Memory tip: Pralay (doomsday) = land-to-land, 150–500 km; don't confuse it with BrahMos, a supersonic cruise missile.

Q 93 | his | Modern India & National Movement

Q 94 | geo | Indian & World Geography
Consider the following statements with reference to climate :
I. Indian ocean dipole is also known as ‘Indian Nino’.
II. It is similar to the El nino phenomenon.
Select the correct answer from the code given below :
Code :
(1) Only I
(2) Both I and II
(3) Only II
(4) Neither I nor II
= 2
~ Both statements are correct. The Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD) is the difference in sea-surface temperature between the western Indian Ocean (the Arabian Sea side, near Africa) and the eastern Indian Ocean (near Sumatra and Java in Indonesia). Like El Niño in the Pacific, it is an ocean–atmosphere 'see-saw' that swings between positive, neutral and negative phases, so it is also called the 'Indian Niño' (statement I) and behaves in a way similar to El Niño (statement II). In a positive IOD the western side is warmer than normal, which tends to bring more rain to India during the south-west monsoon and drier weather to Australia and Indonesia; a negative IOD does the opposite. A positive IOD can partly offset the drying effect of El Niño, as it did in 1997. Scientists formally described the IOD in 1999. Memory tip: ENSO (El Niño–Southern Oscillation) = Pacific Ocean; IOD = Indian Ocean. Trap: the IOD is a separate system, not part of El Niño, even though the two often interact and both are watched by IMD before each monsoon.

Q 95 | eco | Indian Economy
Which Five Year Plan introduced the concept of ‘Rolling Plan’ in India ?
(1) First Plan
(2) Second Plan
(3) Third Plan
(4) None of the above
= 4
~ The Rolling Plan was not part of the First, Second or Third Plan, so the answer is 'None of the above'. It was adopted by the Janata Party government of Morarji Desai, which ended the Fifth Five Year Plan (1974–79) a year early, in 1978, and ran a Rolling Plan for 1978–80. The idea comes from the economist Gunnar Myrdal. Under it there were three plans: an annual plan with the budget, a five-year plan whose targets were revised every year (so it 'rolled' forward), and a long-term perspective plan of 10, 15 or 20 years. It gave flexibility, but frequent changes made targets unstable, and the Congress government that returned in 1980 dropped it and started the Sixth Plan (1980–85). The First Plan (A, 1951–56) focused on agriculture (Harrod–Domar model), the Second (B, 1956–61) on heavy industry (Mahalanobis model), and the Third (C, 1961–66) on a self-reliant economy; after it came the plan holiday of 1966–69. Trap: the plan holiday and the Rolling Plan are two different breaks.

Q 97 | eco | Indian Economy
Which tax reform in India aimed to unify multiple indirect taxes into a single tax ?
(1) GST
(2) VAT
(3) Excise Duty
(4) Customs Duty
= 1
~ GST (Goods and Services Tax) is the reform that merged many indirect taxes into one. It came into force on 1 July 2017 under the 101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016. It is a destination-based tax levied at each stage of supply, with credit for tax already paid, and it has three parts: CGST and SGST on sales within a State, and IGST on sales between States. VAT (B) was a State-level sales-tax reform of 2005; excise duty (C) was one of the taxes absorbed; and basic customs duty (D) on imports still stays outside GST. Q 100 | eco | Indian Economy
Which of the following is NOT a component of India’s fiscal deficit ?
(1) Revenue Deficit
(2) Primary Deficit
(3) Monetary Deficit
(4) Effective Revenue Deficit
= 3
~ 'Monetary deficit' is not a component of India's fiscal deficit; it is not even a standard budget term. Fiscal deficit = total expenditure − (revenue receipts + non-debt capital receipts such as loan recoveries and disinvestment), that is, the government's total borrowing need in a year. Its parts and related measures are: revenue deficit (A) = revenue expenditure − revenue receipts, which shows borrowing for day-to-day spending; effective revenue deficit (D) = revenue deficit − grants given for creating capital assets, a measure introduced in the 2011–12 Budget; and primary deficit (B) = fiscal deficit − interest payments, which shows borrowing for needs other than paying interest on old debts. The similar-sounding 'monetised deficit' means the increase in the RBI's net credit to the Government, that is, deficit financed by creating money; automatic monetisation through ad hoc treasury bills ended in 1997. Targets for these deficits are set under the FRBM Act, 2003. Trap: 'monetised deficit' is a real term, but 'monetary deficit' is the odd one out here.
