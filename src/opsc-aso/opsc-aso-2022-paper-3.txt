# Paper III English (Q1-100) and Odia (Q101-200), OPSC Assistant Section Officer (Odisha Secretariat) written exam of 27 August 2022, Advt. No. 26 of 2021-22, Series A (OPSC's own booklet); answers: OPSC's answer key published with the result (9 January 2024)

Q 1 | eng | Grammar
Change the active voice into passive voice : I will clean the house every Saturday. The house ______ by me every Saturday.
(1) cleaned
(2) will cleaned
(3) will be cleaned
(4) None of these
= 3
~ The passive form is 'will be cleaned'. In the simple future tense, the passive voice is made with will + be + past participle. The object of the active sentence ('the house') becomes the subject, and the doer ('I') moves to a by-phrase as 'by me'. So 'I will clean the house every Saturday' becomes 'The house will be cleaned by me every Saturday'. The tense stays future, and the time phrase 'every Saturday' stays as it is. 'Cleaned' alone turns the sentence into the simple past and even makes the house the doer ('The house cleaned'). 'Will cleaned' is ungrammatical, since a modal such as will must be followed by a base verb, here 'be'. 'None of these' is wrong because option C is correct. Another example: 'She will write a letter' becomes 'A letter will be written by her'. Memory tip for passives: keep the tense of the active verb, add the right form of 'be', then the past participle (V3), and change the pronoun 'I' to 'me' in the by-phrase.

Q 2 | eng | Grammar
People ______ walk on grass.
(1) couldn't
(2) needn't
(3) mustn't
(4) may not
= 3
~ 'Mustn't' (must not) expresses a prohibition, which is exactly what a public rule such as 'People mustn't walk on grass' means: walking on the grass is not allowed. 'Needn't' means something is not necessary, so 'People needn't walk on grass' would make walking on it optional, which is not a rule at all. 'Couldn't' refers to inability, usually in the past ('I couldn't swim as a child'), not to a rule. 'May not' can refuse permission in formal English, but it is softer and is also used for possibility ('It may not rain'), so it does not carry the firm, general ban the sentence needs. Key contrast to remember: mustn't = it is forbidden; needn't or don't have to = it is not necessary. Example: 'You mustn't use your phone in the exam hall' (forbidden) versus 'You needn't bring a pen; we will provide one' (not necessary). Signboards such as 'Keep off the grass' express the same idea as the sentence in the question.

Q 3 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct word order :
(1) Why she did leave so early ?
(2) Why did she leave so early ?
(3) Why did so early she leave ?
(4) Why so early did she leave ?
= 2
~ The correct order is 'Why did she leave so early?' In English wh-questions the order is: question word + auxiliary + subject + main verb + the rest. Here: Why (question word) + did (auxiliary) + she (subject) + leave (main verb in base form) + so early (adverbial). When the statement has no auxiliary ('She left early'), we add do, does or did and change the main verb to its base form, so 'left' becomes 'did ... leave'. Option A, 'Why she did leave so early?', keeps the statement order (subject before auxiliary); that order is used only in indirect questions such as 'I wonder why she left so early.' Option C wrongly puts 'so early' between the auxiliary 'did' and the subject 'she', and option D moves 'so early' in front of the auxiliary, which is unnatural in ordinary English. Another example: 'Where does he work?' (not 'Where he does work?'). Memory tip: QASV, that is, Question word, Auxiliary, Subject, Verb.

Q 4 | eng | Vocabulary
Select same meaning of the underlined word : Take care not to 'get hurt' with the steam from the Kettle.
(1) Burnt
(2) Bruised
(3) Scalded
(4) Cut
= 3
~ 'Scalded' is the exact word, because to scald means to burn the skin with hot liquid or steam. Steam from a kettle injures by scalding, so 'get hurt' here means 'get scalded'. 'Burnt' is broader and is mostly used for injury by fire or dry heat, such as touching a hot iron, so it is less precise for steam. 'Bruised' describes an injury from a blow that discolours the skin without breaking it, such as after a fall. 'Cut' is an injury from a sharp edge, such as a knife or broken glass. Such questions test the precise word for a particular situation, not just a general synonym. Related words: a 'scald' is also the name of the injury itself; 'singe' means to burn slightly on the surface, as with hair; and 'sear' means to burn the surface quickly with strong heat, as in cooking. Example: 'He scalded his tongue on the hot tea.' Memory tip: scald = hot liquid or steam; burn = fire or dry heat.

Q 5 | eng | Grammar
______ his principles, he has to be very careful.
(1) With regard of
(2) With regard on
(3) With regard to
(4) None of these
= 3
~ 'With regard to' is the fixed phrase, meaning 'concerning', 'about' or 'in respect of'. The sentence becomes: 'With regard to his principles, he has to be very careful.' In this phrase the noun 'regard' always takes 'to', so 'with regard of' and 'with regard on' are not idiomatic English. Related correct forms are 'in regard to', 'as regards' and 'regarding', which all mean the same. But 'in regards to' and 'with regards to' are treated as errors in formal writing; the plural 'regards' is used in greetings such as 'Kind regards' or 'Give him my regards'. Option D is wrong because option C is correct. Another example: 'With regard to your application, we will reply within a week.' Fixed prepositional phrases like this are common in official letters and notes, so they are useful in an Assistant Section Officer's drafting work too. Memory tip: regard goes with 'to', as in 'with regard to' and 'in regard to'.

Q 6 | eng | Grammar
He was seen ______ to the school.
(1) went
(2) going
(3) gone
(4) go
= 2
~ 'Going' is correct: 'He was seen going to the school.' Verbs of perception such as see, hear, watch and notice follow two patterns. In the active voice they take an object + bare infinitive (for a complete action) or an object + present participle (for an action in progress): 'I saw him go' or 'I saw him going'. In the passive voice, the bare infinitive is not allowed; we use the to-infinitive or the -ing form: 'He was seen to go' or 'He was seen going'. Here the action was in progress, so 'going' fits. 'Go' (bare infinitive) is wrong after the passive 'was seen'. 'Went' is a finite past-tense verb and cannot follow another finite verb directly. 'Gone' is a past participle, which would need 'have' or a passive meaning. Another example: 'The thief was heard climbing the stairs' (active: 'We heard the thief climb' or 'climbing the stairs'). Memory tip: active saw him go; passive was seen to go or was seen going.

Q 7 | eng | Grammar
They organized a party ______ his honour. That Ajita was not invited ______ it, was a shame. She admired him most and it seems unlikely that she would forgive the organizers ______ their omission.
(1) at, for, towards
(2) about, at, against
(3) in, to, for
(4) after, on, with
= 3
~ The answer is 'in, to, for'. Three fixed expressions are tested. First, 'in his honour' means to show respect for someone, as in 'a party in his honour' or 'a memorial in honour of the martyrs'. Second, a person is 'invited to' an event: 'That Ajita was not invited to it was a shame.' Third, we 'forgive someone for' a fault: 'forgive the organizers for their omission'. Option A fails because 'at his honour' and 'forgive ... towards' are not idiomatic. Option B's 'about his honour' and 'forgive ... against' are not English. Option D's 'after his honour' is wrong (though 'named after' is a correct, different phrase), and 'invited on it' and 'forgive ... with' are wrong too. Tip: with several blanks, solve the easiest blank first. 'Forgive ... for' alone points to option C, the only option ending in 'for'. Similar verb + for pairs to remember: apologise for, thank someone for, blame someone for; but congratulate someone on.

Q 8 | eng | Vocabulary
When I say that the benefits of demonetization are moot, what do I mean ?
(1) That I do not understand what the benefits are.
(2) That I do not understand what demonetization is.
(3) That the benefits of demonetization are surely seen.
(4) That the benefits of demonetization are debatable.
= 4
~ 'Moot' means open to argument or doubtful, so saying that the benefits of demonetization are moot means they are debatable. The word comes from the Old English 'moot', a meeting or assembly where matters were discussed, and a 'moot point' is one on which people can reasonably disagree. Law students argue practice cases in a 'moot court'. Option C says the opposite: if the benefits are 'surely seen', they are certain, not moot. Options A and B are about the speaker not understanding the benefits or the policy, which is a different idea; moot is about disagreement, not ignorance. Note a second use, common in American legal English: a moot point can also be one that no longer matters because the situation has changed. Here, with the benefits of a policy (India's withdrawal of old ₹500 and ₹1,000 notes in November 2016), the sense is clearly 'debatable'. Synonyms: arguable, disputable, controversial. Antonyms: certain, settled, indisputable. Memory tip: moot → meeting → discussion → debatable.

Q 9 | eng | Grammar
Choose matching prepositions to complete the following phrases :
(I) According ______ the source ... — (i) of
(II) Different ______ the first draft ... — (ii) to
(III) Based ______ this information ... — (iii) by
(IV) Bored ______ the events ... — (iv) from
(V) Consisting ______ cards ... — (v) on
According to the code, the correctly matched pairs would be :
(1) I – (ii), II – (iv), III – (v), IV – (iii), V – (i)
(2) I – (i), II – (ii), III – (iii), IV – (v), V – (iv)
(3) I – (v), II – (iii), III – (i), IV – (ii), V – (iv)
(4) I – (ii), II – (v), III – (i), IV – (iii), V – (iv)
= 1
~ The correct matching is I–(ii), II–(iv), III–(v), IV–(iii), V–(i), that is: according to, different from, based on, bored by and consisting of. These are fixed (dependent) prepositions that go with particular words. 'According to the source' is the only standard form. 'Different from the first draft' is the form preferred in formal British and Indian English, while 'different than' is common in American usage. 'Based on this information' is correct; 'based off' is informal. 'Bored by the events' fits here, and 'bored with' is also standard, but 'bored of' is informal. 'Consisting of cards' is correct, since consist takes 'of' when it means 'made up of'. Options B and C go wrong in the very first pair ('according of', 'according on'). Option D starts correctly with 'according to' but then gives 'different on' and 'based of', so it fails. Tip: use one sure pair to eliminate options quickly; only A and D have 'according to', and only A has 'different from'.

Q 10 | eng | Grammar
He had earned his well-paid job after a long struggle, but when he laughed at the most inopportune time, it cost him the job.
List the verbs in this sentence :
(1) earned, struggle, laughed
(2) had earned, laughed, cost
(3) struggle, laughed, cost
(4) job, laughed, inopportune
= 2
~ The verbs are 'had earned', 'laughed' and 'cost'. A verb shows an action or state and has a subject: 'he had earned' (past perfect: had + past participle), 'he laughed' (simple past) and 'it cost' (simple past; cost has the same form in all three parts: cost–cost–cost). Option A wrongly includes 'struggle': after 'a long' it is a noun (the thing he went through), though the same word is a verb in 'They struggle daily'. Option C makes the same mistake. Option D lists 'job', a noun, and 'inopportune', an adjective meaning 'badly timed', which describes 'time'. Also notice that 'well-paid' is a compound adjective describing 'job', and that 'earned' alone in option A is incomplete, since the full verb phrase is 'had earned'. Tip: to test whether a word is a verb, look for its subject and tense; to test for a noun, see whether 'a', 'the' or an adjective comes before it. Another example: 'Their fight (noun) ended, but now they fight (verb) again.'

Q 11 | eng | Grammar
Which of the statements about the following sentence is TRUE ?
The Raos had been living so frugally all year that they saved enough money for a Mercedes.
(1) The present perfect continuous tense used here indicates a state that continues into the present.
(2) The past perfect continuous tense used here indicates an ongoing state prior to a past action.
(3) The perfect tense used here is not clear enough to justify the state indicated in the present.
(4) The past perfect tense used here does not indicate an ongoing state prior to a past action.
= 2
~ Statement B is true: 'had been living' is the past perfect continuous tense, which shows an action or state that went on over a period up to a point in the past. Its form is had + been + verb-ing. Here the Raos' frugal living continued all year before a past result, namely that they saved enough money for a Mercedes. Option A is wrong because the present perfect continuous uses 'has/have been living' and links to the present, not to a past point. Option C is wrong because the tense is clear and the sentence is about the past, not about a state in the present. Option D is wrong because the sentence uses the past perfect continuous, not the simple past perfect ('had lived'), and it does show an ongoing state before a past action. The 'so ... that' structure shows the result: they lived so frugally that they saved enough. Another example: 'She had been studying for three hours when the power went off.' Memory tip: had been + -ing = a long action before another past event.

Q 12 | eng | Grammar
Choose from the options how the sentence can be improved :
They could not admire his bright performance because of they dislike him.
(1) because of their disliking of him
(2) because of their dislike him
(3) because they dislike him
(4) No correction required
= 3
~ The improvement is 'because they dislike him'. 'Because of' is a compound preposition, so it must be followed by a noun or noun phrase ('because of their dislike for him'), never by a clause with its own subject and verb. 'Because' alone is a conjunction and introduces a clause ('because they dislike him'). The original sentence puts a clause ('they dislike him') after 'because of', which is the error. Option A, 'because of their disliking of him', is clumsy and unnatural, since the noun 'dislike' is the normal form. Option B, 'because of their dislike him', is ungrammatical: 'their dislike' is a noun phrase, so 'him' is left hanging and would need 'of' or 'for' before it. Option D is wrong because there is an error to correct. Another example: 'The match was stopped because of rain' (noun) versus 'The match was stopped because it rained' (clause). Similar pairs to learn together: despite / although, during / while, due to / since.

Q 13 | eng | Grammar
Choose from the options how the sentence can be improved :
She was uneasy because she never be on a plane before.
(1) had never been
(2) never been
(3) is never been
(4) No improvement
= 1
~ The improvement is 'had never been'. The sentence describes a past moment (she was uneasy), and her lack of flying experience comes before that moment, so the past perfect (had + past participle) is needed: 'She was uneasy because she had never been on a plane before.' The word 'before' also signals a time earlier than the past moment. 'Never be' in the original is wrong because a past-time clause cannot use the bare form 'be' without an auxiliary. Option B, 'never been', lacks the auxiliary 'had'; 'been' is a past participle and cannot stand alone as the verb. Option C, 'is never been', wrongly joins the present 'is' with 'been' and also clashes with 'was'. Option D is wrong because the error must be fixed. Another example: 'He was nervous because he had never spoken in public before.' Memory tip: past + earlier past → had + V3. If the main clause were in the present ('She is uneasy'), the right form would be 'has never been'.

Q 14 | eng | Grammar
Choose from the options how the sentence can be improved :
They only work when they have no money.
(1) When they have no money, they only work
(2) they only work
(3) work only when
(4) No improvement
= 3
~ The improvement is 'work only when': 'They work only when they have no money.' The rule is that 'only' should be placed just before the word or phrase it limits. The intended meaning is that their working is limited to the times when they have no money, so 'only' must qualify the when-clause. In the original, 'They only work when ...' can be read as 'they do nothing except work' at those times, which is not the idea. Option A, 'When they have no money, they only work', keeps the same misplacement and suggests they do nothing but work when they are broke. Option B repeats the faulty order, and option D is wrong because the sentence needs correction. In casual speech 'only' often drifts towards the verb, but exams expect the strict rule. Example: 'Only I met him' (no one else met him); 'I only met him' (I did nothing more than meet him); 'I met only him' (I met no one else). Memory tip: put 'only' right next to what it limits.

Q 15 | eng | Grammar
Choose from the options how the sentence can be improved :
I. T. experts may have to stop hunting for better job opportunities as these are not so easily available now.
(1) No correction required
(2) may be stopping the hunting
(3) should have to stop hunt
(4) might have to stop hunting
= 1
~ No correction is required. 'May have to' combines the modal 'may' (possibility) with 'have to' (necessity), so 'may have to stop' means they could be forced to stop. 'Stop + -ing' means to give up an activity, so 'stop hunting' means giving up the search for better jobs. The reason clause 'as these are not so easily available now' is also correct, with 'these' referring to job opportunities. Option B, 'may be stopping the hunting', is wrong; the continuous form changes the sense and 'the hunting' is awkward. Option C, 'should have to stop hunt', is wrong twice: 'should have to' is unidiomatic, and 'stop' needs the gerund 'hunting', not the base form 'hunt'. Option D, 'might have to stop hunting', is grammatical, but it only swaps one modal of possibility for another, so it is not a correction. Note the difference: 'stop hunting' (give up hunting) versus 'stop to hunt' (pause something else in order to hunt). Example: 'He stopped smoking' versus 'He stopped to smoke'.

Q 16 | eng | Grammar
Choose from the options how the sentence can be improved :
The NYC building at New York City is as high as every other building in the United States of America.
(1) high than every other building
(2) as high as every other building
(3) higher than any other building
(4) No improvement
= 3
~ The improvement is 'higher than any other building'. To show that one thing is greater than all the others of its group, use comparative + than + any other + singular noun: 'The NYC building ... is higher than any other building in the United States of America.' The original 'as high as every other building' only says it is equal in height to each one, which defeats the point of the comparison. Option A, 'high than every other building', is ungrammatical because 'than' needs a comparative form (higher). Option B repeats the original error, and option D is wrong because a correction is needed. The word 'other' is essential: 'higher than any building in the USA' would compare the building with itself, since it is also in the USA. The same idea can be written with the superlative: 'the highest building in the United States of America'. Another example: 'Mount Everest is higher than any other mountain in the world.' Note also that 'in New York City' would be better than 'at New York City'.

Q 17 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct part of speech for the words in bracket :
She (as well as) her sister is engaged.
(1) Conjunction
(2) Preposition
(3) Adjective
(4) Adverb
= 1
~ 'As well as' works here as a conjunction (a compound or phrasal conjunction), because it joins two subjects of the verb, 'She' and 'her sister'. Traditional school grammar lists it among compound conjunctions, along with 'as soon as', 'so that' and 'even if'. It is not an adjective, as it does not describe a noun, and it is not an adverb here, although 'as well' alone can be an adverb meaning 'too' ('She came as well'). Some modern grammars treat 'as well as' like a preposition, but the key follows the conjunction label. An important agreement rule goes with it: when a subject is followed by 'as well as', 'along with', 'together with' or 'in addition to', the verb agrees with the first subject. So 'She as well as her sister is engaged' takes the singular 'is', because 'She' is singular. Another example: 'The teacher, as well as the students, was present.' Memory tip: as well as / along with / together with → the verb follows the first subject.

Q 18 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct part of speech for the word in bracket :
This politician is notorious (for) corruption.
(1) Conjunction
(2) Preposition
(3) Adjective
(4) Adverb
= 2
~ 'For' is a preposition here. A preposition stands before a noun or pronoun and shows its relation to another word in the sentence. In 'This politician is notorious for corruption', 'for' links the noun 'corruption' to the adjective 'notorious', telling us what he is notorious for. Many adjectives take a fixed preposition: notorious for, famous for, known for, responsible for, fond of, good at. 'For' can also be a conjunction, but only when it joins two clauses and means 'because', usually after a comma: 'He did not come, for he was ill.' Here it is followed by a single noun, not a clause, so it cannot be a conjunction. It is not an adjective, since it describes nothing, and not an adverb, since it does not modify a verb. Note the meaning as well: 'notorious' means well known for something bad, while 'famous' is used for something good. Memory tip: 'for' + noun → preposition; 'for' + clause → conjunction.

Q 19 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct part of speech for the word in bracket :
This golden ring is too (costly) to purchase.
(1) Conjunction
(2) Preposition
(3) Adjective
(4) Adverb
= 3
~ 'Costly' is an adjective here. It describes the noun 'ring', telling us what kind of ring it is (expensive), and it comes after the linking verb 'is' as a subject complement: 'This golden ring is costly.' The adverb 'too' modifies it, and the pattern too + adjective + to-infinitive shows that a quality is more than enough to prevent an action: 'too costly to purchase' means so costly that it cannot be bought. Trap: words ending in -ly are not always adverbs. Costly, friendly, lovely, lonely, lively, ugly and elderly are adjectives, several of them formed from nouns (cost + ly, friend + ly). An adverb in -ly usually comes from an adjective (quick → quickly) and modifies a verb, an adjective or another adverb. 'Costly' is not a conjunction, as it joins nothing, and not a preposition, as it has no object. A useful test: if 'very' can go before it and it can follow 'is' to describe a noun, it is an adjective. Another example: 'She has a friendly smile.'

Q 20 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct part of speech for the word in bracket :
A(drowning) man catches at a straw.
(1) Complement
(2) Infinitive
(3) Gerund
(4) Participle
= 3
~ OPSC's key marks 'drowning' as a gerund, treating this -ing word, formed from the verb 'drown' and placed before the noun 'man', as the verbal-noun form. Both gerunds and present participles end in -ing, so students should also know the standard test that many grammar books use. When an -ing word before a noun shows purpose or use, it is a gerund: 'a swimming pool' is a pool for swimming, and 'a walking stick' is a stick for walking. When it shows an action that the noun itself is doing, it is a present participle used as an adjective: 'a drowning man' is a man who is drowning, like 'a sleeping baby' or 'a running train'. By that test, many books call 'drowning' here a participle. The other options do not fit: a complement completes the meaning of a verb such as 'is' ('He is a doctor'), and an infinitive is 'to' + base verb ('to drown'). The proverb means that a person in great trouble will grab even the smallest chance of help. (Official OPSC key; some sources differ.)

Q 21 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct part of speech for the word in bracket :
A great talker is a great (liar).
(1) Article
(2) Verb
(3) Pronoun
(4) Noun
= 4
~ 'Liar' is a noun: it names a person, someone who tells lies. Two clues confirm it. It comes after the article 'a', and it is qualified by the adjective 'great' ('a great liar'), exactly as 'talker' is in the first half of the sentence. It also works as the complement of the linking verb 'is', renaming the subject 'a great talker'. It is not a verb: the verb is 'lie' (lie, lied, lied when it means telling an untruth), while 'liar' is the agent noun made by adding a suffix, like teach → teacher, beg → beggar and act → actor. It is not a pronoun, because it does not stand in place of a noun, and it is not an article, since only 'a', 'an' and 'the' are articles. The proverb means that people who talk too much tend to exaggerate or say untrue things. Memory tip: a word coming right after 'a/an/the' + adjective is almost always a noun. Don't mix up 'lie' (tell an untruth) with 'lie' (rest flat: lie, lay, lain).

Q 22 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct part of speech for the words in bracket :
Eat (to live), but do not live to eat.
(1) Complement
(2) Infinitive
(3) Gerund
(4) Participle
= 2
~ 'To live' is an infinitive: the marker 'to' + the base form of the verb ('live'). In 'Eat to live, but do not live to eat', it expresses purpose, meaning 'eat in order to live'; this use is called the infinitive of purpose. The same structure appears in the second half, 'live to eat'. It is not a gerund, which is a verb + -ing used as a noun ('Living simply is a virtue'). It is not a participle, which is a verb form used like an adjective ('a living legend', 'a broken chair'). It is not a complement, which completes the meaning of a linking verb, as in 'She is a doctor'. A quick test: if you can replace 'to + verb' with 'in order to + verb', it is an infinitive of purpose: 'He went to the market to buy vegetables' means 'in order to buy vegetables'. Infinitives can also act as nouns ('To err is human') or as adjectives ('water to drink'). The saying advises eating to stay healthy, not living just for food.

Q 23 | eng | Grammar
Anita ______ for the charitable cause.
(1) come to contributed
(2) came to contributed
(3) came to contribute
(4) comes to contributed
= 3
~ 'Came to contribute' is correct. The word 'to' before a verb is the infinitive marker, and it must be followed by the base form of the verb: to contribute, to help, to give. So 'came to contribute' means came in order to contribute, an infinitive of purpose. Options A, B and D all use 'to contributed', which is wrong because a past-tense or past-participle form never follows the infinitive 'to'. Option A ('come') also fails on agreement, since the singular subject Anita would need 'comes' in the present tense. Option D ('comes') has the right agreement but the same 'to contributed' error. Only option C has both a correct finite verb ('came', simple past) and a correct infinitive ('to contribute'). Note that the usual preposition after contribute is 'to' (contribute to a cause), but the question tests only the verb form. Another example: 'He went to the bank to deposit money', not 'to deposited money'. Memory tip: 'to' + V1 always; only the main verb shows the tense.

Q 24 | eng | Grammar
Everyone at the party ______ amazed by his performance.
(1) was
(2) were
(3) are
(4) can
= 1
~ 'Was' is correct: 'Everyone at the party was amazed by his performance.' Indefinite pronouns such as everyone, everybody, someone, anyone, no one and each take a singular verb, even though they refer to many people. The phrase 'at the party' only describes the subject; it does not change its number. The context, a party and a performance that have already happened, needs the past tense, so the singular past 'was' fits. 'Were' is plural and wrong with 'everyone'. 'Are' is plural and also in the present tense. 'Can' is a modal that would need 'be' after it ('can be amazed') and does not suit a past event. Common trap: 'everyone' feels plural, so students choose 'were'; remember it is grammatically singular, just as you say 'Everyone is here', not 'Everyone are here'. A later pronoun may still be 'their' in modern usage ('Everyone took their seats'), but the verb stays singular. Another example: 'Each of the students has a book.'

Q 25 | eng | Grammar
Alice is not at home at the moment. She ______ work.
(1) was on
(2) was at
(3) is on
(4) is at
= 4
~ 'Is at' completes the sentence: 'Alice is not at home at the moment. She is at work.' Two rules decide this. First, the phrase 'at the moment' and the present 'is not at home' show present time, so the verb must be 'is', not 'was'. Second, the fixed expression for being at one's workplace is 'at work', just like 'at home', 'at school' and 'at college', all used without an article. 'On work' is not the standard idiom; instead we say 'on duty', 'on leave' or 'on holiday'. Option A ('was on') is wrong in both tense and preposition. (2) whenever
(3) where
(4) whereas

(3) don't have to
(4) should

(2) corroded
(3) has corroded
(4) were corroded
= 4
~ 'Were corroded' is correct: 'The metal beams were corroded by the salt-water.' The active verb 'corroded' is in the simple past, so the passive uses was/were + past participle. Memory tip: think of a 'group' when you see gregarious, and of the windmill-fighting knight when you see quixotic.

~ ନିଷ୍ଫଳ (B), meaning fruitless or futile, is correct. It is a visarga sandhi: ନିଃ (without) + ଫଳ (fruit, result). The rule: when a visarga that follows ଇ or ଉ meets କ, ଖ, ପ or ଫ, it changes to ଷ୍. So ନିଃ + ଫଳ → ନିଷ୍ଫଳ, just as ନିଃ + ପାପ → ନିଷ୍ପାପ, ନିଃ + କାମ → ନିଷ୍କାମ, ଦୁଃ + କର → ଦୁଷ୍କର and ଆବିଃ + କାର → ଆବିଷ୍କାର. The second word does not change its own letter, so the aspirated ଫ of ଫଳ stays. ନିଷ୍ପଳ (A) wrongly changes ଫ to ପ. ନିଃଷ୍ପଳ (C) keeps the visarga and also adds ଷ୍, though the visarga has already turned into ଷ୍, and it too has ପ. ନୀଷ୍ଫଳ (D) lengthens the prefix to ନୀ, but this prefix is always short. Usage: ସମସ୍ତ ଚେଷ୍ଟା ନିଷ୍ଫଳ ହେଲା (all efforts failed). Its opposite is ସଫଳ. Tip: the same prefix becomes ନିର୍ before a vowel or a voiced letter (ନିରାଶା, ନିର୍ଧନ), ନିଶ୍ before ଚ (ନିଶ୍ଚିନ୍ତ) and ନିସ୍ before ତ (ନିସ୍ତେଜ), so learn the four forms ନିଷ୍, ନିର୍, ନିଶ୍ and ନିସ୍ together.

‘ପ୍ରସନ୍ନ’ର ବିପରୀତ :
(1) ଦୁଃଖୀ
(2) ଅଧୀର
(3) କ୍ରୋଧାନ୍ୱିତ
(4) ବିଷଣ୍ଣ
= 4

Of the four, only ବିଷଣ୍ଣ is the standard antonym of ପ୍ରସନ୍ନ given in Odia grammar books. Memory tip: in antonym questions, first look for a word built from the same root or pattern with an opposite prefix, as in ପ୍ରସନ୍ନ × ବିଷଣ୍ଣ, ପ୍ରସାଦ × ବିଷାଦ and ଉନ୍ନତି × ଅବନତି.

(4) ବିଭକ୍ତି
= 3
~ ପୁରୁଷ ଓ ବଚନ (both person and number) is right: in Odia the finite verb takes the person and number of its subject. ‘ପ୍ରତ୍ୟହ ଈଶ୍ୱରଙ୍କର ଉପାସନା କରିବା ବିଧେୟ’ — ଉକ୍ତ ବାକ୍ୟରେ ବ୍ୟବହୃତ ‘ଈଶ୍ୱରଙ୍କର’ ପଦରେ କେଉଁ କାରକ ସମ୍ବନ୍ଧ ପ୍ରକାଶିତ ?

(2) କର୍ମ
(3) କରଣ
(4) ଅପାଦାନ
= 2
~ କର୍ମ (object) is the relation shown by ଈଶ୍ୱରଙ୍କର. In ଈଶ୍ୱରଙ୍କର ଉପାସନା ('the worship of God'), ଉପାସନା is a verbal noun that hides an action, and God is not the one who worships but the one who is worshipped. = 3
~ ପ୍ରୟୋଜକ କର୍ତ୍ତା (the causer) is the subject who does not do the action himself but makes someone else do it. Such sentences use a causative (ଣିଜନ୍ତ) verb, made by adding ଆ to the root: ପଢ଼୍ → ପଢ଼ା, ଲେଖ୍ → ଲେଖା, ଖା → ଖୁଆ. In ଶିକ୍ଷକ ଛାତ୍ରକୁ ପଢ଼ାଉଛନ୍ତି, the teacher is the ପ୍ରୟୋଜକ କର୍ତ୍ତା and the student, who actually reads, is the ପ୍ରଯୋଜ୍ୟ କର୍ତ୍ତା. Likewise in ମା' ପିଲାକୁ ଖୁଆଉଛନ୍ତି, the mother is the causer and the child the one who eats. Option A, printed as ପ୍ରଯୁଜ୍ୟ, stands for ପ୍ରଯୋଜ୍ୟ କର୍ତ୍ତା, the person made to act, which is the opposite role and the main trap. ମୁଖ୍ୟ କର୍ତ୍ତା (main subject) and ମୂଳକର୍ତ୍ତା (original subject) are not the grammar names for the causer. Memory tip: the suffix -ଅକ marks the doer (ପାଠକ, ଲେଖକ, and ପ୍ରୟୋଜକ, the one who employs another), while -ୟ marks the one acted upon (ପୂଜ୍ୟ, the one to be worshipped; ପ୍ରଯୋଜ୍ୟ, the one to be employed).

Q 134 | odi | Grammar
‘ପବନ ଅତି ଧୀରେ ବହୁଅଛି’ — ଉକ୍ତ ବାକ୍ୟରେ କେଉଁଟି କ୍ରିୟା ବିଶେଷଣର ବିଶେଷଣ ?
(1) ପବନ
(2) ଅତି
(3) ଧୀରେ
(4) ବହୁଅଛି
= 2
~ ଅତି ('very') is the modifier of an adverb (କ୍ରିୟା ବିଶେଷଣର ବିଶେଷଣ). Work outward from the verb: ବହୁଅଛି is the verb; ଧୀରେ (slowly) tells how the wind blows, so it qualifies the verb and is a କ୍ରିୟା ବିଶେଷଣ; ଅତି tells how slowly, so it qualifies ଧୀରେ and becomes the adverb's modifier. ପବନ is the subject, a noun (ବିଶେଷ୍ୟ), so it cannot be any kind of modifier, and ବହୁଅଛି, being the verb itself, is what the others describe. Compare the look-alike category ବିଶେଷଣର ବିଶେଷଣ: in ଅତି ସୁନ୍ଦର ଫୁଲ, ଅତି qualifies the adjective ସୁନ୍ଦର, which in turn qualifies the noun ଫୁଲ. So the same word ଅତି can be either type; what it qualifies decides the name. More examples: ସେ ବହୁତ ଶୀଘ୍ର ଦୌଡ଼େ (ବହୁତ modifies the adverb ଶୀଘ୍ର) and ଗାଡ଼ିଟି ଖୁବ୍ ଜୋରରେ ଗଲା (ଖୁବ୍ modifies ଜୋରରେ). Tip: ask 'how?' of the verb to find the adverb, then ask 'how much?' of that adverb to find its modifier.

Q 135 | odi | Grammar
‘ତିନି ମାସର ଦରମା’ — ଏହି ବାକ୍ୟରେ ବ୍ୟବହୃତ ‘ମାସର’ ପଦର ସମ୍ବନ୍ଧ ଦର୍ଶାଅ :
(1) ନିର୍ଦ୍ଧାରଣ
(2) ବ୍ୟାପ୍ତି
(3) ବିଶେଷଣ
(4) କାର୍ଯ୍ୟକାରଣ
= 2
~ ବ୍ୟାପ୍ତି (extent) is the relation shown by ମାସର. In ତିନି ମାସର ଦରମା (three months' salary), the genitive does not show ownership; it tells over what span of time the salary is spread. A genitive that measures the spread of time or distance expresses ବ୍ୟାପ୍ତି ସମ୍ବନ୍ଧ, as in ଦୁଇ ଦିନର ଛୁଟି (two days' leave), ଏକ ବର୍ଷର ଚୁକ୍ତି (a one-year contract) and ଦଶ କିଲୋମିଟରର ବାଟ (a distance of ten kilometres). The other options name different relations: ନିର୍ଦ୍ଧାରଣ (selection) singles one out of a group, as in କବିମାନଙ୍କର କାଳିଦାସ ଶ୍ରେଷ୍ଠ (among poets, Kalidasa is the best); କାର୍ଯ୍ୟକାରଣ links an effect to its cause, as in ପରିଶ୍ରମର ଫଳ (the fruit of hard work); and ବିଶେଷଣ is used when the genitive simply describes a quality of the noun, as an adjective would. None of these fits a time span. Two more genitive relations worth revising are ଉପାଦାନ (material: ସୁନାର ହାର) and ଅଙ୍ଗାଙ୍ଗୀ (part and whole: ହାତୀର ଶୁଣ୍ଢ). Tip: if the genitive answers 'how long?' or 'how far?', it is ବ୍ୟାପ୍ତି.

Q 136 | odi | Grammar
‘ସେମାନେ ଖେଳିଥିବେ’ — ଏହା କେଉଁ କାଳସୂଚକ ?
(1) ସଂପନ୍ନ ଅତୀତ
(2) ଅସଂପନ୍ନ ଭବିଷ୍ୟତ
(3) ସଂପନ୍ନ ଭବିଷ୍ୟତ
(4) ସାଧାରଣ ଅତୀତ
= 3
~ ସଂପନ୍ନ ଭବିଷ୍ୟତ (future perfect) is right. ଖେଳିଥିବେ splits as ଖେଳ୍ + ଇ + ଥିବେ: the ଇ-form (ଖେଳି) shows the action as complete, and ଥିବେ places that completion in the future, so it means 'they will have played', e.g. ସନ୍ଧ୍ୟା ସୁଦ୍ଧା ସେମାନେ ଖେଳିଥିବେ (by evening they will have played). Compare other forms of the same verb: ଖେଳିଥିଲେ is ସଂପନ୍ନ ଅତୀତ (past perfect, 'had played'); ଖେଳୁଥିବେ is ଅସଂପନ୍ନ ଭବିଷ୍ୟତ (future continuous, 'will be playing'); ଖେଳିଲେ is ସାଧାରଣ ଅତୀତ (simple past, 'played'); and ଖେଳିବେ is ସାଧାରଣ ଭବିଷ୍ୟତ (simple future). The pattern is easy: ଇ + ଥି… = completed (ସଂପନ୍ନ), ଉ + ଥି… = ongoing (ଅସଂପନ୍ନ); then ଥିଲେ points to the past and ଥିବେ to the future. Trap: ଖେଳିଥିଲେ and ଖେଳିଥିବେ differ by one letter (ଲ / ବ), so read the ending carefully. In speech ଖେଳିଥିବେ can also express a guess ('they must have played'), but the grammar label asked for here is ସଂପନ୍ନ ଭବିଷ୍ୟତ.

Q 137 | odi | Grammar
ବାକ୍ୟରେ ସମାପିକା କ୍ରିୟାପଦ ସବୁବେଳେ ବାକ୍ୟର ଶେଷରେ ରହେ ।
(1) ଠିକ୍
(2) ଭୁଲ୍
(3) ଆଦୌ ରହେ ନାହିଁ
(4) କେଉଁଟି ବି ନୁହଁ
= 2
~ ଭୁଲ୍ (false) is right, because the word ସବୁବେଳେ (always) makes the statement too strong. Odia is usually a subject–object–verb language, so in a simple sentence such as ରାମ ବହି ପଢ଼େ the finite verb comes last. But this is not a fixed law. In a complex sentence like ସେ କହିଲା ଯେ ସେ ଆସିବ, the main finite verb କହିଲା stands in the middle. In questions, commands, poetry and emotional speech the order can change for emphasis: ଯାଅ ତୁମେ ଘରକୁ puts the verb first. Option C (ଆଦୌ ରହେ ନାହିଁ, 'is never there') is wrong because the end position is in fact the normal one, and D (none of these) is wrong because B fits. Recall the terms: a ସମାପିକା କ୍ରିୟା is the finite verb that completes a sentence (ପଢ଼େ, ଗଲା), while an ଅସମାପିକା କ୍ରିୟା leaves it incomplete (ପଢ଼ି, ଯାଇ). Tip: in true/false grammar statements, watch for absolute words like ସବୁବେଳେ, କେବେ ନୁହେଁ and କେବଳ; they often make the statement false.

Q 138 | odi | Grammar
ଏକାଧିକ ପଦରେ ପ୍ରକାଶିତ କ୍ରିୟାକୁ ______ କୁହାଯାଏ ।
(1) ସମାପିକା କ୍ରିୟା
(2) ଅସମାପିକା କ୍ରିୟା
(3) ମିଶ୍ର କ୍ରିୟା
(4) ଅମିଶ୍ର କ୍ରିୟା
= 3
~ ମିଶ୍ର କ୍ରିୟା (compound or mixed verb) is the name for a verb expressed through more than one word. Usually a noun or adjective joins a verb such as କର୍ (do) or ହୁ (become) to express one action: ସ୍ନାନ କଲେ (bathed), ଭୋଜନ କଲେ (ate), ଦେଖା କଲା (met), ଉଜ୍ଜ୍ୱଳ ହେଲା (shone). Two words are written, but the meaning is a single action, so it counts as one verb. The other terms classify verbs on a different basis. ସମାପିକା (finite) and ଅସମାପିକା (non-finite) depend on whether the verb completes the sentence, not on the number of words: in ସେ ଖାଇ ଶୋଇଲା (he ate and slept), ଖାଇ is ଅସମାପିକା and ଶୋଇଲା is ସମାପିକା. ଅମିଶ୍ର ('unmixed') would rather describe a one-word verb such as ଗଲା or ପଢ଼େ, the reverse of what is asked. More examples to remember: ପ୍ରଣାମ କଲା, ବିଶ୍ରାମ ନେଲା, ଖୁସି ହେଲା. Tip: two or more words, one action = ମିଶ୍ର କ୍ରିୟା.

Q 139 | odi | Grammar
ଓଡ଼ିଆ ଭାଷାରେ କର୍ତ୍ତାର ଲିଙ୍ଗମତେ କ୍ରିୟାପଦର ପରିବର୍ତ୍ତନ ______ ।
(1) ହୁଏ
(2) ହୁଏ ନାହିଁ
(3) ବିଶେଷ କ୍ଷେତ୍ରରେ ହୋଇଥାଏ
(4) କେଉଁଟି ନୁହଁ
= 2
~ ହୁଏ ନାହିଁ (does not change) is right: Odia verbs agree with the subject in person and number, but never in gender. ପୁଅଟି ସ୍କୁଲ୍ ଯାଉଛି and ଝିଅଟି ସ୍କୁଲ୍ ଯାଉଛି use exactly the same verb, and Odia even uses one pronoun, ସେ, for both 'he' and 'she'. Hindi is different: लड़का जाता है but लड़की जाती है, and in the past गया but गई. Odia shares this gender-free verb with its neighbours Bengali and Assamese, a useful point when comparing Indian languages. Tip: an Odia verb ending carries person, number and tense; gender is never part of it.

Q 140 | odi | Grammar

(1) କରାଯାଏ
(2) କରାଯାଇପାରେ
(3) କରାଯାଏ ନାହିଁ
(4) ବିଶେଷ କ୍ଷେତ୍ରରେ କରାଯାଏ
= 3
~ କରାଯାଏ ନାହିଁ (is not done) is the key's answer. A ଗୁଣବାଚକ ବିଶେଷ୍ୟ (abstract noun of quality or state) such as ସୌନ୍ଦର୍ଯ୍ୟ, ସାହସ, ଦୟା, ମଧୁରତା or ସତ୍ୟତା names a quality, not a thing that can be counted, so Odia grammar does not put it in the plural. We say ତାଙ୍କର ସାହସ ପ୍ରଶଂସନୀୟ (his courage is praiseworthy), never ସାହସମାନେ or ସାହସଗୁଡ଼ିକ. By contrast, ଜାତିବାଚକ (common) nouns take plural markers freely: ପିଲାମାନେ, ବହିଗୁଡ଼ିକ, ଗଛସବୁ, ଦେବଗଣ. Options A (କରାଯାଏ) and B (କରାଯାଇପାରେ, 'can be done') contradict the rule. D (ବିଶେଷ କ୍ଷେତ୍ରରେ, 'in special cases') is a tempting middle path, but the rule as taught in Odia grammar is simply 'no plural'. English sometimes allows forms like 'kindnesses', so don't carry that habit over to Odia grammar questions. Tip: put a number in front of the noun; if it sounds wrong (ଦୁଇଟି ସାହସ, ତିନୋଟି ସୌନ୍ଦର୍ଯ୍ୟ), the noun is an abstract one and is not pluralised. This test also helps you spot ଗୁଣବାଚକ nouns in 'identify the noun type' questions.

Q 141 | odi | Grammar
ବାକ୍ୟ ମଧ୍ୟରେ ______ ସର୍ବନାମ ପରସ୍ପରକୁ ଅପେକ୍ଷା କରନ୍ତି ।
(1) ବ୍ୟକ୍ତିବାଚକ
(2) ନିର୍ଦ୍ଦେଶାତ୍ମକ
(3) ସମ୍ବନ୍ଧବାଚକ
(4) ସମୁଚ୍ଚୟୀ
= 3
~ ସମ୍ବନ୍ଧବାଚକ (relative) pronouns are the ones that 'wait for each other' (ପରସ୍ପରକୁ ଅପେକ୍ଷା କରନ୍ତି) in a sentence. They come in linked pairs, one in each clause: ଯେ…ସେ, ଯିଏ…ସିଏ, ଯାହା…ତାହା, ଯେଉଁ…ସେହି. The ଯ-word opens the dependent clause and the ସ/ତ-word picks it up in the main clause, and neither half is a complete sentence alone: ଯେ ପରିଶ୍ରମ କରେ, ସେ ସଫଳ ହୁଏ (he who works hard succeeds); ଯାହା ଚକଚକ କରେ, ତାହା ସବୁ ସୁନା ନୁହେଁ (all that glitters is not gold). ବ୍ୟକ୍ତିବାଚକ (personal) pronouns such as ମୁଁ, ତୁମେ, ସେ stand alone and need no partner. ନିର୍ଦ୍ଦେଶାତ୍ମକ (demonstrative) pronouns such as ଏହା, ସେହି, ଏଇଟା simply point to something near or far. ସମୁଚ୍ଚୟ means joining or gathering together, the idea behind connecting words like ଓ and ଏବଂ, not a pair of pronouns that depend on each other. Tip: when you see a ଯ-pronoun, look for its ସ/ତ partner later in the sentence; that pair is the mark of the relative pronoun.

‘ଖେଳୁଅଛି’ କ୍ରିୟାପଦର ପୁରୁଷ ଓ ବଚନ ନିର୍ଣ୍ଣୟ କର :
(1) ପ୍ରଥମ ପୁରୁଷ ବହୁବଚନ
(2) ତୃତୀୟ ପୁରୁଷ ବହୁବଚନ
(3) ଦ୍ୱିତୀୟ ପୁରୁଷ ଏକବଚନ
(4) ତୃତୀୟ ପୁରୁଷ ଏକବଚନ

Q 143 | odi | Grammar
‘ଦେଖ୍’ ଧାତୁ ମୂଳ ସଂସ୍କୃତ ______ ଧାତୁରୁ ଆସିଛି ।
(1) ଦା
(2) ଧୃ
(3) ଖାଦ୍
(4) ଦ୍ରକ୍ଷ୍
= 4
~ ଦ୍ରକ୍ଷ୍ is the Sanskrit source of the Odia root ଦେଖ୍ ('to see'). Sanskrit √ଦୃଶ୍ ('to see') uses the stem ଦ୍ରକ୍ଷ୍ in forms such as ଦ୍ରକ୍ଷ୍ୟତି ('will see'); in Prakrit this became dekkh- (dekkhaï), and Odia smoothed it to ଦେଖ୍. Because the shape changed on the way, ଦେଖ୍ is a ତଦ୍ଭବ ଧାତୁ; it gives ଦେଖିବା, ଦେଖା and ଦେଖାଇବା. The other options are the Sanskrit sources of other Odia verbs: ଦା ('to give') lies behind ଦେ (ଦେବା), ଧୃ ('to hold') behind ଧର୍ (ଧରିବା), and ଖାଦ୍ ('to eat') behind ଖା (ଖାଇବା). Hindi देखना and Bengali দেখা come from the same source. Don't mix up the two lines from √ଦୃଶ୍: tatsama words such as ଦର୍ଶନ, ଦୃଶ୍ୟ and ଦୃଷ୍ଟି keep the Sanskrit shape, while the everyday verb ଦେଖ୍ came through Prakrit. Tip: to trace a tadbhava root, look for the Sanskrit form whose sounds are closest (ଦ + କ୍ଷ → ଦେ + ଖ); କ୍ଷ often turns into ଖ, as in ଅକ୍ଷି → ଆଖି and କ୍ଷୀର → ଖିରି.

Q 144 | odi | Grammar
‘ଗୁଣ ନ ଥିଲେ ଧନରେ କି ଫଳ’ — ଉକ୍ତ ବାକ୍ୟରେ କେଉଁ ଅର୍ଥରେ ‘ଧନ’ ଶବ୍ଦର ତୃତୀୟା ବିଭକ୍ତି ହୋଇଛି ?
(1) ହେତୁ
(2) ପ୍ରୟୋଜନ
(3) ପ୍ରକୃତି
(4) ବିନିମୟ
= 2
~ ପ୍ରୟୋଜନ (purpose, use) is the sense of the ତୃତୀୟା here. In ଗୁଣ ନ ଥିଲେ ଧନରେ କି ଫଳ ('without virtue, what use is wealth?'), the speaker questions what purpose wealth serves, and with expressions such as କି ଫଳ, କି ଲାଭ and କି ପ୍ରୟୋଜନ, the thing whose use is questioned takes the third case. Sanskrit does the same: धनेन किम्? ('what is the use of wealth?'). Note that ରେ in ଧନରେ is not always the locative (ସପ୍ତମୀ); Odia ତୃତୀୟା also uses ରେ, as in ଛୁରୀରେ କାଟିଲା (cut with a knife). The other senses of ତୃତୀୟା: ହେତୁ (cause), as in ଭୟରେ ପଳାଇଲା (ran away out of fear); ପ୍ରକୃତି (nature), as in ସ୍ୱଭାବରେ ସେ ଶାନ୍ତ (he is calm by nature); ବିନିମୟ (exchange or price), as in ଶହେ ଟଙ୍କାରେ ବହିଟି କିଣିଲା (bought the book for a hundred rupees). None of these fits, because the sentence questions the usefulness of wealth, not its cause, nature or price. Tip: 'X-ରେ କି ଫଳ / କି ଲାଭ' = ପ୍ରୟୋଜନ ଅର୍ଥରେ ତୃତୀୟା.

Q 145 | odi | Grammar
କେଉଁଟି ଦେଶଜ ଧାତୁ ?
(1) ଉଡ୍
(2) ବନ୍ଧ୍
(3) ଭିଦ୍
(4) ଭୁଲ୍
= 4
~ ଭୁଲ୍ ('to forget, to err') is the ଦେଶଜ (native) root: it has no Sanskrit source and comes from the local, non-Sanskrit stock of the language. It gives ଭୁଲିବା, ଭୁଲିଗଲା and the noun ଭୁଲ୍ (mistake). The other three go back to Sanskrit. ବନ୍ଧ୍ ('to tie') and ଭିଦ୍ ('to split, pierce') are Sanskrit roots used in their original shape (ତତ୍ସମ ଧାତୁ), seen in words such as ବନ୍ଧନ, ସମ୍ବନ୍ଧ, ଭେଦ and ଭିନ୍ନ. ଉଡ୍ ('to fly') is a tadbhava root from Sanskrit ଉଡ୍ଡୀ (ud + ḍī, 'to fly up'), so it is not ଦେଶଜ either. Trap: ଭୁଲ୍ sounds like a short Sanskrit root, and ଉଡ୍ looks homely, so check the source, not the shape. Tip: if you can name a Sanskrit verb behind it, it is not ଦେଶଜ.

ଯେଉଁ କ୍ରିୟାବାଚକ ବିଶେଷ୍ୟରୁ କାଳ, ପୁରୁଷ, ବଚନ ପ୍ରଭୃତିର ଧାରଣା ମିଳେ ନାହିଁ, ତାହା ______ ।
(1) ସଂପୂର୍ଣ୍ଣ କ୍ରିୟା
(2) ଦ୍ୱିକର୍ମକ କ୍ରିୟା
(3) ସିଦ୍ଧ କ୍ରିୟା
(4) ସାଧ୍ୟ କ୍ରିୟା
= 3
~ ସିଦ୍ଧ କ୍ରିୟା is the name for a verbal noun (କ୍ରିୟାବାଚକ ବିଶେଷ୍ୟ) that presents an action only as a settled idea, with no tense, person or number. Words such as ଗମନ (going), ଭୋଜନ (eating) and ପଠନ (reading), or ଯିବା, ଖାଇବା, ପଢ଼ିବା used as nouns (ପଢ଼ିବା ଭଲ ଅଭ୍ୟାସ, 'reading is a good habit'), name the action like a thing, so you cannot tell who did it or when. Its partner term is ସାଧ୍ୟ କ୍ରିୟା: an inflected verb that presents the action as something being carried out, so it shows tense, person and number, as in ସେ ଯାଉଛି or ମୁଁ ଖାଇଲି. That makes D the opposite of what the question describes. ଦ୍ୱିକର୍ମକ କ୍ରିୟା is a verb with two objects, as in ମା' ପିଲାକୁ ଦୁଧ ଦେଲେ (ପିଲାକୁ and ଦୁଧ) or ଶିକ୍ଷକ ଛାତ୍ରକୁ ଗଣିତ ଶିଖାଉଛନ୍ତି. ସଂପୂର୍ଣ୍ଣ କ୍ରିୟା is not the grammar name for a tenseless verbal noun. The terms come from Sanskrit grammar, where siddha means 'already accomplished' and sādhya 'still to be accomplished'. Tip: ସିଦ୍ଧ = noun-like, no tense; ସାଧ୍ୟ = verb-like, with tense.

Q 147 | odi | Grammar
ବିଶେଷ୍ୟ ପଦଗୁଡ଼ିକ ବିଭକ୍ତିଯୁକ୍ତ ହେଲେ ବିଶେଷ୍ୟର ଆକୃତିରେ ______ ।
(1) ପରିବର୍ତ୍ତନ ଘଟେ
(2) ପରିବର୍ତ୍ତନ ଘଟେ ନାହିଁ
(3) କେତେକ କ୍ଷେତ୍ରରେ ପରିବର୍ତ୍ତନ ଘଟେ
(4) କେଉଁଟି ନୁହଁ
= 2
~ ପରିବର୍ତ୍ତନ ଘଟେ ନାହିଁ (no change) is right: in Odia a case ending (ବିଭକ୍ତି) is simply attached after the noun, and the noun itself keeps its shape. ଘର gives ଘରକୁ, ଘରରେ, ଘରରୁ, ଘରର; ପିଲା gives ପିଲାକୁ, ପିଲାର; even with the honorific marker the base stays whole: ପିତା → ପିତାଙ୍କୁ, ପିତାଙ୍କର. So 'it changes' (A) and 'it changes in some cases' (C) are wrong for nouns, and D falls away because B is correct. This simple joining is a key feature of Odia; in Sanskrit, by contrast, the ending fuses with the word (ରାମଃ, ରାମେଣ, ରାମାୟ). The trap is to mix up nouns with pronouns. Pronouns do change before a case ending: ମୁଁ → ମୋତେ, ମୋର; ତୁ → ତୋତେ; ସେ → ତାକୁ, ତାର; କିଏ → କାହାକୁ. That changed pronoun base is called the oblique form (ତିର୍ଯ୍ୟକ ଆକୃତି), a separate exam favourite. Memory tip: noun = no change; pronoun = oblique change.

Q 148 | odi | Grammar
‘ହଳିଆକୁ’ ଶବ୍ଦର ଗଠନ କ୍ଷେତ୍ରରେ ______ ।
(1) ସରଳ ପ୍ରକୃତି ସହ ପ୍ରତ୍ୟୟ ଯୋଗ ହୋଇଛି
(2) ସରଳ ପ୍ରକୃତି ସହ ବିଭକ୍ତି ଯୋଗ ହୋଇଛି
(3) ବ୍ୟୁତ୍ପନ୍ନ ପ୍ରକୃତି ସହ ବିଭକ୍ତି ଯୋଗ ହୋଇଛି
(4) ସରଳ ପ୍ରକୃତି ସହ କେଉଁଟି ବି ଯୋଗ ହୋଇନାହିଁ
= 1
~ OPSC's key reads the question as asking how ହଳିଆ was built: the simple base (ସରଳ ପ୍ରକୃତି) ହଳ 'plough' takes the suffix (ପ୍ରତ୍ୟୟ) -ଇଆ, giving ହଳିଆ 'ploughman, one who works the plough'. A simple base is a word that cannot be broken down further (ହଳ, ଘର, ଗଛ), while a base built with a suffix is a derived base (ବ୍ୟୁତ୍ପନ୍ନ ପ୍ରକୃତି). Option B, simple base plus case ending, would describe ହଳକୁ, not ହଳିଆକୁ, and D is wrong because something has clearly been added. Strictly, though, the full word ହଳିଆକୁ is the derived base ହଳିଆ plus the case ending -କୁ, which is exactly what option C says, so C is also defensible; the final key accepts only A. For this question, mark A as the key does. (Official OPSC key; some sources differ.)

Q 149 | odi | Grammar
ଧାତୁରେ ଅସମାପିକା କ୍ରିୟା ବିଭକ୍ତି ______ ।
(1) ସର୍ବଦା ଯୁକ୍ତ ହୁଏ
(2) ଆଦୌ ଯୁକ୍ତ ହୁଏ ନାହିଁ
(3) ଯୁକ୍ତ ହୋଇପାରେ
(4) କେଉଁଟି ନୁହଁ
= 3
~ ଯୁକ୍ତ ହୋଇପାରେ ('may be attached') is right. A verb root (ଧାତୁ) can take either kind of ending, depending on the job the verb does in the sentence. With non-finite endings it becomes a non-finite verb (ଅସମାପିକା କ୍ରିୟା) that leaves the sentence incomplete: ଖା + ଇ = ଖାଇ, ପଢ଼ + ଇବାକୁ = ପଢ଼ିବାକୁ, and repeated forms like ଦେଖୁ ଦେଖୁ. With finite endings the same root forms a finite verb (ସମାପିକା କ୍ରିୟା) that completes it: ଖାଏ, ପଢ଼ିଲା, ଦେଖିବ. In 'ସେ ଭାତ ଖାଇ ସ୍କୁଲ ଗଲା', ଖାଇ is non-finite and ଗଲା is finite. So 'always attached' (A) is wrong because finite forms exist, 'never attached' (B) is wrong because forms like ଖାଇ and ପଢ଼ିବାକୁ exist, and D falls away. A trap worth knowing: one form can play both roles. ପଢ଼ିଲେ is finite in 'ସେମାନେ ପଢ଼ିଲେ' (they studied) but non-finite in 'ପଢ଼ିଲେ ପାସ୍ କରିବ' (if you study, you will pass). Always judge by whether the word finishes the sentence or not.

Q 150 | odi | Grammar
କ୍ରିୟା ବହୁବଚନାନ୍ତ ହୋଇଥିଲେ କର୍ତ୍ତାରେ ______ ।
(1) ପୂର୍ବସର୍ଗ ଯୋଗ କରିବାକୁ ହୁଏ
(2) ପରସର୍ଗ ଯୋଗ କରିବାକୁ ହୁଏ
(3) ଉଭୟ ସର୍ଗ ଯୋଗ କରିବାକୁ ହୁଏ
(4) କୌଣସି ଗୋଟିଏ ସର୍ଗ ଯୋଗ କରିବା ଦରକାର ହୁଏ ନାହିଁ
= 4
~ No marker is needed (option D): when the verb already carries a plural ending, the subject needs no plural-marking word (ସର୍ଗ) at all. Odia shows plurality by adding a word either before the noun (ପୂର୍ବସର୍ଗ: ସବୁ, ସମସ୍ତ, ଅନେକ, ବହୁ, as in ସବୁ ପିଲା) or after it (ପରସର୍ଗ: ମାନେ, ଗୁଡ଼ିକ, ଗଣ, ବୃନ୍ଦ, as in ପିଲାମାନେ, ଛାତ୍ରଗଣ). But if the verb is in a plural form (-ଲେ, -ନ୍ତି, -ବେ), the number is already clear from the verb, so the bare noun is enough: 'ଗାଁ ଲୋକ ଆସିଲେ', 'ଲୋକ କହୁଛନ୍ତି'. Adding a marker (ଲୋକମାନେ ଆସିଲେ) is not wrong, only unnecessary, so A, B and C, which say a marker 'has to be' added, are wrong. The same plural verb is also used for respect with a single person, 'ବାପା ଆସିଲେ', 'ଶିକ୍ଷକ କହିଲେ', and again the subject takes no marker. Memory tip: in Odia either the noun or the verb can carry the plural; one marker is enough.

Q 151 | odi | Grammar
‘କାଳିଦାସଙ୍କୁ ଭାରତର ସେକ୍ସପିଅର୍ କହନ୍ତି’
— ଉକ୍ତ ବାକ୍ୟରେ ‘ସେକ୍ସପିଅର୍’ କେଉଁ ବିଶେଷ୍ୟ ରୂପେ ଗୃହୀତ ?
(1) ଜାତିବାଚକ
(2) ବସ୍ତୁବାଚକ
(3) ଗୁଣବାଚକ
(4) କ୍ରିୟାବାଚକ
= 1
~ ଜାତିବାଚକ (common noun) is right. Normally ସେକ୍ସପିଅର୍ is a proper noun (ବ୍ୟକ୍ତିବାଚକ ବିଶେଷ୍ୟ), the name of one English dramatist. In 'କାଳିଦାସଙ୍କୁ ଭାରତର ସେକ୍ସପିଅର୍ କହନ୍ତି' it no longer names that one man; it stands for a class, 'the greatest poet-dramatist of a land', and Kalidasa, author of Abhijñānaśākuntalam and Meghadūta, is placed in that class. Trap: don't answer ବ୍ୟକ୍ତିବାଚକ just because the word is a person's name; ask whether it points to one individual or to a type.

Q 152 | odi | Grammar
‘ତପସ୍ୱୀ’ ଶବ୍ଦ କିପରି ବିଶେଷ୍ୟରୁ ବିଶେଷଣରେ ପରିଣତ ହୋଇଛି, ଦର୍ଶାଅ ?
(1) ସ୍ୱତଃପ୍ରବୃତ୍ତ ଭାବେ
(2) ତଦ୍ଧିତ ପ୍ରକ୍ରିୟାରେ
(3) କୃଦନ୍ତ ପ୍ରକ୍ରିୟାରେ
(4) ଏକ ଭିନ୍ନ ପ୍ରକ୍ରିୟାରେ
= 2
~ ତଦ୍ଧିତ ପ୍ରକ୍ରିୟାରେ (by the taddhita process) is right. ତପସ୍ୱୀ = ତପସ୍ ('penance', a noun) + the taddhita suffix ବିନ୍, meaning 'one who has or practises tapas', i.e. an ascetic. The feminine of ତପସ୍ୱୀ is ତପସ୍ୱିନୀ. Memory tip: root + suffix = kṛt; word + suffix = taddhita.

Q 153 | odi | Grammar
‘ସିନା’ ଏକ ______ ଅବ୍ୟୟ ।
(1) ବିକଳ୍ପାର୍ଥକ
(2) ନିଷେଧାର୍ଥକ
(3) ସମ୍ଭାବନାସୂଚକ
(4) ସମ୍ମତିସୂଚକ
= 4
~ ସମ୍ମତିସୂଚକ (assent or concession) is the key's answer. ସିନା is used to grant or admit one point, usually before a contrast: 'ସେ ଧନୀ ସିନା, କିନ୍ତୁ କୃପଣ' (he is rich, granted, but miserly); 'ପାଠ ସହଜ ସିନା, କିନ୍ତୁ ଅଭ୍ୟାସ ଦରକାର' (the lesson is easy, true, but needs practice). By admitting the first part, the speaker agrees to it, which is why it is classed as an indeclinable of assent. It does not offer a choice like the alternative (ବିକଳ୍ପାର୍ଥକ) words ବା, କିମ୍ବା, ଅଥବା, ନଚେତ୍; it does not forbid or deny like the negative (ନିଷେଧାର୍ଥକ) ନା, ନାହିଁ, ମନା; and it does not suggest a chance like the possibility (ସମ୍ଭାବନାସୂଚକ) words ବୋଧହୁଏ, ହୁଏତ, ସମ୍ଭବତଃ. ସିନା also often stresses the word before it ('ମୁଁ ସିନା କହିଥିଲି', I was the one who said it), so some books call it emphatic; but among these four options, assent fits best. Indeclinables (ଅବ୍ୟୟ) never change for gender, number or case, so learn them by meaning groups.

Q 154 | odi | Grammar
କେଉଁଟି କ୍ରିୟାଜ ବିଶେଷଣ ?
(1) ପରମ ଦୟାଳୁ
(2) ହଜିଲା ଧନ
(3) ତୀକ୍ଷ୍ଣତର ଅସ୍ତ୍ର
(4) ପ୍ରଭୂତ ଶକ୍ତି
= 2
~ ହଜିଲା ଧନ ('lost wealth') is right. ହଜିଲା comes from the verb root ହଜ୍ ('to be lost') + ଇଲା, and here it qualifies the noun ଧନ, so it is an adjective made from a verb, a verbal adjective (କ୍ରିୟାଜ ବିଶେଷଣ). Odia has many: ଭଙ୍ଗା ଘର (broken house), ପଢ଼ା ବହି (a book already read), ଫୁଟନ୍ତା ପାଣି (boiling water), ବୁଡ଼ନ୍ତା ଡଙ୍ଗା (sinking boat). The other options use ordinary adjectives not built from verbs: ପରମ in ପରମ ଦୟାଳୁ qualifies another adjective (ଦୟାଳୁ), so it is an adjective of an adjective (ବିଶେଷଣର ବିଶେଷଣ); ତୀକ୍ଷ୍ଣତର is the comparative degree of ତୀକ୍ଷ୍ଣ (sharp) formed with the suffix ତର (ତୀକ୍ଷ୍ଣ, ତୀକ୍ଷ୍ଣତର, ତୀକ୍ଷ୍ଣତମ); and ପ୍ରଭୂତ (plentiful) shows quantity. Test: if you can trace the describing word back to a ଧାତୁ (ହଜ୍, ଭାଙ୍ଗ୍, ଫୁଟ୍), it is କ୍ରିୟାଜ. Don't confuse it with a finite verb: in 'ଧନ ହଜିଲା' the same ହଜିଲା is the main verb; only before a noun does it describe.

Q 155 | odi | Grammar
‘ମୁଁ ତାଙ୍କ ବିଷୟରେ ସବୁ ଜାଣେ’
— ଉକ୍ତ ବାକ୍ୟରେ ‘ସବୁ’ କେଉଁ ସର୍ବନାମ ?
(1) ଆତ୍ମବାଚକ
(2) ସମ୍ବନ୍ଧବାଚକ
(3) ଗୁରୁତ୍ୱ ଆରୋପକ
(4) ନିର୍ଣ୍ଣାୟକ
= 4
~ ନିର୍ଣ୍ଣାୟକ (determinative) is the key's answer. In 'ମୁଁ ତାଙ୍କ ବିଷୟରେ ସବୁ ଜାଣେ', ସବୁ stands alone for 'everything (about him)', so it is a pronoun, and it marks the whole amount in a fixed, definite way. OPSC's key places such totality pronouns (ସବୁ, ସମସ୍ତେ) under the determinative pronoun. It is not reflexive (ଆତ୍ମବାଚକ: ନିଜେ, ଆପେ, ସ୍ୱୟଂ, which point back to the doer), not relative (ସମ୍ବନ୍ଧବାଚକ: ଯେ, ଯିଏ, ଯାହା, which join two clauses), and not emphatic (ଗୁରୁତ୍ୱ ଆରୋପକ), the use of ନିଜେ to stress a person, as in 'ମୁଁ ନିଜେ ଦେଖିଛି'. Trap: ସବୁ is a pronoun only when it stands in place of a noun. In 'ସବୁ ପିଲା ଆସିଲେ' it qualifies ପିଲା, so there it is an adjective, not a pronoun. Similarly, 'ସମସ୍ତେ ଖୁସି ହେଲେ' has a pronoun, but 'ସମସ୍ତ ଲୋକ' has an adjective. Always check whether the word stands alone or sits before a noun.

Q 156 | odi | Grammar
‘ଜ୍ୱଲ୍’ ଧାତୁରୁ ______ ‘ଜାଜ୍ୱଲ୍ୟ’ ଗଠିତ ।
(1) କର୍ମବାଚ୍ୟ ଧାତୁ
(2) ପ୍ରେରଣାର୍ଥକ କର୍ମବାଚ୍ୟ ଧାତୁ
(3) ନାମଧାତୁ
(4) ଅତିଶୟାର୍ଥକ ଧାତୁ
= 4
~ ଅତିଶୟାର୍ଥକ ଧାତୁ (intensive root) is right. ଜାଜ୍ୱଲ୍ୟ is built on ଜ୍ୱଲ୍ ('to burn, shine'): the root is doubled (ଜା + ଜ୍ୱଲ୍) and the intensive suffix ଯ (Sanskrit yaṅ) is added, so the action gains force or repetition, 'to blaze fiercely'. It appears in ଜାଜ୍ୱଲ୍ୟମାନ (blazing, glaring), as in 'ଜାଜ୍ୱଲ୍ୟମାନ ଉଦାହରଣ' (a glaring example). The doubled first syllable is the giveaway; the same pattern gives ଦୀପ୍ → ଦେଦୀପ୍ୟମାନ (dazzling). The other options: a passive root (କର୍ମବାଚ୍ୟ ଧାତୁ) shows the action being done to someone and has no doubling; a causative root (ପ୍ରେରଣାର୍ଥକ ଧାତୁ) means making another do it, as in ଜଳ୍ → ଜଳାଇବା (to make something burn), and B merely combines causative with passive; a denominative root (ନାମଧାତୁ) is made from a noun, e.g. ଚୋର → ଚୋରେଇବା (to steal). Memory tip: doubling + ଯ = intensity; ଆଇ = causation; noun-based root = ନାମଧାତୁ.

Q 157 | odi | Grammar
କେଉଁଟି ଶୁଦ୍ଧପଦ, ଦର୍ଶାଅ ?
(1) ନୀ + ତ୍ର = ନେତ୍ର
(2) ନ + ଏ + ତ୍ର = ନେତ୍ର
(3) ନେ + ତ୍ର + ଅ = ନେତ୍ର
(4) ନେ + ତ + ର = ନେତ୍ର
= 1
~ ନୀ + ତ୍ର = ନେତ୍ର is right. ନେତ୍ର ('eye', literally 'that which leads or guides') comes from the root ନୀ ('to lead') plus the kṛt suffix ତ୍ର. When the suffix is added, the long ଈ of ନୀ undergoes guṇa and becomes ଏ, so ନୀ + ତ୍ର → ନେତ୍ର. The other splits are wrong: B breaks the word into loose letters (ନ + ଏ + ତ୍ର) instead of root + suffix; C starts from ନେ, which is only the changed form, and adds an extra ଅ that is not a suffix; D chops the conjunct ତ୍ର into ତ + ର. The same root gives ନୀ + ତୃ = ନେତା (leader) and ନୀ + ଅନ = ନୟନ (eye), where ଏ + ଅ → ଅୟ. Other ତ୍ର words: ଶ୍ରୁ + ତ୍ର = ଶ୍ରୋତ୍ର (ear, with ଉ → ଓ), ପା + ତ୍ର = ପାତ୍ର (vessel), ଅସ୍ + ତ୍ର = ଅସ୍ତ୍ର, ଶସ୍ + ତ୍ର = ଶସ୍ତ୍ର. Memory tip: guṇa turns ଇ/ଈ into ଏ and ଉ/ଊ into ଓ, so always look for the plain root behind a word with ଏ or ଓ.

Q 158 | odi | Grammar
ସଂସ୍କୃତ ‘ତୃ’ ପ୍ରତ୍ୟୟ ଓଡ଼ିଆରେ ______ ହୁଏ ।
(1) ତ୍ର
(2) ତ୍ରି
(3) ତୀ
(4) ତା
= 4
~ ତା is right. Sanskrit agent nouns with the suffix ତୃ (tṛc) enter Odia in their nominative singular form, which ends in ତା: ଦାତୃ → ଦାତା (giver), କର୍ତ୍ତୃ → କର୍ତ୍ତା (doer), ନେତୃ → ନେତା (leader), ଶ୍ରୋତୃ → ଶ୍ରୋତା (listener), ବକ୍ତୃ → ବକ୍ତା (speaker), ଦ୍ରଷ୍ଟୃ → ଦ୍ରଷ୍ଟା (seer). Relationship words follow the same pattern: ପିତୃ → ପିତା, ଭ୍ରାତୃ → ଭ୍ରାତା. The other choices are wrong: ତ୍ର is a different kṛt suffix (ନେତ୍ର, ପାତ୍ର), and the feminine of a ତୃ word ends in ତ୍ରୀ, not ତ୍ରି or ତୀ (ଦାତ୍ରୀ, ନେତ୍ରୀ, କର୍ତ୍ତ୍ରୀ). Trap: inside compounds and before further suffixes, the original ତୃ form stays: ପିତୃଭକ୍ତି, ମାତୃଭୂମି, ନେତୃତ୍ୱ, କର୍ତ୍ତୃପକ୍ଷ. So write ନେତୃତ୍ୱ (leadership), never 'ନେତାତ୍ୱ'. Memory tip: standalone word = ତା; inside a compound = ତୃ; feminine = ତ୍ରୀ.

Q 159 | odi | Grammar
କୃତ୍ ପ୍ରତ୍ୟୟରେ ସ୍ଥଳ ବିଶେଷରେ ବର୍ଣ୍ଣମାନଙ୍କର ପ୍ରଧାନତଃ ______ ପ୍ରକାରର ସାମାନ୍ୟ ପରିବର୍ତ୍ତନ ହୁଏ ।
(1) ଦୁଇ
(2) ତିନି
(3) ଚାରି
(4) ପାଞ୍ଚ
= 4
~ ପାଞ୍ଚ (five) is the key's answer. When a kṛt suffix is added to a verb root, the letters of the root sometimes change, and Odia grammar groups these changes into five main kinds. Typical changes you should recognise include guṇa, where ଇ/ଈ becomes ଏ and ଉ/ଊ becomes ଓ (ନୀ + ତୃ → ନେତା, ଶ୍ରୁ + ତୃ → ଶ୍ରୋତା); vṛddhi, where ଅ becomes ଆ and ଋ becomes ଆର୍ (ପଠ୍ + ଅକ → ପାଠକ, କୃ + ଅକ → କାରକ); dropping a letter (ଗମ୍ + ତ → ଗତ, the ମ୍ disappears); adding a letter (ପଠ୍ + ତ → ପଠିତ, with ଇ inserted); and replacing a letter (ପଚ୍ → ପାକ, ଭୁଜ୍ → ଭୋଗ, where ଚ/ଜ become କ/ଗ). Since the key counts five main kinds, two, three and four are too few. Why it matters: once you know these changes, you can split words like ପାଠକ, ଶ୍ରୋତା or ଗତ back into root + suffix, a very common question type (for example ନେତ୍ର = ନୀ + ତ୍ର). Trap: don't mistake a changed form such as ନେ or ପାଠ୍ for the original root.

Q 160 | odi | Grammar
‘ଗଲି ଅଇଲି, ଯାହା ଦେଖିଲି ତାହା କହିଲି’
— ଉକ୍ତ ବାକ୍ୟରେ ‘ଯାହା’ ପଦର ସର୍ବନାମ ଚିହ୍ନିତ କର :
(1) ଆତ୍ମବାଚକ
(2) ନିର୍ଣ୍ଣାୟକ
(3) ନିର୍ଦ୍ଦେଶକ
(4) ସମ୍ବନ୍ଧବାଚକ
= 4
~ ସମ୍ବନ୍ଧବାଚକ (relative) is right. In 'ଗଲି ଅଇଲି, ଯାହା ଦେଖିଲି ତାହା କହିଲି' (I went, came back, and told whatever I saw), ଯାହା opens the clause 'ଯାହା ଦେଖିଲି' and links it to ତାହା in the main clause. A pronoun that joins a dependent clause to the main clause in this way is a relative pronoun. Odia relatives come in pairs with a matching correlative: ଯେ … ସେ, ଯିଏ … ସିଏ, ଯାହା … ତାହା, ଯାହାର … ତାହାର, e.g. 'ଯିଏ ପରିଶ୍ରମ କରେ, ସିଏ ସଫଳ ହୁଏ'. The other options do not fit: ଆତ୍ମବାଚକ (reflexive) pronouns are ନିଜେ, ଆପେ, ସ୍ୱୟଂ; ନିର୍ଣ୍ଣାୟକ (determinative) covers words like ସବୁ, ସମସ୍ତେ; and ନିର୍ଦ୍ଦେଶକ (demonstrative) pronouns such as ଏ, ସେ, ଏହା, ତାହା simply point to someone or something. Trap: the partner word ତାହା, seen alone, looks demonstrative; but the question asks about ଯାହା, and a ଯ-pronoun that introduces a clause is relative. Memory tip: ଯ-words relate, ସ/ତ-words answer.

Q 161 | odi | Grammar
ଦ୍ୱିକର୍ମକ କ୍ରିୟାରେ ସାଧାରଣତଃ କେଉଁ କର୍ମ ଗୌଣ ?
(1) ପ୍ରାଣୀବାଚକ
(2) ଅପ୍ରାଣୀବାଚକ
(3) ସ୍ଥାନବାଚକ
(4) କାଳବାଚକ
= 1
~ ପ୍ରାଣୀବାଚକ (animate) is right. A ditransitive verb (ଦ୍ୱିକର୍ମକ କ୍ରିୟା) such as ଦେବା, ପଚାରିବା, ପଢ଼ାଇବା, ଦେଖାଇବା or ମାଗିବା takes two objects. Generally the thing given or asked is the main object (ମୁଖ୍ୟ କର୍ମ) and is inanimate, while the person or animal who receives it is the secondary object (ଗୌଣ କର୍ମ). In 'ମା ପିଲାକୁ କ୍ଷୀର ଦେଲେ', କ୍ଷୀର (inanimate) is ମୁଖ୍ୟ and ପିଲାକୁ (animate) is ଗୌଣ; in 'ଶିକ୍ଷକ ଛାତ୍ରଙ୍କୁ ପ୍ରଶ୍ନ ପଚାରିଲେ', ପ୍ରଶ୍ନ is ମୁଖ୍ୟ and ଛାତ୍ରଙ୍କୁ is ଗୌଣ. Notice that the animate object usually carries କୁ/ଙ୍କୁ, while the main object often stands bare. So B (inanimate) names the main object, not the secondary one, and place (ସ୍ଥାନବାଚକ) or time (କାଳବାଚକ) words normally show where or when (ଅଧିକରଣ), not an object. Quick test: ask 'କ'ଣ?' to find the ମୁଖ୍ୟ କର୍ମ and 'କାହାକୁ?' to find the ଗୌଣ କର୍ମ. It matches English: in 'She gave the child milk', milk is the direct object and child the indirect one.

Q 162 | odi | Grammar
ସର୍ବନାମରେ ବିଭକ୍ତି ଯୁକ୍ତ ହୋଇ ପରିବର୍ତ୍ତିତ ହୋଇଥିବା ପଦକୁ ______ କୁହାଯାଏ ।
(1) ପରିବର୍ତ୍ତିତ ଆକୃତି
(2) ମିଶ୍ର ଆକୃତି
(3) ଯୌଗିକ ଆକୃତି
(4) ତିର୍ଯ୍ୟକ ଆକୃତି
= 4
~ ତିର୍ଯ୍ୟକ ଆକୃତି (oblique form) is right. Many Odia pronouns change their base before a case ending (ବିଭକ୍ତି), and the changed base is called the oblique form: ମୁଁ → ମୋ (ମୋତେ, ମୋର), ତୁ → ତୋ (ତୋତେ, ତୋର), ସେ → ତା (ତାକୁ, ତାର), ଆମେ → ଆମ (ଆମକୁ, ଆମର), ତୁମେ → ତୁମ (ତୁମକୁ), କିଏ → କାହା (କାହାକୁ), ଯିଏ → ଯାହା (ଯାହାକୁ). The word ତିର୍ଯ୍ୟକ means 'slanting, oblique', i.e. a form that has bent away from the straight (direct) form. 'ପରିବର୍ତ୍ତିତ ଆକୃତି' (changed form) is only a description, not the grammar term, and ମିଶ୍ର ଆକୃତି and ଯୌଗିକ ଆକୃତି are not names used for this. Compare nouns: ଘର → ଘରକୁ, ପିଲା → ପିଲାକୁ, where the base never changes. So the oblique form is a special feature of pronouns. Memory tip: the direct form ମୁଁ/ତୁ/ସେ serves as the subject; the oblique form ମୋ/ତୋ/ତା is what takes the case ending.

Q 163 | odi | Grammar

(1) ଖେଳୁଛି
(2) ଖେଳିବ
(3) ଖେଳିଲା
(4) ଖେଳୁଛନ୍ତି
= 1
~ ଖେଳୁଛି is right, because it serves both 'ମୁଁ ଖେଳୁଛି' (I am playing) and 'ସେ ଖେଳୁଛି' (he or she is playing). Note the person labels used here: ପ୍ରଥମ ପୁରୁଷ is the speaker (ମୁଁ), ମଧ୍ୟମ the listener (ତୁ/ତୁମେ), ତୃତୀୟ the person spoken about (ସେ). In Odia, present-tense forms are shared by ମୁଁ and ସେ: ମୁଁ ଖେଳେ / ସେ ଖେଳେ, ମୁଁ ଖେଳୁଛି / ସେ ଖେଳୁଛି, ମୁଁ ଖେଳିଛି / ସେ ଖେଳିଛି. So ଖେଳିବ and ଖେଳିଲା work with ସେ (ଖେଳିବ also with ତୁମେ) but not with ମୁଁ, while ଖେଳୁଛନ୍ତି is the plural or honorific form (ସେମାନେ / ଆପଣ ଖେଳୁଛନ୍ତି). Trap: in Sanskrit grammar ପ୍ରଥମ ପୁରୁଷ means the third person ('he'); here, because the question names ପ୍ରଥମ and ତୃତୀୟ separately, ପ୍ରଥମ = ମୁଁ. Memory tip: in the present tense 'I' and 'he' share one form; in the past and future they differ.

‘ମାଧ୍ୟାକର୍ଷଣ-ଶକ୍ତି ସମସ୍ତ ଦ୍ରବ୍ୟକୁ ପୃଥିବୀକୁ ଆକର୍ଷିତ କରେ’ — ଏହି ବାକ୍ୟରେ କେଉଁ ପଦ କର୍ତ୍ତାକାରକ ?
(1) ମାଧ୍ୟାକର୍ଷଣ ଶକ୍ତି
(2) ସମସ୍ତ
(3) ଦ୍ରବ୍ୟକୁ
(4) ପୃଥିବୀକୁ
= 1
~ ମାଧ୍ୟାକର୍ଷଣ ଶକ୍ତି is the nominative (କର୍ତ୍ତାକାରକ). To find the କର୍ତ୍ତା, ask who or what does the action: 'କ'ଣ ଆକର୍ଷିତ କରେ?' The answer is ମାଧ୍ୟାକର୍ଷଣ ଶକ୍ତି (the force of gravity), so it is the doer even though it is not a living being; a କର୍ତ୍ତା can be inanimate, as in 'ପବନ ବହୁଛି'. The other words: ଦ୍ରବ୍ୟକୁ answers 'what is attracted?', so it is the object (କର୍ମକାରକ), marked by କୁ; ସମସ୍ତ only qualifies ଦ୍ରବ୍ୟ, so it is an adjective (ବିଶେଷଣ) and not a kāraka at all; ପୃଥିବୀକୁ shows the direction in which things are pulled. Bonus sandhi: ମାଧ୍ୟ + ଆକର୍ଷଣ = ମାଧ୍ୟାକର୍ଷଣ (ଅ + ଆ = ଆ, ଦୀର୍ଘ ସ୍ୱରସନ୍ଧି). Memory tip: ask the verb 'who/what?' to get the କର୍ତ୍ତା and 'whom/what is acted on?' to get the କର୍ମ.

Q 166 | odi | Grammar
ବାକ୍ୟରେ କ୍ରିୟାପଦ ସହିତ ଯେଉଁ ପଦର ଅନ୍ୱୟ ନ ଥାଏ, ତାହା ______ ।
(1) କର୍ତ୍ତାକାରକ
(2) କର୍ମକାରକ
(3) ସମ୍ବନ୍ଧପଦ
(4) ଅଧିକରଣ କାରକ
= 3
~ ସମ୍ବନ୍ଧପଦ (the genitive word) is right. A kāraka (କାରକ) is a noun or pronoun directly connected (ଅନ୍ୱୟ) with the verb, and Odia grammar counts six: କର୍ତ୍ତା, କର୍ମ, କରଣ, ସମ୍ପ୍ରଦାନ, ଅପାଦାନ and ଅଧିକରଣ. The genitive word links only with another noun, not with the verb. In 'ରାମର ବହି ହଜିଲା', ରାମର tells whose book it is; it has nothing to do with the action ହଜିଲା, which is linked to ବହି. That is why it is called ସମ୍ବନ୍ଧପଦ and not a kāraka, even though it takes the sixth case ending (ଷଷ୍ଠୀ: ର, ଙ୍କର). The other options are all linked to the verb: the doer (କର୍ତ୍ତାକାରକ, 'ରାମ ଖାଉଛି'), the object (କର୍ମକାରକ, 'ଭାତ ଖାଉଛି') and the place or time (ଅଧିକରଣ, 'ଘରେ ଖାଉଛି'). Likewise the vocative (ସମ୍ବୋଧନ, 'ହେ ପ୍ରଭୁ') is not a kāraka. Memory tip: if the word answers a question put to the verb (who? what? where?), it is a kāraka; if it answers 'whose?', it is ସମ୍ବନ୍ଧ.

‘ସୈନ୍ୟମାନେ ଅସ୍ତ୍ର ଧରି ଯୁଦ୍ଧକୁ ଗଲେ’ — ଏହି ବାକ୍ୟରେ ‘ଧରି’ ପଦ କେଉଁ କ୍ରିୟାକୁ ବୁଝାଉଛି ?
(1) ସମାପିକା
(2) ଅସମାପିକା
(3) ଦ୍ୱିକର୍ମକ
(4) କେଉଁଟିକୁ ନୁହଁ
= 2

‘ପିଲାମାନେ ପଢୁଥିବେ’ — ଉକ୍ତ ବାକ୍ୟରେ ପଢୁଥିବେ କେଉଁ କାଳସୂଚକ ?
(1) ବିଶୁଦ୍ଧ ବର୍ତ୍ତମାନ
(2) ଆନୁମାନିକ ବର୍ତ୍ତମାନ
(3) ସମ୍ଭାବ୍ୟ ବର୍ତ୍ତମାନ
(4) ନିତ୍ୟପ୍ରବୃତ୍ତ ବର୍ତ୍ତମାନ
= 2
~ ଆନୁମାନିକ ବର୍ତ୍ତମାନ (presumptive present) is right. 'ପିଲାମାନେ ପଢୁଥିବେ' means 'the children must be studying (now)'. The speaker does not see it; he infers it from the situation, e.g. 'ଏବେ ଦଶଟା, ପିଲାମାନେ ସ୍କୁଲରେ ପଢୁଥିବେ'. A guess about an action going on at present is the presumptive present, formed with -ଉଥିବ / -ଉଥିବେ. The other options differ: a plain, certain present statement would be 'ପିଲାମାନେ ପଢ଼ୁଛନ୍ତି'; a habitual or regular action (ନିତ୍ୟପ୍ରବୃତ୍ତ) would be 'ପିଲାମାନେ ପ୍ରତିଦିନ ପଢ଼ନ୍ତି'; and ସମ୍ଭାବ୍ୟ expresses a mere possibility or condition, not an inference about what is surely happening. Trap: the same form becomes future continuous when a future time is given: 'କାଲି ଏତିକିବେଳେ ପିଲାମାନେ ପଢୁଥିବେ' (at this time tomorrow the children will be studying). Without such a time word, read it as a guess about now. Similarly, 'ସେ ଘରେ ଥିବ' means 'he must be at home'.

Q 169 | odi | Grammar
ଯାହା ସମ୍ବନ୍ଧରେ କୌଣସି କଥା କୁହାଯାଇଥାଏ, ତାହା ______ ।
(1) ବିଧେୟ
(2) ଉଦ୍ଦେଶ୍ୟ
(3) କ୍ରିୟା
(4) କ୍ରିୟାର ବିବର୍ଦ୍ଧକ
= 2
~ ଉଦ୍ଦେଶ୍ୟ (subject) is right. Every sentence has two parts: the ଉଦ୍ଦେଶ୍ୟ, the person or thing about which something is said, and the ବିଧେୟ (predicate), what is said about it. In 'ରାମ ବହି ପଢ଼ୁଛି', ରାମ is the ଉଦ୍ଦେଶ୍ୟ and 'ବହି ପଢ଼ୁଛି' is the ବିଧେୟ, so option A names the opposite part. Both C and D belong inside the ବିଧେୟ. The subject can be enlarged too: in 'ମୋ ସାନ ଭାଇ ବହି ପଢ଼ୁଛି', 'ମୋ ସାନ' is the extension of the subject (ଉଦ୍ଦେଶ୍ୟର ବିବର୍ଦ୍ଧକ). Quick test: ask 'whom or what is this sentence about?' The answer is the ଉଦ୍ଦେଶ୍ୟ; everything else is the ବିଧେୟ. Memory tip: ଉଦ୍ଦେଶ୍ୟ = topic, ବିଧେୟ = comment on it.

Q 170 | odi | Grammar
ସମ୍ବନ୍ଧ ପଦ ସହିତ କେଉଁ ବିଭକ୍ତି ଯୋଗ ହୁଏ ?
(1) ଚତୁର୍ଥୀ
(2) ଷଷ୍ଠୀ
(3) ସପ୍ତମୀ
(4) ପ୍ରଥମା
= 2
~ ଷଷ୍ଠୀ (the sixth case) is right. The genitive or possessive relation, the answer to 'whose?', is shown by the ଷଷ୍ଠୀ ବିଭକ୍ତି with the endings ର, ଙ୍କ, ଙ୍କର, ମାନଙ୍କର: ରାମର ବହି, ପିତାଙ୍କ ଘର, ପିଲାମାନଙ୍କର ଖେଳ. The other cases do different jobs: ଚତୁର୍ଥୀ (କୁ, ପାଇଁ, ନିମନ୍ତେ) marks the receiver or purpose, the ସମ୍ପ୍ରଦାନ, as in 'ଗରିବକୁ ଦାନ ଦେଲେ'; ସପ୍ତମୀ (ରେ, ଠାରେ, ଏ) marks place or time, the ଅଧିକରଣ, as in 'ଘରେ ଅଛି', 'ସକାଳେ ଉଠେ'; and ପ୍ରଥମା marks the subject (କର୍ତ୍ତା), as in 'ରାମ ଯାଉଛି'. Note a related rule: the ସମ୍ବନ୍ଧ word takes ଷଷ୍ଠୀ but is not a kāraka, because it links one noun to another noun, not to the verb. Pronouns use their oblique base for this case: ମୋର, ତୋର, ତାର, ଆମର. Memory tip: ର = relation = ଷଷ୍ଠୀ; and since the vibhaktis are numbered ପ୍ରଥମା to ସପ୍ତମୀ, ଷଷ୍ଠୀ is simply the sixth.

Q 171 | odi | Grammar
‘ଖେଳିବେ’ କ୍ରିୟାପଦର ବିକରଣ ଚିହ୍ନିତ କର :
(1) ଖେଳ
(2) ଖେଳ୍
(3) ଇବ୍
(4) ଏ
= 3
~ ଇବ୍ is right. An Odia finite verb can be split into three parts: the root (ଧାତୁ), the vikaraṇa (ବିକରଣ), which is the tense marker placed between root and ending, and the person-number ending (ବିଭକ୍ତି). So ଖେଳିବେ = ଖେଳ୍ (root) + ଇବ୍ (future vikaraṇa) + ଏ (ending for the third person plural or honorific, as in 'ସେମାନେ ଖେଳିବେ'). The other options are not the vikaraṇa: ଖେଳ is the noun 'game' or the root written with its inherent vowel, ଖେଳ୍ is the bare root, and ଏ is the ending. Compare the past tense: ଖେଳିଲେ = ଖେଳ୍ + ଇଲ୍ + ଏ, where ଇଲ୍ is the past vikaraṇa; and ଖେଳିବି = ଖେଳ୍ + ଇବ୍ + ଇ (first person). The middle part changes with tense, while the last part changes with person and number, so you can find the vikaraṇa by changing the tense and seeing which piece moves. Memory tip: ଇଲ୍ = past, ଇବ୍ = future; root first, tense in the middle, person at the end.

Q 172 | odi | Grammar
କେଉଁଟି ଗୁଣବାଚକ ବିଶେଷ୍ୟ ?
(1) ଲୀନ
(2) ପୂର୍ଣ୍ଣ
(3) ଧୈର୍ଯ୍ୟ
(4) ମୁକ୍ତ
= 3
~ ଧୈର୍ଯ୍ୟ (patience) is the name of a quality, so it is a ଗୁଣବାଚକ ବିଶେଷ୍ୟ (abstract noun of quality). It comes from the adjective ଧୀର ('calm, patient') with the Sanskrit taddhita suffix ୟ, and the first vowel takes vṛddhi (ଧୀ → ଧୈ), just as ସୁନ୍ଦର → ସୌନ୍ଦର୍ଯ୍ୟ, ମଧୁର → ମାଧୁର୍ଯ୍ୟ and ଶୂର → ଶୌର୍ଯ୍ୟ. The other three are adjectives (ବିଶେଷଣ), not nouns: ଲୀନ ('absorbed'), ପୂର୍ଣ୍ଣ ('full') and ମୁକ୍ତ ('free') describe a person or thing, as in ଧ୍ୟାନରେ ଲୀନ ସାଧୁ, ପୂର୍ଣ୍ଣ କଳସ, ମୁକ୍ତ ପକ୍ଷୀ. To turn them into quality nouns you must add a suffix: ଲୀନତା, ପୂର୍ଣ୍ଣତା, ମୁକ୍ତି. Odia grammar books list noun types such as ସଂଜ୍ଞାବାଚକ, ଜାତିବାଚକ, ସମୂହବାଚକ, ବସ୍ତୁବାଚକ, ଗୁଣବାଚକ and କ୍ରିୟାବାଚକ; a quality noun names something we cannot touch, only feel, like ସାହସ, ଦୟା or ସତ୍ୟତା. Tip: if a word fits the frame 'ତାଙ୍କର ବହୁତ ___ ଅଛି' (ତାଙ୍କର ବହୁତ ଧୈର୍ଯ୍ୟ ଅଛି), it is a noun; if it sits before a noun and describes it (ପୂର୍ଣ୍ଣ କଳସ), it is an adjective.

Q 173 | odi | Grammar
______ ରୁ କ୍ରିୟାପଦର ପୁରୁଷ ଓ ବଚନ ଜଣାପଡ଼େ ।
(1) କାଳ
(2) ଧାତୁ
(3) ପ୍ରତ୍ୟୟ
(4) ବିଭକ୍ତି
= 4
~ The person (ପୁରୁଷ) and number (ବଚନ) of an Odia verb are shown by its ବିଭକ୍ତି, the ending added to the root. Keep the root ପଢ଼ and the future tense, and change only the subject: ମୁଁ ପଢ଼ିବି, ତୁ ପଢ଼ିବୁ, ଆମେ ପଢ଼ିବା, ସେମାନେ ପଢ଼ିବେ. Only the last sound (ଇ, ଉ, ଆ, ଏ) changes, and it tells us who acts and whether one or many. In ପଢ଼ିବି, ପଢ଼ is the root, ଇବ marks the future and the final ଇ marks the first person singular. Why not the others: କାଳ (tense) is the time of the action — ପଢ଼ିଲି (past) versus ପଢ଼ିବି (future) — not person or number; ଧାତୁ (root) gives only the basic meaning 'read'; ପ୍ରତ୍ୟୟ is the general name for suffixes that build new words, such as ପଢ଼ + ଉଆ = ପଢ଼ୁଆ (student). Odia verbs agree with the subject in person and number but not in gender: ପୁଅ ଯାଉଛି, ଝିଅ ଯାଉଛି. Trap: Odia grammar calls the first, second and third persons ଉତ୍ତମ, ମଧ୍ୟମ and ପ୍ରଥମ ପୁରୁଷ, so ପ୍ରଥମ ପୁରୁଷ means 'he/she/they', not 'I'.

Q 174 | odi | Grammar
ସମ୍ବୋଧନ ପଦର ପୂର୍ବରେ ଅଥବା ପରେ ଅବ୍ୟୟ ପଦ ଲାଗେ ।
(1) ଭୁଲ୍
(2) କେବେ ଲାଗେ ନାହିଁ
(3) ଠିକ୍
(4) କେଉଁଟି ନୁହଁ
= 3
~ The statement is correct (ଠିକ୍): an indeclinable of address (ସମ୍ବୋଧନସୂଚକ ଅବ୍ୟୟ) can stand before or after the word used to call someone. Before: ହେ ପ୍ରଭୁ, ଆରେ ପିଲା, ଅହେ ବନ୍ଧୁ. After: ମା ଗୋ, ରାମ ହେ, ଭାଇ ହୋ, ବାପା ରେ. The word chosen also shows the speaker's attitude: ହେ and ଗୋ are respectful or tender, ରେ and ଆରେ are familiar (for juniors or close friends), and ଲୋ is used in familiar address to girls and women. So 'କେବେ ଲାଗେ ନାହିଁ' (never added) is wrong, and so is 'none of these'. Remember that Odia grammar does not count the vocative word as a kāraka, because it has no direct link with the verb: in 'ହେ ରାମ, ତୁମେ ଆସ', the verb ଆସ connects with ତୁମେ, while ରାମ is only being called. In writing, a comma or an exclamation mark normally follows the vocative: 'ମା ଗୋ!', 'ହେ ଭଗବାନ, ରକ୍ଷା କର'. Tip: a vocative is a 'call' standing outside the sentence, and the ଅବ୍ୟୟ is the calling sound attached to it.

Q 175 | odi | Grammar
କୌଣସି କାରକକୁ କ୍ରିୟାପଦ ନ ମିଳିଲେ ତାହା ______ ବଳରେ ଅନ୍ୟ କ୍ରିୟାପଦ ସହିତ ସଂପୃକ୍ତ ହୋଇଯାଏ ।
(1) କର୍ତ୍ତା
(2) ଅବ୍ୟୟ
(3) କ୍ରିୟା ବିଶେଷଣ
(4) ବିଭକ୍ତି
= 4
~ As per OPSC's key, the blank is ବିଭକ୍ତି (the case ending). A kāraka is a noun or pronoun that stands in a relation to the verb, and that relation is carried by its ending: କୁ shows the object or goal, ରେ the place or instrument, ରୁ the source, and so on. Sometimes a kāraka has no verb of its own, usually because the verb is left out to avoid repeating it. In 'ବାପା ହାଟକୁ, ପୁଅ ସ୍କୁଲକୁ ଗଲେ', the phrase ବାପା ହାଟକୁ has no verb, yet the ending କୁ tells us the market is the destination of going, so the phrase attaches to ଗଲେ. The ending works like an address label: even when the word's own verb is missing, it shows which verb the word belongs to and in what role. Why not the others: କର୍ତ୍ତା is itself one of the kārakas, not the link; ଅବ୍ୟୟ words such as ଓ and ଏବଂ can join phrases but carry no case relation, since indeclinables take no ending; and a କ୍ରିୟା ବିଶେଷଣ (adverb) only tells how, when or where an action happens.

Q 176 | odi | Grammar
କେଉଁଟି ଯୌଗିକ ବାକ୍ୟ ?
(1) ତୁମେ ଯଦି ପୁରୀ ଯିବ, ମୋ ପାଇଁ ମହାପ୍ରସାଦ ଆଣିବ ।
(2) ଯେଉଁ ଲୋକ ନ୍ୟାୟ ପରାୟଣ, ତାକୁ ନ୍ୟାୟ ରକ୍ଷା କରେ ।
(3) ଗୋବିନ୍ଦ ବାବୁ ଗୋଟିଏ କଥା ଭାବୁଛନ୍ତି ଏବଂ ଦୁଃଖିତ ହେଉଛନ୍ତି ।
(4) ଆପଣ ମୋ କଥା ନ ଶୁଣନ୍ତୁ, ତଥାପି ମୁଁ କହିବି ।
= 4
~ OPSC's key takes (D), 'ଆପଣ ମୋ କଥା ନ ଶୁଣନ୍ତୁ, ତଥାପି ମୁଁ କହିବି', as the compound (ଯୌଗିକ) sentence. A compound sentence joins two or more independent clauses, each able to stand alone, with a coordinating ଅବ୍ୟୟ such as ଓ, ଏବଂ, କିନ୍ତୁ, ମାତ୍ର, କିମ୍ବା, ତେଣୁ or ତଥାପି. In (D) the two clauses have different subjects (ଆପଣ, ମୁଁ) and are joined by the contrast word ତଥାପି ('even so'). (A) is complex (ଜଟିଳ): 'ତୁମେ ଯଦି ପୁରୀ ଯିବ' is a conditional clause that depends on the main clause. (B) is also complex, linked by the relative pair ଯେଉଁ…ତାକୁ. (C) keeps a single subject, ଗୋବିନ୍ଦ ବାବୁ, for both actions, so the key reads it as one clause with two verbs rather than two independent clauses, although many grammar books would call (C) compound too, because ଏବଂ joins two finite verbs. Tip: the 'ଯ' words — ଯେ, ଯେଉଁ, ଯଦି, ଯେତେବେଳେ — usually signal a complex sentence, while ଓ, କିନ୍ତୁ, ତଥାପି and କିମ୍ବା signal a compound one. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 177 | odi | Grammar
ଯେଉଁ କ୍ରିୟାପଦ ବିଶେଷ୍ୟ ବା ବିଶେଷଣ ଶବ୍ଦରୁ ଗଠିତ ହୋଇଥାଏ, ତାହାକୁ ______ କ୍ରିୟାପଦ କୁହାଯାଏ ।
(1) ମିଶ୍ର
(2) ଅମିଶ୍ର
(3) ପ୍ରେରଣାର୍ଥକ
(4) ନାମଧାତୁ
= 4
~ A verb made from a noun (ବିଶେଷ୍ୟ) or an adjective (ବିଶେଷଣ) is called a ନାମଧାତୁ (denominative verb); 'ନାମ' here means a naming word. The noun or adjective takes a verb-forming suffix and then behaves like any root: ଗୋଡ଼ → ଗୋଡ଼େଇବା (to chase), ଲାଜ → ଲାଜେଇବା (to feel shy), ଚିକଣ → ଚିକଣେଇବା (to make smooth), ଆଗ → ଆଗେଇବା (to move forward), ଅଙ୍କୁର → ଅଙ୍କୁରିବା (to sprout). Why not the others: a ପ୍ରେରଣାର୍ଥକ (causative) verb is made from another verb, not from a noun, and means 'make someone do' — କର → କରାଇବା, ପଢ଼ → ପଢ଼ାଇବା; ମିଶ୍ର ('mixed') and ଅମିଶ୍ର ('unmixed') speak of whether a verb is combined with another word, as in ସ୍ନାନ କରିବା, where the noun stays a separate word beside କରିବା — in a ନାମଧାତୁ, by contrast, the noun itself becomes the verb. Tip: if you can find a noun or adjective hiding inside a single verb word (ଲାଜ in ଲାଜେଇବା), it is a ନାମଧାତୁ; if you find another verb root inside it (ପଢ଼ in ପଢ଼ାଇବା), it is causative.

Q 178 | odi | Grammar
‘ପବନ ଧୀରେ ବହୁଛି’ — ‘ବହୁଛି’ କେଉଁ ଧରଣର କ୍ରିୟାପଦ ?
(1) ସକର୍ମକ
(2) ଅକର୍ମକ
(3) ଦ୍ୱିକର୍ମକ
(4) ପ୍ରେରଣାର୍ଥକ
= 2
~ ବହୁଛି here is intransitive (ଅକର୍ମକ): in 'ପବନ ଧୀରେ ବହୁଛି' (the wind is blowing gently) the action stays with the subject ପବନ and passes to no object. Test it by asking 'କ'ଣ?' or 'କାହାକୁ?' after the verb: 'ପବନ କ'ଣ ବହୁଛି?' has no answer, so there is no object. ଧୀରେ only tells how the wind blows, so it is an adverb (କ୍ରିୟା ବିଶେଷଣ), not an object. The same root can be transitive in another sentence: 'ମୂଲିଆ ବୋଝ ବହୁଛି' (the labourer is carrying a load) has the object ବୋଝ, so there ବହୁଛି is ସକର୍ମକ. That is the trap — decide from the sentence, not from the dictionary. Why not the others: ସକର୍ମକ needs an object; ଦ୍ୱିକର୍ମକ needs two objects, as in 'ଶିକ୍ଷକ ଛାତ୍ରକୁ ପ୍ରଶ୍ନ ପଚାରିଲେ'; ପ୍ରେରଣାର୍ଥକ (causative) means making someone else do the action, which here would be ବହାଇବା ('make someone carry'). Other common intransitive verbs: ଶୋଇବା, ହସିବା, କାନ୍ଦିବା, ଉଡ଼ିବା.

Q 179 | odi | Grammar
‘ଋଷିମାନେ ବଣର ଫଳମୂଳ ଆହାର କରୁଥିଲେ’
— ଉକ୍ତ ବାକ୍ୟର ଯୌଗିକ କ୍ରିୟା ଚିହ୍ନିତ କର :
(1) ବଣର
(2) ଫଳମୂଳ
(3) ଆହାର
(4) କରୁଥିଲେ
= 4
~ କରୁଥିଲେ is the compound verb (ଯୌଗିକ କ୍ରିୟା) in 'ଋଷିମାନେ ବଣର ଫଳମୂଳ ଆହାର କରୁଥିଲେ' (the sages used to eat the fruits and roots of the forest). It is made of two verb parts: କରୁ, a non-finite form of the root କର୍, and ଥିଲେ, a finite form of the helping root ଥା. A ଯୌଗିକ କ୍ରିୟା is formed when a non-finite verb joins a finite verb to give a single sense, as in ଖାଇଗଲା, ପଢ଼ୁଥିଲା, ଦେଇଦେଲା and ଉଠିପଡ଼ିଲା. Why not the others: ବଣର is a noun in the possessive case (ବଣ + ର, 'of the forest'); ଫଳମୂଳ is a noun formed by dvandva compounding (ଫଳ ଓ ମୂଳ); ଆହାର ('food, eating') is a noun. Together ଆହାର କରୁଥିଲେ means 'ate', but the noun ଆହାର is not itself a verb. The ଉ + ଥିଲ pattern also shows an action going on, or done habitually, in the past ('used to eat'). Tip: when asked for the compound verb, pick the word that has two verb roots inside it — here କର୍ and ଥା.

Q 180 | odi | Grammar
ପରସ୍ପର ଅନ୍ୱୟର ଉପଯୋଗୀ ଦୁଇ ବା ତତୋଧିକ ଶବ୍ଦକୁ ଏକପଦରେ ପରିଣତ କରିବାର ନାମ ______ ।
(1) ସନ୍ଧି
(2) ସମାସ ବାକ୍ୟ
(3) ପଦାନ୍ୱୟ
(4) ସମାସ
= 4
~ Turning two or more mutually connected words into one word is ସମାସ (compounding). When the words join, the case endings or linking words between them drop: ରାଜାଙ୍କର ପୁତ୍ର → ରାଜପୁତ୍ର, ପିତା ଓ ମାତା → ପିତାମାତା, ନୀଳ ଯେଉଁ ଆକାଶ → ନୀଳାକାଶ. The word you get is the ସମସ୍ତ ପଦ, its first and second parts are the ପୂର୍ବପଦ and ଉତ୍ତରପଦ, and the expanded phrase that explains it is the ସମାସ ବାକ୍ୟ or ବ୍ୟାସ ବାକ୍ୟ. Why not the others: ସନ୍ଧି joins sounds or letters, not related words (ବିଦ୍ୟା + ଆଳୟ = ବିଦ୍ୟାଳୟ, where ଆ + ଆ = ଆ); ସମାସ ବାକ୍ୟ is the opposite process, the broken-up form; ପଦାନ୍ୱୟ means arranging the words of a sentence, especially a line of poetry, in their natural prose order to get the meaning. Odia grammar, following Sanskrit, counts six main kinds of samāsa: ଦ୍ୱନ୍ଦ୍ୱ, ତତ୍ପୁରୁଷ, କର୍ମଧାରୟ, ଦ୍ୱିଗୁ, ବହୁବ୍ରୀହି and ଅବ୍ୟୟୀଭାବ. Tip: in ସନ୍ଧି letters meet, in ସମାସ words meet; a word like ହିମାଳୟ shows both (ହିମର ଆଳୟ; ହିମ + ଆଳୟ).

Q 181 | odi | Grammar
‘ଅନ୍ତା’ ______ କାଳସୂଚକ ପ୍ରତ୍ୟୟ ।
(1) ନିତ୍ୟପ୍ରବୃତ୍ତ ଭବିଷ୍ୟତ
(2) ଅସଂପନ୍ନ ଭବିଷ୍ୟତ
(3) ପ୍ରାଗ୍ ଭବିଷ୍ୟତ
(4) ସମ୍ଭାବ୍ୟ ଭବିଷ୍ୟତ
= 4
~ ଅନ୍ତା is the marker of the possible future (ସମ୍ଭାବ୍ୟ ଭବିଷ୍ୟତ): it shows an action that might or would happen, often depending on a condition. Examples: 'ସେ ହୁଏତ କାଲି ଆସନ୍ତା' (he might come tomorrow), 'ଯଦି ବର୍ଷା ହୁଅନ୍ତା, ଫସଲ ଭଲ ହୁଅନ୍ତା' (if it rained, the crop would be good). The ending changes with the subject — ମୁଁ ଯାଆନ୍ତି, ତୁ ଯାଆନ୍ତୁ, ତୁମେ ଯାଆନ୍ତ, ସେ ଯାଆନ୍ତା, ସେମାନେ ଯାଆନ୍ତେ — but the ନ୍ତ part, the mark of possibility, stays. Joined with ଥା, it gives the unreal past: 'ସେ ଆସିଥାନ୍ତା' (he would have come). Why not the others: the other kinds of future in the options are built on the future marker ଇବ or on ଥିବ, not on ଅନ୍ତା; for instance the continuing future 'ସେ ଯାଉଥିବ' (he will be going) is the ଅସମ୍ପନ୍ନ ଭବିଷ୍ୟତ, and the plain future is 'ସେ ଯିବ'. Tip: 'ଯିବ' states that something will happen; 'ଯାଆନ୍ତା' only says that it could or would happen — possibility, hence ସମ୍ଭାବ୍ୟ.

Q 182 | odi | Grammar
ଧାତୁସହ ଅନ୍ୟୁନ ______ ବିଭକ୍ତିକୁ ଯୋଗ କରାଯାଇ କ୍ରିୟାପଦ ଗଠିତ ହୁଏ ।
(1) ଗୋଟିଏ
(2) ଦୁଇଟି
(3) ତିନୋଟି
(4) ଚାରୋଟି
= 1
~ A finite verb (କ୍ରିୟାପଦ) is formed by adding at least one (ଗୋଟିଏ) ending (ବିଭକ୍ତି) to the root (ଧାତୁ). ଅନ୍ୟୁନ means 'not less than' (ଅ + ନ୍ୟୂନ, 'not short of'), so the sentence reads 'a verb is formed by adding not less than one ending to a root'. Examples: କର୍ + ଏ = କରେ (ସେ କରେ), ଯା + ଅ = ଯାଅ (ତୁମେ ଯାଅ). Tense markers and helping verbs may also be added — ପଢ଼ + ଇଲ + ଆ = ପଢ଼ିଲା, କରୁ + ଥିଲେ = କରୁଥିଲେ — but one ending is the minimum, so 'two', 'three' and 'four' are wrong: a verb works with just one. The ending is what fixes person and number, which is why no finite verb can do without it. Trap: don't mix up ଅନ୍ୟୁନ ('at least, not less than') with ଅନଧିକ ('at most, not more than'); both appear in exam instructions, and 'ଅନଧିକ ୫୦ ଶବ୍ଦରେ ଉତ୍ତର ଦିଅ' means 'answer in not more than 50 words'.

Q 185 | odi | Grammar
ଧାତୁସହ କେଉଁ ବିଭକ୍ତି ଯୁକ୍ତ ହେଲେ ଅସମାପିକା କ୍ରିୟା ହୁଏ ?
(1) କୁ
(2) ରୁ
(3) ଉ
(4) ର
= 3
~ Adding ଉ to a root (ଧାତୁ) gives a non-finite verb (ଅସମାପିକା କ୍ରିୟା), which shows an action going on alongside another and cannot finish a sentence by itself. ହସ୍ + ଉ = ହସୁ ('ସେ ହସୁ ହସୁ କହିଲା', he said it smiling), ଯା + ଉ = ଯାଉ ('ଯାଉ ଯାଉ ବାଟରେ ଦେଖା ହେଲା', we met on the way while going), ଦେଖ୍ + ଉ = ଦେଖୁ ('ଦେଖୁ ଦେଖୁ ଗାଡ଼ି ଚାଲିଗଲା', the train left as we watched). In each sentence the main, finite verb is a different word: କହିଲା, ହେଲା, ଚାଲିଗଲା. Other non-finite endings in Odia are ଇ (ଖାଇ ଶୋଇଲା, 'having eaten'), ଇଲେ (ପଢ଼ିଲେ ପାସ୍ କରିବ, 'if you study') and ଇବାକୁ (ଖେଳିବାକୁ ଗଲା, 'went to play'). Why not the others: କୁ, ରୁ and ର are case endings (କାରକ ବିଭକ୍ତି) of nouns and pronouns, not verb endings — କୁ marks the object or recipient (ପିଲାକୁ), ରୁ the source (ଗଛରୁ), ର possession (ରାମର). Tip: the ଉ form is often doubled (ହସୁ ହସୁ, ଚାଲୁ ଚାଲୁ) to stress a continuing action.

Q 186 | odi | Grammar
ବିଶେଷ୍ୟ ପଦର ଏକବଚନ ଶବ୍ଦକୁ ବହୁବଚନ କରିବାବେଳେ ପୂର୍ବସର୍ଗ ଓ ପରସର୍ଗ ଉଭୟ ଲାଗେ ନାହିଁ ।
(1) ଠିକ୍
(2) ଭୁଲ୍
(3) ବିଶେଷ କ୍ଷେତ୍ରରେ ଲାଗେ
(4) କେଉଁଟି ନୁହଁ
= 1
~ The statement is correct (ଠିକ୍): to make a singular noun plural, use either a plural word before it (ପୂର୍ବସର୍ଗ) or a plural marker after it (ପରସର୍ଗ), not both. Before: ସବୁ ପିଲା, ଅନେକ ଲୋକ, ସମସ୍ତ ଛାତ୍ର, ବହୁତ ଗଛ. After: ପିଲାମାନେ, ବହିଗୁଡ଼ିକ, ଲୋକସବୁ, and with Sanskrit-origin words ଛାତ୍ରବୃନ୍ଦ, ଦେବଗଣ, ବନ୍ଧୁବର୍ଗ, ପକ୍ଷୀକୁଳ. Using both marks the plural twice and is a common error: 'ସମସ୍ତ ଛାତ୍ରମାନେ', 'ଅନେକ ଲୋକମାନେ' and 'ସବୁ ପିଲାମାନେ' should become 'ସମସ୍ତ ଛାତ୍ର' or 'ଛାତ୍ରମାନେ'. Why not the others: 'ଭୁଲ୍' denies the rule; 'ବିଶେଷ କ୍ଷେତ୍ରରେ ଲାଗେ' (used in special cases) is not the standard rule; 'none of these' fails because ଠିକ୍ fits. This double plural is a favourite of sentence-correction (ଅଶୁଦ୍ଧି ସଂଶୋଧନ) questions. Also, when a number word comes before the noun, Odia adds ଜଣ or ଟି instead of ମାନେ: ପାଞ୍ଚଜଣ ଲୋକ, ଦୁଇଟି ବହି, not ପାଞ୍ଚ ଲୋକମାନେ. Tip: one plural signal per noun is enough.

Q 187 | odi | Grammar
ଅମୂର୍ତ୍ତବାଚକ ବିଶେଷ୍ୟଗୁଡ଼ିକୁ ______ ।
(1) ଦେଖିହୁଏ
(2) ସ୍ପର୍ଶ କରିହୁଏ
(3) ଅନୁଭବ କରିହୁଏ
(4) ଦେଖିହୁଏ ଏବଂ ସ୍ପର୍ଶ କରିହୁଏ
= 3
~ Abstract nouns (ଅମୂର୍ତ୍ତବାଚକ ବିଶେଷ୍ୟ) can only be felt or experienced (ଅନୁଭବ କରିହୁଏ). ଅମୂର୍ତ୍ତ means 'without form' (ଅ + ମୂର୍ତ୍ତ), so these nouns name feelings, qualities, states and ideas that have no body: ସୁଖ, ଦୁଃଖ, ପ୍ରେମ, କ୍ରୋଧ, ସାହସ, ଦୟା, ଶୈଶବ, ସ୍ୱାଧୀନତା. You cannot see or touch ପ୍ରେମ, but you can feel it. Concrete nouns (ମୂର୍ତ୍ତବାଚକ ବିଶେଷ୍ୟ) are the opposite: ବହି, ଗଛ, ପାଣି and ପଥର can be seen and touched. So 'can be seen', 'can be touched' and 'both' describe concrete nouns, not abstract ones. Most quality nouns (ଗୁଣବାଚକ: ଧୈର୍ଯ୍ୟ, ସୌନ୍ଦର୍ଯ୍ୟ) and action nouns (କ୍ରିୟାବାଚକ: ଗମନ, ଭୋଜନ) are abstract. Many abstract nouns are made from adjectives with suffixes such as ତା, ତ୍ୱ and ୟ: ସରଳ → ସରଳତା, ମହତ୍ → ମହତ୍ତ୍ୱ, ଧୀର → ଧୈର୍ଯ୍ୟ. Trap: ସୌନ୍ଦର୍ଯ୍ୟ is abstract even though a beautiful flower can be seen — what you see is the flower, while its beauty is felt.

Q 188 | odi | Grammar
ବିଶେଷ୍ୟ ପଦ ପରେ ବ୍ୟବହୃତ ବିଶେଷଣ ପଦକୁ ______ ବିଶେଷଣ କୁହାଯାଏ ।
(1) ବିଶେଷ୍ୟର
(2) ବିଧେୟ
(3) ଗୁଣବାଚକ
(4) ଅବସ୍ଥାବାଚକ
= 2
~ An adjective used after the noun, in the predicate part of the sentence, is a ବିଧେୟ ବିଶେଷଣ (predicative adjective). In 'ଫୁଲଟି ସୁନ୍ଦର' or 'ଗାଈଟି କଳା', the adjective comes after the noun and completes what is said about it (the ବିଧେୟ, or predicate). When the same adjective stands before the noun and qualifies it directly — 'ସୁନ୍ଦର ଫୁଲ', 'କଳା ଗାଈ' — it is a ବିଶେଷ୍ୟର ବିଶେଷଣ (attributive adjective), option A, which is the opposite case. ଗୁଣବାଚକ (quality: ଭଲ, ସୁନ୍ଦର, ମିଠା) and ଅବସ୍ଥାବାଚକ (state: ଭଙ୍ଗା ଘର, ଶୁଖିଲା ପତ୍ର) classify adjectives by meaning, not by position, so they cannot answer a question about where the adjective sits. One adjective can be classed both ways: ସୁନ୍ଦର is ଗୁଣବାଚକ in meaning, attributive in 'ସୁନ୍ଦର ଫୁଲ' and predicative in 'ଫୁଲଟି ସୁନ୍ଦର'. English works the same way: 'a red rose' (attributive) versus 'The rose is red' (predicative). Tip: ବିଧେୟ = predicate, so a ବିଧେୟ ବିଶେଷଣ lives in the predicate, after the noun.

(2) ଇ
(3) ୟ
(4) ଏୟ
= 3
~ ଦୈତ୍ୟ = ଦିତି + ୟ: the Sanskrit taddhita suffix ୟ (ṇya) is added to ଦିତି to mean 'son or descendant of Diti'. Q 191 | odi | Grammar
ବିଶେଷ୍ୟ ପଦ ‘ଅଙ୍କୁର’ରୁ ______ ଧାତୁ ‘ଅଙ୍କୁରିବା’ ଗଠିତ ।
(1) ପ୍ରୟୋଜନ
(2) ଣିଜନ୍ତ
(3) ସନନ୍ତ
(4) ନାମ
= 4
~ ଅଙ୍କୁରିବା ('to sprout') comes from a ନାମ ଧାତୁ (denominative root), because it is built from the noun ଅଙ୍କୁର ('sprout, shoot'). When a noun or adjective is turned into a verb root by adding a suffix, the new root is called a ନାମଧାତୁ: ଅଙ୍କୁର → ଅଙ୍କୁରି → ଅଙ୍କୁରିବା, as in 'ଓଦା ମାଟିରେ ବୀଜ ଅଙ୍କୁରିଲା'. Other examples: ଗୋଡ଼ → ଗୋଡ଼େଇବା, ଲାଜ → ଲାଜେଇବା, ଆଗ → ଆଗେଇବା. Why not the others: ଣିଜନ୍ତ (causative) roots are made from verb roots to mean 'make someone do' — କର → କରା (କରାଇବା), ଶିଖ → ଶିଖା (ଶିଖାଇବା); ସନନ୍ତ (desiderative) roots, found in Sanskrit words, express a wish to do something — ଜିଜ୍ଞାସା ('desire to know', from ଜ୍ଞା), ପିପାସା ('desire to drink', from ପା), ବୁଭୁକ୍ଷା ('desire to eat', from ଭୁଜ୍); ପ୍ରୟୋଜନ just means 'need' and is not a kind of root. Both ଣିଜନ୍ତ and ସନନ୍ତ start from a verb root, while a ନାମଧାତୁ starts from a noun or adjective. Tip: ask 'what was this word before it became a verb?' — if the answer is a thing or a quality (ଅଙ୍କୁର, ଲାଜ), it is a ନାମଧାତୁ.

Q 192 | odi | Grammar
କେଉଁଟି ହେତୁବୋଧକ ଅବ୍ୟୟ ?
(1) ପରନ୍ତୁ
(2) ମାତ୍ର
(3) ସୁତରାଂ
(4) ଅଦ୍ୟାପି
= 3
~ ସୁତରାଂ ('therefore, hence') is a causal indeclinable (ହେତୁବୋଧକ ଅବ୍ୟୟ): it joins a cause to its result. In 'ରାତିସାରା ବର୍ଷା ହେଲା, ସୁତରାଂ ସକାଳର ଖେଳ ବନ୍ଦ ରହିଲା', the rain is the cause and the cancelled match the result. It belongs with ତେଣୁ, ଏଣୁ, ଅତଏବ, କାରଣ and ଯେହେତୁ, which also show reason or result. Why not the others: ପରନ୍ତୁ and ମାତ୍ର both mean 'but' and show contrast (ବିରୋଧବୋଧକ), as in 'ସେ ଗରିବ, ମାତ୍ର ସତ୍ୟବାଦୀ'; ଅଦ୍ୟାପି means 'even now, till today' and is a time word (କାଳବାଚକ ଅବ୍ୟୟ), as in 'ଅଦ୍ୟାପି ସେ ଫେରିନାହାନ୍ତି'. Odia connecting words (ସଂଯୋଜକ ଅବ୍ୟୟ) are usually grouped as joining (ଓ, ଏବଂ), alternative (ବା, କିମ୍ବା, ଅଥବା), contrast (କିନ୍ତୁ, ମାତ୍ର, ପରନ୍ତୁ), cause-result (ତେଣୁ, ସୁତରାଂ, ଅତଏବ) and condition (ଯଦି…ତେବେ). Tip: if you can put ତେଣୁ in the word's place without changing the meaning, it is ହେତୁବୋଧକ; if କିନ୍ତୁ fits instead, it shows contrast.

(2) ଧା
(3) ଧ୍ରା
(4) ଧୃ
= 4
~ ଧୃ ('to hold, bear') is the original root of the causative (ଣିଜନ୍ତ) root ଧାରି. When the Sanskrit causative suffix ଣିଚ୍ (ଇ) is added, the ଋ of ଧୃ takes vṛddhi and becomes ଆର୍, so ଧୃ → ଧାର୍ → ଧାରି ('make someone hold'). Other ଋ-roots follow the same pattern: କୃ → କାରି, ହୃ → ହାରି, ସ୍ମୃ → ସ୍ମାରି. Words from this family are everyday Odia: ଧାରଣ, ଧାରଣା, ଧାରକ, ଧାରଣକ୍ଷମତା, and from the plain root ଧୃତ, ଧୃତି and ଧର୍ମ ('that which holds or sustains'). Why not the others: ଧ୍ର and ଧ୍ରା are not the source — they are traps made by rearranging the letters of ଧାରି; ଧା ('to place, put') is a real root, but roots ending in ଆ form their causative with ପ, not ର — compare ସ୍ଥା → ସ୍ଥାପି (as in ସ୍ଥାପନ) and ଦା → ଦାପି. Tip: if a causative root shows ଆର୍ where the plain root would have ଋ, go back to the ଋ-root: ଧାରି → ଧୃ, କାରି → କୃ, ହାରି → ହୃ.

Q 195 | odi | Grammar
ନାମଧାତୁ ‘ଆଗେଇବା’ର ବିଶେଷଣ ପଦ ______ ।
(1) ଆଗ
(2) ଆଗ୍
(3) ଅଗ
(4) ଆଗେଇ
= 1
~ ଆଗ is the base word of the denominative verb (ନାମଧାତୁ) ଆଗେଇବା ('to move forward'). As an adjective ଆଗ means 'front, earlier' — ଆଗ ଧାଡ଼ି (front row), ଆଗ ଦିନ (the previous day) — and it also works as a noun ('the front'). With the verb-forming ending it becomes a root: ଆଗ → ଆଗେଇ → ଆଗେଇବା, as in 'ସେନା ଆଗେଇ ଚାଲିଲା'. This is the same pattern as ଗୋଡ଼ → ଗୋଡ଼େଇବା, ଲାଜ → ଲାଜେଇବା and ଚିକଣ → ଚିକଣେଇବା. Why not the others: ଆଗ୍, with a halanta, is not a separate word in Odia; ଅଗ has a short ଅ and is a different Sanskrit word meaning 'mountain' or 'tree' (literally 'that which does not move'); ଆଗେଇ is already the verb root or the non-finite form ('having moved forward'), not the adjective it was made from. Tip: to find the source of a ନାମଧାତୁ, strip off ବା and the verb-forming ଏଇ — what is left (ଆଗ, ଲାଜ, ଗୋଡ଼) is the noun or adjective.

Q 196 | odi | Grammar
କେଉଁଟି ଜଟିଳ ବାକ୍ୟ ?
(1) ଯେ ସର୍ବଦା ସତ୍ୟ କହେ, ସେ ନିର୍ଭୟ ।
(2) ସେ ତୁମକୁ ଗାଳିଦେଇଥିଲା । ସେ ଆସିଛି ।
(3) ସେ ଧନୀ, ମାତ୍ର ସୁଖୀ ନୁହଁନ୍ତି ।
(4) ସେ ପୁରୀ ଯାଇଥିବ କିମ୍ବା କଟକ ଯାଇଥିବ ।
= 1
~ (A) 'ଯେ ସର୍ବଦା ସତ୍ୟ କହେ, ସେ ନିର୍ଭୟ' is a complex sentence (ଜଟିଳ ବାକ୍ୟ). A complex sentence has one main clause (ପ୍ରଧାନ ବାକ୍ୟ) and at least one dependent clause (ଆଶ୍ରିତ ବାକ୍ୟ). Here 'ସେ ନିର୍ଭୟ' (he is fearless) is the main clause, while 'ଯେ ସର୍ବଦା ସତ୍ୟ କହେ' (who always speaks the truth) cannot stand alone; it only tells which person is meant. The relative pair ଯେ…ସେ links them. Why not the others: (B) is two separate simple sentences (ସରଳ ବାକ୍ୟ) with a full stop between them — though they can be combined into a complex one: 'ଯେ ତୁମକୁ ଗାଳି ଦେଇଥିଲା, ସେ ଆସିଛି'; (C) joins two independent clauses with the contrast word ମାତ୍ର, and (D) with the choice word କିମ୍ବା, so both are compound (ଯୌଗିକ). Linking pairs that usually signal a complex sentence: ଯେ…ସେ, ଯେଉଁ…ସେହି, ଯଦି…ତେବେ, ଯେତେବେଳେ…ସେତେବେଳେ, ଯଦିଓ…ତଥାପି, ଯେହେତୁ…ତେଣୁ. Tip: a 'ଯ' word (ଯେ, ଯେଉଁ, ଯଦି, ଯେତେବେଳେ) usually marks the dependent clause of a complex sentence.

Q 197 | odi | Grammar
‘କାର୍ତ୍ତିକେୟ’ ଶବ୍ଦର ଶୁଦ୍ଧ ଗଠନ ରୂପ ଚିହ୍ନଟ କର :
(1) କାର୍ତ୍ତିକ + ଏୟ
(2) କାର୍ତ୍ତିକ + ୟ
(3) କାର୍ତ୍ତି + କେୟ
(4) କୃତ୍ତିକା + ଏୟ
= 4
~ କାର୍ତ୍ତିକେୟ = କୃତ୍ତିକା + ଏୟ. The name means 'son of the Kṛttikās', the six star-maidens (the Pleiades) who nursed the infant war god Skanda. When the taddhita suffix ଏୟ is added, the first vowel takes vṛddhi (ଋ → ଆର୍, so କୃ → କାର୍), the final ଆ of କୃତ୍ତିକା drops, and the result is କାର୍ତ୍ତିକେୟ. The same suffix gives ଗଙ୍ଗା → ଗାଙ୍ଗେୟ (Bhishma), କୁନ୍ତୀ → କୌନ୍ତେୟ (Arjuna) and ରାଧା → ରାଧେୟ (Karna). Why not the others: in (A) କାର୍ତ୍ତିକ + ଏୟ the base already has the vṛddhi vowel, but vṛddhi happens only when the suffix is added, so the base must be the original word — କାର୍ତ୍ତିକ is itself a derived word, the month named after the Kṛttikā star. (B) has the wrong suffix as well as the wrong base, and (C) splits the word at a meaningless point; there is no suffix କେୟ. In Odisha, Kartikeya is worshipped as Kumar, linked with Kumar Purnima, and Cuttack's Kartikeswar Puja is a major festival. Tip: in 'son of' words, undo the vṛddhi to find the base: କାର୍ → କୃ, ଗା → ଗ, କୌ → କୁ.

Q 198 | odi | Grammar
କେତେକ ସକର୍ମକ କ୍ରିୟା ବ୍ୟବହାରରେ ଦ୍ୱିକର୍ମକ ହୁଏ ।
(1) ଭୁଲ୍
(2) ଠିକ୍
(3) ଆଦୌ ହୁଏ ନାହିଁ
(4) ସମସ୍ତ ସକର୍ମକ କ୍ରିୟା ବ୍ୟବହାରରେ ଦ୍ୱିକର୍ମକ ହୁଏ ।
= 2
~ The statement is correct (ଠିକ୍): only some transitive verbs (ସକର୍ମକ କ୍ରିୟା) take two objects and become double-object verbs (ଦ୍ୱିକର୍ମକ). Verbs of giving, asking, telling and teaching do this — ଦେବା, ପଚାରିବା, କହିବା, ପଢ଼ାଇବା, ଶିଖାଇବା, ମାଗିବା. Examples: 'ଶିକ୍ଷକ ଛାତ୍ରକୁ ପ୍ରଶ୍ନ ପଚାରିଲେ', 'ମା ପିଲାକୁ ଦୁଧ ଦେଲେ', 'ଗୁରୁ ଶିଷ୍ୟକୁ ବେଦ ପଢ଼ାଇଲେ'. In each, the thing (ପ୍ରଶ୍ନ, ଦୁଧ, ବେଦ) is the main object (ମୁଖ୍ୟ କର୍ମ) and the person marked with କୁ (ଛାତ୍ରକୁ, ପିଲାକୁ, ଶିଷ୍ୟକୁ) is the secondary object (ଗୌଣ କର୍ମ). Why not the others: 'ଭୁଲ୍' and 'ଆଦୌ ହୁଏ ନାହିଁ' (never happens) deny the real usage shown above; option D, 'all transitive verbs become double-object verbs', overstates it — verbs such as ଖାଇବା and ଦେଖିବା normally take only one object: 'ସେ ଭାତ ଖାଇଲା', 'ମୁଁ ସିନେମା ଦେଖିଲି'. The key word in the statement is କେତେକ ('some'). Tip: if the verb naturally answers both 'କ'ଣ?' and 'କାହାକୁ?' (what? and to whom?), it is being used as a ଦ୍ୱିକର୍ମକ verb.

Q 199 | odi | Grammar
‘ଶିକ୍ଷକ ଛାତ୍ରକୁ ପ୍ରଶ୍ନ ପଚାରିଲେ’
— ଉକ୍ତ ବାକ୍ୟର ମୁଖ୍ୟ କର୍ମ ଚିହ୍ନଟ କର :
(1) ପ୍ରଶ୍ନ
(2) ଛାତ୍ରକୁ
(3) ଶିକ୍ଷକ
(4) ପଚାରିଲେ
= 1
~ ପ୍ରଶ୍ନ is the main object (ମୁଖ୍ୟ କର୍ମ) in 'ଶିକ୍ଷକ ଛାତ୍ରକୁ ପ୍ରଶ୍ନ ପଚାରିଲେ'. ପଚାରିବା is a double-object verb (ଦ୍ୱିକର୍ମକ): it needs both the thing asked and the person asked. The thing — usually inanimate (ଅପ୍ରାଣୀବାଚକ) and without a case ending — is the main object; the person — usually animate (ପ୍ରାଣୀବାଚକ) and marked with କୁ — is the secondary object (ଗୌଣ କର୍ମ). Ask the verb two questions: 'କ'ଣ ପଚାରିଲେ?' → ପ୍ରଶ୍ନ (main); 'କାହାକୁ ପଚାରିଲେ?' → ଛାତ୍ରକୁ (secondary). Why not the others: ଛାତ୍ରକୁ is the secondary object, not the main one; ଶିକ୍ଷକ is the subject (କର୍ତ୍ତା), the one who asks; ପଚାରିଲେ is the verb itself. English has the same idea: in 'The teacher asked the student a question', 'a question' is the direct object and 'the student' the indirect object. More examples: 'ମା ପିଲାକୁ ଦୁଧ ଦେଲେ' (main object ଦୁଧ), 'ଗୁରୁ ଶିଷ୍ୟକୁ ବେଦ ପଢ଼ାଇଲେ' (main object ବେଦ). Tip: the main object is the thing actually given, asked or taught; the secondary object is the person who receives it.

Q 200 | odi | Vocabulary & Usage
‘ଉଛୁର’ର ବିପରୀତ ଅର୍ଥବୋଧକ ଶବ୍ଦ ବାଛ :
(1) ସଙ୍ଗେ ସଙ୍ଗେ
(2) ଶୀଘ୍ର
(3) ଜଲ୍ଦି
(4) ସଅଳ
= 4
~ ସଅଳ ('early, ahead of time') is the antonym of ଉଛୁର ('late, delay'). ଉଛୁର is a homely, everyday Odia word: 'ଆଜି ଅଫିସରୁ ଫେରିବାକୁ ଉଛୁର ହେଲା' (I was late coming back from the office today). Its natural opposite in the same everyday register is ସଅଳ: 'କାଲି ସଅଳ ଆସିବ' (come early tomorrow). Remember the pair ଉଛୁର × ସଅଳ. Why not the others: ଶୀଘ୍ର (a Sanskrit word) and ଜଲ୍ଦି (from Persian, through Hindi) mean 'quickly, fast', so they describe speed, while ଉଛୁର is about timing — early versus late; ସଙ୍ଗେ ସଙ୍ଗେ means 'at once, immediately' and describes one action following another with no gap. In antonym questions, pick the word that is the exact opposite in the same sense and the same register, not just a word with a related idea. Similar everyday pairs to remember: ଆଗ × ପଛ, ଆଗୁଆ × ପଛୁଆ, ସକାଳ × ସଞ୍ଜ. Tip: test the pair in one sentence — 'ଉଛୁର ନ କରି ସଅଳ ଆସ' (don't be late, come early) reads naturally, which confirms they are opposites.
