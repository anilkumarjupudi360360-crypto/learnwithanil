# Paper III English (Q1-100) and Odia (Q101-200), OPSC Assistant Section Officer (Odisha Secretariat) written exam of 27 August 2022, Advt. No. 26 of 2021-22, Series A (OPSC's own booklet); answers: OPSC's answer key published with the result (9 January 2024)

Q 1 | eng | Grammar
Change the active voice into passive voice : I will clean the house every Saturday. The house ______ by me every Saturday.
(1) cleaned
(2) will cleaned
(3) will be cleaned
(4) None of these
= 3
~ The simple future passive is will + be + past participle. So "I will clean the house" becomes "The house will be cleaned by me". "Will cleaned" is ungrammatical (will needs a base form), and "cleaned" alone changes the tense to the past.

Q 2 | eng | Grammar
People ______ walk on grass.
(1) couldn't
(2) needn't
(3) mustn't
(4) may not
= 3
~ "Mustn't" (must not) expresses a prohibition, which is what a rule like "People mustn't walk on grass" means. "Needn't" means it is not necessary, "couldn't" refers to past inability, and "may not" suggests possibility or mild permission, not a firm prohibition.

Q 3 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct word order :
(1) Why she did leave so early ?
(2) Why did she leave so early ?
(3) Why did so early she leave ?
(4) Why so early did she leave ?
= 2
~ In a wh-question the order is: question word + auxiliary + subject + main verb + rest. So: Why (wh-word) did (auxiliary) she (subject) leave (verb) so early (adverbial)?

Q 4 | eng | Vocabulary
Select same meaning of the underlined word : Take care not to 'get hurt' with the steam from the Kettle.
(1) Burnt
(2) Bruised
(3) Scalded
(4) Cut
= 3
~ To be scalded is to be burnt by hot liquid or steam, so being hurt by steam from a kettle is "scalded". "Burnt" is used for dry heat or fire, "bruised" for a blow, and "cut" for a sharp edge.

Q 5 | eng | Grammar
______ his principles, he has to be very careful.
(1) With regard of
(2) With regard on
(3) With regard to
(4) None of these
= 3
~ The fixed phrase is "with regard to" (meaning concerning or in respect of): "With regard to his principles, he has to be very careful." "With regard of" and "with regard on" are not idiomatic.

Q 6 | eng | Grammar
He was seen ______ to the school.
(1) went
(2) going
(3) gone
(4) go
= 2
~ After the passive "was seen", the verb takes a present participle (or to-infinitive): "He was seen going to the school." The bare infinitive "go" is used only in the active ("I saw him go"), and "went"/"gone" cannot follow "was seen" here.

Q 7 | eng | Grammar
They organized a party ______ his honour. That Ajita was not invited ______ it, was a shame. She admired him most and it seems unlikely that she would forgive the organizers ______ their omission.
(1) at, for, towards
(2) about, at, against
(3) in, to, for
(4) after, on, with
= 3
~ The idioms are "in his honour" (to show respect for someone), "invited to" an event, and "forgive someone for" something they did. Hence: in, to, for.

Q 8 | eng | Vocabulary
When I say that the benefits of demonetization are moot, what do I mean ?
(1) That I do not understand what the benefits are.
(2) That I do not understand what demonetization is.
(3) That the benefits of demonetization are surely seen.
(4) That the benefits of demonetization are debatable.
= 4
~ "Moot" (as in a moot point) means open to argument or doubtful. So saying the benefits are moot means they are debatable, not that they are clearly seen or not understood.

Q 9 | eng | Grammar
Choose matching prepositions to complete the following phrases :
(I) According ______ the source ... — (i) of
(II) Different ______ the first draft ... — (ii) to
(III) Based ______ this information ... — (iii) by
(IV) Bored ______ the events ... — (iv) from
(V) Consisting ______ cards ... — (v) on
According to the code, the correctly matched pairs would be :
(1) I – (ii), II – (iv), III – (v), IV – (iii), V – (i)
(2) I – (i), II – (ii), III – (iii), IV – (v), V – (iv)

Q 10 | eng | Grammar
He had earned his well-paid job after a long struggle, but when he laughed at the most inopportune time, it cost him the job.
List the verbs in this sentence :
(1) earned, struggle, laughed
(2) had earned, laughed, cost
(3) struggle, laughed, cost
(4) job, laughed, inopportune
= 2
~ The verbs (verb phrases) are "had earned" (past perfect), "laughed" and "cost" (simple past of cost). "Struggle" is a noun here (after "a long"), "job" is a noun and "inopportune" is an adjective.

Q 11 | eng | Grammar
Which of the statements about the following sentence is TRUE ?
The Raos had been living so frugally all year that they saved enough money for a Mercedes.
(1) The present perfect continuous tense used here indicates a state that continues into the present.
(2) The past perfect continuous tense used here indicates an ongoing state prior to a past action.
(3) The perfect tense used here is not clear enough to justify the state indicated in the present.
(4) The past perfect tense used here does not indicate an ongoing state prior to a past action.
= 2
~ "Had been living" is the past perfect continuous (had + been + -ing). It shows an action or state that went on over a period before another past event — here, before they saved enough money for a Mercedes. It is not the present perfect continuous (has/have been living).

Q 12 | eng | Grammar
Choose from the options how the sentence can be improved :
They could not admire his bright performance because of they dislike him.
(1) because of their disliking of him
(2) because of their dislike him
(3) because they dislike him
(4) No correction required
= 3
~ "Because of" is a preposition and must be followed by a noun or noun phrase, not a clause. Before a clause with a subject and verb ("they dislike him") we use the conjunction "because": "... because they dislike him."

Q 13 | eng | Grammar
Choose from the options how the sentence can be improved :
She was uneasy because she never be on a plane before.
(1) had never been
(2) never been
(3) is never been
(4) No improvement
= 1
~ Her never having flown came before the past moment when she felt uneasy, so the past perfect is needed: "She was uneasy because she had never been on a plane before." "Never be", "never been" (no auxiliary) and "is never been" are ungrammatical.

Q 14 | eng | Grammar
Choose from the options how the sentence can be improved :
They only work when they have no money.
(1) When they have no money, they only work
(2) they only work
(3) work only when
(4) No improvement
= 3
~ "Only" should be placed next to the word or phrase it limits. The meaning is that they work only at the time when they have no money, so it goes before the when-clause: "They work only when they have no money."

Q 15 | eng | Grammar
Choose from the options how the sentence can be improved :
I. T. experts may have to stop hunting for better job opportunities as these are not so easily available now.
(1) No correction required
(2) may be stopping the hunting
(3) should have to stop hunt
(4) might have to stop hunting
= 1
~ The sentence is already correct: "may have to" expresses a possible necessity, and "stop + -ing" (stop hunting) means to give up an activity. Option D ("might have to") is also grammatical but only swaps the modal, so it corrects nothing, while B and C ("should have to stop hunt") are faulty. Hence no correction is required.

Q 16 | eng | Grammar
Choose from the options how the sentence can be improved :
The NYC building at New York City is as high as every other building in the United States of America.
(1) high than every other building
(2) as high as every other building
(3) higher than any other building
(4) No improvement
= 3
~ To show that one thing exceeds all others of its group, use the comparative with "any other": "higher than any other building". "As high as every other building" is illogical, and "high than" is ungrammatical because "than" needs a comparative form.

Q 17 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct part of speech for the words in bracket :
She (as well as) her sister is engaged.
(1) Conjunction
(2) Preposition
(3) Adjective
(4) Adverb
= 1
~ "As well as" here joins two nouns ("She" and "her sister"), so it works as a conjunction (a phrasal conjunction). The verb agrees with the first subject, hence "She as well as her sister is engaged."

Q 18 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct part of speech for the word in bracket :
This politician is notorious (for) corruption.
(1) Conjunction
(2) Preposition
(3) Adjective
(4) Adverb
= 2
~ "For" links the noun "corruption" to the adjective "notorious" and shows its relation to it, so it is a preposition (notorious for). It does not join clauses, so it is not a conjunction here.

Q 19 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct part of speech for the word in bracket :
This golden ring is too (costly) to purchase.
(1) Conjunction
(2) Preposition
(3) Adjective
(4) Adverb
= 3
~ "Costly" describes the noun "ring" (it is the complement after "is") and is modified by "too", so it is an adjective. Though it ends in "-ly", it is not an adverb; it means expensive.

Q 20 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct part of speech for the word in bracket :
A(drowning) man catches at a straw.
(1) Complement
(2) Infinitive
(3) Gerund
(4) Participle
= 3
~ OPSC's key gives "Gerund". In standard grammar, however, "drowning" here describes the noun "man" (a man who is drowning), so it is a present participle used as an adjective; an -ing word before a noun is a gerund when it shows purpose, as in "a swimming pool" (a pool for swimming). (Official OPSC key; some sources differ.)

Q 21 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct part of speech for the word in bracket :
A great talker is a great (liar).
(1) Article
(2) Verb
(3) Pronoun
(4) Noun

Q 22 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct part of speech for the words in bracket :
Eat (to live), but do not live to eat.
(1) Complement
(2) Infinitive
(3) Gerund
(4) Participle

Q 23 | eng | Grammar
Anita ______ for the charitable cause.
(1) come to contributed
(2) came to contributed
(3) came to contribute
(4) comes to contributed
= 3
~ After "to" (the infinitive marker) the verb must be in its base form: "came to contribute". "Contributed" cannot follow "to", and "come" with the singular subject Anita is also wrong.

Q 24 | eng | Grammar
Everyone at the party ______ amazed by his performance.
(1) was
(2) were
(3) are
(4) can
= 1
~ "Everyone" is singular and takes a singular verb. The past setting (the party, his performance) needs the past tense, so "Everyone at the party was amazed". The phrase "at the party" does not change the subject's number.

Q 25 | eng | Grammar
Alice is not at home at the moment. She ______ work.
(1) was on
(2) was at
(3) is on
(4) is at
= 4
~ "At the moment" shows the present, so the verb is "is"; and the idiom for being at one's workplace is "at work". Hence "She is at work."

Q 26 | eng | Grammar
French people love cooking, ______ the English don't seem very interested.
(1) when
(2) whenever
(3) where
(4) whereas
= 4
~ "Whereas" is a conjunction of contrast, used to compare two opposite facts: the French love cooking, whereas the English are not very interested. "When", "whenever" and "where" express time or place, not contrast.

Q 27 | eng | Comprehension & Usage
Choose the correct statement :
(1) My aunt who lives in Mumbai is a doctor.
(2) My aunt, who lives in Mumbai, is a doctor.
(3) My aunt, who lives in Mumbai is a doctor.
(4) My aunt who lives in Mumbai, is a doctor.
= 2
~ "Who lives in Mumbai" only adds extra information about an aunt already identified, so it is a non-defining relative clause and must be set off by a pair of commas — one before "who" and one after "Mumbai". Options C and D use only one comma, which is wrong.

Q 28 | eng | Grammar
Wait a minute, I ______ this box for you.
(1) carry
(2) will carry
(3) carried
(4) was carrying
= 2
~ A decision or offer made at the moment of speaking is expressed with "will" + base verb: "Wait a minute, I will carry this box for you." The past forms do not fit, and the simple present "carry" is not used for such spontaneous offers.

Q 29 | eng | Grammar
It's wet and rainy outside today. You ______ go out without an umbrella.
(1) shouldn't
(2) won't
(3) don't have to
(4) should
= 1
~ "Should not" gives advice against doing something; since it is raining, the sensible advice is "You shouldn't go out without an umbrella." "Don't have to" means there is no obligation, and "should" would advise going out without one.

Q 30 | eng | Grammar
Change into passive : The salt-water corroded the metal beams. The metal beams ______ by the salt-water.
(1) was corroded
(2) corroded
(3) has corroded
(4) were corroded
= 4
~ Simple past passive = was/were + past participle. The new subject "the metal beams" is plural, so "were corroded": "The metal beams were corroded by the salt-water."

Q 31 | eng | Grammar
The headmaster ______ to talk to you.
(1) want
(2) wants
(3) has wanted
(4) None of these
= 2
~ The subject "The headmaster" is third person singular, so the simple present verb takes -s: "The headmaster wants to talk to you." "Want" does not agree with the subject, and "has wanted" does not suit a wish that exists now.

Q 32 | eng | Grammar
A bomb scare ______ a delay of the flight.
(1) causing
(2) cause
(3) was caused
(4) had caused
= 4
~ Of the options, only "had caused" is a finite active verb that fits the singular subject "A bomb scare" and takes the object "a delay": "A bomb scare had caused a delay of the flight." "Causing" is not a finite verb, "cause" would need -s for a singular subject, and "was caused" (passive) cannot take the object "a delay".

Q 33 | eng | Grammar
Change into passive : Everyone understands English. English ______ by everyone.
(1) is understood
(2) has been understood
(3) was understood
(4) had understood
= 1
~ The active verb "understands" is in the simple present, so the passive is is/am/are + past participle: "English is understood by everyone." The other options change the tense, and "had understood" is not passive at all.

Q 34 | eng | Vocabulary
The diamond ring that her husband bought for her turned ______ to be fake one.
(1) in
(2) of
(3) out
(4) None of these
= 3
~ The phrasal verb "turn out" means to prove to be or be found to be in the end: the ring turned out to be fake. "Turn in" means to hand over or go to bed, and "turn of" is not a phrasal verb.

Q 35 | eng | Grammar
Tom ______ to call Susan yesterday but he has forgotten.
(1) is supposed
(2) supposed
(3) supposes
(4) was supposed
= 4
~ "Be supposed to" means to be expected or meant to do something. As the call was due "yesterday", the past form is needed: "Tom was supposed to call Susan yesterday ...". "Supposed" alone (without was) and "supposes" change the meaning to thinking or assuming.

Q 36 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct statement :
(1) She is senior than me.
(2) She is senior to me.
(3) She is senior then me.
(4) None of these
= 2
~ Latin comparatives such as senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior and anterior are followed by "to", not "than": "She is senior to me." "Then" is an adverb of time and is wrong here in any case.

Q 37 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct statement :
(1) I will inform this them.
(2) I will inform them of this.
(3) I will inform this to them.
(4) I will inform them with this.
= 2
~ "Inform" takes the person as its object and the matter after "of" (or "about"): inform someone of something. Hence "I will inform them of this." "Inform this to them" is a common error.

Q 38 | eng | Grammar
Which sentence is correct ?
(1) She lives in an apartment.
(2) She lives to an apartment.
(3) She lives on an apartment.
(4) She live in apartment.

~ We live "in" an enclosed place such as a house, flat or apartment, and a singular countable noun needs an article ("an apartment"). Option D also has the wrong verb form "live" for the singular subject "She".

Q 39 | eng | Grammar
Which sentence is correct ?
(1) We'll go shopping soon.
(2) We'll go a shopping soon.
(3) We'll go to shopping soon.
(4) We will shopping soon.

~ Activities expressed with an -ing word follow "go" directly, with no article or "to": go shopping, go swimming, go fishing. Option D lacks the main verb "go".

Q 40 | eng | Grammar
Which sentence is correct ?
(1) I'm tired of working.
(2) I am tired to working.
(3) I'm tiring of working.
(4) I'm tired and working.
= 1
~ The idiom "tired of" (+ noun or -ing form) means bored with or fed up with: "I'm tired of working." "Tired to working" is ungrammatical, and "tiring of" or "tired and working" do not give the intended meaning.

Q 43 | eng | Vocabulary
What is the synonym of "veracity" ?

(2) Reality

~ "Veracity" means truthfulness or conformity to facts. Of the options, "reality" (what is actually true) is closest; "inaccuracy" and "falsehood" are its opposites, and "redundancy" is unrelated.

Q 44 | eng | Vocabulary
Give the antonym of MILITARY :

~ "Military" relates to soldiers or the armed forces; its opposite is "civil" (relating to ordinary citizens, as in civil vs military administration). "Civility" means politeness, "militant" means aggressively active, and a "coup" is a seizure of power.

~ In a causative sentence the subject who makes someone else do the action is the ପ୍ରୟୋଜକ କର୍ତ୍ତା (causer); the one who actually does it is the ପ୍ରଯୋଜ୍ୟ କର୍ତ୍ତା (printed in option A as ପ୍ରଯୁଜ୍ୟ). In ମା' ପିଲାକୁ ଖୁଆଉଛନ୍ତି, ମା' is the ପ୍ରୟୋଜକ and ପିଲା the ପ୍ରଯୋଜ୍ୟ.

(3) ଧୀରେ

(2) ଯେଉଁ ଲୋକ ନ୍ୟାୟ ପରାୟଣ, ତାକୁ ନ୍ୟାୟ ରକ୍ଷା କରେ ।
(3) ଗୋବିନ୍ଦ ବାବୁ ଗୋଟିଏ କଥା ଭାବୁଛନ୍ତି ଏବଂ ଦୁଃଖିତ ହେଉଛନ୍ତି ।
(4) ଆପଣ ମୋ କଥା ନ ଶୁଣନ୍ତୁ, ତଥାପି ମୁଁ କହିବି ।
= 4
~ A compound (ଯୌଗିକ) sentence joins independent clauses with a coordinating word. In (D), 'ଆପଣ ମୋ କଥା ନ ଶୁଣନ୍ତୁ' and 'ମୁଁ କହିବି' are independent clauses with different subjects joined by ତଥାପି. (A) with ଯଦି and (B) with ଯେଉଁ…ତାକୁ are complex (ଜଟିଳ) sentences; (C) has one subject with two verbs, which OPSC's key does not treat as compound.

~ ପଚାରିବା here takes two objects. The thing asked — ପ୍ରଶ୍ନ (inanimate) — is the main object (ମୁଖ୍ୟ କର୍ମ), and the person asked — ଛାତ୍ରକୁ (animate) — is the secondary object (ଗୌଣ କର୍ମ). ଶିକ୍ଷକ is the subject and ପଚାରିଲେ the verb.

‘ଉଛୁର’ର ବିପରୀତ ଅର୍ଥବୋଧକ ଶବ୍ଦ ବାଛ :
(1) ସଙ୍ଗେ ସଙ୍ଗେ
(2) ଶୀଘ୍ର
(3) ଜଲ୍ଦି
(4) ସଅଳ
= 4
~ ଉଛୁର means 'late, delay' (e.g. ଉଛୁର ହେଲା — it got late); its exact opposite is ସଅଳ, 'early, in good time' (ସଅଳ ଆସିବ — come early). ଶୀଘ୍ର, ଜଲ୍ଦି and ସଙ୍ଗେ ସଙ୍ଗେ mean 'quickly / at once', which describe speed rather than earliness.
