Q 1 | eng | Error Spotting & Sentence Improvement
Identify the part of the sentence that contains a grammatical error.
A. The group of students, B. along with the professor, C. were discussing the new findings D. in the research lab.
(1) The group of students
(2) along with the professor
(3) were discussing the new findings
(4) in the research lab.
= 3
~ The subject is "The group" (singular); a phrase added with "along with" does not change the number of the subject. So the verb must be singular: "The group of students, along with the professor, was discussing the new findings."

Q 4 | eng | Fill in the Blanks & Cloze Test

She ______ her position on the matter only after repeated questioning by the press.
(1) reiterates
(2) clarified
(3) deflects
(4) assumes
= 2
~ "Clarified her position" means she made her stand clear: she explained where she stood only after the press questioned her repeatedly, a completed past event, so the past tense "clarified" fits. Q 13 | eng | Voice & Narration

Q 17 | eng | Voice & Narration
Choose the correct passive voice transformation of the sentence:
You must finish your homework.
(1) Your homework must be finished by you.
(2) Your homework must being finished by you.
(3) Your homework must have been finished by you.

Q 18 | eng | Voice & Narration
Choose the correct passive voice transformation of the sentence:
The new intern had forgotten to lock the laboratory door before leaving.
(1) The laboratory door was forgotten to be locked by the new intern before leaving.
(2) The laboratory door had been forgotten to be locked by the new intern before leaving.
(3) The laboratory door has been forgotten to be locked by the new intern before leaving.
(4) The laboratory door had forgotten to lock by the new intern before leaving.
= 2
~ Past perfect active (had + V3) becomes past perfect passive (had been + V3), and the infinitive "to lock" becomes the passive infinitive "to be locked", with "the laboratory door" as the new subject. Options 1 and 3 change the tense (was / has been), and option 4 drops "been" and keeps the active "to lock".

Q 19 | eng | Voice & Narration
Select the option that expresses the given sentence in direct voice.
The student told his friend that he had to go home that day because he had been feeling unwell since the day before, and his parents were waiting for him.
(1) "I have to go home today because I was feeling unwell since yesterday, and my parents are waiting for me," the student said to his friend.
(2) "I had to go home that day because I had been feeling unwell since the day before, and my parents were waiting for me," the student said to his friend.
(3) "I must go home today because I was feeling unwell since yesterday, and my parents were waiting for me," the student said to his friend.
(4) "I have to go home today because I had been feeling unwell since yesterday, and my parents were waiting for me," the student said to his friend.
= 1
~ Going back to direct speech reverses the changes: "had to" → "have to", "that day" → "today", "the day before" → "yesterday", "his parents were waiting for him" → "my parents are waiting for me". Only option 1 restores both "have to" and "are waiting".

Q 20 | eng | Reading Comprehension
Read the passage and answer the following questions:
India’s tryst with chess is deeply interwoven with its cultural heritage, for the game itself originally known as “chaturanga” emerged on the subcontinent over a millennium ago. Initially conceived as a strategic simulation of warfare, “chaturanga” evolved into shatranj under Persian influence before migrating westward, ultimately becoming modern chess.
In the contemporary era, India’s prominence on the global chess stage began with the meteoric ascent of Viswanathan Anand in the late 20th century. Anand’s fluid playing style, amalgamating tactical precision with positional depth, earned him the World Chess Championship title multiple times and inspired a generation of players. His success catalysed institutional investment in chess, leading to an upsurge in tournaments, academies, and grassroots initiatives across the country.
The 21st century has witnessed the emergence of prodigies such as Rameshbabu Praggnanandhaa, D. Gukesh, and Nihal Sarin, whose exploits in international arenas have positioned India as a formidable force in team events like the Chess Olympiad. Digital platforms have further accelerated talent development, enabling young aspirants to spar with grandmasters worldwide, thus refining their strategic acumen from an early age.
Today, India stands not merely as the cradle of chess’s origins but as an influential power shaping its future. This continuity from ancient invention to modern innovation underscores the nation’s enduring legacy in the chess world.
The phrase "enabling young aspirants to spar with grandmasters worldwide" functions as:
(1) Noun clause
(2) Adverbial phrase
(3) Participle phrase
(4) Absolute phrase
= 3
~ The phrase begins with the present participle "enabling" (with its object "young aspirants") and has no finite verb, so it is a participle (participial) phrase adding information about the main clause "Digital platforms have further accelerated talent development".

Q 26 | rea | Miscellaneous
Two numbers differ by 12. If their product is increased by the square of their difference, the result is 1008. Find the larger number.
(1) 18
(2) 24
(3) 30
(4) 36
= 3
~ Product + 12² = 1008, so product = 864. With numbers x and x − 12: x(x − 12) = 864 gives x = 36 (36 × 24 = 864; 864 + 144 = 1008). The keyed 30 (with 18) gives 30 × 18 + 144 = 684, not 1008. (SSC key; some sources differ.)
