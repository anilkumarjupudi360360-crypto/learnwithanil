Q 1 | eng | Idioms & One-word Substitution
Choose the one word substitute for the below sentence.
Jennifer asked her colleague, a person who is highly educated in a specific field called?
(1) Expert
(2) Novice
(3) Master

Q 3 | eng | Error Spotting & Sentence Improvement
Identify the incorrect sentence.
(1) The principles of science is difficult to understand.
(2) Reactions to the annual play have been favourable.
(3) The author of the stories in these books is Mary Gomes.
(4) Many lines in the poem are especially good.

Q 4 | eng | Fill in the Blanks & Cloze Test
Fill in the blank with the most appropriate preposition:
The director insisted ______ absolute silence during the rehearsal.
(1) on
(2) at
(3) over
(4) with

Q 5 | eng | Error Spotting & Sentence Improvement
Choose the best option to replace the underlined part to improve the given sentence. If no substitution is required, select 'No substitution.'
Everyone clapped as she stepped from the car.
(1) stepped out the car
(2) stepped off the car
(3) stepped out of
(4) No substitution
= 3

Q 7 | eng | Voice & Narration
From the alternatives given below, select the correct passive voice of the following sentence.
Do not put your hand over the stove lest you should burn your fingers.
(1) Let your hand be not put over the stove lest your fingers should be burnt.
(2) Lest your hand be not put over the stove let your fingers should be burnt.
(3) Let your hand not put over the stove lest your fingers should be burnt.
(4) Let your hand be not put over the stove lest your fingers should burn.
= 1
~ A negative imperative becomes passive as 'Let + object + be not + past participle': 'Let your hand be not put over the stove'. The 'lest' clause also turns passive: 'you should burn your fingers' → 'your fingers should be burnt'.

Q 8 | eng | Voice & Narration
From the alternatives given below, select the correct passive voice of the following sentence.

Q 9 | eng | Voice & Narration

Q 37 | rea | Arrangement & Puzzles
5 persons A, B, C, D and E are sitting around a circular table facing towards the centre (not necessarily in the same order). B is sitting second to the right of D. A is sitting second to the right of B. C is not an immediate neighbour of A. Who is to the immediate right of A?
(1) E
(2) B
(3) C
(4) D
= 4
~ Moving to the right from D: D, _, B, _, A (B is 2nd right of D, A is 2nd right of B). A's neighbours are D and the seat just before A, so C (not next to A) takes the seat after D and E the seat before A: D, C, B, E, A. The seat to A's immediate right is D.
