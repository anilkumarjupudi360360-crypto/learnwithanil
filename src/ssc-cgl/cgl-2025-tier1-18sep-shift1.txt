Q 1 | rea | Series
Select the letter-cluster from among the given options that can replace the question mark (?) in the following series.
CFJ, FIM, ILP, ?
(1) LKN
(2) LOS
(3) NHS
(4) KIS
= 2
~ Each letter of a cluster moves 3 places forward in the next cluster. First letters: C, F, I, L (+3 each). Second letters: F, I, L, O (+3 each). Third letters: J, M, P, S (+3 each). So the missing cluster is LOS. Within each cluster the gaps are also constant (+3, +4: C→F→J, F→I→M, I→L→P, L→O→S), which confirms LOS; LKN, NHS and KIS break this pattern.

Q 2 | rea | Syllogism & Statements
A statement is given followed by two conclusions. Find which conclusion is TRUE based on the given statement.
Statement:
C ≥ T ; A = D ≥ O; G ≥ D < S; T ≥ A
Conclusions:
I: A ≤ O
II: C ≥ G
(1) Either I or II
(2) Only II
(3) Only I
(4) Neither I nor II
= 4
~ Combine the statements: C ≥ T ≥ A = D ≥ O, and G ≥ D. Conclusion I: we know A ≥ O, so A ≤ O is not definitely true (it holds only if A = O). Conclusion II: C ≥ D and G ≥ D, but both are only compared with D, so no definite relation exists between C and G. Neither conclusion is definitely true. "Either I or II" does not apply because the two conclusions are about different pairs.

Q 3 | rea | Blood Relations & Directions
A is the wife of B. B is the son of C. D is the daughter of A. How is D related to C?
(1) Granddaughter
(2) Daughter
(3) Sister
(4) Niece
= 1
~ B is the son of C, and A is B's wife, so A is C's daughter-in-law. D is the daughter of A; taking A and B as D's parents, D is the daughter of C's son, that is, C's granddaughter. D is two generations below C, so "Daughter" (one generation below) and "Sister" (same generation) are wrong, and "Niece" would need D to be the daughter of C's brother or sister.

Q 4 | rea | Analogy & Classification
Solve:
LEAF : ? :: TIME : EMIT
(1) FEAL
(2) FAEL
(3) LFAE
(4) FELA
= 2
~ EMIT is TIME written in reverse order (T-I-M-E becomes E-M-I-T). Applying the same rule to LEAF: L-E-A-F reversed gives F-A-E-L, so the answer is FAEL. FEAL looks close but keeps E and A in their original order, so it is not the reverse of LEAF; LFAE and FELA are random rearrangements.

Q 5 | rea | Series
What comes next: 1, 2, 6, 24, 120, ?
(1) 720
(2) 504
(3) 840
(4) 600
= 1
~ Each term is the previous term multiplied by the next whole number: 1 × 2 = 2, 2 × 3 = 6, 6 × 4 = 24, 24 × 5 = 120, so the next term is 120 × 6 = 720. These are the factorials 1!, 2!, 3!, 4!, 5!, and the missing term is 6! = 720.

Q 6 | rea | Syllogism & Statements
Read the following statement carefully and identify the conclusion that follows.
Statement: All citizens must abide by traffic laws.
Conclusion I: Citizens who disobey traffic laws must be punished.
Conclusion II: Obeying traffic laws shows civic responsibility.
(1) Only I
(2) Only II
(3) Both follow
(4) Neither follows
= 3
~ The word "must" makes obeying traffic laws a compulsory duty of every citizen. Fulfilling such a duty is an act of civic responsibility, so Conclusion II follows. A compulsory law implies enforcement, so those who break it are liable to penalty, which is the basis for Conclusion I. Hence the key takes both conclusions as following. Strictly, the statement says nothing about punishment, so many solvers would accept only II. (SSC key; some sources differ.)

Q 7 | rea | Syllogism & Statements
Instruction: Identify the assumptions that must hold for the statement to be valid, then choose the correct option.
Statement: To reduce traffic congestion in urban areas, cities should invest in public transportation infrastructure, even though it requires significant initial investments.
Assumptions:
I. Public transportation can effectively reduce traffic congestion.
II. The initial cost of building transportation infrastructure is high.
III. People are willing to shift from private vehicles to public transportation.
(1) Only I and II are implicit
(2) Only II and III are implicit
(3) All I, II and III are implicit
(4) Only I is implicit
= 1
~ The advice to invest in public transport "to reduce traffic congestion" makes sense only if public transport can reduce congestion, so I is implicit. The phrase "even though it requires significant initial investments" takes for granted that the initial cost is high, so II is implicit. The statement says nothing about people's present willingness to leave private vehicles; better infrastructure may itself attract riders, so III is not a necessary assumption.

Q 8 | rea | Syllogism & Statements
Read the following statement carefully and identify the conclusion that follows.
Statement: Thousands of students appeared for the entrance exam conducted across the country.
Conclusions:
I. The exam is popular and competitive.
II. All students who appeared will get admission.
(1) Only Conclusion I follows
(2) Only Conclusion II follows
(3) Both I and II follow
(4) Neither I nor II follows
= 1
~ Thousands of candidates taking a nationwide entrance exam shows that the exam is popular, and so many applicants for limited seats make it competitive, so Conclusion I follows. An entrance exam is meant to select candidates; nothing in the statement suggests that every candidate will be admitted, so Conclusion II does not follow. Hence only Conclusion I follows.

Q 9 | rea | Syllogism & Statements
Read the following statement carefully and identify the conclusion that follows.
Statement:
All circles are shapes. Some shapes are squares. All squares are polygons.
Conclusions:
I. Some shapes are polygons.
II. Some polygons are circles.
(1) Only I follows
(2) Only II follows
(3) Both I and II follow
(4) Neither I nor II follows
= 1
~ "Some shapes are squares" and "All squares are polygons" give "Some shapes are polygons" (Some + All = Some), so Conclusion I follows. Circles are linked only to shapes, and the shapes that are squares need not include any circle, so there is no definite link between circles and polygons. Conclusion II is only a possibility, so it does not follow. Hence only I follows.

Q 11 | rea | Analogy & Classification
In each of the following questions, the second word is formed by applying a specific rearrangement or transformation to the first. Identify the same pattern and apply it to the third word to find the correct answer from the given alternatives. FLOWER : REWOLF :: FRUITS : ?
(1) STUIRF
(2) STIURF
(3) STUIFR
(4) STRUIF
= 2
~ REWOLF is FLOWER written backwards (F-L-O-W-E-R reversed gives R-E-W-O-L-F). Reversing FRUITS letter by letter: F-R-U-I-T-S becomes S-T-I-U-R-F, so the answer is STIURF. STUIRF looks similar but has U and I in the wrong order, and STUIFR and STRUIF are not reversals at all.

Q 12 | rea | Coding-Decoding
If LEMON is coded as KDNLN, how is MANGO written?
(1) LZODO
(2) LZLFP
(3) LZMFQ
(4) LZLFO
= 1
~ Compare LEMON with KDNLN letter by letter: L−1 = K, E−1 = D, M+1 = N, O−3 = L, N+0 = N. These shifts (−1, −1, +1, −3, 0) follow no regular pattern, so simply apply the same shift at each position of MANGO: M−1 = L, A−1 = Z (going back from A wraps round to Z), N+1 = O, G−3 = D, O+0 = O, giving LZODO. This matches the key, and LZODO is the only option with O in the third place.

Q 13 | rea | Miscellaneous Reasoning
Rearrange the letters of the word "LOVE" to form the name of an animal.
(1) VOLE
(2) VEOL
(3) VLEO
(4) OLEV
= 1
~ The letters L, O, V and E can be rearranged as VOLE. A vole is a small, mouse-like rodent with a short tail and rounded body that lives in fields and grasslands. VEOL, VLEO and OLEV use the same letters but are not meaningful English words, so VOLE is the only animal name.

Q 14 | rea | Series
Complete the series.
3, 5, 10, 12, 24, 26, ?
(1) 52
(2) 54
(3) 53
(4) 50
= 1
~ The series alternately adds 2 and multiplies by 2: 3 + 2 = 5, 5 × 2 = 10, 10 + 2 = 12, 12 × 2 = 24, 24 + 2 = 26. The next step is multiplication by 2, so the missing term is 26 × 2 = 52. The other options (54, 53, 50) do not fit this alternating +2, ×2 pattern; for example, 54 would need 26 × 2 + 2, two steps at once.

Q 15 | rea | Series
What comes next?
MOA, NPB, OQC, PRD, ?
(1) QRE
(2) QTE
(3) RST
(4) QSE
= 4
~ Each letter position moves one place forward in the alphabet. First letters: M, N, O, P, so next is Q. Second letters: O, P, Q, R, so next is S. Third letters: A, B, C, D, so next is E. The next term is therefore QSE. QRE repeats R in the middle position and QTE skips S, so both break the pattern.

Q 18 | rea | Analogy & Classification
Which of the following groups does not follow the same arithmetic rule?
(1) 10, 20, 40
(2) 5, 15, 45
(3) 2, 6, 18
(4) 3, 9, 27
= 1
~ In 5, 15, 45; 2, 6, 18 and 3, 9, 27 each number is 3 times the previous one (5 × 3 = 15, 15 × 3 = 45; 2 × 3 = 6, 6 × 3 = 18; 3 × 3 = 9, 9 × 3 = 27). In 10, 20, 40 each number is only 2 times the previous one (10 × 2 = 20, 20 × 2 = 40). So 10, 20, 40 does not follow the rule.

Q 19 | rea | Mathematical Operations
If 8 * 2 = 20 and 6 * 3 = 27, then what is 5 * 4 = ?
(1) 32
(2) 24
(3) 36
(4) 22
= 3
~ The rule is a * b = (a + b) × b. Check: 8 * 2 = (8 + 2) × 2 = 20 and 6 * 3 = (6 + 3) × 3 = 27. Therefore 5 * 4 = (5 + 4) × 4 = 9 × 4 = 36. The same rule can also be read as a × b + b²: 16 + 4 = 20, 18 + 9 = 27, and 20 + 16 = 36.

Q 20 | rea | Coding-Decoding
Instruction: In a code language, “The tree grows tall” is coded as “so mo no lo”, “The tree grows short” is coded as “so mo po lo”, “The plant grows tall” is coded as “to mo no lo”.
Question: What is the code for “The plant grows short”?
(1) to mo lo po
(2) so lo to fo
(3) po ko go jo
(4) no ko mo no
= 1
~ Sentences 1 and 2 differ only in tall/short and in no/po, so tall = no and short = po. Sentences 1 and 3 differ only in tree/plant and in so/to, so tree = so and plant = to. The common words "The" and "grows" are coded as mo and lo. Hence "The plant grows short" = to + mo + lo + po, that is, "to mo lo po" (order of codes does not matter).

Q 21 | rea | Miscellaneous Reasoning
A boat travels 60 kilometers upstream in 3 hours and covers the same distance of 60 kilometers downstream in 2 hours. What is the speed of the boat in still water?
(1) 20 km/h
(2) 18 km/h
(3) 25 km/h
(4) 15 km/h
= 3
~ Upstream speed = 60 ÷ 3 = 20 km/h and downstream speed = 60 ÷ 2 = 30 km/h. Speed of the boat in still water = (downstream speed + upstream speed) ÷ 2 = (30 + 20) ÷ 2 = 25 km/h. The speed of the stream is (30 − 20) ÷ 2 = 5 km/h. Option 20 km/h is only the upstream speed.

Q 22 | rea | Blood Relations & Directions
A man says, “The son of my wife's brother is my ______.
(1) Brother-in-law
(2) Nephew
(3) Cousin
(4) Son-in-law
= 2
~ The man's wife's brother is his brother-in-law. Q 23 | rea | Mathematical Operations
If '×' means '−', '−' means '+', '+' means '÷', then 12 + 6 × 2 − 4 = ?
(1) 4
(2) 8
(3) 6
(4) 10
= 1
~ Replace the signs as given: + becomes ÷, × becomes −, and − becomes +. So 12 + 6 × 2 − 4 becomes 12 ÷ 6 − 2 + 4. Following BODMAS, division first: 12 ÷ 6 = 2. Then 2 − 2 + 4 = 4. Hence the answer is 4. Always make all the substitutions first and only then apply BODMAS to the new expression.

Q 24 | rea | Mathematical Operations
If the sum of 31 and 19 is multiplied by 9, what is the result?
(1) 423
(2) 432
(3) 441
(4) 450
= 4
~ First find the sum: 31 + 19 = 50. Then multiply by 9: 50 × 9 = 450. A quick check: since the sum is a multiple of 10, the product must end in 0, which rules out 423, 432 and 441 at once. Hence the result is 450.

Q 25 | rea | Series
The series given below contains a sequence of numbers. Accordingly identify the incorrect combination 4, 5, 14, 14, 34, 36, 74, 68
(1) 74
(2) 34
(3) 36
(4) 68
= 3
~ The series is two alternating series. Odd places: 4, 14, 34, 74, each term × 2 + 6 (4 × 2 + 6 = 14, 14 × 2 + 6 = 34, 34 × 2 + 6 = 74), which is correct. Even places: 5, 14, ?, 68, each term × 2 + 4: 5 × 2 + 4 = 14, 14 × 2 + 4 = 32, 32 × 2 + 4 = 68. So the third even term should be 32, and 36 is the incorrect number.

Q 26 | ih | Art & Culture
Which of the following animals were NOT found on Mauryan stone pillars?
(1) Lions
(2) Bulls
(3) Elephants
(4) Eagle
= 4
~ Mauryan pillar capitals carry the lion (Sarnath, Vaishali, Lauriya Nandangarh), the bull (Rampurva) and the elephant (Sankissa), and the Sarnath abacus shows a lion, elephant, bull and horse. No eagle figure appears on any Mauryan pillar. The well-known Garuda pillar at Besnagar (Vidisha) was raised much later, around 113 BCE, by the Greek envoy Heliodorus during the Shunga period, so it is not a Mauryan pillar.

Q 28 | ih | Ancient India
What was the political significance of Bimbisara’s marriage to a Kosalan princess?
(1) Spread of Buddhism
(2) Peace with Kosala
(3) Trade with South India
(4) Fall of Pasenadi
= 2
~ Bimbisara of the Haryanka dynasty of Magadha strengthened his kingdom through marriage alliances. Q 29 | cur | Static GK & Culture
Which of the following correctly describes the musical form Svarajati?
(1) A bhajan form used only in temples
(2) A dance item without fixed structure
(3) A vocal form later adapted for dance
(4) A film-based item with fast rhythm
= 3
~ Svarajati is a sung composition of Carnatic music with a fixed structure: a pallavi (often with an anupallavi) and charanams, each charanam having a svara (solfa) passage with matching sahitya (lyrics). Beginners learn simple svarajatis after geethams and before varnams, and Shyama Shastri's svarajatis in Bhairavi, Yadukula Kambhoji and Todi are concert classics. It is also an important item of the Bharatanatyam repertoire, so option 3 fits; it is not a temple-only bhajan, a structureless dance item or a film item.

Q 31 | ih | Medieval India
What position did Hindu Beg hold during Humayun’s reign?
(1) Rajput chief
(2) Court scholar
(3) Provincial governor
(4) Spiritual guide
= 3
~ Hindu Beg was a senior Mughal noble who had served Babur. Alarmed by Sher Khan's victory over the Bengal army at Surajgarh (1534), Humayun appointed Hindu Beg governor (hakim) of Jaunpur to keep watch on the eastern region. Sher Khan (later Sher Shah Suri) assured him of his loyalty while he prepared, then sent him a threatening letter and attacked Bengal again in 1537, and Hindu Beg reported his hostile intentions to Humayun. So his post was that of a provincial governor.

Q 32 | ih | Modern India
Which of the following statements regarding the Government of India Act, 1919 is correctly matched with its constitutional implication?
(1) Transferred and Reserved subjects – Created functional overlap between central and provincial powers
(2) Creation of bicameralism – Introduced two houses at the central level for the first time
(3) Establishment of Public Service Commission – Gave financial control to Indian members in the Viceroy’s Council
(4) Introduction of diarchy in provinces – Empowered Indian ministers to control defence and foreign affairs
= 2
~ The Government of India Act, 1919 (Montagu–Chelmsford Reforms) made the central legislature bicameral for the first time, with a Legislative Assembly and a Council of State. Reserved and transferred subjects divided provincial subjects under diarchy; they did not create a central–provincial overlap. Ministers handled only transferred subjects such as education and health, never defence or foreign affairs. The Act merely provided for a Public Service Commission (set up in 1926) and gave no financial control to Indian members.

Q 36 | geo | World & Physical Geography
Which of the following pairs is correctly matched with reference to limestone cave formations?
(1) Stalactite – Rises from cave floor
(2) Stalagmite – Hangs from cave roof
(3) Pillar – Formed by union of stalactite and stalagmite
(4) Stalagmite – Formed by water falling from floor
= 3
~ In limestone (karst) caves, water carrying dissolved calcium carbonate drips from the roof and deposits calcite. Deposits hanging from the roof are stalactites, and those rising from the floor where the drops land are stalagmites. When a stalactite and a stalagmite grow until they meet, they form a pillar or column. Options 1 and 2 swap the two definitions, and stalagmites form from water dripping onto the floor, not water falling from it.

Q 37 | sci | Science & Technology
Which instrument description on XPoSat is correct?
(1) XSPECT extends POLIX’s polarimetry range to 0.5–20 keV.
(2) POLIX performs X-ray spectroscopy and timing.
(3) XSPECT measures X-ray polarization.
(4) POLIX measures X-ray polarization (8–30 keV) to study black holes.
= 4
~ XPoSat, India's first dedicated X-ray polarimetry satellite, was launched by PSLV-C58 on 1 January 2024. Its POLIX payload, built by the Raman Research Institute, measures the polarisation of X-rays in the 8–30 keV band from bright sources such as black holes and neutron stars. The second payload, XSPECT (built by URSC), carries out X-ray spectroscopy and timing in the 0.8–15 keV band; it does not measure polarisation or extend POLIX's range.

Q 38 | geo | Indian Geography
Which of the following statements best explains why age-specific rates are used in demography?
(1) They help identify regional literacy levels
(2) They reflect age-wise variation in fertility or mortality
(3) They show the economic condition of elderly people
(4) They reduce the total number of indicators needed
= 2
~ Crude birth and death rates are worked out for the whole population, so they are affected by its age structure. Age-specific rates, such as age-specific fertility rates and age-specific death rates, are calculated separately for each age group (for example 20–24 years). They show how fertility and mortality vary with age and allow fair comparison between populations with different age compositions. The total fertility rate is built from age-specific fertility rates.

Q 42 | pol | Judiciary & Constitutional Bodies
Which of the following statements correctly applies the weighted voting mechanism under Article 279A(9) of the Indian Constitution?
(1) The Centre holds 75% of the voting power in the GST Council.
(2) A GST Council decision can be passed solely with the support of the Centre and one State.
(3) The States collectively have twice the voting weight of the Centre in the GST Council.
(4) Each State has equal weightage as the Centre in GST Council voting.
= 3
~ Under Article 279A(9), every decision of the GST Council needs a majority of at least three-fourths of the weighted votes of members present and voting. The Central Government's vote carries one-third of the total votes cast, and all the State Governments together carry two-thirds, so the States collectively have twice the Centre's weight. The Centre therefore holds about 33%, not 75%, and cannot pass a decision alone or with one State; equally, the States cannot pass one without the Centre.

Q 43 | sci | Chemistry
Consider the given statements regarding the behavior of ideal gases:
