Q 1 | rea | Mathematical Operations
What will come in place of the question mark (?) in the following equation, if ‘+’ and ‘−’ are interchanged and ‘×’ and ‘÷’ are interchanged?
4515 × 5 − 431 ÷ 3 + 821 = ?
(1) 1335
(2) 1775
(3) 1575
(4) 1375
= 4
~ Make the interchanges first: × becomes ÷, − becomes +, ÷ becomes × and + becomes −. The equation reads 4515 ÷ 5 + 431 × 3 − 821. By BODMAS, do division and multiplication first: 4515 ÷ 5 = 903 and 431 × 3 = 1293. Then 903 + 1293 = 2196, and 2196 − 821 = 1375. A common slip is to change only one pair of signs, which gives a different total.

Q 2 | rea | Analogy & Classification
31 is related to 152 by certain logic. Following the same logic, 47 is related to 168. To which of the following is 66 related, following the same logic? (NOTE : Operations should be performed on the whole numbers, without breaking down the numbers into its constituent digits. E.g. 13 – Operations on 13 such as adding /deleting /multiplying etc., to 13 can be performed. Breaking down 13 into 1 and 3 and then performing mathematical operations on 1 and 3 is not allowed.)
(1) 180
(2) 187
(3) 185
(4) 190
= 2
~ Find the difference in each given pair: 152 − 31 = 121 and 168 − 47 = 121. So the rule is "add 121" (121 = 11²) to the first number. Applying it to 66: 66 + 121 = 187. The other options give differences of 114 (180), 119 (185) and 124 (190), which do not fit the rule.

Q 3 | rea | Non-verbal Reasoning
Six letters Q, Z, V, T, L and A are written on different faces of a dice. Two positions of this dice are shown in the figures below. Find the letter on the face opposite to Q.
(1) Z
(2) T
(3) V
(4) A
= 2
! img/c24s1-q3.png
~ V is common to both positions and sits on the same (right) face in both, so the dice has only been turned about the axis through V. When the common face is in the same position, the faces in matching positions are opposite: top Q is opposite top T, and left Z is opposite left L. V touches Q, Z, T and L, so V is opposite A. Hence the letter opposite Q is T.

Q 4 | rea | Analogy & Classification
In the following number-pairs, the second number is obtained by applying certain mathematical operations to the first number. In three of the four pairs, the same pattern is applied and hence they form a group. Select the number-pair that DOES NOT belong to this group.
(NOTE: Operations should be performed on the whole numbers, without breaking down the numbers into their constituent digits. E.g. 13 – Operations on 13 such as adding/subtracting/multiplying to 13 can be performed. Breaking down 13 into 1 and 3 and then performing mathematical operations on 1 and 3 is not allowed.)
(1) 335 - 119
(2) 358 - 152
(3) 317 - 101
(4) 308 - 92
= 2
~ Subtract the second number from the first in each pair. 335 − 119 = 216, 317 − 101 = 216 and 308 − 92 = 216, so the rule is "subtract 216" (216 = 6³). For 358 − 152 the difference is 206, not 216; following the rule, 358 should give 142. So the pair 358 - 152 does not belong to the group.

Q 5 | rea | Coding-Decoding
In a certain code language, 'NAME’ is written as 'FNBO' and 'NANO' is written as 'POBO’. How will 'NAIL’ be written in that language?
(1) MOBJ
(2) MJOB
(3) MOJB
(4) MJBO
= 4
~ Write the word in reverse order and then move each letter one step forward. NAME reversed is EMAN; +1 gives FNBO. NANO reversed is ONAN; +1 gives POBO. So NAIL reversed is LIAN, and +1 on each letter gives L→M, I→J, A→B, N→O, that is MJBO. The other options use the right letters in a wrong order.

Q 6 | rea | Analogy & Classification
The question contains pairs of words that are related to each other in a certain way. Three of the following four word pairs are alike as these have the same relationship and thus form a group. Which word pair is the one that DOES NOT belong to that group?
(The words must be considered as meaningful English words and must not be related to each other based on the number of letters/number of consonants/vowels in the word.)
(1) Pentagon - 5 sides
(2) Hexagon - 8 sides
(3) Triangle - 3 sides
(4) Square - 4 sides
= 2
~ Each pair matches a polygon with its number of sides. A pentagon has 5 sides, a triangle 3 and a square 4, so these three pairs are correct. A hexagon has 6 sides (hexa = six); 8 sides belong to an octagon. So "Hexagon - 8 sides" breaks the relationship and is the odd pair.

Q 7 | rea | Miscellaneous Reasoning
The position of how many letters will remain unchanged if each of the letters in the word ‘NIGHTMARES’ is re-arranged in the English alphabetical order from left to right?
(1) One
(2) Four
(3) Two
(4) Three
= 2

Compare position by position: 3rd G = G, 4th H = H, 6th M = M and 8th R = R; all other positions differ. So the positions of four letters (G, H, M and R) remain unchanged.

Q 8 | rea | Mathematical Operations
What will come in the place of ‘?’ in the following equation, if ‘+’ and ‘−’ are interchanged and ‘×’ and ‘÷’ are interchanged?
5 − 4 ÷ 9 + 80 × 10 = ?
(1) 31
(2) 33
(3) 35
(4) 30
= 2
~ After the interchanges, − becomes +, ÷ becomes ×, + becomes − and × becomes ÷. The equation becomes 5 + 4 × 9 − 80 ÷ 10. Apply BODMAS: 4 × 9 = 36 and 80 ÷ 10 = 8. Then 5 + 36 − 8 = 33. Solving without following BODMAS (left to right) would give a wrong value.

Q 12 | rea | Syllogism & Statements
Three statements are given followed by three conclusions numbered I, II and III. Assuming the statements to be true, even if they seem to be at variance with commonly known facts, decide which of the conclusions logically follow(s) from the statements.
Statements:
No card is a parcel.
All cards are envelopes.
Some envelopes are bags.
Conclusions:
I. All envelopes can never be parcels.
II. No card is a bag.
III. At least some bags are postcards.
(1) Only I follows
(2) Only II follows
(3) Only I and III follow
(4) Only II and III follow
= 1
~ All cards are envelopes and no card is a parcel, so some envelopes (the cards) are definitely not parcels; therefore "all envelopes are parcels" is impossible and I follows. Cards and bags have no definite relation (some cards may or may not be bags), so II does not follow. Postcards are not mentioned in the statements, so III does not follow. Only I follows.

Q 20 | rea | Coding-Decoding
In a certain code language, ‘what where how’ is written as ‘aa dd ff’; ‘where there that’ is written as ‘dd zz pp’ and ‘which what here’ is written as ‘ff kk ll’. How is ‘how’ written in that language?
(1) aa
(2) kk
(3) zz
(4) ff
= 1
~ Compare sentences that share a word. "where" is common to the first two sentences and "dd" is common to their codes, so where = dd. "what" is common to the first and third, and "ff" is common to their codes, so what = ff. In the first sentence the remaining word "how" must take the remaining code "aa". So how = aa.

Q 21 | rea | Syllogism & Statements
Three statements are given followed by three conclusions numbered I, II and III. Assuming the statements to be true, even if they seem to be at variance with commonly known facts, decide which of the conclusions logically follow(s) from the statements.
Statements:
Some tears are drops.
Some drops are streams.
All streams are rivers.
Conclusions:
I. All tears can never be rivers.
II. Some drops are rivers.
III. All tears being streams is a possibility.
(1) Only conclusion I follows
(2) Both II and III conclusion follow
(3) Only conclusion III follows
(4) Both I and II conclusion follow
= 2
~ Some drops are streams and all streams are rivers, so those drops are rivers: II follows. Tears and streams have no definite relation, so all tears being streams is possible: III follows. Since all tears could be streams (and so rivers), "all tears can never be rivers" is false, so I does not follow. Hence both II and III follow.

Q 23 | rea | Blood Relations & Directions
In a certain code language,
‘A + B’ means ‘A is the mother of B’,
‘A − B’ means ‘A is the father of B’,
‘A $ B’ means ‘A is the son of B’,
‘A % B’ means ‘A is the sister of B’,
‘A / B’ means ‘A is the wife of B’ and
‘A = B’ means ‘A is the brother of B’.
Which of the following means A is the daughter of K?
i. A + K / J − P = D
ii. J % P = K + A = D
iii. K + A / P − D % J
iv. D + K = A / P − J
(1) i
(2) iii
(3) ii
(4) iv
= 2
~ Decode iii: K + A means K is the mother of A, and A / P means A is the wife of P, so A is female. Hence A is the daughter of K. In i, A is the mother of K. In ii, K + A = D makes A the brother of D, so A is K's son. In iv, D is the mother of K and A, and K is A's brother, so A is D's daughter, not K's.

Q 24 | rea | Analogy & Classification

(NOTE: Operations should be performed on the whole numbers, without breaking down the numbers into its constituent digits. Q 29 | ih | Ancient India

Q 31 | pol | Judiciary & Constitutional Bodies
Which article has a similar provision to that of Article 32 and deals with writ jurisdiction?
(1) Article 228
(2) Article 227
(3) Article 226
(4) Article 225
= 3
~ Article 32 lets a person move the Supreme Court to enforce Fundamental Rights through writs such as habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari and quo warranto. Article 226 gives every High Court a similar power to issue these writs, and it is wider, since High Courts can issue them for Fundamental Rights and "for any other purpose", that is, other legal rights. Article 227 gives High Courts superintendence over subordinate courts, Article 228 lets them withdraw cases involving substantial constitutional questions, and Article 225 continues their existing jurisdiction.

Q 33 | sci | Biology
Which of the following statements best defines the monoecious?
(1) A flower with gynoecium only
(2) A flower with androecium only
(3) A flower with both androecium and gynoecium
(4) A flower with dithecous
= 3
~ Monoecious ("one house") means that male and female reproductive organs are borne on the same individual; NCERT equates it with the bisexual condition, and option 3, a flower with both androecium (stamens) and gynoecium (carpels), is the only choice showing both. Strictly, botanists call such a flower bisexual or perfect and use "monoecious" for a plant bearing separate male and female flowers, as in maize, cucurbits and coconut. Options 1 and 2 are unisexual (pistillate, staminate) flowers, and "dithecous" describes a typical angiosperm anther (with two thecae), not a type of flower.

Q 36 | sci | Chemistry
Which of the following decomposition reactions is NOT a redox reaction?
(1) Decomposition of sodium hydride
(2) Decomposition of calcium carbonate
(3) Decomposition of potassium chlorate
(4) Decomposition of dihydrogen monoxide
= 2
~ A reaction is redox only if oxidation numbers change. In CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂, calcium stays +2, carbon +4 and oxygen −2, so nothing is oxidised or reduced; NCERT cites it as a decomposition that is not a redox reaction. The others are redox: 2NaH → 2Na + H₂ (Na +1 to 0, H −1 to 0), 2KClO₃ → 2KCl + 3O₂ (Cl +5 to −1, O −2 to 0) and 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂ (H +1 to 0, O −2 to 0); dihydrogen monoxide is water.

Q 40 | sci | Chemistry

(2) KOH>CsOH>NaOH>LiOH
(3) LiOH>KOH>CsOH>NaOH
(4) CsOH>KOH>NaOH>LiOH
= 4
~ Alkali metal hydroxides are strong bases, and their basic strength increases down group 1: LiOH < NaOH < KOH < RbOH < CsOH.
