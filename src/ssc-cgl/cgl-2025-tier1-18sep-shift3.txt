Q 1 | rea | Series
Select the letter-cluster from among the given options that can replace the question mark (?) in the following series.
D I N, I N S, N S X, S X C, ?
(1) LKA
(2) JNA
(3) GVA
(4) XCH
= 4
~ Within each cluster the letters move +5: D (4) → I (9) → N (14). Each new cluster drops the first letter of the previous one and adds a letter 5 steps ahead: DIN, INS, NSX, SXC. So the next cluster starts with X, C and adds H: C (3) + 5 = H (8), just as X (24) + 5 wraps round to C (3). The answer is XCH. LKA, JNA and GVA break the +5 pattern.

Q 2 | rea | Mathematical Operations
If 6 @ 2 = 9, 15 @ 3 = 125, then 8 @ 4 = ?
(1) 4
(2) 8
(3) 16
(4) 10
= 3
~ The rule is a @ b = (a ÷ b) raised to the power b. Check: 6 @ 2 = (6 ÷ 2)² = 3² = 9 and 15 @ 3 = (15 ÷ 3)³ = 5³ = 125. So 8 @ 4 = (8 ÷ 4)⁴ = 2⁴ = 16. Options 4 and 8 are 2² and 2³, which use the wrong power, and 10 fits no consistent rule.

Q 3 | rea | Mathematical Operations
Choose the correct signs to swap.
40 ÷ 8 + 10 − 5 × 2 = 5
(1) ÷ and ×
(2) + and −
(3) ÷ and +
(4) − and ×
= 2
~ The printed equation is already true (5 + 10 − 10 = 5), so the question really asks which swap keeps it true. By BODMAS, swapping + and − gives 40 ÷ 8 − 10 + 5 × 2 = 5 − 10 + 10 = 5. The other swaps give 327.5 (÷ and ×: 40 × 8 + 10 − 5 ÷ 2), 30.8 (÷ and +: 40 + 8 ÷ 10 − 5 × 2) and 53 (− and ×: 40 ÷ 8 + 10 × 5 − 2). So + and − is the only valid swap.

Q 4 | rea | Blood Relations & Directions
A is the brother of B. B is the sister of C. C is the brother of D. How is B related to D?
(1) Sister
(2) Aunt
(3) Niece
(4) Can’t say
= 1
~ B is the sister of C, so B is female. C is the brother of D, so C and D are siblings, which makes A, B, C and D children of the same parents. Hence B is D's sister. "Can't say" is tempting because D's gender is not given, but it does not matter: the question asks how B is related to D, and B, being female, is D's sister either way. Aunt and niece would need a different generation.

Q 5 | rea | Analogy & Classification
Solve:
FD : KH :: QM : ?
(1) VP
(2) WT
(3) VQ
(4) UW
= 3
~ Use letter positions (A = 1 … Z = 26). In FD : KH, F (6) + 5 = K (11) and D (4) + 4 = H (8). Apply the same steps to QM: Q (17) + 5 = V (22) and M (13) + 4 = Q (17). So QM : VQ. VP has the right first letter but adds only 3 to M, while WT and UW do not follow either step.

Q 6 | rea | Series
What comes next: 13, 16, 21, 30, 45, ?
(1) 70
(2) 66
(3) 67
(4) 68
= 4
~ Take differences: 16 − 13 = 3, 21 − 16 = 5, 30 − 21 = 9, 45 − 30 = 15. The differences themselves grow by 2, 4, 6, so the next increase is 8 and the next difference is 15 + 8 = 23. The next term is 45 + 23 = 68. 67 or 66 would need a difference of 22 or 21, which breaks the 2, 4, 6, 8 pattern of second differences.

Q 7 | rea | Syllogism & Statements
Read the following statement carefully and identify the conclusion that follows.
Statement: Rising noise pollution in urban areas is affecting children’s concentration.
Conclusion I: Urban children can’t study well.
Conclusion II: Controlling noise pollution may improve children’s concentration.
(1) Only I follows
(2) Only II follows
(3) Both follow
(4) Neither follows
= 2
~ Conclusion I is too sweeping: the statement says noise is affecting concentration, not that urban children cannot study well at all, so I does not follow. Since rising noise is harming concentration, it is reasonable to infer that controlling noise pollution may improve it; the cautious word "may" makes II a valid conclusion. Hence only II follows.

Q 8 | rea | Analogy & Classification
Identify the odd one out:
(1) 121
(2) 131
(3) 444
(4) 544
= 4
~ 121, 131 and 444 are palindromic numbers: each reads the same forwards and backwards. 544 read backwards is 445, so it is not a palindrome and is the odd one out. Other properties do not group the numbers consistently (121 is a perfect square, 131 is prime, 444 is even), so the palindrome property is the intended rule.

Q 9 | rea | Syllogism & Statements
Instruction: Identify the assumptions that must hold for the statement to be valid, then choose the correct option.
Statement: The introduction of Artificial Intelligence into healthcare systems can enhance diagnostic accuracy, but its widespread implementation may lead to ethical dilemmas in patient care.
Assumptions:
I. AI can improve healthcare diagnostics.
II. The ethical concerns surrounding AI in healthcare are significant.
III. AI will eventually replace human healthcare professionals.
(1) Only I and II are implicit
(2) Only II and III are implicit
(3) All I, II and III are implicit
(4) Only I is implicit
= 1
~ An assumption is what the statement takes for granted. Saying AI "can enhance diagnostic accuracy" presumes that AI can improve healthcare diagnostics, so I is implicit. Warning that its use "may lead to ethical dilemmas" presumes the ethical concerns are real and significant enough to mention, so II is implicit. The statement nowhere suggests that AI will replace human healthcare professionals, so III is not implicit. Hence only I and II are implicit.

Q 10 | rea | Syllogism & Statements
Read the following statement carefully and identify the conclusion that follows.
Statement: All metro stations are under CCTV surveillance.
Conclusions:
I. Activities at metro stations are being monitored.
II. No criminal activity can happen in metro stations.
(1) Only Conclusion I follows
(2) Only Conclusion II follows
(3) Both I and II follow
(4) Neither I nor II follows
= 1
~ CCTV surveillance means the stations are being watched, so activities at metro stations are being monitored and conclusion I follows. Surveillance can deter or record crime but cannot make it impossible, so the absolute claim in conclusion II that no criminal activity can happen does not follow. Hence only conclusion I follows.

Q 11 | rea | Arrangement & Puzzles
Direction: Study the information carefully and answer the given questions.
Eight People Tina, Raj, Dia, Viraj, Soma, Meena, Neha and Ria are living in an eight-storey building, but not necessarily in the same order. The ground floor is numbered 1 and the floor above it is numbered 2, and so on, until the topmost floor is numbered 8. Dia lives on the lowermost floor. Only three people live between Dia and Ria. Tina lives on the 4th floor. Only two people live between Tina and Raj. Only five people live between Viraj and Soma. Neha lives on an odd numbered floor. Only one person lives between Meena and Viraj.
Who lives three floors below the one who lives immediate below Raj?
(1) Neha
(2) Tina
(3) Dia
(4) Raj
= 1
~ Dia is on floor 1, so Ria (three people between) is on 5. Tina is on 4, so Raj (two between) is on 7, since 1 is taken. Viraj and Soma have five people between them, so they take floors 2 and 8. Neha and Meena take floors 3 and 6; Neha is on an odd floor, so Neha is on 3 and Meena on 6. One person between Meena and Viraj puts Viraj on 8 and Soma on 2. Immediately below Raj (7) is Meena (6), and three floors below 6 is floor 3: Neha.

Q 12 | rea | Syllogism & Statements
Read the following statement carefully and identify the conclusion that follows.
Statement:
No painters are athletes. Some artists are athletes. All artists are painters.
Conclusions:
I. Some painters are athletes.
II. Some athletes are not painters.
(1) Only I follows
(2) Only II follows
(3) Both I and II follow
(4) Neither I nor II follows
= 2
~ The statements clash: artists who are athletes are also painters (all artists are painters), which contradicts "No painters are athletes". SSC's key takes "No painters are athletes" as given. Conclusion I (Some painters are athletes) directly contradicts it, so I does not follow. It also means no athlete is a painter, and athletes exist (some artists are athletes), so some athletes are not painters: II follows. Combining the other two statements would instead give I, which is why "Both follow" is also argued. (SSC key; some sources differ.)

Find the odd one out:
Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Arctic, Mediterranean
(1) Pacific
(2) Atlantic
(3) Mediterranean
(4) Arctic

Q 14 | rea | Coding-Decoding
If CLOUD is coded as BKNTC, how is RAINY written?
(1) QZHMX
(2) QZHLX
(3) QZHLW
(4) QZHMV
= 1
~ Each letter of CLOUD moves one step back: C − 1 = B, L − 1 = K, O − 1 = N, U − 1 = T, D − 1 = C, giving BKNTC. Apply the same rule to RAINY: R − 1 = Q, A − 1 = Z (wrapping round the alphabet), I − 1 = H, N − 1 = M, Y − 1 = X, giving QZHMX. The other options go wrong at the fourth or fifth letter (L instead of M, or W or V instead of X).

Q 15 | rea | Analogy & Classification
In each of the following questions, a specific alphabetical pattern is applied to the first set of letters to form the second. Apply the same logic to find the missing term that relates to the third set in the same way. Choose the correct option from the given alternatives.
AEFJ : KOPT :: ? : QUVZ
(1) HKQL
(2) GLKP
(3) GKLP
(4) HLKP
= 3
~ In AEFJ : KOPT each letter moves 10 steps forward: A (1) + 10 = K (11), E (5) + 10 = O (15), F (6) + 10 = P (16), J (10) + 10 = T (20). The missing term must give QUVZ after adding 10, so go 10 steps back: Q (17) → G (7), U (21) → K (11), V (22) → L (12), Z (26) → P (16). The answer is GKLP. GLKP has K and L swapped, so it is a trap.

Q 16 | rea | Analogy & Classification
TEACHER is to CLASSROOM as SURGEON is to:
(1) PATIENT
(2) SCALPEL
(3) OPERATION
(4) OT (Operation Theatre)
= 4
~ The relation is person : place of work. A teacher works in a classroom, and a surgeon works in an operation theatre (OT), so OT completes the analogy. A patient is the person a surgeon treats, a scalpel is the surgeon's tool and an operation is the surgeon's work; none of these is the place where the surgeon works, so they break the relation.

Q 17 | rea | Series
What is the sum of the first 10 terms of the series?
1, 3, 5, 7, ...?
(1) 90
(2) 100
(3) 110
(4) 120
= 2
~ The series 1, 3, 5, 7, … is the sequence of odd numbers, an AP with first term 1 and common difference 2. The sum of the first n odd numbers is n², so the sum of the first 10 terms is 10² = 100. Check with the AP formula: S = n/2 × [2a + (n − 1)d] = 10/2 × [2 + 18] = 5 × 20 = 100 (the 10th term is 19).

Q 18 | rea | Series
What comes next?
BYD, DWF, FUH, ?
(1) HSJ
(2) IQK
(3) HQJ
(4) JQL
= 1
~ Track each position separately. First letters: B, D, F move two steps forward, so the next is H. Second letters: Y, W, U move two steps backward, so the next is S. Third letters: D, F, H move two steps forward, so the next is J. The next cluster is HSJ. HQJ goes wrong on the second letter (Q instead of S), while IQK and JQL break the pattern in every position.

Q 19 | rea | Analogy & Classification
Find the odd one out from the given alternatives:
(1) Troposphere
(2) Stratosphere
(3) Hydrosphere
(4) Mesosphere
= 3
~ Troposphere, stratosphere and mesosphere are layers of the Earth's atmosphere (from the ground up: troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere, exosphere). The hydrosphere is not an atmospheric layer; it is all the water on Earth, including oceans, rivers, lakes, ice and groundwater. So hydrosphere is the odd one out. All four end in "-sphere", so the suffix alone does not decide the answer.

Find the odd one out from the given alternatives:
(1) Retina
(2) Cornea
(3) Iris
(4) Femur
= 4
~ Retina, cornea and iris are parts of the human eye: the cornea is the transparent front layer, the iris is the coloured part that controls the size of the pupil, and the retina is the light-sensitive layer at the back. Q 21 | rea | Coding-Decoding
In a certain code, NIGHT is written as OHJIS How will LIGHT be written?
(1) MHJIS
(2) KJHIS
(3) LIHIS
(4) MIHIS
= 1
~ Compare NIGHT with OHJIS letter by letter: N + 1 = O, I − 1 = H, G + 3 = J, H + 1 = I, T − 1 = S. Apply the same steps to LIGHT: L + 1 = M, I − 1 = H, G + 3 = J, H + 1 = I, T − 1 = S, giving MHJIS. Quicker: NIGHT and LIGHT share IGHT, so the code must end in HJIS and only the first letter changes (L + 1 = M). KJHIS, LIHIS and MIHIS alter the common part.

Q 22 | rea | Blood Relations & Directions
Pointing to a woman, John says, “She is the wife of my uncle’s only son.” How is the woman related to John?
(1) Sister
(2) Cousin
(3) Sister-in-law
(4) Niece
= 3
~ John's uncle's only son is John's cousin, and the woman is that cousin's wife. Strictly, English would call her a cousin-in-law, but in Indian usage a cousin's wife is called sister-in-law (bhabhi, the word the Hindi option uses), and it is the only option that fits. "Cousin" describes her husband, not her; sister and niece are blood relations that do not fit a cousin's wife. Hence the woman is John's sister-in-law.

Q 23 | rea | Mathematical Operations
If × = +, + = −, − = ×; then 4 × 5 + 2 − 3 = ?
(1) 3
(2) 7
(3) 35
(4) 29
= 1
~ Replace the signs as given: × becomes +, + becomes − and − becomes ×. So 4 × 5 + 2 − 3 becomes 4 + 5 − 2 × 3. By BODMAS, multiply first: 2 × 3 = 6, then 4 + 5 − 6 = 3. Option 7 comes from 4 + 5 − 2, which ignores the multiplication by 3; 35 and 29 do not come from the correct substitution.

Q 24 | rea | Miscellaneous Reasoning
If the sum of 13 and 39 is multiplied by 2, what is the result?
(1) 100
(2) 104
(3) 106
(4) 110
= 2
~ The wording "the sum … is multiplied by 2" means (13 + 39) × 2. First add: 13 + 39 = 52. Then multiply: 52 × 2 = 104. Check with the distributive law: 13 × 2 + 39 × 2 = 26 + 78 = 104, the same result. 100, 106 and 110 are close figures that come from slips in the addition or multiplication.

Q 25 | rea | Series
The series given below contains a sequence of numbers. Accordingly identify the incorrect combination. 25, 32, 46, 67, 97
(1) 32
(2) 46
(3) 97
(4) 67
= 3
~ Look at the differences: 32 − 25 = 7, 46 − 32 = 14, 67 − 46 = 21, 97 − 67 = 30. The first three are consecutive multiples of 7 (7, 14, 21), so the next difference should be 28 and the last term 67 + 28 = 95. Hence 97 is the incorrect term. 32, 46 and 67 all fit the +7, +14, +21 pattern, so they are correct.

Q 26 | sci | Science & Technology
What is the contribution of Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre (VSSC) to India’s space program?
(1) Launch site for ISRO missions
(2) Designs and builds launch vehicles
(3) Trains Indian astronauts
(4) Controls ISRO satellites
= 2
~ The Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre at Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala, is ISRO's lead centre for launch vehicle technology. It designs and develops India's rockets, from SLV-3 and ASLV to PSLV, GSLV and LVM3, as well as sounding rockets. It grew out of the Thumba Equatorial Rocket Launching Station (1963) and was named after Vikram Sarabhai after his death in 1971. Launches take place from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre, Sriharikota; astronaut training is the job of the Human Space Flight Centre, and satellite tracking and control that of ISTRAC and the Master Control Facility.

Q 28 | ih | Modern India
The integration of the princely states into the Indian Union after 1947 involved a complex interplay of political negotiation, popular movements, and, at times, coercion. Which of the following factors most significantly undermined the initial aspirations of some princely rulers to maintain independent or autonomous status?
(1) The overwhelming military superiority of the newly formed Pakistani state, compelling the rulers to seek protection within India.
(2) The consistent and unwavering support provided by the British government to the princely states seeking independence.
(3) The strong undercurrent of popular will within the princely states favoring accession to India, often expressed through organized movements and protests.
(4) The economic prosperity and stability offered by remaining independent, making integration into the financially strained Indian Union unattractive.
= 3
~ When British paramountcy lapsed in 1947, rulers such as those of Travancore, Hyderabad, Bhopal and Jammu and Kashmir hoped to stay independent. But the people of most states wanted to join India, and the States' People's movements and Praja Mandals, linked to the All India States People's Conference (1927), organised agitations, as in Travancore, Junagadh, Hyderabad and the Orissa states. Together with the diplomacy of Sardar Patel and V.P. Menon through the Instrument of Accession, this popular pressure made independence unworkable. Britain did not back independent states, and Pakistan was not militarily dominant.

Q 29 | pol | Judiciary & Constitutional Bodies
The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) was introduced as part of:
(1) The effort to codify personal laws.
(2) The effort to modernize and decolonize India’s criminal justice system.
(3) The new taxation policy.
(4) A trade agreement.
= 2
~ The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 replaced the colonial-era Indian Penal Code, 1860. It was enacted together with the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (replacing the CrPC, 1973) and the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (replacing the Indian Evidence Act, 1872); all three received assent in December 2023 and came into force on 1 July 2024. The government presented them as decolonising and modernising criminal justice, with a focus on justice rather than punishment, new offences such as organised crime and terrorism, and community service as a punishment.

Q 31 | cur | Static GK & Culture
The term 'svaravalis' refers to what in the context of Indian classical music?
(1) A specific type of raga
(2) A melody composed using the seven notes of the scale
(3) Ornamentation and embellishment of a raga
(4) A rhythmic cycle used in carnatic music
= 2
~ Svaravali (svara + avali, 'a row of notes'), also called sarali varisai, is the first set of exercises taught in Carnatic music. Organised by Purandara Dasa and sung in raga Mayamalavagowla, these exercises are melodic patterns of the seven svaras of the scale (sa ri ga ma pa dha ni), sung up and down in three speeds to train the voice and pitch. They are not a separate raga; ornamentation of a raga is called gamaka, and a rhythmic cycle is a tala.

Q 32 | geo | Indian Geography
Consider the following physiographic features.
1. Tors
2. Spurs
3. Block Mountains
Which of the feature/features given above is/are found in the peninsular plateau of India?
(1) 1 and 3 only
(2) 3 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2, and 3
= 4
~ The Peninsular plateau is an old, stable tableland of crystalline, igneous and metamorphic rocks that has gone through repeated phases of uplift and submergence with crustal faulting and fracturing. NCERT (India: Physical Environment) lists its typical relief features as tors, block mountains, rift valleys, spurs, bare rocky structures, series of hummocky hills and wall-like quartzite dykes that offer natural sites for water storage. Q 33 | ih | Art & Culture
Which of the following building materials were primarily used in constructing Purana Qila?
(1) Granite and lime plaster
(2) Concrete and red bricks
(3) Sandstone and marble
(4) Wood and terracotta
= 3
~ Purana Qila in Delhi stands on the site of Humayun's city Dinpanah (begun 1533) and was largely completed by Sher Shah Suri in the 1540s. Q 34 | cur | Static GK & Culture
During the Solung festival celebrated by the Adi tribe of Arunachal Pradesh, which traditional drink is shared to promote social bonding?
(1) Soma
(2) Apong
(3) Thukpa
(4) Chhang
= 2

Q 35 | ih | Ancient India
The drainage system at Mohenjodaro was notable because each house was connected to:
(1) An underground water tank
(2) A central pillar for support
(3) A covered street drain
(4) A rooftop reservoir
= 3
~ Harappan cities had carefully planned drainage. As NCERT explains, streets were laid out on a grid with their drains planned first, and every house had one wall along a street so that domestic waste water could flow out into the street drains. Bathrooms paved with bricks sloped towards these drains. The street drains were built of brick and covered with bricks or stone slabs, with sumps and removable covers at intervals for cleaning. Ernest Mackay called it the most complete ancient drainage system yet discovered.

Q 36 | cur | Sports
Which of the following statements is correct regarding the Golden Raid and card system in Kabaddi?
(1) A team is allowed two Golden Raids before the result is decided.
(2) A Green Card leads to a 2-minute suspension from the game.
(3) A fresh toss is taken if the match is tied even after 5–5 raids.
(4) A Red Card results in a warning with no match suspension.
= 3
~ A knockout kabaddi match that ends level is decided first by five raids for each team, made by five different raiders. If the score is still tied after these 5–5 raids, a fresh toss is taken; the toss winner gets the Golden Raid, and only if it fails to score does the other team get one, so no team gets two. Under the card system, a green card is a warning, a yellow card means a 2-minute suspension, and a red card means suspension for the rest of the match. So only statement 3 is correct.

Q 37 | ih | Medieval India
What was a major challenge for agriculture in the Chola kingdom apart from rocky land?
(1) Lack of seeds
(2) Lack of labour
(3) Irrigation water
(4) Pest infestation
= 3
~ The Chola heartland was the Kaveri delta, but much of the surrounding region was dry and rocky, so farming depended on artificial watering. NCERT (Our Pasts II) explains that forests had to be cleared and land levelled, embankments built in the delta to prevent floods and canals dug to carry water to the fields. As crops often had to be watered artificially, wells were dug and huge tanks were built to collect rainwater, and village assemblies (sabhas) had committees to look after irrigation works. So the supply of irrigation water was the main challenge.

Q 39 | cur | International
The “Kaladan Multimodal Transit Transport Project” links India with which country?
(1) China
(2) Myanmar
(3) Bangladesh
(4) Nepal
= 2
~ The Kaladan Multi-Modal Transit Transport Project, based on a 2008 India–Myanmar framework agreement, gives Mizoram and the North-East a route to the sea that avoids the narrow Siliguri corridor. Cargo goes by sea from Kolkata to Sittwe port in Myanmar's Rakhine State, by inland waterway up the Kaladan river to Paletwa, and then by road to Zorinpui on the Mizoram border. Sittwe port, built with Indian funding, was operationalised in May 2023. The project is a key part of India's Act East Policy.

Q 40 | cur | Awards, Books & Persons
'The Namesake' is a famous novel by which Indian-American author?
(1) Jhumpa Lahiri

Q 45 | sci | Physics
Read the following statements about the photoelectric effect:

= 2
