Q 1 | qa | Algebra
Find the value of Y, if X − 2Y + 2Z = 16, X − Y + Z = 9 and 2X − 3Y − Z = 9.
(1) 2
(2) −3
(3) −4
(4) 4
= 2
~ Subtract the second equation from the first: (X − 2Y + 2Z) − (X − Y + Z) = 16 − 9, so −Y + Z = 7, i.e. Z = Y + 7. Put this in the second equation: X − Y + (Y + 7) = 9, so X = 2. Now the third equation: 2(2) − 3Y − (Y + 7) = 9 gives −4Y = 12, so Y = −3 (and Z = 4). Check in the first: 2 + 6 + 8 = 16.

Q 2 | qa | Algebra
(a⁷ × b⁸ × c⁷)/(a⁹ × b⁵ × c⁴) in simplified form is:
(1) (a⁰) × (b²) × (c¹)
(2) (a⁻⁷) × (b²) × (c⁻⁴)
(3) (a⁻²) × (b³) × (c³)
(4) (a⁻⁵) × (b⁻⁸) × (c⁰)
= 3
~ When powers of the same base are divided, subtract the exponents: aᵐ/aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ. So a⁷/a⁹ = a⁷⁻⁹ = a⁻², b⁸/b⁵ = b⁸⁻⁵ = b³ and c⁷/c⁴ = c⁷⁻⁴ = c³. The expression is therefore (a⁻²) × (b³) × (c³), which can also be written b³c³/a². Only option 3 has all three exponents right.

Q 3 | qa | Percentage, Profit & Interest
A man buys 10 identical articles for a total of ₹15. If he sells each of them for ₹1.7, then his profit percentage will be ______% (rounded off to two decimal place).
(1) 14.33
(2) 12.76
(3) 13.33
(4) 11.76
= 3
~ Cost price of one article = ₹15/10 = ₹1.50 and selling price = ₹1.70, so the profit on each article = ₹0.20. Profit % = (profit/CP) × 100 = (0.20/1.50) × 100 = 40/3 = 13.33%. In totals: SP of 10 articles = ₹17, profit = ₹2 on a cost of ₹15, again 13.33%. Option 11.76 is the slip of dividing the profit by the selling price (2/17) instead of the cost price.

Q 4 | qa | Time, Work, Speed & Distance
Rani and Adya, working separately, can finish a task in 12 days and 16 hours, respectively. They work in stretches of one day alternately. If Rani starts working first, then the task will be completed in
(1) 12 1/3 days
(2) 13 2/3 days
(3) 13 1/3 days
(4) 12 2/3 days
= 2
~ The keyed answer works only if Adya's time is 16 days; with 16 hours as printed, the task would end on day 2 (after 1 11/18 days), which is not an option. Taking 16 days: Rani does 1/12 and Adya 1/16 of the task per day, so each two-day pair does 1/12 + 1/16 = 7/48. After 6 pairs (12 days), 42/48 is done. Day 13 (Rani) adds 4/48, leaving 2/48; Adya does 3/48 a day, so she needs 2/3 of day 14. Total = 13 2/3 days. (SSC key; some sources differ.)

Q 5 | qa | Trigonometry & Heights
Let ABC be a right-angled triangle with a right angle at B. If tan A = √3, then find the value of sin A cos C + cos A sin C and cos A cos C − sin A sin C.
(1) √3/2, 1/2
(2) √3/2, √3/2
(3) 1/2, 1/2
(4) 1, 0
= 4
~ tan A = √3 gives A = 60°, and since B = 90°, C = 180° − 90° − 60° = 30°. The first expression is sin(A + C) = sin 90° = 1 and the second is cos(A + C) = cos 90° = 0. Direct check: sin A cos C + cos A sin C = (√3/2)(√3/2) + (1/2)(1/2) = 3/4 + 1/4 = 1, and cos A cos C − sin A sin C = (1/2)(√3/2) − (√3/2)(1/2) = 0. So the values are 1, 0.

Q 6 | qa | Percentage, Profit & Interest
Find the simple interest (in ₹) on ₹2000 at 6.5% per annum rate of interest for the period from 14 February 2023 to 28 April 2023
(1) 24
(2) 27
(3) 26
(4) 25
= 3
~ Count the days, leaving out the starting day and counting the last day: February 14 days (15th to 28th; 2023 is not a leap year), March 31 days, April 28 days, total 73 days. Time = 73/365 = 1/5 year. Simple interest = P × R × T/100 = 2000 × 6.5 × (1/5)/100 = ₹26. The usual SSC rule is to count either the first day or the last day, not both.

Q 7 | qa | Data Interpretation & Statistics
Let A and B be two players who are playing the game to hit the target. The probabilities of hitting the target by A and B is 2/3 and 3/4, respectively. What is the probability that exactly one of them hit the target?
(1) 7/12
(2) 1/4
(3) 1/12
(4) 5/12
= 4
~ Exactly one hits in two ways, which cannot happen together: A hits and B misses, or A misses and B hits. P = (2/3)(1 − 3/4) + (1 − 2/3)(3/4) = (2/3)(1/4) + (1/3)(3/4) = 2/12 + 3/12 = 5/12. For comparison, both hit = 6/12 = 1/2 and both miss = 1/12 (option 3); together with 5/12 these add up to 1.

Q 8 | qa | Trigonometry & Heights
If tan(x + y)tan(x − y) = 1, then find the value of tanx.
(1) √3
(2) 1/√3
(3) 1
(4) 1/(2√3)
= 3
~ tan(x + y)tan(x − y) = 1 means tan(x + y) = 1/tan(x − y) = cot(x − y) = tan(90° − (x − y)). For acute angles, x + y = 90° − x + y, so 2x = 90° and x = 45°. Hence tan x = tan 45° = 1, whatever the value of y. (This uses the rule that if tan P × tan Q = 1, then P + Q = 90°.)

Q 9 | qa | Percentage, Profit & Interest
The population of a place increased to 50,000 from 2016 to 2018 at the rate of 6% per annum, and continued the same trend for the next 3 years. If A is the population in 2016 and B is the population in 2020, both are approximated to the next possible integers, then the value of B − A is:
(1) 11680
(2) 16270
(3) 13220
(4) 12850
= 1
~ From 2016 to 2018 is two years of 6% compound growth: A × (1.06)² = 50,000, so A = 50,000/1.1236 = 44,499.8…, which rounds up to 44,500. The same growth continues for two more years to 2020: B = 50,000 × (1.06)² = 50,000 × 1.1236 = 56,180. B − A = 56,180 − 44,500 = 11,680. (The third year of the trend, 2021, is not needed.)

Q 10 | qa | Percentage, Profit & Interest
By how much is 60% of 75 greater than 1/5 of 25?
(1) 37
(2) 38
(3) 40
(4) 34
= 3
~ 60% of 75 = (60/100) × 75 = 0.6 × 75 = 45. One-fifth of 25 = 25/5 = 5. The difference = 45 − 5 = 40. So 60% of 75 exceeds 1/5 of 25 by 40. A quick check: 60% of 75 is the same as 75% of 60, which is 45.

Q 11 | qa | Data Interpretation & Statistics
Find the median of the following data. (Rounded off to 2 decimal places.)
Class interval | 0-5 | 5-10 | 10-15 | 15-20 | 20-25 | 25-30 | 30-35
Frequency | 3 | 5 | 4 | 2 | 7 | 6 | 5
(1) 21.43
(2) 23.40
(3) 20.53
(4) 18.64
= 1
~ Total frequency N = 3 + 5 + 4 + 2 + 7 + 6 + 5 = 32, so N/2 = 16. Cumulative frequencies: 3, 8, 12, 14, 21, 27, 32; the 16th value lies in class 20-25. Median = L + [(N/2 − cf)/f] × h, with L = 20, cf = 14 (before the class), f = 7 and h = 5. Median = 20 + (16 − 14)/7 × 5 = 20 + 10/7 = 21.43.

Q 12 | qa | Data Interpretation & Statistics
Using empirical formula, calculate the mode for the following data.
17, 20, 21, 18, 25, 28, 24, 22, 16, 24, 25, 24
(1) 25.8
(2) 32
(3) 25
(4) 30.6
= 3
~ The empirical relation is Mode = 3 Median − 2 Mean. The 12 values add up to 264, so mean = 22. In order: 16, 17, 18, 20, 21, 22, 24, 24, 24, 25, 25, 28; the median is the average of the 6th and 7th values = (22 + 24)/2 = 23. Mode = 3 × 23 − 2 × 22 = 69 − 44 = 25. (The most frequent value is actually 24, but the question asks for the empirical formula.)

Q 13 | qa | Percentage, Profit & Interest

Q 14 | qa | Geometry
ΔABC is inscribed in a circle with Centre O. If AB = 21 cm, BC = 20 cm and AC = 29 cm, then what is the length of the circumradius of the triangle?
(1) 32.5 cm
(2) 13.5 cm
(3) 21.5 cm
(4) 14.5 cm
= 4
~ AB² + BC² = 21² + 20² = 441 + 400 = 841 = 29² = AC², so the triangle is right-angled at B (a 20-21-29 Pythagorean triple). The hypotenuse of a right triangle is a diameter of its circumcircle (angle in a semicircle), so the circumradius = AC/2 = 29/2 = 14.5 cm. Q 15 | qa | Number System & Simplification
The value of 4³ − 0² + (22/2)² − 8 + 7 × 6 = ______
(1) 224
(2) 229
(3) 219
(4) 218
= 3
~ Follow BODMAS: first the bracket, 22/2 = 11; then the powers, 4³ = 64, 0² = 0 and 11² = 121; then the multiplication, 7 × 6 = 42. Finally add and subtract from left to right: 64 − 0 + 121 − 8 + 42 = 219. Taking 0² as 1 by mistake gives 218, one of the nearby wrong options.

Q 16 | qa | Ratio, Average & Mixtures
A sum of money is to be distributed among 3 friends P, Q and R in the proportion of 5 : 3 : 4. If P gets ₹1,500 more than R, what is Q's share?
(1) ₹4,950
(2) ₹4,680
(3) ₹5,100
(4) ₹4,500
= 4
~ Let the shares be P = 5k, Q = 3k and R = 4k. P gets more than R by 5k − 4k = k, so k = ₹1,500. Q's share = 3k = 3 × 1,500 = ₹4,500. The whole sum is 12k = ₹18,000, with P = ₹7,500 and R = ₹6,000 (difference ₹1,500, as given).

Q 17 | qa | Ratio, Average & Mixtures
The average of first 91 even numbers is
(1) 92
(2) 90
(3) 91
(4) 93
= 1
~ The first 91 even numbers are 2, 4, 6, …, 182 (the last is 2 × 91). They form an arithmetic progression, so the average = (first + last)/2 = (2 + 182)/2 = 92. In general, the sum of the first n even numbers is n(n + 1), so their average is n + 1 = 91 + 1 = 92. Option 91 would be the average of the first 91 odd numbers (1 to 181), not of the even numbers.

Q 18 | qa | Ratio, Average & Mixtures
If the average age of three persons is 56 years and their ages are in the ratio 2 : 5 : 7, then find the age of the youngest person.
(1) 20 years
(2) 22 years
(3) 24 years
(4) 26 years
= 3
~ Total of the three ages = 3 × 56 = 168 years. The ratio 2 : 5 : 7 has 2 + 5 + 7 = 14 parts, so one part = 168/14 = 12 years. The youngest person has the smallest share, 2 parts = 2 × 12 = 24 years (the others are 60 and 84 years). Check: (24 + 60 + 84)/3 = 168/3 = 56.

Q 19 | qa | Trigonometry & Heights
If (secθ − tanθ)/(secθ + tanθ) = 1/9, θ lies in the first quadrant, then the value of (sinθ − tan²θ)/(sinθ + tan²θ) is:
(1) −13/27
(2) −11/29
(3) 13/27
(4) 11/29
= 2
~ Since sec²θ − tan²θ = 1, (secθ − tanθ)(secθ + tanθ) = 1. Let secθ + tanθ = s; then secθ − tanθ = 1/s, and the ratio is 1/s² = 1/9, so s = 3. Then secθ = (3 + 1/3)/2 = 5/3 and tanθ = (3 − 1/3)/2 = 4/3, so sinθ = tanθ/secθ = 4/5 and tan²θ = 16/9. Value = (4/5 − 16/9)/(4/5 + 16/9) = (36 − 80)/(36 + 80) = −44/116 = −11/29.

Q 20 | qa | Geometry
If the areas of two similar triangles are in the ratio 121 : 225, what would be the ratio of the corresponding sides?
(1) 9 : 11
(2) 11 : 15
(3) 15 : 13
(4) 11 : 13
= 2
~ For similar triangles, the ratio of their areas equals the square of the ratio of their corresponding sides. So the ratio of the sides = √121 : √225 = 11 : 15. Check: 11² : 15² = 121 : 225. Note that the order is kept: the first triangle has the smaller area, so it also has the shorter sides; 15 : 13 and 11 : 13 do not square to the given ratio.

Q 22 | qa | Ratio, Average & Mixtures
Anil and Beena are friends, and the difference between their ages is 5 years. Anil’s father Dinesh is three times as old as Anil, and Beena is twice as old as her sister Charu. The ages of Dinesh and Charu differ by 45 years. If Beena is older than Anil, then find the sum of the present ages of both Dinesh and Charu.
(1) 78 years
(2) 69 years
(3) 91 years
(4) 100 years
= 2
~ Let Anil be A years old. Beena is older, so Beena = A + 5 and Charu = (A + 5)/2; Dinesh = 3A. Dinesh is the older of Dinesh and Charu, so 3A − (A + 5)/2 = 45, i.e. 6A − A − 5 = 90 and A = 19. Q 23 | qa | Time, Work, Speed & Distance
On a 2200 m long circular track, Sarita and Kavita drove their cycles from the same point but in opposite direction with the speeds 20 km/hr and 16 km/hr, respectively. After how much time will they meet again for the first time?
(1) 2 minutes 20 seconds
(2) 3 minutes 20 seconds
(3) 2 minutes 50 seconds
(4) 3 minutes 40 seconds
= 4
~ Moving in opposite directions, they close the gap at the sum of their speeds: 20 + 16 = 36 km/hr = 36 × 5/18 = 10 m/s. They meet for the first time when together they have covered one full round of 2200 m. Time = 2200/10 = 220 seconds = 3 minutes 40 seconds. (Riding in the same direction would use the difference, 4 km/hr, and take much longer.)

Q 29 | qa | Geometry
In a triangle HJK, HJ = HK. G is a point on HJ such that HG = GK = JK. What is the degree measure of two-third of (∠HGK + ∠GKJ)?
(1) 96°
(2) 136°
(3) 84°
(4) 90°
= 1

Q 32 | rea | Miscellaneous Reasoning

Q 34 | rea | Analogy & Classification
Which of the following letter-clusters should replace # and % so that the pattern and relationship followed between the letter-cluster pair on the left side of :: is the same as that on the right side of ::?

(1) # = BBJ % = ORW
(2) # = BEJ % = QRW
(3) # = BEJ % = ORP
(4) # = BEJ % = ORW
= 4

~ In 1923 Johannes Brønsted of Denmark and Thomas Lowry of England independently defined an acid as a proton (H⁺) donor and a base as a proton acceptor. When an acid gives up a proton, what remains is its conjugate base, and a base that takes a proton becomes its conjugate acid; in HCl + H₂O → H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻, Cl⁻ is the conjugate base of HCl. Arrhenius (1880s) defined acids and bases by H⁺ and OH⁻ ions in water, and Lewis (also 1923) by accepting or donating an electron pair.

(2) Repeater
(3) Switch
(4) Modem
= 3

Q 136 | comp | Internet, Networking & Security

(2) Hashing
(3) Secure Socket Layer (SSL)
(4) Compression
= 3

Q 143 | comp | Computer Basics & Hardware
Select which of the given statement(s) is/are True or False for the Central Processing Unit (CPU) of the Computer Systems.

(2) (i) False, (ii) True
(3) (i) True, (ii) False
(4) (i) False, (ii) False
= 1

Q 146 | comp | Computer Basics & Hardware
What is the primary reason DRAM needs to be refreshed periodically?
(1) To clear unused data
(2) To allow the CPU to access new data
(3) To prevent data corruption caused by magnetic fields
(4) To recharge the capacitors storing data bits
= 4
~ Dynamic RAM stores each bit as an electric charge in a tiny capacitor paired with a transistor. The charge leaks away within milliseconds, so a stored 1 would fade into a 0; the memory controller therefore reads and rewrites every row at regular intervals (typically within 64 ms) to recharge the capacitors, which is why the memory is called dynamic. Static RAM, used for cache, keeps bits in flip-flops and needs no refresh while power is on. Magnetic fields concern hard disks, not DRAM.
