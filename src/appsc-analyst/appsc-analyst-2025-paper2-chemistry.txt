Q 1 | chm | Inorganic Chemistry
A statement is given, followed by two conclusions numbered I and II. You have to consider the statement to be true, even if it seems to be at variance from commonly known facts. You are to decide which of the given conclusions can definitely be drawn from the given statement.
Statement:
Group 1 elements have lower ionization energies than Group 2 elements.
Conclusions:
I: Group 1 elements form +1 cations by losing one electron.
II: Group 1 elements are more reactive than Group 2 elements.
(1) Only conclusion I is correct
(2) Only conclusion II is correct
(3) Both conclusion I and conclusion II are correct
(4) Neither conclusion I nor conclusion II is correct
= 3
~ A low ionization energy means an atom gives up an electron easily. Group 1 atoms have a single outer s electron, so they readily lose it to form M+ ions (I), and the same ease of electron loss makes them more reactive than Group 2 metals (II). Both conclusions are therefore accepted.

Q 2 | chm | Inorganic Chemistry
When aluminium carbide (Al4C3) reacts with water, the products formed are:
(1) carbon dioxide and aluminium hydroxide
(2) methane and aluminium oxide
(3) ethane and aluminium hydroxide
(4) methane and aluminium hydroxide
= 4
~ Aluminium carbide is a methanide containing C4- ions, so water protonates each carbon to give methane: Al4C3 + 12H2O → 4Al(OH)3 + 3CH4. The hydroxide (not the oxide) forms because the reaction takes place in excess water; ethane is not formed (calcium carbide, CaC2, is the one that gives ethyne).

Q 3 | chm | Inorganic Chemistry
Which of the following statements is FALSE about non-transition elements?
(1) They have a full outermost electron shell.
(2) Non-transition elements typically show multiple oxidation states.
(3) Their compounds are usually colourless.
(4) They include elements from Groups 1, 2, and 13-18.
= 2
~ In the keyed reading, non-transition (main-group) elements mostly show one characteristic oxidation state (+1 for Group 1, +2 for Group 2), and variable oxidation states are the hallmark of transition elements. Their compounds are usually colourless because they lack d-d transitions, and the group range in option 4 is correct. Note that "full outermost shell" in option 1 is true only of the noble gases, and p-block elements do show variable oxidation states, so this item is open to argument. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 4 | chm | Inorganic Chemistry
Borazine is often referred to as 'inorganic benzene'. Which of the following structural similarities does borazine share with benzene?
(1) Borazine is a non-polar molecule.
(2) Borazine has a six-membered ring structure with alternating bonds.
(3) Borazine has identical bond lengths.
(4) Borazine undergoes electrophilic substitution reactions similar to benzene.
= 2
~ Borazine, B3N3H6, is isoelectronic with benzene: a planar six-membered ring in which B and N alternate and the ring has delocalised pi bonding. Because the B-N bonds are polar (N is delta-minus, B is delta-plus), borazine is far more reactive than benzene and typically undergoes addition (for example with HCl or H2O) rather than benzene-type substitution, so option 4 is wrong. Electron-diffraction work shows equal B-N lengths and a zero dipole moment, so options 1 and 3 can also be argued. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 5 | chm | Inorganic Chemistry
In the given question two statements are provided: Assertion (A) and Reason (R). Read both statements carefully, determine their relationship and choose the most appropriate option.
Assertion (A): Silicon dioxide (SiO2) is considered an acidic oxide.
Reason (R): Silicon dioxide reacts readily with water to form silicic acid.
(1) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(2) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(3) A is true, but R is false.
(4) A is false, but R is true.
= 3
~ SiO2 is an acidic oxide because it reacts with bases (for example NaOH or Na2CO3 on fusion) to give silicates, so A is true. R is false: SiO2 is a giant covalent network that does not react with or dissolve in water, and silicic acid is prepared by acidifying a soluble silicate instead.

Q 6 | chm | Inorganic Chemistry
Select the correct option based on the Assertion (A) and Reason (R) given below.
Assertion (A): P₂O₃ has a structure that includes P–O single bonds and P=P double bonds.
Reason (R): The bonding in P₂O₃ allows phosphorus to attain the octet configuration.
(1) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(2) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(3) A is true, but R is false.

Q 8 | chm | Inorganic Chemistry
In the hydroboration reaction of diborane with alkene, the reaction proceeds as follows.
(i) ½ B2H6 + 3 RCH=CHR → B(CH2-CH2R)3
(ii) B(CH2-CH2R)3 + H2O2 → X
Identify the product ‘X’ formed in this reaction.
(1) RCH2CH2OH
(2) RCH2COOH
(3) RCH2COCH2R
(4) RCH2CH3
= 1
~ In hydroboration-oxidation the trialkylborane is treated with alkaline H2O2, which replaces each B-C bond by a C-OH bond with retention of configuration, so the alkyl group becomes an alcohol. With the anti-Markovnikov boron placement shown in step (i), the product is the primary alcohol RCH2CH2OH. A carboxylic acid or ketone would need a stronger oxidant, and an alkane (RCH2CH3) forms when the borane is protonolysed with a carboxylic acid, not with H2O2.

Q 9 | chm | Inorganic Chemistry
Which of the following statements about the structure of closo-carboranes is correct?
(1) Closo-carboranes consist of a cage-like structure where all vertices are occupied only by boron atoms.
(2) Closo-carboranes have a polyhedral structure where carbon and boron atoms occupy vertices, and the structure follows the Wade–Mingos rules.
(3) Closo-carboranes have a linear arrangement of boron and carbon atoms bonded alternately.
(4) Closo-carboranes form a 2D honeycomb-like planar structure similar to graphene.
= 2
~ Closo-carboranes such as C2B10H12 are closed polyhedral cages (deltahedra) in which B-H and C-H units occupy the vertices; an n-vertex closo cage has n+1 skeletal electron pairs, as the Wade-Mingos rules predict. A cage with only boron vertices is a closo-borane anion (BnHn2-), not a carborane, and carboranes are neither linear nor planar.

Q 10 | chm | Inorganic Chemistry
Which of the following oxides would most likely exhibit acidic behaviour in aqueous solutions?
(1) Na2O
(2) P4O10
(3) CaO
(4) Al2O3
= 2
~ P4O10 is the oxide of a non-metal; it reacts vigorously with water to give phosphoric acid, P4O10 + 6H2O → 4H3PO4, so its solution is acidic. Na2O and CaO give strongly basic hydroxides, and Al2O3 is amphoteric and insoluble in water.

Q 12 | chm | Inorganic Chemistry
Which method is commonly used to synthesize ortho-carborane starting from boron hydride?
(1) High-temperature reaction with acetylene
(2) Hydrogenation reaction
(3) Acid-catalyzed cyclisation
(4) Oxidation of boron compounds
= 1
~ Ortho-carborane (1,2-C2B10H12) is made from decaborane(14), B10H14, by first forming a Lewis-base adduct such as B10H12(SEt2)2 and then heating it with acetylene (ethyne), which inserts the two carbon atoms into the boron cage and releases H2. Hydrogenation, acid-catalysed cyclisation and oxidation are not routes to the carborane cage.

Q 14 | chm | Inorganic Chemistry
Read the given statement and conclusions and select the most appropriate option.
Statement:
Carboranes are polyhedral compounds consisting of boron, carbon, and hydrogen atoms, often used as precursors for designing boron-rich materials due to their unique stability and electronic properties.
Conclusions:
I. The stability of carboranes is a result of delocalised bonding involving multicenter bonding interactions.
II. Carboranes can be functionalised at carbon and boron sites to create derivatives with tailored electronic and chemical properties.
(1) Only conclusion I is true
(2) Only conclusion II is true
(3) Both I and II are true
(4) Neither I nor II is true
= 3
~ The cage skeleton of a carborane is held together by delocalised, three-dimensional multicentre bonding, which accounts for its very high thermal and chemical stability (I). The C-H vertices can be metallated and substituted, and the B-H vertices can be halogenated or otherwise substituted, giving tailored derivatives (II). Both conclusions are true.

Q 15 | chm | Inorganic Chemistry
Silicates have various structures due to the ways SiO₄⁴⁻ tetrahedra link together. Which of the following statements is correct regarding different types of silicate structures?
(1) In orthosilicates, SiO₄⁴⁻ tetrahedra share all four oxygen atoms with neighbouring tetrahedra, forming a 3D network.
(2) Pyrosilicates contain discrete Si₂O₇⁶⁻ units, SiO₄⁴⁻ tetrahedra are linked by sharing a single oxygen atom.
(3) Sheet silicates consist of single chains of SiO₄⁴⁻ tetrahedra that are not connected to any other chains, resulting in a one-dimensional structure.
(4) In ring silicates, all oxygen atoms in each SiO₄⁴⁻ tetrahedra are shared with adjacent tetrahedra, forming continuous 2D sheets.
= 2
~ Pyrosilicates (disilicates) contain Si2O7 6- units made of two SiO4 tetrahedra sharing one corner oxygen. In orthosilicates the tetrahedra are isolated and share no oxygen, sheet silicates share three oxygens per tetrahedron to give 2D layers (single chains are chain silicates), and ring silicates share two oxygens per tetrahedron to form closed rings rather than sheets.

Q 16 | chm | Inorganic Chemistry
Which of the following statements about polymorphic forms of phosphorus is correct?
(1) Black phosphorus has a layered structure and is the most thermodynamically stable allotrope.
(2) Red phosphorus is highly reactive in air and spontaneously ignites at room temperature.
(3) White phosphorus is stable in air but ignites only when heated to around 400°C in the absence of oxygen.
(4) White phosphorus consists of discrete P₄ tetrahedra, whereas red phosphorus is a polymeric form.
= 1,4
~ Both (1) and (4) are accurate: black phosphorus is the most stable allotrope and has a puckered layered structure, and white phosphorus is molecular P4 tetrahedra while red phosphorus is a polymeric network. APPSC noted an ambiguity and gave full marks for either option. Options 2 and 3 are wrong because red phosphorus is fairly stable in air, whereas white phosphorus is reactive and ignites spontaneously in air at about 30 °C (it is stored under water).

Q 18 | chm | Inorganic Chemistry
Which type of silicate structure does mica exhibit, contributing to its characteristic cleavage?
(1) Three-dimensional framework
(2) Double-chain silicate
(3) Sheet silicate
(4) Single-chain silicate
= 3
~ Mica is a sheet (phyllosilicate) mineral: each SiO4 tetrahedron shares three of its oxygens, forming two-dimensional layers that are held together only by weak ionic bonds through K+ ions. The layers therefore split apart easily, giving mica its perfect basal cleavage. Framework silicates (quartz, feldspar) and chain silicates (pyroxenes, amphiboles) do not cleave into thin sheets.

Q 19 | chm | Inorganic Chemistry
In the context of carbon polymorphism, which statement best explains why diamond has a much higher melting point than graphite?
(1) Diamond’s carbon-carbon bonds are much weaker than those in graphite.
(2) Diamond's 3D structure requires more energy to break the bonds, while graphite's 2D structure allows easier bond separation.
(3) The absence of covalent bonds in diamond makes it less stable than graphite.
(4) Graphite contains more delocalized electrons than diamond, making it more stable.
= 2
~ In diamond each carbon is sp3-bonded to four others in a rigid three-dimensional covalent network, so a great deal of energy is needed to break it down. Graphite has layers of sp2 carbon held together by only weak van der Waals forces, so the layers separate easily; this is the textbook reasoning behind option 2. Options 1 and 3 are wrong because diamond's bonds are strong and covalent. The premise itself is shaky, since graphite actually sublimes only at about 3600 °C, so option 2 is simply the one textbook-style answer.

Silicates are compounds containing silicon, oxygen, and one or more metals, where the silicon atom is tetrahedrally coordinated by oxygen atoms.
Conclusions:
I. Silicates can form extended polymeric structures through the sharing of oxygen atoms between silicon-oxygen tetrahedra.

Q 21 | chm | Organic & Natural Products
Which of the following methods is commonly used as the initial step for the isolation of terpenes from plant materials?
(1) Steam distillation
(2) Filtration
(3) Simple distillation
(4) Sublimation
= 1
~ Most mono- and sesquiterpenes are volatile, water-insoluble and heat-sensitive, so the plant material (peel, leaves, flowers) is first steam distilled to carry the essential oil over at a temperature below 100 °C. The oil is then separated into its components by fractional distillation or chromatography. Simple distillation would need a much higher temperature that could decompose terpenes, and filtration or sublimation cannot extract them from plant tissue.

Q 22 | chm | Organic & Natural Products
How are terpenoids classified?
(1) Based on the number of carbon atoms
(2) Based on the type of functional groups attached to the carbon chain
(3) Based on the number of isoprene units in the molecule
(4) Based on their colour and odour
= 3
~ Terpenoids are built from C5 isoprene units and are classified by how many of them they contain: monoterpenoids (2 units, C10), sesquiterpenoids (3, C15), diterpenoids (4, C20), triterpenoids (6, C30) and tetraterpenoids (8, C40). The carbon count follows from the unit count, but the isoprene-unit basis is the standard one; functional groups, colour and odour are not used.

Q 25 | chm | Organic & Natural Products
Which of the following is true about the linkage of isoprene units in terpenoids?
(1) The isoprene units are linked head-to-tail.
(2) The isoprene units are linked tail-to-tail.
(3) Isoprene units are not linked in terpenoids.
(4) Isoprene units are linked through hydrogen bonds.
= 1
~ The isoprene rule (Wallach, refined by Ruzicka as the biogenetic isoprene rule) states that terpenoid skeletons are formed by joining isoprene units head-to-tail, that is, C1 of one unit to C4 of the next. Tail-to-tail linkage occurs only in a few exceptions such as squalene and the carotenoids, and the units are joined by covalent C-C bonds, not hydrogen bonds.

Q 30 | chm | Organic & Natural Products
Read the given statements of Assertion (A) and Reason (R) carefully and select the correct option.
(A): During fractional distillation to isolate terpenoids from essential oils, monoterpenoid hydrocarbons typically distill first.
(R): Monoterpenoid hydrocarbons have lower boiling points, allowing them to separate earlier during distillation.

Q 31 | chm | Organic & Natural Products
During the structure elucidation of Zingiberene, which of the following observations does NOT support that Zingiberene is a sesquiterpene hydrocarbon with a monocyclic structure?
(1) Catalytic hydrogenation converts Zingiberene into hexahydrozingiberene.
(2) The molecular refractivity index of Zingiberene corresponds to a monocyclic structure.
(3) Ozonolysis of Zingiberene yields acetone, levulinic acid and succinic acid.
(4) Reaction with ozone forms a tri-ketone, suggesting a moncyclic structure.
= 4
~ Zingiberene is C15H24, which has four degrees of unsaturation. Uptake of 3 mol H2 to give hexahydrozingiberene (C15H30) shows three C=C bonds, so the fourth unsaturation is a single ring, and molecular refractivity agrees. Ozonolysis of the three C=C bonds breaks the skeleton into smaller fragments such as acetone, levulinic acid and succinic acid, so one intact tri-ketone is not expected and option 4 is the observation that does not support the structure.

Q 32 | chm | Organic & Natural Products
Which reaction is commonly utilised to modify the carboxylic acid group in abietic acid to form its methyl ester derivative?
(1) Friedel-Crafts acylation
(2) Claisen rearrangement
(3) Fischer esterification

Q 34 | chm | Amino Acids & Proteins
Which of the following methods can be used for the synthesis of leucine?
(1) Nitration of isoleucine
(2) Friedel-Crafts acylation
(3) Decarboxylation of valine
(4) Strecker synthesis
= 4
~ Leucine is obtained by the Strecker synthesis: isovaleraldehyde (3-methylbutanal) is treated with NH3 and HCN to give an alpha-aminonitrile, which is hydrolysed to the alpha-amino acid. Nitration and Friedel-Crafts acylation do not build the leucine side chain, and decarboxylating valine would remove the carboxyl group and give an amine, not an amino acid.

Q 35 | chm | Amino Acids & Proteins
At physiological pH (around 7.4), the amino acid glycine predominantly exists in which form due to its amphoteric nature?
(1) Fully protonated form (both amino and carboxyl groups protonated)
(2) Fully deprotonated form (both amino and carboxyl groups deprotonated)
(3) Zwitterionic form (amino group protonated and carboxyl group deprotonated)
(4) Only as a carboxylate anion (carboxyl group deprotonated, amino group neutral)
= 3
~ Glycine has pKa values of about 2.3 (COOH) and 9.6 (NH3+), so at pH 7.4 the carboxyl group has lost its proton and the amino group still holds one. It therefore exists mainly as the zwitterion +H3N-CH2-COO-, with no net charge (its isoelectric point is about 6.0).

Q 36 | chm | Amino Acids & Proteins
Which of the following statements about amino acids is correct?
(1) All amino acids have their amino group attached to the alpha carbon.
(2) Amino acids are amphoteric molecules due to the presence of both acidic and basic groups.
(3) Only non-polar amino acids are used in protein synthesis.
(4) Amino acids cannot exist as zwitterions in solution.

~ Amino acids carry a basic -NH2 group and an acidic -COOH group, so they can react with both acids and bases, which makes them amphoteric. Q 121 | chm | Photochemistry & Spectroscopy

(2) number of molecules reacted per photon absorbed

Q 122 | chm | Photochemistry & Spectroscopy
What is the typical quantum yield for a phosphorescence process?
(1) 0
(2) 0.1 – 0.3
(3) 1
(4) Greater than 1
= 2
~ Phosphorescence is a spin-forbidden triplet to singlet emission with a long-lived excited state that is easily quenched, so its quantum yield is low and far below 1; values of the order of 0.1-0.3 are the figure quoted as typical. Q 124 | chm | Photochemistry & Spectroscopy

Q 141 | chm | Organic & Natural Products
Which of the following statements correctly describes the aromatic character of heterocyclic compounds?
(1) A compound is aromatic if it follows Huckel's rule, which requires 4n+2 π-electrons, where n is a whole number.

= 1
