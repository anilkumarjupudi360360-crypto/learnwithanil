Q 1 | evs | Natural Resources
The optical efficiency of a solar thermal collector is defined as:
(1) fraction of the rays reflected from the mirror to solar radiation absorbed in the absorber
(2) ratio of solar radiation absorbed in the absorber to intercepted by the collector aperture
(3) ratio of solar radiation transmitted from the absorber to intercepted by the collector aperture
(4) transmittance of glass envelope of absorber to solar radiation absorbed in the absorber
= 2
~ Optical efficiency is the share of the radiation that falls on the collector aperture and is finally absorbed by the absorber: optical efficiency = radiation absorbed in the absorber / radiation intercepted by the aperture. It combines the mirror reflectance, the glass-cover transmittance and the absorber absorptance (Duffie and Beckman; Kalogirou). Options 1, 3 and 4 compare other pairs of quantities, such as only the glass transmittance.

Q 2 | evs | Natural Resources
Two statements are given, labelled as Assertion (A) and Reason (R). Select the correct option with reference to the statements.
Assertion (A): Photovoltaic detectors normally use silicon solar cells measuring the short circuit current.
Reason (R): Photovoltaic detectors generate a voltage proportional to the temperature difference between the hot and cold junctions.
(1) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
(2) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
(3) A is true but R is false
(4) A is false but R is true
= 1
~ Photovoltaic detectors such as silicon solar cells are normally read by measuring the short-circuit current, which is proportional to the incident radiation, so A is true. APPSC's key is option 1, which treats R as a correct explanation. However, a voltage proportional to the temperature difference between hot and cold junctions is the working principle of a thermopile (Seebeck effect), not of a photovoltaic cell, so many sources would take R as false (option 3). (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 3 | evs | Natural Resources
Floating Drum Type Biogas Plants are ________ Type Biogas Plants.
(1) Constant Volume
(2) Constant Pressure
(3) Constant Temperature
(4) Constant Enthalpy
= 2
~ In a floating-drum (KVIC) plant the gas holder drum rises and falls as gas is stored and used, so the gas is supplied at a constant pressure fixed by the weight of the drum while the gas volume changes. In a fixed-dome plant the gas space has a fixed volume and the pressure varies, so that one is the constant-volume type.

Q 4 | evs | Natural Resources
In which biomass conversion technologies can pelletization, briquetting and the fuel extraction process be seen?
(1) Direct Combustion
(2) Physical Method
(3) Bio-chemical Method
(4) Thermo-chemical Method
= 2
~ Pelletisation and briquetting (densification) and the extraction of oil or fuel from seeds are mechanical, physical processes that change the form of the biomass but not its chemical nature, so they come under physical (mechanical) conversion. Direct combustion, gasification and pyrolysis are thermo-chemical routes, while anaerobic digestion and fermentation are bio-chemical routes.

Q 5 | evs | Natural Resources
The part of solar radiation that reaches the earth's surface with no change in direction is called:
(1) beam radiation
(2) diffuse radiation
(3) arbitrary radiation
(4) thermal radiation
= 1
~ Beam (direct) radiation is the part of sunlight that reaches the ground without being scattered by the atmosphere, so it keeps its direction from the sun's disc. The scattered part, which arrives from all directions of the sky, is diffuse radiation, and the two together make up global (total) radiation. "Arbitrary radiation" is not a standard term.

Q 6 | evs | Natural Resources
In a solar air heating system, the flow rate through an air collector and storage loop is a compromise between ________ and ________.
(1) pressure drop; energy transfer to the air stream
(2) temperature drop; energy transfer to the air stream
(3) volume drop; energy transfer to the air stream
(4) temperature drop; energy transfer to the water stream
= 1
~ A high air flow improves heat transfer from the collector to the air, but the pressure drop across the collector and storage bed rises steeply with flow and needs more fan power. A low flow saves fan power but transfers less energy. The design flow rate is therefore a compromise between pressure drop and energy transfer to the air stream; option 4 wrongly refers to a water stream.

Q 7 | evs | Natural Resources
____________ is a solar collector using the absorption of solar radiation at the bottom of a 2 m to 3 m deep body of water to generate low-temperature heat.
(1) Non convecting solar pond
(2) Solar photovoltaic
(3) Solar desailation
(4) Solar distillation
= 1
~ A non-convecting (salt-gradient) solar pond is a pond a few metres deep in which a salt gradient stops convection, so heat absorbed at the dark bottom is trapped in the lower layer and can be drawn off as low-temperature heat (up to about 80-90 degrees C). Solar photovoltaic produces electricity, and solar desalination and distillation are water-purification processes, not pond-type heat collectors.

Q 8 | evs | Natural Resources
Two statements are given, labelled as Assertion (A) and Reason (R). Select the correct option with reference to the given statements.
Assertion A: Heliostats consist of several mirror panels supported on a steel structure.
Reason R : The heliostat focal length is approximately equal to the distance of the heliostat from the receiver.
(1) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
(2) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
(3) A is true but R is false
(4) A is false but R is true
= 1
~ In a central-receiver (power tower) plant a heliostat is a large sun-tracking mirror built from several mirror panels (facets) on a steel structure, so A is true. R is also true: each heliostat is focused at roughly its distance (slant range) from the receiver, and APPSC's key accepts this as the explanation, because the facets are angled so that together they act as one concave mirror of that focal length. Some sources would call R true but not the reason for A (option 2). (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 11 | evs | Natural Resources
What is the role of an insulator in Liquid flat plate collector (LFPC)?
(1) To minimise heat loss from the back and the sides
(2) To facilitate the flow of heat-transfer fluid
(3) To maximise heat loss from the back and the sides
(4) To facilitate the incident solar radiation
= 1
~ In a liquid flat-plate collector the insulation (glass wool, mineral wool and similar) is placed behind and at the sides of the absorber plate to cut conduction losses, so more of the absorbed heat goes into the fluid. The tubes carry the fluid and the transparent cover admits the sunlight, so options 2 and 4 describe other components.

Q 12 | evs | Natural Resources
Photovoltaic (PV) conversion is the direct conversion of:
(1) sunlight into electricity
(2) sunlight into heat energy
(3) sunlight into latent heat storage energy
(4) kinetic energy into mechanical energy
= 1
~ Photovoltaic conversion changes sunlight directly into electricity in a semiconductor such as a silicon p-n junction, through the photovoltaic effect. Conversion of sunlight into heat is photothermal conversion, which is how solar thermal collectors work.

Q 13 | evs | Natural Resources
Match the following columns.
List I | List II
A. Direct Solar Radiation | P. Instrument used to measure solar radiation
B. Diffuse Solar Radiation | Q. Combination of beam radiation and diffuse radiation
C. Solar Insolation | R. Radiation undergoes solar attenuation
D. Pyranometer | S. Radiation does not undergo solar attenuation
(1) A-S, B-R, C-Q, D-P
(2) A-P, B-R, C-Q, D-S
(3) A-S, B-P, C-Q, D-R
(4) A-Q, B-R, C-S, D-P
= 1
~ Direct (beam) radiation reaches the surface without being scattered (S), whereas diffuse radiation has been scattered by the atmosphere (R). Solar insolation is the total of beam and diffuse radiation received on a surface (Q), and a pyranometer is the instrument that measures it (P). The question uses "attenuation" loosely to mean scattering that changes the direction of the rays.

Q 14 | evs | Natural Resources
Two statements are given, labelled as Assertion (A) and Reason (R). Select the correct option with reference to the given statements.
Assertion (A): A pyranometer (solar radiation measurement instrument) has a hemispherical view of the surroundings.
Reason (R): Therefore, pyranometer is used to measure the total, direct, and diffuse solar radiation on a surface.
(1) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
(2) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
(3) A is true but R is false
(4) A is false but R is true
= 1
~ A pyranometer sees the whole sky hemisphere, so it receives radiation from every direction and measures the global (total) radiation on a surface. With a shading ring or disc it measures diffuse radiation, and the direct component can then be found by difference, so the hemispherical view is why it is used for all three. (Direct beam radiation on its own is measured by a pyrheliometer.)

Q 15 | evs | Environmental Assessment & Planning
Which of the following best defines eco-planning?
(1) Economic planning for industries
(2) Social welfare planning
(3) Systematic approach to environmental resource management
(4) Political boundary planning
= 3
~ Eco-planning is a systematic, planned approach to managing and using environmental resources such as land, water, forests and energy so that development stays ecologically sustainable. It is not economic, social-welfare or political-boundary planning, although it interacts with all of them.

Q 16 | evs | Natural Resources
Which soil horizon is characterised by maximum biological activity?
(1) A horizon (topsoil)
(2) B horizon (subsoil)
(3) C horizon (parent material)
(4) E horizon (eluviated layer)
= 1
~ The A horizon (topsoil) is rich in humus, roots, bacteria, fungi and earthworms, so biological activity is highest there. The B horizon only accumulates clay and minerals washed down from above, the C horizon is weathered parent material with little life, and the E horizon is a leached layer poor in organic matter.

Q 17 | evs | Natural Resources
Which of the following best describes the role of soil organic matter in ecosystems?
(1) Reducing soil erosion exclusively in arid regions
(2) Serving as a habitat for deep-sea organisms
(3) Enhancing soil fertility and water retention
(4) Promoting the growth of salt-tolerant plant species
= 3
~ Soil organic matter (humus) stores and slowly releases plant nutrients, improves soil structure and acts like a sponge that raises the water-holding capacity, so it enhances fertility and water retention. It helps to control erosion in all climates, not only in arid regions, and has no link with deep-sea organisms.

Q 18 | evs | Natural Resources
Which soil texture classification has the highest water retention capacity?
(1) Sandy loam
(2) Clay
(3) Silt
(4) Pure sand
= 2
~ Clay has the finest particles, the largest surface area and many very small pores, so it holds the most water, although much of it is held so tightly that plants cannot use it. Sand has large pores and drains freely, so it has the lowest retention, and silt and sandy loam are in between.

Match the following environmental strategies with their corresponding benefits.
Strategy | Benefit
A) Organic farming | 1) Reduces pesticide usage
B) Water conservation | 2) Ensures sustainable irrigation
C) Crop rotation | 3) Prevents soil degradation
D) Use of biofertilisers | 4) Enhances soil microbial activity

Q 20 | evs | Natural Resources
What is the primary goal of eco-friendly soil management programs?
(1) To maximise short-term agricultural yields
(2) To preserve soil fertility for long-term productivity
(3) To eliminate the use of all fertilisers
(4) To promote rapid urbanisation
= 2

It does not aim at short-term yield maximisation, and it reduces fertiliser dependence rather than necessarily eliminating all fertilisers.

Q 22 | evs | Ecology & Ecosystems
Match the column A for the components of food web with their characteristics in column B.

1. Apex predator | a. Breaks down dead organic matter
2. Keystone species | b. Energy harnessing

Q 23 | evs | Ecology & Ecosystems
Which of the following is the most significant example of a keystone species in a food chain?
(1) Apex predator
(2) Decomposer
(3) Producer
(4) Mesopredator
= 1
~ A keystone species is one whose removal changes a whole community far more than its numbers would suggest. The classic examples, such as the sea star Pisaster (R. T. Paine) and sea otters, are apex predators that keep herbivore populations in check. Producers and decomposers are foundational but are not usually called keystone species, and a mesopredator is itself controlled by the apex predator.

Q 24 | evs | Natural Resources
How does the depletion of soil nutrients directly affect global food security?
(1) It reduces the carrying capacity of soil for crops.
(2) It enhances the growth of invasive species.
(3) It increases water availability in agricultural regions.
(4) It promotes biodiversity in farmland ecosystems.
= 1
~ When soil nutrients are depleted, the soil can support less crop growth, which means its carrying capacity for crops falls, yields drop and food security is threatened. Options 2, 3 and 4 describe effects that are unrelated to, or the opposite of, what nutrient depletion causes.

Q 26 | evs | Natural Resources
What is the primary reason for decreased soil fertility in intensive farming areas?
(1) Disruption of mycorrhizal associations
(2) Increased soil compaction
(3) Loss of organic matter
(4) Change in soil pH
= 3
~ Repeated cropping, removal of crop residues and heavy tillage speed up the oxidation of soil organic carbon, so organic matter declines. Because organic matter holds nutrients and water and feeds soil life, its loss is the main cause of falling fertility. Compaction, pH change and damage to mycorrhizae occur too, but they are secondary factors.

Q 28 | evs | Ecology & Ecosystems
Which of the following is a characteristic of a mature ecosystem?
(1) High species diversity
(2) Absence of keystone species
(3) Simple food web
(4) Primary succession
= 1
~ A mature (climax) ecosystem has high species diversity, complex food webs, stable energy flow and well-developed nutrient cycles. Simple food webs belong to early successional stages, and primary succession is the colonisation of bare ground, not a feature of a mature community.

Q 29 | evs | Ecology & Ecosystems
Which of the following ecosystem functions is most closely related to the concept of 'tipping points', where small changes can lead to abrupt and irreversible changes in ecosystem behaviour?
(1) Non-linear dynamics
(2) Catastrophic shifts
(3) Threshold effects
(4) Feedback loops
= 3
~ A tipping point is a critical threshold: once a small change in a driver crosses it, the system moves abruptly, and often irreversibly, to a new state. The key therefore picks threshold effects. Non-linear dynamics, catastrophic shifts and feedback loops are closely related ideas, so the question is ambiguous. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 30 | evs | Environmental Assessment & Planning
Which land use pattern is considered most sustainable for a developing urban periphery?
(1) High-density residential development with integrated green spaces
(2) Single-use commercial zones with extensive parking
(3) Scattered low-density housing developments
(4) Industrial parks without buffer zones
= 3
~ Compact, high-density development with integrated green space is normally regarded as the sustainable pattern for an urban periphery, because it limits sprawl and loss of farmland and eases transport and service needs. APPSC's key, however, is the scattered low-density option, which planning texts treat as urban sprawl. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 32 | evs | Climate Change & Global Issues
Which of the following is a limitation of the 'Limits to Growth' model?
(1) Lack of inclusion of technology’s role in resource management
(2) Overestimation of renewable resources
(3) Focus only on rural ecosystems
(4) Ignoring economic factors like GDP growth
= 1
~ "The Limits to Growth" (Club of Rome, 1972) used the World3 computer model to project overshoot and collapse from exponential growth in a finite world. The main criticism is that it did not adequately allow for technological progress, substitution and market adjustment in the use of resources. It was a global model that did include economic variables such as industrial output, so options 3 and 4 are wrong.

Q 33 | evs | Ecology & Ecosystems
Which of the following ecosystems exhibits highest net primary production?
(1) Temperate grassland
(2) Arctic tundra
(3) Tropical rainforest
(4) Coral reef
= 3
~ Of the major terrestrial biomes, tropical rainforests have the highest net primary production per unit area (about 2,200 g/m2/yr in Whittaker's estimates), thanks to year-round warmth, rainfall and sunlight. Temperate grassland and Arctic tundra are far less productive. Some tables give coral reefs and algal beds a slightly higher figure (about 2,500 g/m2/yr), so the question is arguable. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 34 | evs | Environmental Assessment & Planning
Which of the following is a primary objective of urban planning in India?
(1) Enhancing biodiversity in forests
(2) Reducing air pollution in rural areas
(3) Managing land use for sustainable development
(4) Promoting industrial growth without restrictions
= 3
~ A central aim of urban planning in India is to regulate land use (residential, commercial, industrial, open space) so that cities grow in an orderly and sustainable way, as in master plans prepared under state town-planning laws. Forests and rural air quality are outside its scope, and "growth without restrictions" is the opposite of planning.

Q 38 | evs | Ecology & Ecosystems
Which of the following options is an appropriate conclusion for the given statement with reference to ecosystem energetics?

Q 40 | evs | Natural Resources
What role do soil microbes play in maintaining soil health?

Reason (R): Nickel can cause DNA damage and disrupt the balance of other essential elements in the body.

(3) A is false but R is true.

Q 135 | evs | Pollution & Waste Management
A substance that has a harmful effect on living organisms and is a discrete chemical, like lead chromate, is known as a:
(1) Toxin
(2) Poison
(3) Toxicant
(4) Corrosive Substance
= 3
~ In toxicology, a toxicant is a toxic substance that is a specific (often man-made) chemical, such as lead chromate or a pesticide. A toxin is a toxic substance produced by a living organism, for example snake venom or bacterial toxins, while poison is the general everyday word for any substance harmful in small amounts.

Q 136 | evs | Pollution & Waste Management
Which of the following dose-response models is characterised by a non-linear increase in response with increasing dose, with no apparent threshold?
(1) Hormetic model
(2) Heterogenous model
(3) Non-linear model
(4) Threshold model
= 3
~ In the non-linear model the response rises with dose but not in proportion to it, and there is no dose below which the response is absent. The threshold model has a dose below which no effect occurs, and the hormetic model shows stimulation at low doses and inhibition at high doses (a U-shaped or inverted-U curve).

Q 137 | evs | Pollution & Waste Management
What is the 'threshold value' of a toxic substance?
(1) The highest level of a substance that is toxic
(2) The lowest level at which a substance causes harm
(3) The level at which a substance is safe for humans
(4) The amount of a substance that causes no harm at all
= 2
~ The threshold value is the lowest dose or concentration at which a harmful effect first becomes detectable; below it no effect is seen. It is not a ceiling of toxicity, and it is not itself a guaranteed safe level, since safe levels are set lower with safety factors.

Q 140 | evs | Pollution & Waste Management
How is the Acceptable Daily Intake (ADI) typically determined?
(1) By assessing the total environmental concentration of a substance
(2) Through animal testing, using safety margins to account for human variability
(3) By measuring the immediate effects of exposure in humans
(4) By calculating the concentration of a substance in food and beverages
= 2
~ The ADI is usually derived from the no-observed-adverse-effect level (NOAEL) in animal studies, divided by a safety (uncertainty) factor, commonly 100, to allow for differences between species and between individual humans. It is expressed as mg per kg of body weight per day (JECFA/WHO approach).

Q 148 | evs | Pollution & Waste Management
Which of the following is the most specific and important physiological response to heavy metal toxicity?
(1) Activation of metallotheins
(2) Increased calcium levels
(3) Increased blood pressure
(4) Low blood pressure
= 1
~ Metallothioneins are small cysteine-rich proteins that the body makes in larger amounts when exposed to metals such as cadmium, zinc, copper and mercury; they bind and sequester the metal, which is the specific protective response. Changes in calcium level or blood pressure are non-specific and not a defence against metals as such.

Q 150 | evs | Pollution & Waste Management
Which of the given options is the most accurate conclusion to the following statement?
Statement: The element selenium is an essential nutrient for humans, but excessive intake can cause toxicity.
(1) Selenium toxicity is due to its ability to inhibit the activity of calmodulin.
(2) Selenium toxicity is due to its ability to cause oxidative stress and damage to cellular components.
