Q 1 | men | Reasoning
B, D, E, F, P, Q and R are sitting around a circular table, facing the centre of the table. Only one person sits between P and D when counted from the left of D. R sits third to the right of F. B sits third to the left of E. R sits to the immediate right of B. Q is not an immediate neighbour of B.
Column I (Person) | Column II (Neighbour to the immediate left)
(i)Q | (W)B
(ii)P | (X)E
(iii)D | (Y)R
(iv)R | (Z)F
Which is the correct match of Column I with Column II?
(1) i-Y, ii-X, iii-Z, iv-W
(2) i-Y, ii-Z, iii-W, iv-X
(3) i-X, ii-W, iii-Z, iv-Y
(4) i-W, ii-x, iii-Y, iv-Z
= 1
~ Facing the centre, a person's left is the next seat clockwise. Take B as reference: R is immediately right of B; F is second to B's left (R is third to F's right); E is third to B's right. P must be second to D's left and Q cannot sit next to B, so clockwise the order is B, D, F, P, E, Q, R. Immediate left neighbours: Q–R, P–E, D–F, R–B, i.e. i-Y, ii-X, iii-Z, iv-W.

Q 2 | men | Reasoning
Each of P, Q, R, S, T, U and V was born in a different month viz. January, March, May, June, July, November and December of the same year, starting from January and ending in December. Only two people were born between R and V. Only one person was born before R. Only four people were born between T and Q. Only one person was born after T. S was born in a month having only 30 days. P was born before U. Which of the following options represent the correct order in which the people were born?
(1) R, P, S, V
(2) P, S, U, T
(3) T, V, Q, R
(4) Q, P, T, S
= 1
~ Month order: Jan, Mar, May, Jun, Jul, Nov, Dec. One person before R gives R = March; two between R and V gives V = July; one after T gives T = November; four between T and Q gives Q = January. S needs a 30-day month (June or November), so S = June, and P before U gives P = May, U = December. The order is Q, R, P, S, V, T, U, so R, P, S, V is correct.

Q 3 | men | Reasoning
What should come in place of the question mark (?) in the given series based on the English alphabetical order?
BJR DLT FNV HPX ?
(1) KNQ
(2) MOK
(3) JNO
(4) JRZ
= 4
~ Each letter moves 2 places forward from one term to the next: first letters B, D, F, H → J; second letters J, L, N, P → R; third letters R, T, V, X → Z. Within every term the letters are also 8 places apart (B+8 = J, J+8 = R), and JRZ keeps this (J+8 = R, R+8 = Z). So the missing term is JRZ.

Q 4 | men | Reasoning
Anand starts from Point A and drives 7 km towards the south. He then takes a left turn, drives 6 km, turns left and drives 11 km and stops at point P. (All turns are 90 degrees turns only, unless specified.)
Based on the above information, find out which of the given statements can be the reason/s behind the assertion made below.
Assertion: Anand is 3km away from Point A.
Reason A: He then takes a right turn from point P and drives 8 km. He takes a final left turn, drives 4 km and stops.
Reason B: He then takes a left turn from point P and drives 9 km. He takes a final left turn, drives 4 km and stops.
Reason C: He then takes a left turn from point P and drives 12 km. He takes a final right turn, drives 7 km and stops.
(1) Only B is the reason behind the assertion.
(2) Only A and C are reasons behind the assertion.
(3) Only A is the reason behind the assertion.
(4) Only A and B are reasons behind the assertion.
= 1
~ From A: 7 km south, left (east) 6 km, left (north) 11 km, so P is 6 km east and 4 km north of A, and he faces north. Reason B: left (west) 9 km puts him 3 km west and 4 km north of A; a final left (south) of 4 km leaves him exactly 3 km west of A. Reason A ends 14 km east, 8 km north and Reason C 6 km west, 11 km north of A, so only B fits.

Q 5 | men | Reasoning
Three statements are given, followed by one conclusion. Find out which of the given options should replace the question mark (?) in the statement to make the conclusion true.
Statements:
I. Z > X > W
II. X ? Y < U
III. A = B ≥ U
Conclusion:
W < A
(1) Only ≥
(2) Only =
(3) Either < or =
(4) Only >
= 3
~ From I, W < X; from II and III, Y < U ≤ B = A, so Y < A. To link W with A we need X ≤ Y: then W < X ≤ Y < U ≤ A gives W < A for certain. Hence the sign can be either '<' or '='. With '>' or '≥', X could be larger than A, and the conclusion would not definitely follow.

Q 6 | men | Reasoning
What should come in place of the question mark (?) in the given series based on the English alphabetical order?
LIG NKI PMK ROM ?
(1) TQO
(2) TQP
(3) TRO
(4) TRP
= 1
~ Each letter moves 2 places forward from one term to the next: first letters L, N, P, R → T; second letters I, K, M, O → Q; third letters G, I, K, M → O. The pattern inside every term also stays the same (L−3 = I, I−2 = G; and T−3 = Q, Q−2 = O). So the missing term is TQO.

Q 7 | men | Reasoning
Each letter in the word DEVICE is changed to the letter immediately preceding it in the English alphabetical order, and then all the letters thus formed are arranged in the English alphabetical order. Which of the following letters will be second from the right in the new group of letters thus formed?
(1) H
(2) U
(3) B
(4) D
= 1
~ Replacing each letter of DEVICE by the letter just before it gives D→C, E→D, V→U, I→H, C→B, E→D, i.e. C, D, U, H, B, D. Arranged in alphabetical order these become B, C, D, D, H, U. The rightmost letter is U, so the second letter from the right is H.

Q 8 | men | Reasoning
Refer to the following letter, number, symbol series and answer the given question.
(Left) J & 9 D * C V 6 U # B 5 @ A R 3 T S 7 M Y % 2 G (Right)
How many symbols are there that are immediately preceded by a letter and also immediately followed by a number?
(1) None
(2) One
(3) Two
(4) Three
= 3
~ Check every symbol: & (J & 9) has a letter before and a number after, so it counts; * (D * C) and # (U # B) are followed by letters; @ (5 @ A) is preceded by a number; % (Y % 2) has a letter before and a number after, so it counts. Only & and % satisfy both conditions, so the answer is two.

Q 9 | men | Reasoning
In a certain code,
‘milk makes cheese’ is coded as ‘lo ny ph’,
‘milk gives butter’ is coded as ‘ny ji su’ and
‘butter and cheese’ is coded as ‘ji ma ph’.
What does the code 'ph' stand for?
(1) butter
(2) makes
(3) cheese
(4) milk
= 3
~ The word 'cheese' appears in the first and third sentences ('milk makes cheese' and 'butter and cheese'), and the only code common to 'lo ny ph' and 'ji ma ph' is 'ph'. So ph = cheese. In the same way, milk = ny (sentences 1 and 2) and butter = ji (sentences 2 and 3).

Q 10 | men | Reasoning
Three of the following letter-clusters are alike in some manner and hence form a group. Which letter-cluster does not belong to that group?
(1) VPR
(2) RLM
(3) GAB
(4) WQR
= 1
~ Using alphabet positions, RLM (18, 12, 13), GAB (7, 1, 2) and WQR (23, 17, 18) all follow the pattern −6 then +1. VPR (22, 16, 18) follows −6 then +2, so it is the letter-cluster that does not belong to the group.

11, 25, 47, 97, 191, ?
(1) 385
(2) 379
(3) 570
(4) 576
= 1
~ The terms follow ×2 + 3 and ×2 − 3 alternately: 11 × 2 + 3 = 25, 25 × 2 − 3 = 47, 47 × 2 + 3 = 97, 97 × 2 − 3 = 191. So the next term is 191 × 2 + 3 = 385.

Q 13 | men | Reasoning
In this question, a statement is followed by two conclusions, numbered I and II. Identify which conclusion is true based on the given statement.
Statement: P > Q ≥ R ≥ S < T ≤ U
Conclusion I: P > R
Conclusion II: U > S
(1) Only conclusion I is true
(2) Only conclusion II is true
(3) Both conclusions I and II are true
(4) Neither conclusion I nor II is true
= 3
~ From P > Q ≥ R we get P > R, so conclusion I is true. From S < T ≤ U we get S < U, i.e. U > S, so conclusion II is also true. Since a strict '>' or '<' sign appears in each chain, both conclusions follow definitely.

Q 16 | men | Data Interpretation
The number of boys and girls in schools A, B, C, and D is given in the following table.
Schools | Boys | Girls
A | 425 | 375
B | 312 | 383
C | 260 | 310
D | 416 | 208
Match List I with List II.
List I (Schools) | List II (Students)
A | (i) Number of girls is 50 more than that of boys
B | (ii) Number of boys is double of that of girls
C | (iii) Number of girls is 50 less than that of boys
D | (iv) Total number of students is 695
(1) A-(iii), B-(iv), C-(ii), D-(i)
(2) A-(iii), B-(iv), C-(i), D-(ii)
(3) A-(iv), B-(ii), C-(i), D-(iii)
(4) A-(iii), B-(i), C-(iv), D-(ii)

~ A: girls 375 = 425 − 50, so girls are 50 fewer than boys → (iii); B: 312 + 383 = 695 students → (iv); C: girls 310 = 260 + 50, so 50 more than boys → (i); D: boys 416 = 2 × 208, double the girls → (ii). Hence A-(iii), B-(iv), C-(i), D-(ii).

Q 18 | men | Arithmetic
Read the given information, statement and conclusion, and select the most appropriate option.
A fruit vendor bought 40 dozen bananas, 20 pineapples, and 16 dozen kiwis. He sold 40% of the bananas, 40% of the pineapples and 25% of the kiwis on the first day. On the next day, he sold 50% of the remaining bananas, 25% of the remaining pineapples and 50% of the remaining kiwis.
Statement:
The number of bananas sold in 2 days is more than the number of kiwis sold in 2 days.
Conclusion:
The fruit vendor is left with a smaller number of bananas than kiwis.
(1) Both the statement and conclusion are correct.
(2) Both the statement and conclusion are incorrect.
(3) The statement is correct but the conclusion is incorrect.
(4) The statement is incorrect but the conclusion is correct.
= 3
~ Bananas: 40 dozen = 480; day 1 sold 40% = 192, left 288; day 2 sold 50% = 144; total sold 336, left 144. Kiwis: 16 dozen = 192; day 1 sold 25% = 48, left 144; day 2 sold 50% = 72; total sold 120, left 72. Since 336 > 120 the statement is correct, but 144 bananas remain against 72 kiwis, so the conclusion is incorrect.

Q 20 | men | Arithmetic
There are four different stores A, B, C, and D which sell two different products: laptops and tablets. The number of tablets sold in B and D is in the ratio of 4 : 1. The total number of laptops sold in C is double the total number of laptops sold in B. The total number of laptops sold in B is 200 less than the total number of laptops sold in A. The number of tablets sold in A equals the number of laptops sold in C. The total number of laptops sold in D is 33⅓% of the total number of laptops sold in A. The average number of tablets sold in all stores together is 625, which is 175 more than the total number of tablets sold in C. The difference between the number of tablets sold in B and D is 750.
What is the ratio of the total number of laptops to the total number of tablets sold in all the stores together?
(1) 4 : 5
(2) 5 : 4
(3) 3 : 2
(4) 2 : 3

Q 26 | men | Arithmetic
If the standard deviation of the data is 0.012, then the variance is:
(1) 0.144
(2) 0.00144
(3) 0.000144
(4) 0.0000144
= 3
~ Variance is the square of the standard deviation (the standard deviation is the positive square root of the variance). So variance = (0.012)² = 0.012 × 0.012 = 0.000144, i.e. 144 × 10⁻⁶. The other options place the decimal point wrongly.

Q 27 | men | Arithmetic
The harmonic mean of two numbers is 8. If one of the numbers is 12, what is the other number?
(1) 4
(2) 5
(3) 6
(4) 7
= 3
~ For two numbers a and b, HM = 2ab/(a + b). With a = 12: 2 × 12 × b/(12 + b) = 8, so 24b = 96 + 8b, 16b = 96 and b = 6. Check: 2 × 12 × 6/(12 + 6) = 144/18 = 8.

Q 28 | men | Arithmetic
For a set of 8 observations, the standard deviation is 5.2.
Match list I with List II.
List I | List II (Standard Deviation)
A. Every observation is doubled and then increased by 2 | (i) 1.3
B. Every observation is divided by 4 and decreased by 2 | (ii) 7.8
C. Every observation is multiplied by 3 and divided by 2 | (iii) 2.6
D. Every observation is increased by 4 and divided by 2 | (iv) 10.4
(1) A-(iv), B-(i), C-(iii), D-(ii)
(2) A-(iv), B-(ii), C-(i), D-(iii)
(3) A-(iv), B-(i), C-(ii), D-(iii)
(4) A-(iii), B-(i), C-(iv), D-(ii)
= 3
~ Standard deviation does not change when a constant is added or subtracted, but it is multiplied or divided by the same factor as the data. A: ×2 gives 5.2 × 2 = 10.4 (iv); B: ÷4 gives 1.3 (i); C: ×3 ÷ 2 gives 5.2 × 1.5 = 7.8 (ii); D: ÷2 gives 2.6 (iii). So the match is A-(iv), B-(i), C-(ii), D-(iii).

Q 29 | men | Arithmetic
Find the median of the following data.
Class-interval | Frequency
0 – 10 | 12
10 – 20 | 13
20 – 30 | 25
30 – 40 | 20
40 – 50 | 10
(1) 23
(2) 24
(3) 25
(4) 26
= 4
~ N = 12 + 13 + 25 + 20 + 10 = 80, so N/2 = 40. Cumulative frequencies are 12, 25, 50, …, so the median class is 20–30 (l = 20, cf = 25, f = 25, h = 10). Median = l + [(N/2 − cf)/f] × h = 20 + (40 − 25)/25 × 10 = 20 + 6 = 26.

Q 30 | men | Arithmetic
Select the option that is true regarding the following two statements labelled Assertion (A) and Reason (R).
Assertion (A):
Weighted arithmetic mean is preferred over simple arithmetic mean when dealing with datasets, where different data points carry varying degrees of importance.
Reason (R):
Weighted arithmetic mean takes into account the significance of each data point by assigning appropriate weights, resulting in a more accurate representation of the average.
(1) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(2) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(3) A is true, but R is false.
(4) A is false, but R is true.
= 1
~ When values differ in importance (for example marks in subjects with different credits, or prices weighted by quantities), a simple mean treats them all equally and can mislead, so the weighted mean Σwx/Σw is preferred. It is preferred precisely because it gives each value a weight matching its importance, producing a more representative average. So both A and R are true and R explains A.

Q 138 | geo | Disaster Management
Public education about evacuation routes and safety procedures falls under which disaster management phase?
(1) Prevention
(2) Mitigation
(3) Preparedness
(4) Recovery
= 3
~ Preparedness covers measures taken before a disaster so that the response is effective: public education and awareness, evacuation plans and routes, drills, early warning and stockpiles. Teaching people evacuation routes and safety procedures is therefore preparedness. Prevention and mitigation reduce the hazard or its impact (such as building codes or embankments), and recovery is rebuilding after the event.

Q 139 | geo | Disaster Management

(1) evacuation planning
(2) flood control
(3) search and rescue
(4) damage assessment
= 2
~ Planting trees in flood-prone catchments and along riverbanks increases infiltration, slows surface run-off, and reduces soil erosion and siltation of rivers, which lowers flood peaks. It is a long-term flood control (mitigation) measure. Evacuation planning, search and rescue, and damage assessment are preparedness, response and recovery activities.
