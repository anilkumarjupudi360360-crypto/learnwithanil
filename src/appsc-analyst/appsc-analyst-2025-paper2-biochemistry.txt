Q 1 | bch | Enzymes & Coenzymes
Reversible inhibitors:
(1) slow down a chemical reaction but do not stop it completely
(2) prevent an unwanted chemical reaction from occurring
(3) stop down a chemical reaction
(4) speed up a chemical reaction
= 1
~ Reversible inhibitors bind the enzyme through non-covalent bonds, so they only lower the rate of the reaction and the enzyme regains full activity when the inhibitor is removed or diluted out. They do not abolish the reaction altogether (that is typical of irreversible inhibitors), and they never speed it up.

Q 2 | bch | Enzymes & Coenzymes
In a non-competitive inhibition, the Km value is ______ and Vmax is ________.
(1) unchanged, lowered
(2) unchanged, highered
(3) changed, highered
(4) changed, lowered
= 1
~ A non-competitive inhibitor binds a site away from the active site and binds the free enzyme and the enzyme-substrate complex equally well. It removes active enzyme molecules from the reaction, so Vmax falls, while substrate binding is not affected, so Km stays the same.

Q 3 | bch | Enzymes & Coenzymes
Certain molecules that resemble the substrate of the enzyme and compete for the active sites of enzyme, thereby reduce its efficiency. What are they?
(1) Uncompetitive inhibitors
(2) Competitive inhibitors
(3) Partial inhibitors
(4) Feedback inhibitors
= 2
~ Competitive inhibitors are structural analogues of the substrate and occupy the active site, so substrate and inhibitor compete for the same site. Uncompetitive inhibitors bind only the enzyme-substrate complex, and feedback inhibitors are end products that act at an allosteric site.

Q 4 | bch | Enzymes & Coenzymes
In competitive enzyme inhibition, how does the inhibitor affect enzyme activity?
(1) It binds to the enzyme-substrate complex, preventing the reaction.
(2) It binds to the active site, competing with the substrate.
(3) It binds to a site other than the active site, changing the enzyme's shape.
(4) It binds permanently to the enzyme, inactivating it.
= 2
~ A competitive inhibitor binds reversibly to the active site of the free enzyme and competes with the substrate. Option 1 describes uncompetitive inhibition, option 3 describes non-competitive (allosteric) inhibition and option 4 describes irreversible inhibition.

Q 5 | bch | Enzymes & Coenzymes
What is the effect of an uncompetitive inhibitor on the Michaelis-Menten parameters Vmax and Km?
(1) Vmax decreases and Km increases.
(2) Vmax decreases Km decreases.
(3) Vmax increases and Km decreases.
(4) Vmax increases and Km stays the same.
= 2
~ An uncompetitive inhibitor binds only the enzyme-substrate complex, which removes ES and pulls the equilibrium toward more ES. Both Vmax and the apparent Km fall by the same factor (1 + [I]/Ki'), giving parallel lines on a Lineweaver-Burk plot. An increase in Km is the sign of competitive inhibition.

Q 6 | bch | Enzymes & Coenzymes
_______ affects both the Michaelis constant and the maximum reaction rate of an enzyme.
(1) Competitive inhibition
(2) Non-competitive inhibition
(3) Irreversible inhibition
(4) Uncompetitive inhibition
= 4
~ Uncompetitive inhibition lowers both Vmax and the apparent Km. Competitive inhibition changes only Km (raises it), and pure non-competitive inhibition changes only Vmax. Irreversible inhibition simply inactivates enzyme molecules and so lowers Vmax.

Q 7 | bch | Enzymes & Coenzymes
How many types of reversible inhibitors play role in enzyme kinetics?
(1) 2 types
(2) 5 types
(3) 4 types
(4) 6 types
= 3
~ Four kinds of reversible inhibition are described in enzyme kinetics: competitive, non-competitive, uncompetitive and mixed inhibition. Each has a distinct effect on Km and Vmax and a distinct Lineweaver-Burk pattern.

Q 8 | bch | Enzymes & Coenzymes
Which enzyme inhibitor binds to the enzyme-substrate complex and reduce the activity of the enzyme?
(1) Competitive inhibitors
(2) Allosteric inhibitors
(3) Non competitive inhibitor
(4) Mixed inhibitor
= 3,4
~ Competitive inhibitors bind only the free enzyme. Non-competitive and mixed inhibitors can bind both the free enzyme and the enzyme-substrate complex, so both options 3 and 4 fit the question. (A purely uncompetitive inhibitor, which is not listed, binds only ES.) APPSC noted the ambiguity and gave full marks for either option 3 or 4.

Q 9 | bch | Enzymes & Coenzymes
In non-competitive inhibition, an inhibitor binds to an enzyme at a site other than the active site. Which of the following statements best describes the effect of a non-competitive inhibitor on enzyme kinetics?
(1) It decreases the Vmax (maximum velocity) but does not affect Km (Michaelis constant).
(2) It increases both Vmax and Km.
(3) It decreases Km without affecting Vmax.
(4) It affects neither Vmax nor Km.
= 1
~ In pure non-competitive inhibition the inhibitor lowers the concentration of fully functional enzyme, so Vmax decreases, but it does not change how tightly the substrate binds, so Km is unchanged. Lineweaver-Burk lines intersect on the x-axis (the same -1/Km) but have different y-intercepts.

Q 13 | bch | Enzymes & Coenzymes
Carboxypeptidase is involved in protein digestion. What is the product of this enzyme action?
(1) Peptones
(2) Free amino acids
(3) Peptides
(4) Denatured proteins
= 2
~ Carboxypeptidase is a pancreatic exopeptidase that removes amino acids one at a time from the C-terminal end of a peptide, so its product is free amino acids. Endopeptidases such as trypsin and chymotrypsin produce the shorter peptides, and peptones are the products of pepsin action.

Q 14 | bch | Enzymes & Coenzymes
In mixed inhibition, which of the following represents the modified form of the Lineweaver-Burk equation for mixed inhibition?
(1) 1/vo = Km/Vmax[S] + 1/Vmax (1+[I]/Km)
(2) 1/vo = Km (1+[I]/Ki)/Vmax[S] + 1/Vmax (1+[I]/Ki')
(3) 1/vo = Km (1+[I]/Km)/Vmax[S] + 1/Vmax (1+[I]/Km)
(4) 1/vo = Km (1+[I] Ki')/Vmax[S] + 1/Vmax
= 1
~ In mixed inhibition the inhibitor binds both E and ES with different affinities, so the slope term is multiplied by (1+[I]/Ki) and the intercept term by (1+[I]/Ki'). That textbook form is the one written in option 2, whereas APPSC's key is option 1, which alters only the intercept term. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 15 | bch | Enzymes & Coenzymes
Aspartate transcarbamoylase (ATCase) is regulated allosterically by ATP and CTP. Which of the following describes the specific effect of CTP binding on the kinetic properties of ATCase?
(1) CTP binding increases Vmax and decreases K0.5, shifting the curve toward hyperbolic kinetics.
(2) CTP binding decreases Vmax but does not affect K0.5.
(3) CTP binding increases K0.5, resulting in a more sigmoidal substrate saturation curve.
(4) CTP binding decreases K0.5, converting the enzyme to the R state.
= 3
~ CTP is the end-product (negative) allosteric effector of ATCase in pyrimidine synthesis. It stabilises the low-affinity T state, which raises K0.5 and makes the sigmoidal saturation curve steeper and shifted to the right, whereas ATP (the positive effector) does the opposite by favouring the R state.

Q 18 | bch | Enzymes & Coenzymes
Which of the following statements best describes the role of coenzymes in enzyme-catalysed reactions?
(1) Coenzymes act as permanent components of the enzyme active site, facilitating the formation of enzyme-substrate complexes.
(2) Coenzymes increase the activation energy required for the reaction to proceed, thereby enhancing the efficiency of the enzyme.
(3) Coenzymes bind irreversibly to enzymes and modify their active sites, making them more specific for their substrates.
(4) Coenzymes transfer functional groups or electrons between substrates, but are not consumed or permanently altered in the process.
= 4
~ Coenzymes (such as NAD+, FAD and coenzyme A) are small organic carriers that move electrons or chemical groups from one substrate to another and are regenerated for reuse. Catalysts lower, not raise, the activation energy, and coenzymes bind loosely and reversibly (a tightly bound one is called a prosthetic group).

Q 19 | bch | Enzymes & Coenzymes
Coenzyme A is primarily involved in which type of metabolic process?
(1) Protein synthesis
(2) DNA replication
(3) Fatty acid metabolism
(4) Electron transport
= 3
~ Coenzyme A (derived from pantothenic acid, vitamin B5) carries acyl groups as thioesters. It is central to fatty acid oxidation and synthesis (acyl-CoA, acetyl-CoA, malonyl-CoA) and also links to the citric acid cycle.

Q 20 | bch | Enzymes & Coenzymes
Thiamine pyrophosphate is synthesised in the ________.
(1) cytosol
(2) nucleus
(3) endoplasmic reticulum
(4) golgi complex
= 1
~ Thiamine pyrophosphate (TPP) is made from thiamine and ATP by the enzyme thiamine pyrophosphokinase, which is located in the cytosol. TPP is then taken up by mitochondria, where it works as the coenzyme of pyruvate dehydrogenase and alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase.

Q 24 | bch | Enzymes & Coenzymes
What distinguishes flavin nucleotides (FMN and FAD) in their role as cofactors in enzymatic electron transfer reactions?
(1) They are consumed as substrates during the reaction.
(2) They facilitate only two-electron transfer.
(3) They are tightly bound to enzymes and act as intermediates for one- or two-electron transfers.
(4) Their reduction potential is unaffected by the local protein environment.
= 3
~ FMN and FAD are usually tightly (sometimes covalently) bound to flavoproteins as prosthetic groups. Q 25 | bch | Enzymes & Coenzymes
Which of the following coenzymes function as a redox reaction?
(1) Flavin adenine dinucleotide
(2) Alcohol dehydrogenase
(3) Procarboxypeptidase
(4) Deoxyadenosylcobalamin

Q 26 | bch | Enzymes & Coenzymes
Which of the following is a function of Ribozyme?
(1) Transamination
(2) Redox reaction
(3) Transcription
(4) Translation
= 4
~ Ribozymes are catalytic RNA molecules. The best known example is the ribosomal RNA of the large subunit, which forms the peptidyl transferase centre and catalyses peptide bond formation during translation. Transamination and redox reactions are carried out by protein enzymes, and transcription is catalysed by RNA polymerase.

Q 27 | bch | Enzymes & Coenzymes
Select the most appropriate option based on the Assertion (A) and Reason (R) given.
Assertion (A): Enzymatic catalysis is more efficient than traditional synthetic catalysts in terms of turnover number (TON).
Reason (R): Enzymes stabilize the transition state through specific interactions, reducing the activation energy significantly.

Q 34 | bch | Enzymes & Coenzymes
In the case of an oligomeric protein like haemoglobin, which of the following changes occurs as a result of substrate binding to one subunit?
(1) The enzyme undergoes a conformational change that increases the affinity of other subunits for the substrate.
(2) The binding of the substrate to one subunit causes no change in the activity of other subunits.
(3) The enzyme active site becomes less accessible to additional substrates.

= 1
~ Oligomeric proteins such as haemoglobin show positive cooperativity: binding of ligand to one subunit triggers a T-to-R conformational change that raises the affinity of the remaining subunits. This produces the sigmoidal binding curve, rather than the hyperbolic curve of a monomeric protein.

Q 41 | bch | Immunology
Based on the following statement and conclusion, which of the given options is most accurate?
Statement: Flow cytometry is a technique that allows for the detection and measurement of physical and chemical characteristics of a population of cells or particles.
Conclusion: Flow cytometry can be used in immunodiagnostics to analyse immune cell populations by labelling them with fluorescent antibodies specific to cell surface markers.

~ Post-hepatic (obstructive) jaundice results from blockage of bile flow, for example by gallstones or a tumour of the pancreatic head, and causes conjugated hyperbilirubinaemia, pale stools and dark urine. Pre-hepatic jaundice is due to excess haemolysis and hepatic jaundice to hepatocyte damage.

Q 150 | bch | Clinical Biochemistry
Which of the following renal function tests is most sensitive for early detection of kidney damage?

(2) Serum creatinine
(3) Estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR)
(4) Blood urea nitrogen (BUN)
= 3
