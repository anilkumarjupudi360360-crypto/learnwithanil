Q 16 | sci | Chemistry
The drum of a Photostat machine is made up of which one of the following elements ?
(1) Selenium
(2) Aluminium
(3) Barium
(4) Magnesium
= 1
~ A photocopier (xerography) uses a drum coated with a photoconductor, traditionally selenium. Selenium behaves as an insulator in the dark but conducts when light falls on it, so a charge image of the document forms on the drum and attracts the toner. Aluminium is often only the base cylinder; the light-sensitive coating is selenium.

Q 18 | sci | Biology & Health
Which one of the following bacteria present in the soil produces a peculiar smell if it rains on parched soil ?
(1) Diplomycetes
(2) Staphymycetes
(3) Streptomycetes
(4) Micrococcus
= 3
~ The earthy smell when rain falls on dry soil (petrichor) comes mainly from geosmin, a compound produced by Streptomyces (streptomycetes, actinomycete bacteria) living in the soil. Raindrops release the geosmin into the air. Micrococcus does not cause this smell, and "Diplomycetes" and "Staphymycetes" are not real bacterial groups.

Q 20 | sci | Physics
Which gas is the most abundant in Sun ?
(1) Oxygen
(2) Nitrogen
(3) Helium
(4) Hydrogen
= 4
~ Hydrogen is the most abundant element in the Sun, about 73% of its mass (roughly 92% of its atoms), followed by helium at about 25%. In the Sun's core, hydrogen nuclei fuse to form helium, releasing energy. Helium comes second, while oxygen and nitrogen are present only in small amounts.

Q 21 | sci | Physics
A sensitive magnetic field instrument can be effectively shielded from the external magnetic field by placing it inside which of the following materials ?
(1) Plastic Material
(2) Teak Wood
(3) Soft Iron of high permeability
(4) A metal of high conductivity
= 3
~ Soft iron has very high magnetic permeability, so external magnetic field lines prefer to pass through the iron walls of an enclosure rather than through the space inside; this is magnetic shielding. Plastic and wood are non-magnetic and let the field pass through. A highly conducting metal shields against electric fields, not against steady magnetic fields.

Q 22 | sci | Physics
What is the principle behind thermocouple ?
(1) Joule effect
(2) Thomson effect
(3) Seebeck effect
(4) Peltier effect
= 3
~ A thermocouple works on the Seebeck effect: when the two junctions of two different metals are kept at different temperatures, an emf (thermo-emf) is produced, which is used to measure temperature. The Peltier effect is the reverse (a current causing heating or cooling at a junction), and the Joule effect is the heating produced by a current.

Q 23 | sci | Chemistry
Which of the following Gas is produced when Baking soda is heated ?
(1) Carbon monoxide
(2) Carbon dioxide
(3) Nitrogen
(4) Hydrogen
= 2
~ Baking soda is sodium hydrogen carbonate (NaHCO3). On heating it decomposes: 2NaHCO3 → Na2CO3 + H2O + CO2. The carbon dioxide released makes cakes and bread rise; carbon monoxide is not produced.

Q 25 | sci | Physics
The combination of which of the following processes results in the formation of a rainbow ?
(1) Dispersion and Total Internal Reflection
(2) Dispersion and Absorption
(3) Refraction and Scattering
(4) Dispersion and Focussing
= 1
~ A rainbow forms when sunlight entering raindrops is refracted and dispersed into its colours, reflected inside the drop and refracted again as it comes out (NCERT Class 10 Science, "The Human Eye and the Colourful World"). Exam books call the reflection inside the drop total internal reflection, though strictly it is internal reflection. Scattering explains the blue colour of the sky, not the rainbow.

Q 30 | geo | Environment & Ecology
If we completely remove the decomposers from an ecosystem, their functioning will be adversely affected because
(1) Herbivores will not receive solar energy
(2) Energy flow will be blocked
(3) The rate of decomposition will be very high
(4) Mineral movement will be blocked
= 4
~ Decomposers such as bacteria and fungi break down dead plants and animals and release the minerals and nutrients back into the soil (mineralisation), so that plants can reuse them. Without decomposers, nutrients stay locked in dead matter and the cycling of minerals stops. Energy flow is one-way, from the Sun through producers, and does not depend on decomposers in this way.

Q 42 | geo | Indian Geography

Q 45 | geo | AP Geography

(1) Continuous mountain range
(2) Young fold mountains
(3) Discontinuous and eroded hills
(4) Volcanic mountains
= 3

Q 46 | geo | Environment & Ecology
Which of these best describes the idea of sustainable development ?
(1) A model for development that focuses on industry growth and using resources for short-term profit.
(2) A idea first mentioned in the Brundtland Report in 1987, it is "development that meets the needs of the present generations without making it harder for future generations to meet their own needs".
(3) A plan put forward at the Stockholm Conference in 1972 that was solely focused on protecting wildlife.
(4) A plan from the UN to help poor countries become more globalized and open up their economies.
= 2
~ The Brundtland Report, "Our Common Future" (1987), of the World Commission on Environment and Development chaired by Gro Harlem Brundtland, defined sustainable development as development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. The 1972 Stockholm Conference dealt with the human environment as a whole, not wildlife alone.

Q 47 | geo | Environment & Ecology
The three main pillars of sustainable development are
(1) Political, Cultural and Economic
(2) Economic, Environmental and Social
(3) Industrial, Agricultural and Educational
(4) Technological, Ecological and Financial
= 2
~ Sustainable development rests on three interlinked pillars: economic growth, environmental protection and social equity (often summed up as profit, planet and people). This framework was stressed at the 2002 Johannesburg World Summit and underlies the UN 2030 Agenda and its SDGs. Political and cultural factors matter, but they are not the three classic pillars.

Q 48 | geo | Environment & Ecology
What does the Precautionary Principle mean in environmental protection ?
(1) We should act to protect the environment only after full scientific proof of damage
(2) We should take preventive action to protect the environment even if we are not completely sure of the harm
(3) Industries can pollute if it helps the economy grow
(4) Environmental protection is needed only when people complain
= 2
~ The Precautionary Principle (Principle 15 of the 1992 Rio Declaration) says that where there is a threat of serious or irreversible damage, lack of full scientific certainty must not be used as a reason to postpone measures to prevent environmental harm. The Supreme Court made it part of Indian environmental law in Vellore Citizens' Welfare Forum v. Union of India (1996). Option (1) states the opposite of the principle.

Q 49 | geo | Environment & Ecology
The Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) set by the UN wants to

(2) Get rid of poverty, take care of the environment and make sure everyone has peace and plenty

Q 50 | geo | Environment & Ecology
The Paris Climate Agreement (2015) mainly aims to
(1) Stop all industrial activities worldwide
(2) Limit global temperature rise to well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels
(3) Promote trade among developed nations
(4) Increase fossil fuel production for energy security
= 2
~ The Paris Agreement, adopted at COP21 in December 2015 under the UNFCCC, aims to hold the rise in global average temperature to well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels and to pursue efforts to limit it to 1.5°C. Each country acts through its Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC); the agreement does not call for stopping industry or raising fossil fuel output.

Q 51 | geo | Environment & Ecology
Which of the following best explains the relationship between deforestation and biodiversity loss ?
(1) Deforestation increases soil fertility and helps species diversity
(2) Deforestation destroys natural habitats, leading to extinction of species and disruption of ecosystems
(3) Deforestation allows migration of species to urban regions where biodiversity improves
(4) Deforestation reduces carbon dioxide levels, protecting biodiversity
= 2
~ Habitat loss is the main driver of biodiversity loss. Cutting forests destroys and fragments the habitats of plants and animals, pushing species towards extinction and breaking food chains and ecosystem services. Deforestation actually raises carbon dioxide levels and leads to soil erosion, so options (1) and (4) are wrong.

Q 52 | geo | Environment & Ecology
How can improper waste management, especially biomedical and e-waste, indirectly contribute to deforestation and biodiversity loss ?
(1) By providing organic fertilizers that help forest regeneration
(2) By releasing toxic substances into soil and water, damaging ecosystems and forcing land-use changes like deforestation
(3) By reducing industrial pollution and improving ecosystem balance
(4) By increasing green cover through waste-to-energy plants in forests
= 2
~ Biomedical and electronic waste contain toxic substances such as heavy metals (lead, mercury, cadmium), chemicals and pathogens. When dumped or burnt improperly, they contaminate soil and water, harm plants and animals, and make land unusable, which pushes dumping and other land use into forests. In India such waste is regulated by the Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016 and the E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022.

Q 53 | geo | Environment & Ecology
What is the main goal of sustainable agriculture ?
(1) To increase crop yield using more chemical fertilizers and pesticides
(2) To clear forests for more agricultural land
(3) To produce food while protecting the environment and conserving resources for the future
(4) To depend only on imported food for national needs
= 3
~ Sustainable agriculture aims to meet present food needs while protecting soil, water and biodiversity, so that future generations can also produce food. It uses practices such as crop rotation, organic manures, integrated pest management and efficient irrigation. Heavy use of chemicals and clearing forests degrade the resource base, so options (1) and (2) are the opposite.

Q 54 | geo | Environment & Ecology
Which of the following is an example of renewable energy used in the energy transition ?
(1) Coal energy
(2) Diesel-based power
(3) Nuclear fission energy
(4) Solar and Wind energy
= 4
~ The energy transition is the shift from fossil fuels to clean, low-carbon sources. Solar and wind energy are renewable because sunlight and wind are naturally replenished. Coal and diesel are fossil fuels, and nuclear fission is low-carbon but not renewable, since it uses finite uranium.

Q 55 | geo | Environment & Ecology
Which of the following best describes the role of biotechnology in environmental sustainability ?
(1) Using genetically modified microbes and plants to reduce pollution and conserve natural resources
(2) Increasing the use of chemical fertilizers and pesticides
(3) Promoting deforestation for industrial development
(4) Producing more non-biodegradable plastics
= 1
~ Environmental biotechnology uses living organisms, including genetically modified microbes and plants, to clean up and protect the environment. Examples are bioremediation (for instance oil-eating bacteria for oil spills), phytoremediation of heavy metals, biofertilisers, biopesticides and biodegradable plastics. These reduce pollution and conserve resources, unlike options (2) to (4).

Q 56 | geo | Environment & Ecology
What is the main difference between Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) and Life-Cycle Assessment (LCA) ?
(1) EIA focuses only on product design, while LCA focuses on project location
(2) EIA and LCA both focus only on air pollution levels
(3) EIA evaluates environmental impacts before a project starts, while LCA assesses impacts throughout the entire life of a product or process
(4) EIA is used for agricultural projects only, while LCA is used for industrial projects only
