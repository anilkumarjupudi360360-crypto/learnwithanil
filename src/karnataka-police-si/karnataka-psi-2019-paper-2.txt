Q 1 | geo | World Geography
What is the similarity between Milwaukee Deep, Java Trench and Challenger Deep?
(1) They are the deepest points of the Atlantic, Indian and Pacific Oceans, respectively
(2) They are all trenches in the Pacific Ocean
(3) They are all trenches in the Indian Ocean
(4) They are all deeps of the Atlantic Ocean
= 1
~ Each of the three features is the deepest point of a different ocean, in the order given. Milwaukee Deep (about 8,400 m) lies in the Puerto Rico Trench north of Puerto Rico and is the deepest point of the Atlantic Ocean. The Java Trench, also called the Sunda Trench (about 7,300 m), runs south of Sumatra and Java and is the deepest part of the Indian Ocean. Challenger Deep (about 10,900 m) lies at the southern end of the Mariana Trench near Guam and is the deepest known point on Earth, in the Pacific Ocean. So option (1) is correct. Options (2), (3) and (4) are wrong because each puts all three features in a single ocean, while they are actually spread over three oceans. Exam tip: remember the pairs Atlantic – Puerto Rico Trench (Milwaukee Deep), Indian – Java/Sunda Trench, Pacific – Mariana Trench (Challenger Deep).

Q 2 | geo | World Geography
A submarine mountain or peak rising more than 1000 m above the ocean floor is known as
(1) Guyots
(2) Seamounts
(3) Abyssal hills
(4) Trenches
= 2
~ A seamount is an isolated submarine mountain, usually of volcanic origin, that rises more than about 1,000 m above the surrounding ocean floor but does not reach the sea surface. Hence option (2) is correct. Guyots are a special kind of seamount with a flat top, formed when wave erosion planed off the summit of a volcano that once stood near sea level and later subsided; the definition in the question (simply a peak rising over 1,000 m) describes seamounts in general, not specifically flat-topped ones. Abyssal hills are much smaller rises, usually a few hundred metres high, scattered over the deep ocean plains. Trenches are not elevations at all; they are long, narrow and very deep depressions found along subduction zones. Exam tip: seamount = pointed submarine peak; guyot = flat-topped seamount; abyssal hill = small hill on the abyssal plain; trench = deepest depression of the ocean floor.

Q 3 | geo | World Geography
Which of the following is a warm current?
(1) Oya Shio current
(2) Kuro Shio current
(3) Okhotsk current
(4) Peru current
= 2
~ The Kuroshio (Kuro Shio, 'black current' in Japanese) is a warm ocean current that flows north-eastward from the Philippines along the east coast of Taiwan and Japan in the North Pacific; it is the Pacific counterpart of the Gulf Stream and keeps southern Japan's climate mild. So option (2) is correct. The Oyashio is a cold current flowing south from the Bering Sea along the Kuril Islands and north-eastern Japan; where it meets the Kuroshio, rich fishing grounds develop. The Okhotsk current is also cold, flowing from the Sea of Okhotsk. The Peru (Humboldt) current is a cold current flowing north along the west coast of South America, causing the Atacama desert and supporting the Peruvian anchovy fishery. Exam tip: as a rule, currents flowing from the equator towards the poles are warm and those flowing from the poles towards the equator are cold.

Q 5 | cur | International & Sports
The International team of scientists has recently cracked the genetic code of which cultivated crop for the first time?
(1) Wheat
(2) Rice
(3) Maize
(4) Pulses
= 1
~ In August 2018, the International Wheat Genome Sequencing Consortium (IWGSC), involving about 200 scientists from 20 countries including India, published in the journal Science the first fully annotated reference genome of bread wheat (variety Chinese Spring). So option (1) is correct. The wheat genome was considered especially hard to decode because it is huge (about 16 billion base pairs, roughly five times the human genome) and hexaploid, with three sub-genomes. Indian scientists decoded chromosome 2A as part of the effort. Rice was sequenced much earlier (draft genomes in 2002 and the finished genome in 2005), and maize's reference genome was published in 2009, so they were not 'cracked for the first time' recently; 'pulses' is a whole group of crops, not one crop. Exam tip: the wheat genome is expected to help breed higher-yielding, climate-resilient and disease-resistant varieties.

Q 6 | pol | Judiciary & Constitutional Bodies
Which one of the following is the guardian of Fundamental Rights?
(1) Legislature
(2) Executive
(3) Political Parties
(4) Judiciary
= 4
~ The judiciary, headed by the Supreme Court, is called the guardian and protector of Fundamental Rights. Under Article 32 a person can move the Supreme Court directly, and under Article 226 the High Courts, to enforce these rights through writs such as habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari and quo warranto. Through judicial review (Article 13) the courts can also declare any law or executive action that violates Fundamental Rights void. So option (4) is correct. The legislature makes laws and the executive implements them; they are the organs most likely to be checked by the courts when they infringe rights, so they cannot be the guardian. Political parties are not constitutional organs at all and have no power to enforce rights. Exam tip: the Supreme Court is described as the 'guardian of the Constitution' and the 'protector of Fundamental Rights'; Article 32 itself is a Fundamental Right.

Q 7 | pol | Union & State Government
The first General Election in Independent India was held in
(1) 1947
(2) 1950
(3) 1951
(4) 1948
= 3
~ India's first general election to the Lok Sabha began on 25 October 1951 and was completed in February 1952, so the year usually given is 1951 (or 1951–52). Option (3) is correct. The election was conducted by the Election Commission headed by the first Chief Election Commissioner, Sukumar Sen, with about 17 crore eligible voters under universal adult franchise; the Indian National Congress won a large majority and Jawaharlal Nehru became the first elected Prime Minister. The first polling took place in Chini tehsil of Himachal Pradesh. 1947 is the year of independence, 1948 had no national election because the Constitution was still being drafted, and 1950 is the year the Constitution came into force (26 January) and the Election Commission was set up (25 January). Exam tip: the first Lok Sabha was constituted in April 1952 with G. V. Mavalankar as its first Speaker.

Q 8 | pol | Constitution & Rights
Under which Article of the Constitution can an individual move the Supreme Court directly in case of any violation of Fundamental Rights?
(1) Article 31
(2) Article 32
(3) Article 28
(4) Article 29
= 2
~ Article 32 (Right to Constitutional Remedies) guarantees the right to move the Supreme Court directly for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights, and empowers the Court to issue writs such as habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari and quo warranto. Dr B. R. Ambedkar called Article 32 the 'heart and soul' of the Constitution. So option (2) is correct. Article 31 dealt with the right to property; it was repealed by the 44th Amendment (1978), and property is now a legal right under Article 300A. Article 28 concerns freedom from religious instruction in certain educational institutions, and Article 29 protects the language, script and culture of minorities. Exam tip: Article 32 lets a person go straight to the Supreme Court, while Article 226 lets High Courts issue writs; the High Court's writ power is wider because it also covers legal rights.

Q 9 | cur | National
Kisan Diwas is celebrated on which date in India?
(1) December 24
(2) December 21
(3) December 22
(4) December 23
= 4
~ Kisan Diwas (National Farmers' Day) is observed on 23 December every year to mark the birth anniversary of Chaudhary Charan Singh, the fifth Prime Minister of India (1979–80), who championed farmers' causes and land reforms. The Government of India declared the day in 2001. So option (4) is correct. The other dates belong to other observances: 24 December is National Consumer Day, 22 December is National Mathematics Day (birth anniversary of Srinivasa Ramanujan), and 21 December is not a national farmers' observance. Charan Singh's memorial, Kisan Ghat, is in New Delhi, and he also served as Deputy Prime Minister and Union Home Minister. Exam tip: December has many 'days' – 22 Mathematics, 23 Kisan, 24 Consumer – so learn them as a sequence.

Q 11 | cur | National
Which telecom company has launched a VOIP based phone service "WINGS"?
(1) Airtel
(2) BSNL
(3) Reliance Jio
(4) Vodafone
= 2
~ In July 2018, the state-owned Bharat Sanchar Nigam Limited (BSNL) launched 'Wings', India's first internet telephony (VoIP) service, after the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI) and the Department of Telecommunications allowed licensed telecom operators to offer it. Using the Wings app, a subscriber can make calls to any mobile or landline number in India over Wi-Fi or any operator's mobile data, without a SIM card, using a virtual number. So option (2) is correct. Airtel, Reliance Jio and Vodafone are private operators; Jio, for instance, offered its own 'Jio Call' app, but the service named Wings belongs to BSNL. VoIP stands for Voice over Internet Protocol, which converts voice into data packets sent over the internet. Exam tip: BSNL was formed on 1 October 2000 and is headquartered in New Delhi.

Q 12 | cur | Awards, Books & Persons
Which of the following is not a work of late V. S. Naipaul, the Trinidad-born Indian origin Nobel prize winning author who passed away recently?
(1) A Bend in the River
(2) Magic Seeds
(3) A House for Mr. Biswas
(4) The Remains of the Day
= 4
~ The Remains of the Day (1989) was written by Kazuo Ishiguro, the Japanese-born British novelist who won the Booker Prize for it and the Nobel Prize in Literature in 2017, so it is not Naipaul's work, making option (4) the answer. Sir V. S. Naipaul (1932–2018), born in Trinidad to a family of Indian origin, died in London on 11 August 2018. He won the Booker Prize in 1971 for In a Free State and the Nobel Prize in Literature in 2001. A House for Mr. Biswas (1961) is his best-known novel, based on his father's life; A Bend in the River (1979) is set in an African country after independence; and Magic Seeds (2004) was his last novel. His India books include An Area of Darkness, India: A Wounded Civilization and India: A Million Mutinies Now. Exam tip: 'not a work of' questions usually slip in a famous book by another prize-winning author.

Q 13 | geo | Environment & Ecology
What is the current number of India's Biosphere reserves included in the World Network of Biosphere Reserves?
(1) 8
(2) 9
(3) 10
(4) 11
= 4
~ In July 2018, UNESCO's Man and the Biosphere (MAB) programme added the Khangchendzonga Biosphere Reserve in Sikkim to the World Network of Biosphere Reserves, raising India's count to 11 out of its 18 biosphere reserves at that time. So option (4) is correct. The earlier ten were: Nilgiri (the first, in 2000), Gulf of Mannar, Sundarbans, Nanda Devi, Nokrek, Pachmarhi, Similipal, Achanakmar–Amarkantak, Great Nicobar and Agasthyamala. Options 8, 9 and 10 represent older counts before the latest additions (Agasthyamala was added in 2016). Karnataka is part of the Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve, which spreads across Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and Kerala. Exam tip: the MAB programme was launched by UNESCO in 1971; India's first biosphere reserve (Nilgiri) was notified in 1986. Later additions have raised the count further, so always check the year of the question.

Q 14 | pol | Laws, Police & Society
The Karnataka Police Flag Day is celebrated on which date?
(1) 1st November
(2) 2nd April
(3) 26th July
(4) 23rd October
= 2
~ The Karnataka State Police observe Police Flag Day on 2 April every year. The day marks the coming into force of the Karnataka Police Act, 1963 on 2 April 1965, which gave the state a single, uniform police law after the unification of Karnataka. On this day, the police flag is unfurled, a parade is held and sale of police flags is used to raise funds for the welfare of police personnel and their families. So option (2) is correct. 1 November is Karnataka Rajyotsava (formation of the state in 1956), 26 July is Kargil Vijay Diwas, and 23 October is not linked to police observances (Police Commemoration Day, honouring martyrs, is on 21 October). Exam tip: for the PSI exam, remember both 2 April (Karnataka Police Flag Day) and 21 October (Police Commemoration Day, all-India).

Q 15 | pol | Laws, Police & Society
The Union Government has constituted which high-level committee to suggest laws against mob lynching and violence?
(1) A. K. Dhasmana Committee
(2) Rajiv Gauba Committee
(3) Bhaskar Khulbe Committee
(4) Inderjit Singh Committee
= 2
~ In July 2018, after the Supreme Court's judgment in Tehseen S. Poonawalla v. Union of India (17 July 2018) asked Parliament to make a separate law against mob lynching, the Union Government set up a high-level committee headed by Union Home Secretary Rajiv Gauba to suggest ways and a legal framework to deal with mob violence and lynching. Its report was to be examined by a Group of Ministers headed by Home Minister Rajnath Singh. So option (2) is correct. A. K. Dhasmana was then chief of the Research and Analysis Wing (RAW); Bhaskar Khulbe was a senior officer in the Prime Minister's Office; and Inderjit Singh is not linked to such a committee. The Supreme Court also issued preventive, remedial and punitive guidelines, such as appointing a nodal officer in each district. Exam tip: later, the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 made mob lynching a specific offence.

Q 16 | cur | National
The first draft of the National Register of Citizens (NRC) was released for which State?
(1) Nagaland
(2) Assam
(3) Punjab
(4) Kerala
= 2
~ A National Register of Citizens was first prepared after the 1951 Census, but Assam is the only state where it has been updated. Under Supreme Court supervision the Assam NRC was revised to identify genuine Indian citizens and illegal migrants, using 24 March 1971 as the cut-off date, as agreed in the Assam Accord of 1985. The first (partial) draft of the updated NRC was released at midnight on 31 December 2017 with about 1.9 crore names out of 3.29 crore applicants; the complete draft came out on 30 July 2018, leaving out about 40 lakh people. So option (2) is correct. Nagaland, Punjab and Kerala have no such register. The updating was coordinated by NRC State Coordinator Prateek Hajela. Exam tip: the final NRC was published on 31 August 2019, excluding about 19 lakh applicants.

Q 18 | geo | World Geography
The cities of 'Hiroshima' and 'Nagasaki' are located on which among the following Islands of the Japanese Archipelago?
(1) Honshu and Kyushu respectively
(2) Honshu and Shikoku respectively
(3) Hokkaido and Kyushu respectively
(4) Kyushu and Shikoku respectively
= 1
~ Hiroshima lies in the south-western part of Honshu, the largest and main island of Japan, on the Seto Inland Sea. Nagasaki is a port city on the north-western coast of Kyushu, the southernmost of the four main islands. So option (1) is correct. Japan's four main islands from north to south are Hokkaido, Honshu, Shikoku and Kyushu. Shikoku (option 2 and 4) is the smallest of the four and has neither city, and Hokkaido (option 3) is the northernmost island, with Sapporo as its main city. The two cities are remembered for the atomic bombings by the USA at the end of the Second World War: Hiroshima on 6 August 1945 ('Little Boy') and Nagasaki on 9 August 1945 ('Fat Man'). Exam tip: Tokyo, Osaka, Kyoto and Hiroshima are on Honshu; Nagasaki and Fukuoka are on Kyushu.

Q 19 | his | Karnataka History
Who among the following had built beautiful temples at Pattadakallu and Aihole?
(1) Chalukya
(2) Hoysala
(3) Shatavahana
(4) Rashtrakuta
= 1
~ The Chalukyas of Badami (Vatapi), who ruled from the 6th to the 8th century CE, built the famous temples at Aihole, Badami and Pattadakal in present-day Bagalkot district of Karnataka. Aihole is called the 'cradle of Indian temple architecture' (Lad Khan and Durga temples), and Pattadakal has temples in both Nagara and Dravida styles; the Virupaksha temple there was built by Queen Lokamahadevi to celebrate Vikramaditya II's victory over the Pallavas. Pattadakal is a UNESCO World Heritage Site (1987). So option (1) is correct. The Hoysalas built the temples at Belur, Halebidu and Somanathapura (12th–13th centuries); the Rashtrakutas are famous for the Kailasa temple at Ellora (although the Jain temple at Pattadakal is attributed to them); the Satavahanas ruled much earlier and are linked to Buddhist stupas and caves. Exam tip: Aihole inscription of Pulakeshin II was composed by Ravikirti.

Q 20 | his | Karnataka History
Among the Kalyani Chalukyas, the title of Jagadekamalla was held by
(1) Jayasimha II
(2) Vijayaditya VII
(3) Vijayaditya II
(4) Someshwara I
= 1
~ Jayasimha II (ruled c. 1015–1042 CE) of the Western Chalukyas of Kalyani bore the title 'Jagadekamalla' (unique wrestler of the world); he is often called Jagadekamalla I. He fought against the Cholas under Rajendra Chola and the Paramaras of Malwa, and patronised Kannada scholars. So option (1) is correct. Someshwara I, his son, founded the capital Kalyani and was known by titles such as Ahavamalla and Trailokyamalla, not Jagadekamalla. Vijayaditya II and Vijayaditya VII were rulers of the Eastern Chalukyas of Vengi (Andhra), not of the Kalyani line. A later Kalyani king, Jagadekamalla II (1138–1151), also used the title. Exam tip: Kalyani Chalukya titles end in 'malla': Tailapa II – Ahavamalla, Jayasimha II – Jagadekamalla, Someshwara I – Ahavamalla/Trailokyamalla, Vikramaditya VI – Tribhuvanamalla.

Who received the Golden Ball Award for best player at the 2018 FIFA World Cup?

Q 22 | sci | Science & Technology
Which of the following missiles of India is a "supersonic cruise missile"?
(1) Agni-V
(2) BrahMos
(3) Prithvi-III
(4) Aakash
= 2
~ BrahMos is a supersonic cruise missile developed by BrahMos Aerospace, a joint venture of India's DRDO and Russia's NPO Mashinostroyeniya; its name comes from the Brahmaputra and Moskva rivers. It flies at about Mach 2.8–3 and can be launched from land, ships, submarines and aircraft. So option (2) is correct. Agni-V is a long-range (over 5,000 km) intercontinental-class ballistic missile, not a cruise missile. Prithvi-III is a short-range ballistic missile (naval version Dhanush). Aakash is a medium-range surface-to-air missile used for air defence. A cruise missile flies within the atmosphere with jet propulsion along a guided path, while a ballistic missile is rocket-powered and follows a high arching trajectory. Exam tip: Agni and Prithvi – ballistic; BrahMos – supersonic cruise; Nirbhay – subsonic cruise; Akash and Trishul – surface-to-air; Nag – anti-tank.

Q 23 | cur | Awards, Books & Persons
Which novel has won the 2018 Golden Man Booker Prize?
(1) In a Free State
(2) The English Patient
(3) Wolf Hall
(4) Moon Tiger
= 2
~ In July 2018, to mark the 50th anniversary of the Booker Prize, a one-off Golden Man Booker Prize was awarded to the best Booker-winning novel of the previous five decades. The winner, chosen by a public vote, was The English Patient (1992) by Sri Lankan-born Canadian writer Michael Ondaatje. So option (2) is correct. The other options were on the five-book shortlist, one from each decade: In a Free State by V. S. Naipaul (1971), Moon Tiger by Penelope Lively (1987), Wolf Hall by Hilary Mantel (2009), and Lincoln in the Bardo by George Saunders (2017). The novel was famously filmed in 1996 and won nine Oscars. Earlier special prizes, the Booker of Bookers (1993) and the Best of the Booker (2008), both went to Salman Rushdie's Midnight's Children. Exam tip: remember Golden Man Booker (2018) – Ondaatje.

Q 24 | sci | Science & Technology
The Nag missile has been developed by which Indian organisation?
(1) DRDO
(2) BARC
(3) ISRO
(4) All of the above
= 1
~ Nag is a third-generation, 'fire-and-forget' anti-tank guided missile developed by the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) under the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (IGMDP), launched in 1983 under Dr A. P. J. Abdul Kalam. The other IGMDP missiles were Prithvi, Agni, Trishul and Akash. So option (1) is correct. BARC (Bhabha Atomic Research Centre, Mumbai) is India's premier nuclear research centre and does not build missiles; ISRO (Indian Space Research Organisation, Bengaluru) develops launch vehicles and satellites for peaceful space use. Since only DRDO developed Nag, 'All of the above' is also wrong. Nag uses an imaging infrared seeker and has a range of about 4 km; its carrier vehicle is NAMICA, and the helicopter-launched version is called Helina. Exam tip: DRDO was formed in 1958 and works under the Ministry of Defence.

Q 25 | pol | Constitution & Rights
Which Amendment to the Constitution inserted a new Article 21 A providing Right to Education in the Constitution?
(1) 86th Amendment
(2) 87th Amendment
(3) 88th Amendment
(4) 89th Amendment
= 1
~ The Constitution (86th Amendment) Act, 2002 inserted Article 21A, which makes free and compulsory education for all children of 6 to 14 years a Fundamental Right. The same amendment changed Article 45 (the Directive Principle now covers early childhood care and education below 6 years) and added Article 51A(k), a Fundamental Duty of parents or guardians to provide opportunities for education to their children of 6–14 years. So option (1) is correct. To give effect to Article 21A, Parliament passed the Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education (RTE) Act, 2009, which came into force on 1 April 2010. The 87th Amendment (2003) dealt with readjustment of constituencies on the 2001 census, the 88th (2003) with service tax (Article 268A), and the 89th (2003) created a separate National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (Article 338A). Exam tip: 86th Amendment – 21A, 45 and 51A(k) together.

Q 26 | cur | National
National Commission for Women has partnered with __________ to launch a Digital literacy program to train women on safe use of internet.
(1) Facebook
(2) Google
(3) Amazon
(4) Microsoft
= 1
~ In June 2018 the National Commission for Women (NCW) partnered with Facebook, along with the Cyber Peace Foundation, to launch a digital literacy programme that trains women on the safe use of the internet. The programme aimed to reach about 60,000 women, mainly in universities across several states, covering topics such as online privacy settings, recognising fake profiles and phishing, reporting cyber harassment and protecting personal data. Google, Amazon and Microsoft run their own digital-skills initiatives in India (for example Google's 'Internet Saathi' with Tata Trusts for rural women), but the NCW tie-up mentioned in the news of 2018 was with Facebook, so options (2), (3) and (4) are wrong. NCW itself is a statutory body set up in January 1992 under the National Commission for Women Act, 1990, to safeguard and promote women's rights. Exam tip: remember the pairs 'NCW + Facebook = digital literacy for women (2018)' and 'Google + Tata Trusts = Internet Saathi'.

Q 27 | cur | Awards, Books & Persons
The United Nations Environment Program (UNEP) has recognised __________ airport as the World's first fully solar energy powered airport.
(1) Jaipur
(2) Bengaluru (KIAL)
(3) Cochin (CIAL)
(4) Mangaluru (MIAL)
= 3
~ Cochin International Airport Limited (CIAL) in Kerala became the world's first airport to run fully on solar power in August 2015, using a large solar plant built near its cargo complex that produces more electricity than the airport consumes. In September 2018 UNEP recognised this achievement by giving CIAL the 'Champions of the Earth' award, the UN's highest environmental honour, in the Entrepreneurial Vision category. CIAL is also notable as India's first airport built under a public-private partnership model with investment from thousands of non-resident Indians. Bengaluru's Kempegowda International Airport (KIAL), Mangaluru (MIAL) and Jaipur use some solar power, but none was declared the world's first fully solar-powered airport, so options (1), (2) and (4) are wrong. Exam tip: 'Kochi – CIAL – fully solar (2015) – UNEP Champions of the Earth (2018)' is a frequent current-affairs combination.

Q 28 | cur | National
Atal Innovation Mission is a Government of India's endeavour to promote a culture of innovation and is taken up under
(1) Ministry of Environment
(2) Ministry of Science and Technology
(3) NITI Ayog
(4) Ministry of Defence
= 3
~ The Atal Innovation Mission (AIM) was set up in 2016 under NITI Aayog, following the announcement in the Union Budget 2015-16, to promote a culture of innovation and entrepreneurship across the country. Its main programmes include Atal Tinkering Labs (ATLs) in schools, where students work with tools such as 3-D printers, robotics kits and electronics; Atal Incubation Centres (AICs) for start-ups in universities and industry; and later the Atal New India Challenges and Atal Community Innovation Centres. The Ministry of Science and Technology runs its own programmes through DST and DBT, and the Ministries of Environment and Defence have no role in AIM, so options (1), (2) and (4) are wrong. NITI Aayog itself replaced the Planning Commission on 1 January 2015 and is chaired by the Prime Minister. Exam tip: link AIM, Atal Tinkering Labs and the SDG India Index with NITI Aayog.

Q 29 | cur | Awards, Books & Persons

Q 32 | comp | Computer Fundamentals
BIOS stands for ?
(1) Basic Input Output System
(2) Binary Input Output System
(3) Basic Input Off System
(4) All the above
= 1
~ BIOS means Basic Input Output System. It is firmware stored on a ROM/flash chip on the computer's motherboard and is the first program that runs when a computer is switched on. It performs the POST (Power-On Self-Test) to check hardware such as RAM, keyboard and storage, initialises the devices, and then loads the operating system from the boot device through the boot loader; this process is called booting. BIOS settings are kept in CMOS memory powered by a small battery. Modern computers use UEFI (Unified Extensible Firmware Interface), a successor to BIOS, but the term BIOS is still widely used. Exam tip: remember the boot sequence: power on → BIOS → POST → boot loader → operating system.

Q 34 | sci | Physics
The reason for a mirage is
(1) Interference of light
(2) Diffraction of light
(3) Polarization of light
(4) Total internal reflection of light
= 4
~ A mirage is an optical illusion seen in deserts or on hot roads, where a distant object appears with an inverted image below it, as if reflected in a pool of water. On a hot day the air near the ground is hotter and optically rarer (lower refractive index) than the cooler, denser layers above. Light from a distant object bends away from the normal as it passes into rarer layers, and when the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle it undergoes total internal reflection and travels upward to the observer's eye, producing the inverted image. Interference explains the colours of soap bubbles and oil films, diffraction explains the bending of light around obstacles, and polarization is used in polaroid glasses, so options (1), (2) and (3) are wrong. Other examples of total internal reflection are the sparkle of diamonds and optical fibres. Exam tip: in cold regions the reverse effect, 'looming', shows objects floating above the horizon.

(2) Motor
(3) Generator
(4) Moving coil meter
= 1
~ A battery (an electrochemical cell or a group of cells) converts chemical energy into electrical energy through redox reactions between its electrodes and electrolyte. Q 41 | sci | Physics
Which instrument is used in a submarine to see objects above sea level ?
(1) Periscope
(2) Polygraph
(3) Photometer
(4) Pyrometer
= 1
~ A periscope is used in a submarine to view objects above the sea surface while the submarine stays submerged. Q 42 | sci | Physics
Acceleration due to gravity(g) is independent of
(1) Mass of the body
(2) Size
(3) Shape
(4) All of the above
= 4
~ The acceleration due to gravity is g = GM/R², where G is the universal gravitational constant, M is the mass of the Earth and R is the distance from the Earth's centre. The mass m of the falling body does not appear in this formula, because the gravitational force (GMm/R²) and the body's inertia (m) both increase in the same proportion and cancel. Hence, in the absence of air resistance, all bodies, whatever their mass, size or shape, fall with the same acceleration, about 9.8 m/s², as Galileo argued and as shown in a vacuum (a feather and a coin fall together). The value of g does depend on the Earth's mass and radius, on altitude, depth and latitude (more at the poles, less at the equator), but not on the falling body's properties. Since mass, size and shape all do not affect g, option (4) is correct. Exam tip: g is maximum at the poles and zero at the Earth's centre.

Q 43 | sci | Chemistry
Due to rusting the weight of iron
(1) Decreases
(2) Increases
(3) Remains the same
(4) Uncertain
= 2
~ Rusting is the slow oxidation of iron in the presence of both oxygen and water (moisture). Iron combines with oxygen and water to form hydrated iron(III) oxide, Fe₂O₃·xH₂O, the reddish-brown substance called rust. Because oxygen and water from the surroundings get chemically added to the iron, the mass of the rusted object increases, so option (2) is correct. Rust is flaky and porous, so it can fall off and expose fresh metal; but as a chemical change, rusting adds mass rather than reducing it, which makes options (1), (3) and (4) wrong. Rusting is prevented by painting, oiling or greasing, galvanisation (coating with zinc), tinning, alloying (stainless steel with chromium and nickel) and cathodic protection. Salt water speeds up rusting, which is why ships and coastal structures rust faster. Exam tip: rusting needs both air and moisture; iron in dry air or boiled (air-free) water does not rust.

(4) Nitrous oxide

Q 51 | sci | Chemistry
In which one of the following situations a chemical reaction does not occur?

(2) Coal is burnt in air
(3) Sodium is placed in water
(4) Iron is kept in moist air
= 1

Q 52 | apt | Reasoning
Directions (52-54): Study the following information carefully to answer the given questions:
Point D is 14 m towards the West of Point A. Point B is 4 m towards the South of point D. Point F is 9 m towards the South of Point D. Point E is 7 m towards the East of point B. Point C is 4 m towards the North of point E. Point G is 4 m towards the South of point A.

(2) E, G, C
(3) D, B, G
(4) E, G, B
= 4
~ Fix A at the origin (0, 0), with East as +x and North as +y. Then D = (–14, 0); B is 4 m south of D = (–14, –4); F is 9 m south of D = (–14, –9); E is 7 m east of B = (–7, –4); C is 4 m north of E = (–7, 0); G is 4 m south of A = (0, –4). Q 53 | apt | Reasoning
Directions (52-54): Point D is 14 m towards the West of Point A. Point B is 4 m towards the South of point D. Point F is 9 m towards the South of Point D. Point E is 7 m towards the East of point B. Point C is 4 m towards the North of point E. Point G is 4 m towards the South of point A.
In which direction is A with respect to C?
(1) East
(2) West
(3) North
(4) South
= 1
~ Take A as (0, 0). D is 14 m west of A, so D = (–14, 0). B is 4 m south of D = (–14, –4). E is 7 m east of B = (–7, –4). C is 4 m north of E = (–7, 0). Q 54 | apt | Reasoning
Directions (52-54): Point D is 14 m towards the West of Point A. Point B is 4 m towards the South of point D. Point F is 9 m towards the South of Point D. Point E is 7 m towards the East of point B. Point C is 4 m towards the North of point E. Point G is 4 m towards the South of point A.
A person walks 5 m towards North from point F and then takes a right turn, which of the following points would he reach first?
(1) G
(2) D
(3) E
(4) A
= 3
~ With A at (0, 0): D = (–14, 0), B = (–14, –4), F = (–14, –9), E = (–7, –4), G = (0, –4). F is 9 m south of D and B is 4 m south of D, so F is 5 m south of B. Walking 5 m north from F therefore brings the person exactly to B (–14, –4). A right turn while facing north means he now faces East and moves along the line y = –4. On this line the points ahead of him are E at 7 m and then G at 14 m, so the first point he reaches is E, option (3). (4) 8
= 4
~ Let her normal daily use be x litres. A full tank lasts 10 days, so the tank holds 10x litres. If she uses 25% more each day, her new daily use is 1.25x = (5/4)x litres. Number of days the tank will last = 10x ÷ (5/4)x = 10 × 4/5 = 8 days, option (4). Quick check: with a tank of 100 litres and 10 litres a day, the new use is 12.5 litres a day, and 100 ÷ 12.5 = 8. The other options come from common mistakes, for example subtracting 25% of 10 days (giving 7.5, close to 7) or halving the days (5); quantity and daily consumption are inversely proportional, so the days fall by the factor 4/5, i.e. by 20%, not by 25%. Exam tip: if consumption rises by r%, the duration falls by r/(100 + r) × 100%; here 25/125 × 100 = 20%, and 20% of 10 days is 2 days, leaving 8.

(2) Original jurisdiction
(3) Advisory jurisdiction
(4) Writ jurisdiction
= 2

Q 59 | pol | Constitution & Rights
Which of the following writs literally means "We Command"?

(2) Mandamus
(3) Quo-Warranto
(4) Certiorari
= 2
~ Mandamus is a Latin word meaning 'we command'. Q 60 | apt | Reasoning
Directions (60-61): The following questions are based on the five, three letter words given below:
TUB HUB MUG PAR FOR
If the positions of the first and second letters of all the words are interchanged, how many words will form meaningful English words?
(1) None
(2) Three
(3) One
(4) Two
= 1
~ Interchange the first and second letters of each word: TUB becomes UTB, HUB becomes UHB, MUG becomes UMG, PAR becomes APR and FOR becomes OFR. None of these is a meaningful English word (APR is only an abbreviation for April or 'annual percentage rate', not a word). So the answer is None, option (1). Options (2), (3) and (4) would require at least one valid word to be formed, which does not happen. Note that the last letter is untouched in this operation, and none of the five new forms (UTB, UHB, UMG, APR, OFR) is listed as a word in an English dictionary. Exam tip: in word-rearrangement questions, write all the new forms down in a row and test each one; abbreviations, proper nouns and words that are only sounds are normally not counted as meaningful English words.

Q 61 | apt | Reasoning
Directions (60-61): The following questions are based on the five, three letter words given below:
TUB HUB MUG PAR FOR
If all the letters in each of the words are arranged alphabetically (within the word), how many words will remain unchanged?
(1) Two
(2) Three
(3) One
(4) None
= 3
~ Arrange the letters of each word in alphabetical order: TUB (T, U, B) becomes BTU; HUB (H, U, B) becomes BHU; MUG (M, U, G) becomes GMU; PAR (P, A, R) becomes APR; FOR (F, O, R) stays FOR, because F comes before O and O comes before R in the alphabet. So only one word, FOR, remains unchanged, option (3). TUB, HUB, MUG and PAR all change because their letters are not already in alphabetical order (for example, in PAR the A should come before P). Options (1), (2) and (4) therefore do not fit. A quick way to check: a word stays unchanged only if its letters are already in increasing alphabetical order from left to right. Exam tip: write the alphabet position numbers (F = 6, O = 15, R = 18) under each letter; if the numbers increase left to right, the word is unchanged.

Q 66 | sci | Biology
Deficiency of "Vitamin D" leads to lack of absorption of
(1) Iodine
(2) Phosphorus
(3) Calcium
(4) Iron
= 3
~ Vitamin D (calciferol) is converted in the body into its active form, calcitriol, which increases the absorption of calcium from the intestine and helps deposit it in bones and teeth. When vitamin D is deficient, calcium absorption falls, leading to rickets in children (soft, bent bones) and osteomalacia in adults. So the expected answer is calcium, option (3). Q 67 | apt | Arithmetic
A person travelled a distance of 50 Km in 8 Hrs, he covered a part of a distance on foot at 4 Km/hr. and part on bicycle at 10 Km/hr. How much distance did he travel on foot?
(1) 10 Km
(2) 20 Km
(3) 30 Km
(4) 40 Km
= 2
~ Let the distance walked be x km. Then the distance cycled is (50 – x) km. Time = distance ÷ speed, so the total time is x/4 + (50 – x)/10 = 8. Multiply both sides by 20: 5x + 2(50 – x) = 160, so 5x + 100 – 2x = 160, giving 3x = 60 and x = 20 km. So he walked 20 km, option (2). Check: walking 20 km at 4 km/h takes 5 hours and cycling 30 km at 10 km/h takes 3 hours; 5 + 3 = 8 hours, as given. The other options fail the check: 10 km walking would take 2.5 + 4 = 6.5 hours, 30 km would take 7.5 + 2 = 9.5 hours, and 40 km would take 10 + 1 = 11 hours. Exam tip: in mixed-speed problems, option testing (time on foot + time on bicycle = total time) is often faster than forming the equation.

Q 68 | apt | Reasoning
In a meeting a map of a village was placed in such manner that South-East becomes North and North-East becomes West. What will South become?
(1) North
(2) North-East
(3) North-West
(4) West
= 2
~ Measure directions clockwise from North: N = 0°, NE = 45°, E = 90°, SE = 135°, S = 180°, W = 270°. South-East (135°) has become North (0°), so every direction has been turned 135° anticlockwise. Check with the second clue: North-East (45°) turned 135° anticlockwise gives 45° – 135° = –90°, i.e. 270°, which is West, as stated. Now South (180°) turned 135° anticlockwise gives 180° – 135° = 45°, which is North-East. So the answer is option (2). North would be the new name of South-East, not South; North-West (315°) would need a 225° turn; West is the new position of North-East. Exam tip: find the rotation angle from one given pair, verify it with the second pair, and then apply the same rotation to the asked direction; drawing a small compass rose and rotating it also works.

Q 69 | apt | Arithmetic
If Kiran, while selling two goats at the same price makes a profit of 10% on one goat and suffers a loss of 10% on the other
(1) He makes no profit and no loss
(2) He makes a profit of 1%
(3) He suffers a loss of 1%
(4) He suffers a loss of 2%
= 3
~ When two articles are sold at the same selling price, one at x% profit and the other at x% loss, the overall result is always a loss of x²/100 per cent. Here x = 10, so the loss = 10²/100 = 1%, option (3). Worked check: let each goat be sold for ₹99. The first goat's cost = 99 × 100/110 = ₹90 and the second goat's cost = 99 × 100/90 = ₹110. Total cost = ₹200 and total selling price = ₹198, so the loss is ₹2 on ₹200, i.e. 1%. It is not 'no profit no loss' because the loss is on a larger cost price (₹110) than the profit (on ₹90). A 1% profit or 2% loss does not match the calculation. Exam tip: equal SP with equal gain% and loss% always gives a loss of (x/10)² per cent; but if the cost prices are equal instead, there is no profit and no loss.

Q 70 | apt | Reasoning
Ramya is prettier than Rashmi but not as pretty as Rekha, then
(1) Rashmi is not as pretty as Ramya
(2) Rashmi is prettier than Rekha
(3) Rekha is not as pretty as Ramya
(4) Ramya is prettier than Rekha
= 1
~ Write the statements as an order. 'Ramya is prettier than Rashmi' gives Ramya > Rashmi. 'Ramya is not as pretty as Rekha' gives Rekha > Ramya. Combining: Rekha > Ramya > Rashmi. Q 80 | sci | Biology

(2) Oxalic Acid

= 4

Q 82 | his | Medieval India
The Grand Trunk Road was built during the reign of which ruler ?
(1) Shershah Suri
(2) Babar
(3) Shah Jahan
(4) Akbar
= 1
~ The Grand Trunk Road is credited to Sher Shah Suri (ruled 1540–1545), the Afghan ruler who defeated Humayun. He built sarais (rest houses) at regular intervals, planted shade trees and used the sarais as dak chowkis for a horse-post system. The British later improved it and named it the Grand Trunk Road. Babur spent his short reign (1526–1530) fighting wars; Akbar and Shah Jahan maintained and used the road but did not build it. Sher Shah is also known for introducing the silver rupiya and for his land-revenue reforms, which Akbar later followed. Exam tip: Sher Shah = Sadak-e-Azam (GT Road), rupiya coin and sarais.

Q 83 | his | Medieval India
The prime Minister in the Council of Ministers, in the Maratha Empire was called ?
(1) Peshwa
(2) Sachiv
(3) Mantri
(4) Samanta
= 1
~ Shivaji's council of eight ministers was called the Ashta Pradhan. Its head, the prime minister in charge of general administration, was the Peshwa (also called Mukhya Pradhan). The other posts were the Amatya or Mazumdar (finance), the Sachiv or Surnavis (royal correspondence), the Mantri or Waqia-navis (the king's personal affairs and records), the Senapati (army), the Sumant or Dabir (foreign affairs), the Nyayadhish (justice) and the Panditrao (religious affairs). So Sachiv, Mantri and Sumant (printed here as 'Samanta') were ministers, but not the prime minister. In the 18th century, from Balaji Vishwanath (1713) onwards, the Peshwas became the real rulers of the Maratha Empire from Pune, while the Chhatrapati at Satara was only a figurehead. Exam tip: Ashta Pradhan = 8 ministers; Peshwa = prime minister.

Q 84 | eco | Money, Banking & Budget
Devaluation of a currency means
(1) Reduction in the value of a currency vis-a-vis major internationally traded currencies
(2) Permitting the currency to seek its worth in the international market
(3) Fixing the value of the currency in conjunction with the movement in the value of a basket of pre-determined currencies
(4) Fixing the value of currency in multilateral consultation with the IMF, the World Bank and major trading partners
= 1
~ Devaluation is a deliberate, official reduction by the government or central bank in the value of the home currency in terms of foreign currencies (or gold) under a fixed or managed exchange-rate system. It makes exports cheaper and imports dearer, and is used to correct a balance-of-payments deficit. India devalued the rupee in June 1966 and again in two steps in July 1991. Option (2) describes a floating (market-determined) exchange rate, which leads to 'depreciation' rather than devaluation. Option (3) describes a basket peg, as used for the rupee from 1975 until the early 1990s. Option (4) describes a multilaterally negotiated fixed rate, which is not devaluation itself. Exam tip: devaluation and revaluation are policy decisions under fixed rates; depreciation and appreciation are market movements under floating rates.

Q 85 | eco | Money, Banking & Budget
The central banking functions in India are performed by the

II. Reserve Bank of India
III. State Bank of India
IV. Punjab National Bank
(1) I, II
(2) II
(3) I
(4) II, III
= 2
~ India's central bank is the Reserve Bank of India, set up on 1 April 1935 under the RBI Act, 1934 and nationalised on 1 January 1949. Q 91 | sci | Science & Technology

(2) Narora
(3) Rawat Bhata
(4) Tarapore
= 1

Q 92 | geo | Indian Geography
Which among the following river is an example of a superimposed drainage system ?
(1) Banas
(2) Chambal
(3) Saraswati
(4) Gomti
= 2
~ A superimposed (epigenetic) drainage forms when a river develops on a younger cover of rocks and, as it erodes downwards, keeps its course while cutting into the older, differently structured rocks beneath, so the river does not match the underlying structure. The Chambal, flowing across the Malwa plateau and cutting deep gorges and ravines through the Vindhyan rocks, is the textbook Indian example, often cited with the Damodar, Subarnarekha and Son. The Gomti is a Ganga-plain tributary with a normal dendritic pattern, and the Saraswati is a lost or seasonal river, so they do not fit. Note, however, that standard texts (e.g. Khullar, PMF IAS) list the Banas, a tributary of the Chambal, among the superimposed rivers as well, so option (1) is also defensible; the key takes the Chambal, the best-known example. Exam tip: when 'superimposed drainage' appears, think of the Chambal and the Damodar–Subarnarekha group on old plateaus.

Q 96 | his | Karnataka History
Which of the following is false with regards to the treaty of Mangalore of 1780-1784 ?

(2) The treaty was signed between Tippu Sulthan and the British
(3) As per the treaty British left from the Malabar and regained control over Carnatic
(4) The treaty was a moral and psychological victory to the British
= 4
~ The Second Anglo-Mysore War (1780–1784) was begun by Hyder Ali and continued by Tipu Sultan after Hyder's death in December 1782.
