# KSP PSI (Civil) 545 posts re-examination, Paper-2, held 23 January 2024, KEA booklet version A1 (DE2023GA, options printed A-D = 1-4); answers: KEA final key answers (1 March 2024)

Q 1 | cur | National
The operation to evacuate stranded Indian nationals during the Israel-Hamas conflict – 2023 is :
(1) Operation Vijay
(2) Operation Ajay
(3) Operation Ganga
(4) Operation Abhay
= 2
~ Operation Ajay was launched by the Government of India on 11 October 2023, a few days after Hamas attacked Israel on 7 October 2023, to bring back Indian nationals who wished to return from Israel. Special charter flights were operated from Tel Aviv, and the first flight carrying 212 Indians landed in Delhi on 13 October 2023, with the Ministry of External Affairs and the Indian Embassy in Israel coordinating the effort. Operation Vijay is the name of the 1999 Kargil operation (and of the 1961 liberation of Goa), not an evacuation from Israel. Operation Ganga (2022) brought Indian students and citizens out of war-hit Ukraine through neighbouring countries such as Romania, Hungary and Poland. Operation Abhay is not the name of this evacuation. Memory tip for evacuation operations: Rahat – Yemen (2015), Devi Shakti – Afghanistan (2021), Ganga – Ukraine (2022), Kaveri – Sudan (2023) and Ajay – Israel (2023). Questions on such rescue operations appear regularly in KSP and KEA papers, so keep a small table of operation name, country and year.

Q 2 | his | Medieval India
Identify the dynasty to which Queen Rudramadevi belonged :
(1) Chalukyas of Badami
(2) Pandyas of Madurai
(3) Kakatiyas of Warangal
(4) Eastern Chalukyas of Vengi
= 3
~ Rudramadevi (ruled c. 1262–1289) was one of the very few women rulers of medieval South India and belonged to the Kakatiya dynasty, whose capital was Orugallu (Warangal) in present-day Telangana. Her father Ganapatideva named her as his successor, and she ruled under the title 'Rudradeva Maharaja'. The Venetian traveller Marco Polo, who visited the Kakatiya port of Motupalli, praised her rule. She was succeeded by her grandson Prataparudra II, in whose time the Delhi Sultanate conquered Warangal (1323). The Chalukyas of Badami (famous for Pulakeshin II) ruled from Karnataka in the 6th–8th centuries, the Pandyas ruled from Madurai in Tamil Nadu, and the Eastern Chalukyas ruled the Vengi region of coastal Andhra; Rudramadevi belonged to none of them. Karnataka link: the Kakatiyas began as feudatories of the Chalukyas of Kalyana (Basavakalyan, Bidar district) before becoming independent. Remember also that the Kakatiya-era Ramappa Temple at Palampet became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2021.

Q 3 | his | Modern India & Freedom Struggle
Who wrote the book 'Economic History of India under Early British Rule' ?
(1) Dadabhai Naoroji
(2) R.C. Dutt
(3) Charles Wood
(4) M.N. Roy
= 2
~ Romesh Chunder Dutt (R.C. Dutt), a retired ICS officer and economic historian, wrote 'The Economic History of India under Early British Rule' (1902) and its sequel 'The Economic History of India in the Victorian Age' (1904). Using official data, he showed how heavy land revenue, the decline of Indian handicrafts and the drain of wealth to Britain impoverished India. He also presided over the Lucknow session of the Indian National Congress in 1899. Dadabhai Naoroji, the 'Grand Old Man of India', is linked with the drain theory and wrote 'Poverty and Un-British Rule in India' (1901), not this book. Sir Charles Wood is remembered for Wood's Despatch (1854), called the Magna Carta of English education in India. M.N. Roy was a revolutionary who helped found the Communist Party of India at Tashkent (1920) and later formed the Radical Democratic Party. Memory tip: Naoroji – 'Poverty', Dutt – 'Economic History'; together they are the chief economic critics of British rule among the early nationalists.

Q 4 | his | Modern India & Freedom Struggle
Which of the following is the correct chronological order of Viceroys ? Choose from the codes given below :
(i) Lord Northbrook
(ii) Lord Minto
(iii) Lord Linlithgow
(iv) Lord Mayo
Codes :
(1) (iv), (i), (ii), (iii)
(2) (ii), (iii), (i), (iv)
(3) (i), (ii), (iii), (iv)
(4) (iii), (iv), (ii), (i)
= 1
~ Arrange the Viceroys by their tenure. Lord Mayo (1869–72) comes first; he began financial decentralisation, the first (non-synchronous) census was attempted in his time in 1872, Mayo College at Ajmer is named after him, and he was assassinated at Port Blair in 1872. Lord Northbrook (1872–76) succeeded him. Lord Minto II (1905–10) comes next; the Morley–Minto Reforms (Indian Councils Act, 1909) introduced separate electorates for Muslims. Lord Linlithgow (1936–43) is last; he was the longest-serving Viceroy, and the August Offer (1940), the Cripps Mission (1942) and the Quit India Movement (1942) took place in his time. So the order is (iv) Mayo, (i) Northbrook, (ii) Minto, (iii) Linlithgow, which is option (A). Option (C) simply repeats the list numbering, while (B) and (D) mix up the 19th- and 20th-century Viceroys. Note: the title 'Viceroy' began with Lord Canning in 1858, so Lord Minto I (1807–13) was only a Governor-General; 'Viceroy Minto' here means Minto II.

Q 5 | geo | Indian Geography
The largest producer of Sugarcane in South India is
(1) Karnataka
(2) Tamilnadu
(3) Kerala
(4) Telangana
= 1
~ Karnataka is the largest sugarcane-producing state in South India and the third largest in the country, after Uttar Pradesh (first) and Maharashtra (second). Black and red soils, irrigation from the Krishna and Kaveri river systems and a long growing season support high output. Belagavi district leads the state in cane area and number of sugar factories, and Bagalkot, Vijayapura and Mandya are other major cane districts; Mandya, home of the Mysore Sugar Company (1933), is popularly called 'Sakkare Nadu' (land of sugar). Tamil Nadu also grows sugarcane with good yields, but its total production is lower than Karnataka's. Kerala grows very little cane, and Telangana is a small producer. Exam facts: sugarcane is a tropical crop needing about 21–27°C temperature and 75–150 cm rainfall, and the Centre fixes the Fair and Remunerative Price (FRP) of cane on the recommendation of the Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP). India is the world's second largest sugarcane producer after Brazil.

Q 6 | geo | Indian Geography
The first modern silk textile factory in India was started by East India Company at
(1) Ramanagar
(2) Chennai
(3) Mumbai
(4) Howrah
= 4
~ In the colonial period Bengal was the East India Company's chief source of raw silk, with Company silk-reeling centres around Kasimbazar in Murshidabad. Standard Indian geography references record that the modern silk textile industry in India began when the East India Company set up a silk factory at Howrah, near Calcutta (Kolkata), during Company rule; hence Howrah is the answer. Chennai and Mumbai became centres of the modern cotton textile industry (the first successful cotton mill was set up in Bombay in 1854 by C.N. Davar), not of the first silk factory. Ramanagara is the tempting option for Karnataka students: it is famous as the 'Silk City' with one of Asia's largest cocoon markets, but it was not the site of the Company's first factory. Karnataka facts worth remembering: Tipu Sultan encouraged sericulture in Mysore in the 18th century, the Mysore silk weaving factory (now KSIC) was started in 1912 under Krishnaraja Wadiyar IV, Mysore Silk has a GI tag, and Karnataka is India's leading producer of mulberry silk.

Q 7 | geo | Environment & Ecology
What is/are unique about 'Kharai Camel', a breed found in India ?
(i) It is capable of swimming upto three kilometers in sea water.
(ii) It survives by grazing on mangroves.
(iii) It lives in the wild and cannot be domesticated.
Select the correct answer using the codes given below :
(1) (i) and (ii)
(2) only (iii)
(3) (i) and (iii)
(4) (i), (ii) and (iii)
= 1
~ The Kharai camel is a special breed of the coastal Kutch region of Gujarat, also found in Jamnagar and Devbhumi Dwarka districts. 'Kharai' means saline, and the camel is adapted to salty coastal land. Statement (i) is correct: it can swim up to about 3 km through shallow sea water to reach mangrove islands, so locals call it 'Dariyataru' (sea swimmer). Statement (ii) is correct: unlike other camels it depends on mangroves for fodder, browsing on mangrove leaves and saline plants and often staying on the islands for several days during the monsoon. Statement (iii) is wrong: Kharai camels are domesticated animals, owned and herded for generations by the pastoral Rabari and Fakirani Jat communities. Hence the answer is (i) and (ii). Exam facts: the National Bureau of Animal Genetic Resources (NBAGR), Karnal, recognised it as a distinct camel breed around 2015, and the breed is threatened by the loss of mangroves to industries and salt pans. Memory tip: Kharai = salty coast + swimming + mangroves.

Q 8 | his | Medieval India
Who is the founder of the Nizam Shahi dynasty of Ahmadnagar ?
(1) Abdul Muzaffar
(2) Malik Ahmed
(3) Muhammad Shah
(4) Ibrahim Shah
= 2
~ The Nizam Shahi dynasty was founded by Malik Ahmed (Ahmad Nizam Shah I), son of the Bahmani minister Nizam-ul-Mulk Malik Hasan Bahri. As governor of Junnar, he declared independence from the weakening Bahmani Sultanate in 1490 and founded the city of Ahmadnagar in 1494, which became his capital. The dynasty is famous for Chand Bibi, who defended Ahmadnagar against the Mughals in 1595; the kingdom was finally absorbed by the Mughals under Shah Jahan in 1636. Muhammad Shah was the name of several Bahmani sultans, and Ibrahim Shah was borne by later Adil Shahi and Qutb Shahi rulers; neither founded the Nizam Shahi line, and Abdul Muzaffar is a distractor. Karnataka angle: the Bahmani kingdom broke into five Deccan Sultanates – Ahmadnagar (Nizam Shahi, Malik Ahmed), Bijapur (Adil Shahi, Yusuf Adil Shah), Berar (Imad Shahi), Golconda (Qutb Shahi) and Bidar (Barid Shahi). Two of these capitals, Bijapur (Vijayapura) and Bidar, lie in present-day Karnataka, which makes this a favourite topic in KSP papers.

Q 9 | cur | Awards, Books & Persons
In May 2023 which of the following Booker Prize winning authors received the “Royal Companion of Honour Award” ?
(1) Salman Rushdie
(2) Arundathi Roy
(3) Aravind Adiga
(4) Yann Martel
= 1
~ Sir Salman Rushdie was named a Companion of Honour (CH) for services to literature in the 2022 Birthday Honours, and he formally received the honour at a royal investiture in May 2023, which is the event this question refers to. This came about nine months after the knife attack on him in New York State in August 2022. The Order of the Companions of Honour, founded in 1917, is a British honour for outstanding achievement in the arts, science, medicine or public service and is limited to 65 members at a time. Rushdie won the Booker Prize in 1981 for 'Midnight's Children', which was later chosen as the 'Booker of Bookers' (1993) and the 'Best of the Booker' (2008). The other options are also Booker winners but did not receive this honour: Arundhati Roy ('The God of Small Things', 1997), Aravind Adiga ('The White Tiger', 2008; he grew up in Mangaluru, Karnataka) and Yann Martel ('Life of Pi', 2002).

Q 10 | geo | Indian Geography
India's first multi-modal terminal on river Ganga inaugurated in November 2018 is in
(1) Varanasi
(2) Allahabad
(3) Kolkata
(4) Patna
= 1
~ Prime Minister Narendra Modi inaugurated India's first multi-modal terminal on the river Ganga at Varanasi (Uttar Pradesh) on 12 November 2018, and on the same day received the country's first container cargo vessel on an inland waterway, which had sailed from Kolkata. A multi-modal terminal links water, road and rail transport at one point. It was built by the Inland Waterways Authority of India (IWAI) under the World Bank-assisted Jal Marg Vikas Project on National Waterway-1 (Ganga–Bhagirathi–Hooghly system, Haldia to Prayagraj, about 1,620 km), India's longest national waterway. Allahabad (renamed Prayagraj in 2018) is the upstream end of NW-1, Kolkata is the port city near its lower end, and Patna also lies on this waterway, but none of them got the first multi-modal terminal. The other multi-modal terminals of the project are at Sahibganj (Jharkhand) and Haldia (West Bengal). Remember: NW-1 – Ganga, NW-2 – Brahmaputra, NW-3 – West Coast Canal in Kerala.

Q 11 | cur | International & Sports
Total number of medals won by the Indians in Special Olympics World Games – 2023 is
(1) 198
(2) 200
(3) 202
(4) 204
= 3
~ At the Special Olympics World Summer Games held in Berlin, Germany, from 17 to 25 June 2023, the Indian contingent won a total of 202 medals – 76 gold, 75 silver and 51 bronze (check: 76 + 75 + 51 = 202). The figures 198, 200 and 204 are close-number distractors meant to test exact recall. Special Olympics is different from the Paralympics: it is a sports movement for persons with intellectual disabilities, started by Eunice Kennedy Shriver in the USA in 1968, and it stresses participation and inclusion, grouping athletes into ability-based divisions. The Paralympics, by contrast, are for athletes with physical, visual and intellectual impairments and are held after the Olympics in the same host city. Exam tip: remember the 2023 tally as '76-75-51 = 202', and do not confuse it with India's tally of 107 medals at the 2023 Asian Games in Hangzhou, China.

Q 12 | cur | Awards, Books & Persons
Who is the recipient of “Award for Global Leadership-2023” given by Harvard Law School ?
(1) D.Y. Chandrachud
(2) Fali Nariman
(3) N.V. Ramana
(4) U.U. Lalit
= 1
~ Chief Justice of India D.Y. Chandrachud was honoured with the Award for Global Leadership by the Center on the Legal Profession at Harvard Law School, and the paper refers to it as the 2023 award. As the name suggests, it recognises leadership in the legal profession at the global level. Justice Chandrachud is himself a Harvard Law School alumnus, having earned his LL.M. and doctorate (SJD) there. He served as the 50th Chief Justice of India from 9 November 2022 to 10 November 2024 and is known for landmark judgments on the right to privacy (Puttaswamy, 2017) and the decriminalisation of homosexuality (Navtej Johar, 2018). Exam tip: remember the sequence 48 Ramana – 49 Lalit – 50 Chandrachud – 51 Sanjiv Khanna. Also note that his father, Y.V. Chandrachud (16th CJI), is the longest-serving Chief Justice of India.

Q 13 | cur | Awards, Books & Persons
The only Indian till date to receive the “Honorary Academy Award” is
(1) A.R. Rahman
(2) Bhanu Athaiya
(3) Satyajit Ray
(4) Resul Pookutty
= 3
~ Satyajit Ray is the only Indian to receive an Honorary Academy Award (Honorary Oscar). It was presented at the 64th Academy Awards in March 1992 for his 'rare mastery of the art of motion pictures'; as Ray was seriously ill, he accepted it by video from his hospital bed in Calcutta, and he died a few weeks later in April 1992. The same year he received the Bharat Ratna. His 'Apu Trilogy' (Pather Panchali, Aparajito, Apur Sansar) made Indian cinema famous worldwide. The other three won competitive Oscars, not honorary ones: Bhanu Athaiya became the first Indian Oscar winner, for Best Costume Design for 'Gandhi' (1983), while A.R. Rahman (two awards, for original score and the song 'Jai Ho') and Resul Pookutty (sound mixing) won in 2009 for 'Slumdog Millionaire'. Memory tip: honorary Oscar – only Ray; first competitive Oscar – Bhanu Athaiya. Ray also received the Dadasaheb Phalke Award for 1984.

Q 14 | cur | International & Sports
Which is the 31st member country of NATO, a Western Alliance ?
(1) Iceland
(2) Greenland
(3) Finland
(4) Ireland
= 3
~ Finland became the 31st member of the North Atlantic Treaty Organization (NATO) on 4 April 2023, the alliance's 74th foundation day, giving up its long-standing military non-alignment after Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022. Finland shares a border of about 1,340 km with Russia, so its entry roughly doubled NATO's land border with Russia. Iceland is a founding member of NATO (1949), even though it has no standing army. Greenland is an autonomous territory of Denmark and is covered through Denmark's membership; it is not a separate member. Ireland is a militarily neutral country and is not a member of NATO. Update: Sweden joined as the 32nd member on 7 March 2024, after this exam. Key NATO facts: founded by the Washington Treaty on 4 April 1949 with 12 members; headquarters in Brussels, Belgium; Article 5 says an attack on one member is an attack on all, and it has been invoked only once, after the 9/11 attacks in 2001.

Q 15 | cur | International & Sports
Which of the following group of four countries are members of G20 ?
(1) Argentina, Mexico, South Africa, Turkey
(2) Australia, Canada, Malaysia, New Zealand
(3) Brazil, Iran, Saudi Arabia, Vietnam
(4) Indonesia, Japan, Singapore, South Korea
= 1
~ All four countries in option (A) – Argentina, Mexico, South Africa and Turkey (Türkiye) – are members of the G20. The G20 has 19 member countries plus the European Union, and the African Union became a permanent member at the New Delhi Summit held under India's presidency on 9–10 September 2023. The 19 countries are Argentina, Australia, Brazil, Canada, China, France, Germany, India, Indonesia, Italy, Japan, Mexico, Russia, Saudi Arabia, South Africa, South Korea, Turkey, the United Kingdom and the United States. Each other option contains non-members: Malaysia and New Zealand in (B), Iran and Vietnam in (C), and Singapore in (D), although Singapore is often invited as a guest country. The G20 began in 1999, after the Asian financial crisis, as a forum of finance ministers and central bank governors and was upgraded to a leaders' summit in 2008. India's G20 theme was 'Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam – One Earth, One Family, One Future'. Tip: eliminate each option by spotting one non-member.

Q 16 | geo | Environment & Ecology
International Tiger Day is observed on __________.
(1) 19th July
(2) 29th July
(3) 09th July
(4) 25th July
= 2
~ International Tiger Day (Global Tiger Day) is observed every year on 29 July. It was agreed upon at the St. Petersburg Tiger Summit in Russia in 2010, where the 13 tiger-range countries adopted the goal of doubling the number of wild tigers by 2022 (the 'TX2' goal). The other dates have no link with tigers. India is home to about three-quarters of the world's wild tigers. Project Tiger was launched in 1973, and its 50 years were marked in April 2023 at Mysuru, where a minimum count of 3,167 tigers for 2022 was released; the detailed report released on 29 July 2023 put the estimate at about 3,682. In that report Madhya Pradesh had the most tigers (785), followed by Karnataka (563) and Uttarakhand (560). Karnataka angle: Bandipur was among the first nine tiger reserves of 1973, and Nagarahole, Bhadra, Dandeli-Anshi (Kali) and Biligiri Ranganathaswamy Temple (BRT) are the state's other tiger reserves. The National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA) is a statutory body under the Wildlife (Protection) Amendment Act, 2006.

Q 17 | his | Karnataka History
The Rastrakuta King who defeated the Pratihara ruler Nagabhatta-II was :
(1) Dhruva
(2) Krishna
(3) Govinda-III
(4) Govinda-I
= 3
~ Govinda III (c. 793–814 CE), son of Dhruva, was the greatest warrior among the Rashtrakuta kings. During the tripartite struggle for Kannauj among the Rashtrakutas, the Gurjara-Pratiharas and the Palas, he marched into North India and defeated the Pratihara king Nagabhata II (around 800 CE); the Pala king Dharmapala and his nominee at Kannauj, Chakrayudha, also submitted to him, and his campaign is said to have reached the Himalayan region. His father Dhruva Dharavarsha had earlier defeated Nagabhata's father Vatsaraja and Dharmapala, which is why Dhruva is the common wrong choice. Krishna I built the rock-cut Kailasa temple at Ellora, and Govinda I was an early ancestor of the line, before the dynasty's rise under Dantidurga. Karnataka angle: the Rashtrakutas ruled from Manyakheta (Malkhed, Kalaburagi district); Govinda III's son Amoghavarsha I Nripatunga patronised Kannada, and 'Kavirajamarga', the earliest available Kannada work on poetics, was written in his court. Memory tip: Dhruva beat Vatsaraja; Govinda III beat Nagabhata II.

Q 18 | geo | Environment & Ecology
Match the List – I with List – II and choose the appropriate option :
List – I / List – II
i. National Institute of Animal Welfare – a. Kolkata
ii. Zoological Survey of India – b. New Delhi
iii. Wildlife Institute of India – c. Faridabad
iv. Wildlife Crime Control Bureau – d. Dehradun
Codes :
(1) i-d, ii-c, iii-b, iv-a
(2) i-c, ii-a, iii-d, iv-b
(3) i-a, ii-d, iii-c, iv-b
(4) i-b, ii-d, iii-a, iv-c
= 2
~ The correct matching is i-c, ii-a, iii-d, iv-b, that is option (B). The National Institute of Animal Welfare (NIAW) is located at Ballabgarh in Faridabad district, Haryana, and trains officials and volunteers in animal welfare and laws such as the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, 1960. The Zoological Survey of India (ZSI), founded in 1916, is headquartered in Kolkata and surveys and documents India's animal life. The Wildlife Institute of India (WII), set up in 1982, is in Dehradun and trains forest officers and conducts wildlife research; Dehradun also houses the Forest Research Institute and the Forest Survey of India. The Wildlife Crime Control Bureau (WCCB), a statutory body constituted in 2007 under the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972, is headquartered in New Delhi and fights organised poaching and illegal wildlife trade. Option (A) wrongly places NIAW in Dehradun, (C) places ZSI in Dehradun and (D) places WII in Kolkata. Police tip: WCCB works with state police, forest and customs departments, so remember it along with the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972.

Q 19 | sci | Biology
Match the following List – I with List – II and choose matches/combinations :
List – I / List – II
i. Odontology – a. Study of Bones
ii. Ornithology – b. Study of Teeth
iii. Osteology – c. Study of Snakes
iv. Ophiology – d. Study of Birds
Codes :
(1) i-c, ii-b, iii-d, iv-a
(2) i-b, ii-d, iii-a, iv-c
(3) i-d, ii-a, iii-b, iv-c
(4) i-a, ii-c, iii-b, iv-d
= 2
~ The matching is i-b, ii-d, iii-a, iv-c, which is option (B). The names come from Greek roots: 'odont' means tooth, so odontology is the study of teeth; 'ornis' means bird, so ornithology is the study of birds; 'osteon' means bone, so osteology is the study of bones; and 'ophis' means snake, so ophiology (also called serpentology) is the study of snakes. Option (A) swaps teeth and snakes, (C) gives birds to odontology, and (D) gives bones to odontology, so all three are wrong. Police angle: forensic odontology (identifying persons from teeth and bite marks) and forensic osteology (estimating age and sex from skeletal remains) are routinely used in crime investigation and disaster victim identification. Related terms worth remembering: herpetology – reptiles and amphibians, ichthyology – fishes, entomology – insects. Karnataka link: Salim Ali, the 'Birdman of India', is the best-known Indian ornithologist; Ranganathittu in Mandya district is a famous bird sanctuary, and Agumbe in Shivamogga district is known for king cobra research.

Q 20 | his | Modern India & Freedom Struggle
The last Governor General of India is
(1) Lord Mountbatten
(2) C. Rajagopalachari
(3) Dr. Rajendra Prasad
(4) Lord Wavell
= 2
~ C. Rajagopalachari (Rajaji) was the last Governor-General of India and also the only Indian to hold that office. He succeeded Lord Mountbatten on 21 June 1948 and served until 26 January 1950, when the Constitution came into force, India became a Republic and the office of Governor-General was abolished. Lord Mountbatten was the last Viceroy of British India and the first Governor-General of independent India (15 August 1947 to June 1948). Dr. Rajendra Prasad, President of the Constituent Assembly, became the first President of India on 26 January 1950; he replaced the Governor-General as head of state but never held that post. Lord Wavell was Viceroy from 1943 to 1947 and convened the Shimla Conference (1945). Memory tip: first Governor-General of Bengal – Warren Hastings; first Governor-General of India – William Bentinck (1833); first Viceroy – Canning (1858); first Governor-General of free India – Mountbatten; last and only Indian Governor-General – Rajaji. Rajaji was also among the first three Bharat Ratna awardees in 1954.

Q 21 | cur | International & Sports
The G7 2023 Summit was held at the following venue :
(1) Osaka
(2) Nagasaki
(3) Tokyo
(4) Hiroshima
= 4
~ The 49th G7 Summit was held at Hiroshima, Japan, from 19 to 21 May 2023 under Japan's presidency, hosted by Prime Minister Fumio Kishida, whose home constituency is Hiroshima. Q 22 | pol | Laws, Police & Society
Which of the following drugs is used in Narco Test ?
(1) Potassium Hydroxide
(2) Sodium Pentothal
(3) Methanol
(4) Sodium Carbonate
= 2
~ In a narco-analysis (narco) test, a controlled dose of sodium pentothal (thiopental sodium), a short-acting barbiturate anaesthetic, is injected so that the subject enters a semi-conscious, hypnotic state in which inhibitions are lowered; it is popularly called a 'truth serum'. The test is done in a hospital under the supervision of an anaesthetist, a psychologist and forensic experts. Potassium hydroxide (caustic potash) is a strong alkali used in soaps, methanol is a poisonous alcohol behind many hooch tragedies, and sodium carbonate is washing soda; none of them is used in narco tests. Important for police aspirants: in Selvi v. State of Karnataka (2010), the Supreme Court held that narco-analysis, polygraph and brain-mapping (BEAP) tests cannot be conducted without the person's free consent, because forced tests violate Article 20(3) (protection against self-incrimination) and Article 21. Even with consent, the statements are not admissible as evidence by themselves, though facts discovered later with their help may be used. The case arose from Karnataka, so it is a favourite KSP question.

Q 23 | cur | International & Sports
Recently COP 28 Summit was held in __________.
(1) Singapore
(2) Dubai
(3) New York
(4) Paris
= 2
~ COP28, the 28th Conference of the Parties to the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC), was held at Expo City, Dubai, in the United Arab Emirates from 30 November to 13 December 2023, under the presidency of Sultan Al Jaber. Its outcome, called the 'UAE Consensus', concluded the first Global Stocktake of the Paris Agreement and, for the first time, called on countries to transition away from fossil fuels in energy systems. The Loss and Damage Fund for vulnerable countries was also operationalised on the opening day. Paris hosted COP21 in 2015, which produced the Paris Agreement to keep warming well below 2°C; New York hosts the UN headquarters but has never hosted a COP, and neither has Singapore. At COP28, Prime Minister Narendra Modi launched India's Green Credit initiative. Memory chain: COP26 Glasgow (2021), COP27 Sharm el-Sheikh (2022), COP28 Dubai (2023), COP29 Baku (2024). The UNFCCC itself was opened for signature at the 1992 Rio Earth Summit.

Q 24 | sci | Science & Technology
DRDO successfully Flight-tested the following Ballistic Missile in June 2023 :
(1) Agni-IV
(2) Agni-V
(3) Agni Prime
(4) Agni-VI

Q 43 | sci | Physics
The loss in the signal power as light travels down the fiber is called
(1) Attenuation
(2) Propagation
(3) Scattering
(4) Interruption
= 1
~ The gradual loss of signal power as light travels along an optical fibre is called attenuation, so option (A) is correct. It is measured in decibels per kilometre (dB/km); good silica fibres lose only about 0.2 dB/km near the 1550 nm wavelength, which is why fibre links can run long distances before amplifiers or repeaters are needed. Do not confuse attenuation with dispersion, which is the spreading of light pulses that limits bandwidth. Tip: optical fibres work on total internal reflection because the core has a higher refractive index than the cladding; Narinder Singh Kapany is called the father of fibre optics.

Q 45 | sci | Biology
The pacemaker of the human heart is
(1) Atrio-ventricular node
(2) Ventro-atrial node
(3) Sino-atrial node
(4) Purkinje fibres
= 3
~ The sino-atrial (SA) node is the natural pacemaker of the human heart, so option (C) is correct. It is a small patch of specialised nodal tissue in the upper part of the wall of the right atrium, near the opening of the superior vena cava. It generates the maximum number of action potentials, about 70–75 per minute, and therefore sets the heart rate. The impulse spreads over both atria, making them contract, and reaches the atrio-ventricular (AV) node, which delays it slightly so that the ventricles can fill; the AV node, option (A), is only a relay and acts as a backup pacemaker if the SA node fails. A 'ventro-atrial node', option (B), does not exist. Hence (A), (B) and (D) are wrong. Exam tip: the human heart is myogenic because its beat starts in its own muscle tissue; an artificial pacemaker is fitted when the SA node or conducting system fails.

Q 46 | sci | Biology
This part of a animal cell called as suicide bag is
(1) Lysosome
(2) Ribosome
(3) Mitochondria
(4) Centrosome
= 1
~ Lysosomes are called the 'suicide bags' of the cell, so option (A) is correct. They are small membrane-bound vesicles formed by packaging in the Golgi apparatus and filled with hydrolytic (digestive) enzymes such as lipases, proteases and nucleases, which work best in an acidic medium. They digest food particles, worn-out organelles and invading germs. When a cell is badly damaged or very old, the lysosome membrane may burst and its enzymes digest the cell itself (autolysis); hence the name 'suicide bag'. Lysosomes were discovered by the Belgian scientist Christian de Duve, who shared the 1974 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. Ribosomes are membrane-less sites of protein synthesis; mitochondria are the 'powerhouse of the cell' that produce ATP by cellular respiration; and the centrosome, with its two centrioles, helps form the spindle during animal cell division. Hence (B), (C) and (D) are wrong. Memory tip: lysosomes are found mainly in animal cells, while in plant cells the vacuole does much of this digestive work.

Q 57 | apt | Reasoning
How many times in a day are the hands of a clock pointing opposite to each other ?
(1) 20
(2) 04
(3) 24
(4) 22
= 4
~ Option (D) 22 is correct. The hands point in opposite directions (180° apart) when the minute hand is 30 minute-spaces ahead of or behind the hour hand. The minute hand gains 55 minute-spaces on the hour hand every 60 minutes, so in 12 hours it gains 660 spaces, and the hands are opposite 11 times, not 12. The reason is that the opposition between 5 and 6 o'clock and the one between 6 and 7 o'clock are the same event, at exactly 6:00. In a full day of 24 hours: 11 × 2 = 22 times. Option (C) 24 wrongly assumes one opposition every hour; options (A) 20 and (B) 04 have no basis. Memory chart for clock questions in a day: the hands coincide 22 times, are opposite 22 times (so they are in a straight line 44 times), and are at right angles 44 times. The time between two successive oppositions is 720/11 = 65 5/11 minutes, the same as the gap between two successive coincidences.

Q 65 | apt | Arithmetic
The average age of three sisters is 20 years and their ages are in the ratio 3 : 5 : 7. The age of the youngest sister is :
(1) 20
(2) 28
(3) 25
(4) 12
= 4
~ Option (D) 12 is correct. Step 1: the average age of the three sisters is 20 years, so the sum of their ages = 3 × 20 = 60 years. Step 2: the ages are in the ratio 3 : 5 : 7, so let them be 3x, 5x and 7x. Then 3x + 5x + 7x = 15x = 60, which gives x = 4. Step 3: the ages are 3 × 4 = 12, 5 × 4 = 20 and 7 × 4 = 28 years, so the youngest sister is 12 years old. Check: (12 + 20 + 28) ÷ 3 = 60 ÷ 3 = 20, which matches the given average. Q 66 | apt | Arithmetic
What will be the cost of gardening 1 metre broad boundary around a rectangular plot having perimeter of 340 meters at the rate of ₹ 10 per square meter ?
(1) ₹ 3,400
(2) ₹ 1,700
(3) ₹ 3,440
(4) ₹ 2,990
= 3
~ Option (C) ₹3,440 is correct. The 1-metre-wide garden strip runs all around the outside of the plot. Let the plot be l metres long and b metres wide, with 2(l + b) = 340, so l + b = 170. Q 67 | apt | Arithmetic
In how many ways can the letters of DELHI be arranged ?

(2) 60
(3) 120
(4) 24
= 3
~ Option (C) 120 is correct. DELHI has 5 letters, D, E, L, H and I, and all of them are different; no letter repeats. The number of arrangements of n distinct objects taken all at a time is n! Q 74 | apt | Arithmetic
Shubham walks 30 km at a speed of 6 km/h and remaining 40 km in 5 hours. His average speed for the whole journey is
(1) 7 km/hr.
(2) 6 km/hr.
(3) 5.5 km/hr.
(4) 6.5 km/hr.
= 1
~ Option (A) 7 km/hr is correct. Average speed = total distance ÷ total time; it is not simply the average of the speeds. Step 1: first part, 30 km at 6 km/h, so time = 30 ÷ 6 = 5 hours. Step 2: second part, 40 km in 5 hours (a speed of 8 km/h). Step 3: total distance = 30 + 40 = 70 km and total time = 5 + 5 = 10 hours. Step 4: average speed = 70 ÷ 10 = 7 km/h. Option (B) 6 km/hr is only the speed of the first part; options (C) 5.5 km/hr and (D) 6.5 km/hr come from wrong averaging of distances or times. Note that here both parts take the same time (5 hours each), so the average speed happens to equal the simple average of the two speeds: (6 + 8) ÷ 2 = 7 km/h. When equal distances are covered at speeds a and b, use the formula 2ab ÷ (a + b) instead. Tip: write the distance and time of each part in a small table before dividing; this prevents the most common mistake in speed questions.

Bharatanatyam (Tamil Nadu) is linked with Rukmini Devi Arundale and Karnataka's own Pratibha Prahlad; Kuchipudi (Andhra Pradesh) with Vempati Chinna Satyam and Raja–Radha Reddy; Kathakali (Kerala) with Kalamandalam Gopi. Odissi grew out of the temple (Mahari) tradition of Odisha and is known for the 'tribhanga' (three-bend) posture and the square 'chauka' stance. Other famous Odissi dancers are Sonal Mansingh, Sanjukta Panigrahi and Protima Gauri Bedi, who founded Nrityagram, the dance village near Bengaluru – a Karnataka connection worth remembering. Memory tip: 'Madhavi – Mohapatra – Odisha'.

Q 95 | sci | Science & Technology
The first spaceship sent to explore planet Mars was :
(1) Venera-I
(2) Mariner Pole
(3) Mars Orbiter Mission
(4) Voyager-II
= 2
~ The key gives option (B), which refers to NASA's Mariner programme. Mariner 4, launched in November 1964, made the first successful flyby of Mars on 14–15 July 1965 and sent back the first close-up photographs of another planet, showing a cratered, Moon-like surface. Mariner 9 (1971) later became the first spacecraft to orbit Mars. Strictly, the Soviet Union had sent earlier Mars probes from 1960 onwards, such as Mars 1 in 1962, but they failed. Venera-1 (1961) was a Soviet probe sent towards Venus, not Mars. India's Mars Orbiter Mission (Mangalyaan), launched by ISRO on 5 November 2013 from Sriharikota, entered Mars orbit on 24 September 2014; India became the first country to succeed in its first attempt and the first Asian nation to reach Mars orbit, but it was not the first spacecraft to Mars. Voyager-2 (1977) explored Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune. Karnataka link: Mangalyaan was built at the ISRO Satellite Centre (now URSC) and tracked from ISTRAC, both in Bengaluru. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 96 | sci | Science & Technology
Chandrayan-3 was launched on :
(1) August 23, 2023
(2) August 14, 2023
(3) July 14, 2023
(4) July 23, 2023
= 3
~ Chandrayaan-3 was launched on 14 July 2023 at 2:35 pm from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre, Sriharikota, on ISRO's LVM3-M4 rocket. So option (C) is correct. August 23, 2023 (option A) is the trap: that is the date on which the Vikram lander soft-landed near the lunar south pole, making India the fourth country to soft-land on the Moon and the first to land near its south pole. The Government declared 23 August as 'National Space Day', and the landing site was named 'Shiv Shakti Point'. August 14 and July 23, 2023 are not the launch date. The mission had a propulsion module, the Vikram lander and the Pragyan rover; Pragyan confirmed the presence of sulphur on the lunar surface. Karnataka connection: ISRO is headquartered in Bengaluru, the spacecraft was built at the U R Rao Satellite Centre (URSC) in Bengaluru, the mission was controlled from ISTRAC in Bengaluru, and the Indian Deep Space Network antennas are at Byalalu near Bengaluru. ISRO's chairman during the mission was S. Somanath. Memory tip: 'Launch 14 July, Land 23 August'.

Q 97 | eco | Money, Banking & Budget
The head office of the State Bank of India is located in :
(1) Delhi
(2) Bengaluru
(3) Mumbai
(4) Kolkata
= 3
~ The State Bank of India (SBI) has its corporate centre (head office) at State Bank Bhavan, Madame Cama Road, Nariman Point, Mumbai. So option (C) is correct. Delhi, Bengaluru and Kolkata have SBI local head offices or circle offices, not the central head office. SBI traces its origin to the Bank of Calcutta (1806), later the Bank of Bengal; the three Presidency banks of Bengal, Bombay and Madras merged in 1921 to form the Imperial Bank of India, which was nationalised as the State Bank of India on 1 July 1955 on the recommendation of the A.D. Q 98 | geo | Indian Geography
In India which one of the following has the largest inland saline wet land ?
(1) Gujarat
(2) Haryana
(3) Madhya Pradesh
(4) Rajasthan
= 4
~ Sambhar Lake in Rajasthan is India's largest inland saline wetland (salt lake). It lies about 80 km south-west of Jaipur, spread over Jaipur, Ajmer and Nagaur districts, and covers roughly 190–230 sq km depending on the season. It was declared a Ramsar site in 1990. So option (D) Rajasthan is correct. Gujarat's Rann of Kutch is a vast seasonal salt marsh, but the largest inland saline lake-wetland is Sambhar; Haryana and Madhya Pradesh have no saline wetland of this scale. Sambhar is fed mainly by the Mendha and Rupangarh rivers and is a major source of common salt, produced by Sambhar Salts Ltd, a joint venture of Hindustan Salts and the Rajasthan government. It is a wintering ground for flamingos and other migratory birds; in 2019 thousands of birds died there due to avian botulism. Related facts: Chilika in Odisha is India's largest brackish-water lagoon, and Wular in Jammu and Kashmir is the largest freshwater lake. Karnataka angle: Ranganathittu Bird Sanctuary near Srirangapatna became Karnataka's first Ramsar site in 2022.

Q 99 | geo | Indian Geography
Specially designed platform for extract oil in Mumbai High is ?
(1) Samudraditya
(2) Sindhughosh
(3) Sagar Samrat
(4) Navantia
= 3
~ Sagar Samrat is ONGC's famous jack-up drilling rig, specially built for drilling oil wells at sea. It was built at Mitsubishi's yard in Japan, commissioned in 1973, and in 1974 drilled ONGC's first offshore well in Bombay High (Mumbai High) in the Arabian Sea off the Mumbai coast, opening up India's largest oil field. So option (C) is correct. After decades of drilling it was converted into a Mobile Offshore Production Unit (MOPU) and brought back into service as a production platform. Sindhughosh is the name of a class of Indian Navy submarines, Navantia is a Spanish state-owned shipbuilding company, and Samudraditya is not the Mumbai High platform. Mumbai High lies on the continental shelf, where oil is trapped in sedimentary rocks. Karnataka link: Mangalore Refinery and Petrochemicals Ltd (MRPL), an ONGC subsidiary, is the State's oil refinery, and India's strategic petroleum reserves are located at Mangaluru and Padur (Udupi) besides Visakhapatnam.

Q 100 | his | Art & Culture
One of the popular plays of Vijay Tendulkar is :
(1) Sita Swayamvar
(2) Sakharam Binder
(3) Navanna
(4) Bahuroopi
= 2
~ Vijay Tendulkar (1928–2008) was one of India's greatest Marathi playwrights. His play 'Sakharam Binder' (1972), printed in the booklet as 'Shakkaram Binder', is about a book-binder who keeps abandoned women in his house; its bold treatment of sex and violence led to a long censorship battle. So option (B) is correct. His other famous plays are 'Shantata! Court Chalu Aahe' (Silence! The Court Is in Session), 'Ghashiram Kotwal', 'Gidhade' (The Vultures) and 'Kanyadaan'. He also wrote screenplays for films such as 'Manthan', 'Aakrosh' and 'Ardh Satya', and received the Padma Bhushan in 1984. 'Sita Swayamvar' (1843) was written by Vishnudas Bhave and is regarded as the first modern Marathi play. 'Navanna' is a Bengali play by Bijon Bhattacharya on the Bengal famine, staged by IPTA in 1944. 'Bahurupee' is the famous Bengali theatre group founded by Sombhu Mitra. Karnataka link: 'Bahuroopi' is also the name of Rangayana Mysuru's annual national theatre festival, and Kannada's Girish Karnad, along with Tendulkar, Badal Sircar and Mohan Rakesh, shaped modern Indian theatre.
