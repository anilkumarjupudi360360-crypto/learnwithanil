# KSP PSI (Civil) 402 posts, Paper-2 General Studies, held 3 October 2024, KEA booklet version A1 (PD2209P224); answers: KEA final key of 6 November 2024

Q 1 | apt | Reasoning
Which of the given interchanges in signs would make the given equation 10 – 2 + 9 × 2 ÷ 4 = 19 correct?
(1) – and ÷
(2) – and +
(3) ÷ and ×
(4) × and ÷
= 1
~ Interchange the signs – and ÷ in the equation: 10 – 2 + 9 × 2 ÷ 4 becomes 10 ÷ 2 + 9 × 2 – 4. Apply BODMAS: division and multiplication first, so 10 ÷ 2 = 5 and 9 × 2 = 18; then 5 + 18 – 4 = 19, which equals the right-hand side. So option (1) is correct. Check the others: interchanging – and + gives 10 + 2 – 9 × 2 ÷ 4 = 12 – 4.5 = 7.5, not 19. Interchanging ÷ and × (options 3 and 4 describe the same swap) gives 10 – 2 + 9 ÷ 2 × 4 = 8 + 18 = 26, again not 19. Exam tip: in sign-interchange questions, never simplify the original expression first; rewrite it after each swap and apply BODMAS strictly (division and multiplication left to right, then addition and subtraction left to right). Testing the options one by one is usually the fastest method, and this question type appears regularly in the mental ability part of KSP PSI and constable papers.

Q 2 | apt | Series & Puzzles
In the following series, one number is incorrect. Find out the incorrect number.
25, 32, 64, 71, 74, 291, 1746
(1) 291
(2) 1746
(3) 74
(4) 32
= 3
~ The series uses two alternating steps: add 7, then multiply by an increasing even number (2, 4, 6). 25 + 7 = 32; 32 × 2 = 64; 64 + 7 = 71; 71 × 4 = 284; 284 + 7 = 291; 291 × 6 = 1746. So the fifth term should be 284, not 74, which makes 74 the incorrect number, option (3). The other options fit the rule perfectly: 32 comes from 25 + 7, 291 comes from 284 + 7, and 1746 is exactly 291 × 6, so none of them can be the wrong term. A quick check that helps here: 1746 ÷ 291 = 6 exactly and 291 – 7 = 284, which points straight at the faulty middle term. Tip for wrong-number series: large jumps usually reveal multiplication and small, equal gaps reveal addition; test the pattern from both ends of the series, and the term that breaks the rule from both sides is the answer.

Q 3 | apt | Arithmetic
Out of total 150 students, 45 passed in Management, 50 in Accounts, 30 in Costing, 30 in both Management and Accounts, 32 in both Accounts and Costing, 35 in both Management and Costing and 25 students passed in all the three subjects. Find the number of students who passed at least in any one of the subjects.
(1) 53
(2) 63
(3) 43
(4) 73
= 1
~ Use the inclusion–exclusion formula for three sets: n(M ∪ A ∪ C) = n(M) + n(A) + n(C) – n(M∩A) – n(A∩C) – n(M∩C) + n(M∩A∩C). Substituting the values: 45 + 50 + 30 – 30 – 32 – 35 + 25 = 125 – 97 + 25 = 53. So 53 students passed in at least one subject, option (1). The total of 150 is not needed for this part; it would only be used to find the students who failed in all three subjects (150 – 53 = 97). The other values, 63, 43 and 73, do not match the formula result. Note that the figures given are not fully consistent (the 'Costing only' region works out to a negative number), so the question can only be solved mechanically with the formula, which is what KEA's key follows. Memory tip for three-set problems: add the singles, subtract the pairs, add back the triple. If the question asks for 'none', subtract this union from the total.

Q 4 | apt | Arithmetic
A person walks on an escalator at the rate of 4 steps per second and reaches the other end in 15 seconds, while coming back walking at the same speed he reaches the starting point in 45 seconds. What is the number of steps on the escalator?
(1) 60
(2) 90
(3) 72
(4) 45
= 2
~ Let the escalator have N steps and move at e steps per second. Going in the direction of the escalator, the man's effective speed is (4 + e), so N = (4 + e) × 15. Coming back against the escalator, his effective speed is (4 – e), so N = (4 – e) × 45. Equating: 60 + 15e = 180 – 45e, so 60e = 120 and e = 2 steps per second. Then N = (4 + 2) × 15 = 90; check: (4 – 2) × 45 = 90. Hence the escalator has 90 steps, option (2). Option 60 is only the number of steps the man himself takes in 15 seconds (4 × 15), ignoring the escalator's movement; 72 and 45 do not satisfy both equations. Tip: escalator problems are upstream–downstream (boat and stream) problems in disguise: moving with the escalator, the speeds add; moving against it, they subtract, while the number of steps on the escalator stays the same in both directions.

Q 5 | apt | Arithmetic
If the price of a ticket for a show is increased by 14%, then the number of audience are reduced by 22%. What is the effect on revenue?
(1) Decrease by 10.18%
(2) Increase by 10.18%
(3) Decrease by 11.08%
(4) Increase by 11.08%
= 3
~ Revenue = ticket price × number of viewers. Take the original price and audience as 100 each, so the original revenue is 100 × 100 = 10,000. New price = 114 and new audience = 78, so the new revenue = 114 × 78 = 8,892. Change = 8,892 – 10,000 = –1,108, i.e. a fall of 1,108 on 10,000, which is 11.08%. Using the successive-percentage formula: x + y + xy/100 = 14 + (–22) + (14 × –22)/100 = –8 – 3.08 = –11.08%. The negative sign means a decrease, so option (3) is correct. Option (4) has the right figure but the wrong direction, and 10.18% in options (1) and (2) is a digit-swap trap. Tip: whenever price and quantity change together (ticket sales, bus fares, fuel consumption), either multiply the factors (1.14 × 0.78 = 0.8892) or use x + y + xy/100, remembering to put a minus sign before every decrease.

Q 7 | apt | Arithmetic
The average of 10 numbers is 40. Later it was found that by mistake the numbers 18 and 21 were misread as 81 and 12. Find the correct average of 10 numbers.
(1) 63.6
(2) 34.6
(3) 43.6
(4) 24.6
= 2
~ Wrong total = 10 × 40 = 400. This total wrongly contains 81 and 12 (sum 93) in place of the true numbers 18 and 21 (sum 39). Correct total = 400 – 93 + 39 = 346. Correct average = 346 ÷ 10 = 34.6, option (2). Shortcut: the total was too high by 93 – 39 = 54, so the average was inflated by 54 ÷ 10 = 5.4; correct average = 40 – 5.4 = 34.6. The other options do not match this calculation: 43.6 would need the total to rise rather than fall, and 63.6 and 24.6 are far from 40, which is impossible when only two of ten numbers change by a combined 54. Tip: in 'misread' average questions, find the net difference between the wrongly read values and the correct values, divide it by the number of items, and adjust the old average up or down. This method takes a few seconds and avoids recomputing the whole total.

Q 8 | apt | Arithmetic
Out of 15 applicants for a job, there are 5 women and 10 men. It is desired to select 2 persons for the job. The probability that at least one of the selected persons will be a woman is _____
(1) 4/7
(2) 3/7
(3) 1/5
(4) 4/5
= 1
~ Total ways of choosing 2 people from 15 = 15C2 = (15 × 14)/2 = 105. 'At least one woman' is the complement of 'no woman', that is, both selected persons are men. Ways of choosing 2 men from 10 = 10C2 = (10 × 9)/2 = 45. So P(no woman) = 45/105 = 3/7, and P(at least one woman) = 1 – 3/7 = 4/7, option (1). Direct check: exactly one woman = 5 × 10 = 50 ways and two women = 5C2 = 10 ways; (50 + 10)/105 = 60/105 = 4/7. Option 3/7 is the probability of the opposite event (both men), a common trap for students who stop halfway. The values 1/5 and 4/5 do not come from any correct counting of the cases. Tip: whenever a probability question says 'at least one', calculate 1 – P(none); it is quicker and reduces mistakes, especially under the time pressure of the PSI exam.

Q 9 | apt | Arithmetic
An examination paper contains 9 questions of which 5 have 3 possible answers each, 3 have 2 possible answers each and the remaining one question has 5 possible answers. The total number of possible answers to all the questions is _____
(1) 9820
(2) 9610
(3) 9720
(4) 8140
= 3
~ By the fundamental (multiplication) principle of counting, when independent choices are made one after another, the numbers of choices are multiplied. Each of the 5 three-choice questions can be answered in 3 ways, giving 3^5 = 243. Each of the 3 two-choice questions can be answered in 2 ways, giving 2^3 = 8. The last question can be answered in 5 ways. Total = 243 × 8 × 5 = 243 × 40 = 9,720, option (3). The figures 9,820, 9,610 and 8,140 do not equal this product. A candidate who adds instead of multiplying would get only 5 × 3 + 3 × 2 + 5 = 26, which shows why the multiplication rule matters. The count assumes that every question is answered with exactly one of its given choices (leaving a question unanswered is not counted as a possibility). Memory tip: 'and' (doing one task after another) means multiply, while 'or' (choosing one of several alternatives) means add. This one rule solves most counting questions in police recruitment papers.

Q 10 | apt | Arithmetic
If cube root of 300763 is 67, then the value of ∛300.763 + ∛0.300763 + ∛0.000300763 is
(1) 67.007
(2) 6.347
(3) 71.437
(4) 7.437
= 4
~ Given ∛300763 = 67 (indeed 67 × 67 × 67 = 300,763). For a cube root, every 3 decimal places in the number become 1 decimal place in the root. 300.763 has 3 decimal places, so ∛300.763 = 6.7. 0.300763 has 6 decimal places, so ∛0.300763 = 0.67. 0.000300763 has 9 decimal places, so ∛0.000300763 = 0.067. Sum = 6.7 + 0.67 + 0.067 = 7.437, option (4). Option 6.347 has similar digits but is not the sum; 67.007 and 71.437 come from misplacing the decimal point, for example taking ∛300.763 as 67 instead of 6.7. Memory tip: square roots group decimal places in pairs, cube roots group them in threes; count the decimal places, divide by 3, and place the decimal point in the root accordingly. Knowing the cubes of 1 to 20 by heart also speeds up such simplification questions in KSP PSI and constable papers.

Q 11 | apt | Arithmetic
An error 2% in excess is made while measuring the side of a square. The percentage of error in the calculated area of the square is
(1) 2%
(2) 2.02%
(3) 4.04%
(4) 4%
= 3
~ Let the true side be 100 units, so the true area is 100 × 100 = 10,000 square units. With a 2% excess error the measured side is 102, so the calculated area is 102 × 102 = 10,404. Error = 404 on 10,000 = 4.04%, option (3). Formula method: for a square both dimensions carry the same error, so use x + y + xy/100 with x = y = 2: 2 + 2 + (2 × 2)/100 = 4.04%. Option (4), 4%, is only the approximate answer obtained by doubling the side error; it ignores the small 0.04% term, whereas the question needs the exact value. Option (1), 2%, forgets that area depends on the square of the side, and 2.02% has no basis in the calculation. Tip: for a square, area error = 2x + x²/100; for a cube, volume error = 3x + 3x²/100 + x³/10,000. Learn these two formulas; mensuration error questions appear often in KSP mental ability sections.

Q 12 | apt | Reasoning
Find out which one of the following figures does not belong to the group.
a)
N | H | Z
C | F | D
A | E | Q
b)
Z | C | H
F | A | N
Q | D | E
c)
H | D | N
F | D | Z
C | E | A
d)
A | C | G
Z | D | E
N | F | H
(1) a
(2) b
(3) c
(4) d
= 3,4
~ Figures (a) and (b) contain exactly the same nine letters, A, C, D, E, F, H, N, Q and Z, only arranged differently, so they define the group. Figure (c) has the letter D twice and no Q (H, D, N, F, D, Z, C, E, A). Figure (d) has G in place of Q (A, C, G, Z, D, E, N, F, H). Thus both (c) and (d) break the common pattern, and neither can be singled out as the only odd one. Because two figures do not belong to the group, KEA's final key accepts both option (3) c and option (4) d; a candidate marking either one gets the mark. Options (1) and (2) are wrong because a and b are the two figures that agree with each other. Tip: in letter-grid odd-one-out questions, first write the letters of each box in alphabetical order and compare the sets; a repeated or missing letter shows up at once. Look for positional patterns (rows, diagonals, centre letter) only if the letter sets are identical.

Q 13 | apt | Reasoning
If 23rd October 2024 is Wednesday, then what was the day of the week on 10th March 2021?
(1) Monday
(2) Tuesday
(3) Wednesday
(4) Thursday
= 3
~ Count the days from 10 March 2021 to 23 October 2024. From 10 March 2021 to 10 March 2024 there are 365 + 365 + 366 = 1,096 days (the third year includes 29 February 2024). From 10 March 2024 to 23 October 2024: March 21 + April 30 + May 31 + June 30 + July 31 + August 31 + September 30 + October 23 = 227 days. Total = 1,096 + 227 = 1,323 days = 189 weeks exactly, so there are 0 odd days. Hence 10 March 2021 fell on the same weekday as 23 October 2024, that is Wednesday, option (3). Using odd days: 1,096 days give 1 + 1 + 2 = 4 odd days and 227 days give 3 odd days; 4 + 3 = 7, which is 0 odd days. Tuesday, Monday or Thursday would need 1, 2 or 6 odd days respectively. Tip: an ordinary year has 1 odd day and a leap year has 2; when counting backwards from a known date, subtract the odd days from the known weekday.

Q 15 | apt | Series & Puzzles
The English alphabet letter to be placed in the place of '?' (Question mark) in the figure given is
(1) M
(2) N
(3) Q
(4) R
= 1
~ Each corner box has three numbers and one letter, and the letter's position in the English alphabet equals the sum of the three numbers. Top-left box: 3 + 4 + 5 = 12, and the 12th letter is L. Top-right box: 9 + 6 + 4 = 19, and the 19th letter is S. Bottom-left box: 7 + 1 + 8 = 16, and the 16th letter is P. So for the bottom-right box: 2 + 8 + 3 = 13, and the 13th letter is M, option (1). N would need a sum of 14, Q a sum of 17 and R a sum of 18, none of which matches the numbers 2, 8 and 3. Tip: memorise alphabet positions with the EJOTY trick: E = 5, J = 10, O = 15, T = 20, Y = 25. From these anchors you can reach any letter in a second, for example M = O – 2 = 13. Letter-number figure puzzles of this kind are common in the reasoning section of KSP PSI papers.

Q 16 | apt | Reasoning
Eight persons A, B, C, D, E, F, G and H are sitting around a circle as given in the figure. They are facing the direction opposite to centre. If they move up to five places anticlockwise, then
(1) A will face Southeast
(2) C will face West
(3) E will face Northeast
(4) G will face North
= 1
~ From the figure, A sits at North, B at North-east, C at East, D at South-east, E at South, F at South-west, G at West and H at North-west. Moving anticlockwise means going N → NW → W → SW → S → SE → E → NE. A moves five places: NW, W, SW, S, SE, so A reaches the South-east seat. Since everyone faces away from the centre, a person in the South-east seat faces South-east; so 'A will face Southeast' is correct, option (1). Check the others: C (East) moves through NE, N, NW, W to SW and faces South-west, not West; E (South) ends at North-west and faces North-west, not North-east; G (West) ends at North-east and faces North-east, not North. Note: the Kannada version says they face the centre, but KEA's key follows the English wording (facing outward); with the Kannada wording A would face North-west and none of the four options would be correct. Tip: when people face outward, each person's facing direction is simply the direction of his seat from the centre; when they face inward, it is the opposite direction.

Q 17 | apt | Reasoning
In a certain code language, 'PEN – TAN' is written as '0' (Zero). How is 'DEN – COB' written in that code language?
(1) 8
(2) 3
(3) 9
(4) 7
= 2
~ Replace each letter by its position in the English alphabet and add the positions within each word. PEN = 16 + 5 + 14 = 35 and TAN = 20 + 1 + 14 = 35, so PEN – TAN = 35 – 35 = 0, which matches the given code. Applying the same rule to the second pair: DEN = 4 + 5 + 14 = 23 and COB = 3 + 15 + 2 = 20. Therefore DEN – COB = 23 – 20 = 3, option (2). The other values do not fit: 8, 9 or 7 would require the two word totals to differ by those amounts, which does not happen under this rule. The minus sign in the question is a real subtraction of the two word totals, not a separator. Tip: keep the EJOTY anchor positions in mind (E = 5, J = 10, O = 15, T = 20, Y = 25) so that letter values can be found instantly; coding by letter sums and differences is a favourite in KSP PSI reasoning sections.

Q 18 | his | Art & Culture
Consider the following statements with reference to Moidams – the mound burial system in Assam.
A) Moidams means home for spirit
B) Part of Tai-Ahom culture
C) These mounds were initially built with wood, later with stone and burnt bricks
D) Tai-Ahom people established Charaideo as their first city and site for royal Necropolis
Choose the correct answer from the options given below:
(1) A & B are correct
(2) A, B & C are correct
(3) A, B & D are correct
(4) A, B, C and D are correct
= 4
~ All four statements are correct, so option (4). (A) Official descriptions of the site explain 'moidam' as a 'home for the spirit' of the departed. (B) Moidams are the mound burials of Tai-Ahom kings, queens and nobles and are an integral part of Tai-Ahom culture; the Tai-Ahoms came into the Brahmaputra valley in the 13th century under Chao-lung Siu-ka-pha. (C) Official accounts note that the burial vaults were initially built of wood and later of stone and burnt bricks, then covered with earth to form the mound. (D) The Tai-Ahoms established Charaideo as their first capital and made it their royal necropolis, which holds the main group of royal moidams. Exam fact: 'Moidams – the Mound-Burial System of the Ahom Dynasty' was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List on 26 July 2024 at the 46th session held in New Delhi, becoming India's 43rd World Heritage Site and the first cultural site from the North-East.

Q 19 | his | Art & Culture
With reference to Indian Martial Art forms, which one of the following is NOT matched correctly?
(1) Gatka – Punjab
(2) Thoda – Madhya Pradesh
(3) Inbuan wrestling – Mizoram
(4) Silambam – Tamil Nadu
= 2
~ Thoda is a traditional martial art and sport of archery from Himachal Pradesh, not Madhya Pradesh, so pair (2) is wrongly matched and is the answer. It is played with wooden bows and arrows in the hill districts of Himachal, especially around the Baisakhi festival in April, and combines skill in archery with folk dance and music. The other pairs are correct. Gatka is the stick-and-sword fighting art associated with the Sikhs of Punjab, often displayed during religious processions and fairs. Karnataka link: the traditional kusti wrestling of the garadi mane gymnasiums of Mysuru, showcased during the Dasara wrestling bouts, is Karnataka's own martial heritage. Memory tip: Thoda goes with the Himalayas, Gatka with the Gurdwara.

Q 24 | pol | Union & State Government
Which of the following statements is correct in respect of nomination of Anglo-Indians to the Lok Sabha?
(1) 101st Constitutional Amendment Act provided the Provision
(2) 104th Constitutional Amendment Act removed the Provision
(3) 102nd Constitutional Amendment Act removed the Provision
(4) 99th Constitutional Amendment Act removed the Provision
= 2
~ Article 331 allowed the President to nominate not more than two members of the Anglo-Indian community to the Lok Sabha if the community was not adequately represented, and Article 333 allowed Governors to nominate one Anglo-Indian to the State Legislative Assembly. Under Article 334 this was a time-bound provision, renewed every ten years. The Constitution (104th Amendment) Act, 2019 extended the reservation of seats for SCs and STs in the Lok Sabha and Assemblies for another ten years (till January 2030) but did not extend Anglo-Indian nomination, which therefore ended from 25 January 2020. So option (2) is correct. The 101st Amendment (2016) introduced GST, the 102nd (2018) gave constitutional status to the National Commission for Backward Classes (Article 338B), and the 99th (2014) created the National Judicial Appointments Commission, struck down by the Supreme Court in 2015; none of them dealt with Anglo-Indian nomination. Here are three statements related to the reflection of light by plane mirrors.
i) Reflection of light from a plane mirror is an example for irregular reflection.
ii) A plane mirror forms only a single image of an object.
iii) By varying the angle between two plane mirrors, different number of images of an object can be obtained.

(1) Statement (i) is correct but (ii) and (iii) are wrong
(2) Statement (i) is wrong but (ii) and (iii) are correct

(4) Statements (i) and (iii) are correct but (ii) is wrong
= 2
~ Option 2 is correct: statement (i) is wrong, while (ii) and (iii) are correct. A plane mirror has a smooth, polished surface, so a parallel beam of light falling on it is reflected as a parallel beam; this is regular (specular) reflection. Irregular or diffused reflection occurs at rough surfaces such as paper, walls or a wooden table, which is why such objects can be seen from all directions. Hence (i) is wrong. Q 49 | sci | Physics
Consider the following statements:
Statement I: It is always easier to roll a body than to slide it over another.
Statement II: The rolling friction is smaller than the sliding friction.
Choose the correct option with respect to the above statements.
(1) Both the statements are wrong
(2) Statement I is correct but Statement II is wrong
(3) Statement I is wrong but Statement II is correct
(4) Both the statements are correct
= 4
~ Option 4 is correct: both statements are correct. When a body rolls over a surface, the area of contact and the interlocking of surface irregularities are much smaller than when it slides, so the opposing force – rolling friction – is much smaller than sliding friction. Statement II is therefore correct, and it is the reason why it is always easier to roll a body than to slide it over another, as Statement I says (this sentence comes from the NCERT Class 8 chapter on friction). Options 1, 2 and 3 treat one or both statements as wrong, so they are incorrect. Remember the order of friction: static friction is greater than sliding friction, which is greater than rolling friction. Friction can also be reduced by lubricants such as oil and grease, by polishing surfaces, and by giving streamlined shapes to vehicles, aircraft and boats that move through fluids.

Q 50 | sci | Chemistry
Prevention of corrosion is of prime importance. It not only saves money but also helps in preventing accidents such as a bridge collapse or failure of a key component due to corrosion. Which one of the following is not the correct method to prevent corrosion of metal?

= 4
~ Option 4 is the answer, because keeping a metal in acidified water does not prevent corrosion; it speeds it up. Q 89 | eco | Indian Economy
Consider the following statements:
Statement – I: Carbon tax is levied on the carbon emission that arises while producing goods and services
Statement – II: Carbon taxes are imposed to make hidden social costs visible
Which of the following is correct with respect to above statements?
(1) Statement I is correct but Statement II is not correct
(2) Statement I is incorrect, but Statement II is correct
(3) Both Statements I and II are correct and Statement II explains Statement I
(4) Statements I and II are correct, but Statement II does not explain Statement I
= 3
~ Both statements are correct and Statement II explains Statement I, option 3. A carbon tax is a charge on the carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases released when fossil fuels are burnt to produce goods and services; it is usually levied per tonne of CO2 or on the carbon content of fuels, so Statement I is correct. Emissions cause climate change, air pollution and health damage whose costs are borne by society rather than by the polluter. Economists call this a negative externality, a hidden social cost. A carbon tax, a type of Pigouvian tax, adds this cost to the market price so that it becomes visible to producers and consumers, who then shift to cleaner fuels and technologies. Statement II therefore gives the reason for Statement I, which rules out option 4, while options 1 and 2 wrongly call one statement incorrect. Exam tip: a carbon tax fixes the price and lets the quantity of emissions adjust, whereas cap-and-trade or carbon credits fix the quantity and let the price adjust. India has no explicit carbon tax, but its earlier coal cess, raised to Rs 400 per tonne before GST, worked as an implicit one.

Q 98 | pol | Union & State Government
Under which of the following circumstances anti-defection law does not apply to a Member of Parliament?
(1) If a nominated member of a House joins a political party after six months
(2) If he/she abstains from voting in the House contrary to the directions of the political party
(3) If an independent member joins a political party after election
(4) If he/she goes out of his/her party as a result of the merger of that party with another party
= 4
~ Option 4 is correct. Under Paragraph 4 of the Tenth Schedule, the anti-defection law, a member is not disqualified when his or her original political party merges with another party and at least two-thirds of the members of the legislature party agree to the merger; members who join the merged party and members who reject the merger and choose to function as a separate group are both protected. So leaving the party as a result of a merger is an exception. The other situations lead to disqualification: a nominated member who joins a political party after six months from taking his or her seat (option 1); a member who votes or abstains contrary to the party's direction without prior permission, unless the party condones it within 15 days (option 2); and an independent member who joins a political party after the election (option 3). The Tenth Schedule was added by the 52nd Amendment Act, 1985, and the 91st Amendment, 2003, removed the old one-third 'split' exception. The Speaker or Chairman decides, subject to judicial review (Kihoto Hollohan, 1992). Karnataka angle: in 2019 the Assembly Speaker disqualified 17 MLAs; the Supreme Court upheld the disqualification but allowed them to contest by-elections.

Q 100 | pol | Constitution & Rights
Consider the following provisions:
a) To secure a living wage for all workers
b) To promote the welfare of the people
c) Freedom to manage religious affairs
d) To uphold and protect the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India
Which of the above provisions are non-justiciable?
(1) a and b only
(2) a, b and c only
(3) a, b, c and d
(4) a, b and d only
= 4
~ Provisions a, b and d are non-justiciable, option 4. A non-justiciable provision is one that cannot be enforced through a court. Securing a living wage for all workers (a) is a Directive Principle under Article 43, and promoting the welfare of the people by securing a social order based on justice (b) is a Directive Principle under Article 38; Article 37 expressly says Directive Principles shall not be enforceable by any court, though they are fundamental in the governance of the country.
