Q 2 | cur | Karnataka
The first State in the country to implement National Education Policy-2020 is
(1) Kerala
(2) Tamil Nadu
(3) Karnataka
(4) Telangana
= 3
~ Karnataka became the first State in India to issue the order implementing the National Education Policy (NEP) 2020. The government order was issued on 7 August 2021, and Chief Minister Basavaraj Bommai formally launched the implementation on 23 August 2021, starting with higher education from the 2021-22 academic year (four-year undergraduate programme with multiple entry and exit options). Kerala, Tamil Nadu and Telangana did not adopt NEP in 2021; Tamil Nadu and Kerala in fact opposed parts of it, such as the three-language formula. NEP 2020 itself was approved by the Union Cabinet on 29 July 2020, replacing the 1986 policy; it was drafted by a committee headed by Dr K. Kasturirangan, the former ISRO chairman from Karnataka. It introduces the 5+3+3+4 school structure in place of 10+2. Exam tip: 'first State to implement' questions are common in KSP papers when Karnataka is the answer – remember NEP 2020 (Karnataka, August 2021).

Q 3 | cur | National
Which is the first national park in India to be equipped with Satellite Phones ?
(1) Bandipur
(2) Silent Valley
(3) Pench
(4) Kaziranga
= 4
~ In August 2021, Kaziranga National Park and Tiger Reserve in Assam became the first national park in India whose frontline staff were equipped with satellite phones. The phones were provided to forest officers and guards to strengthen anti-poaching operations and communication in remote areas, especially during the annual floods when mobile networks fail. Bandipur (Karnataka), Silent Valley (Kerala) and Pench (Madhya Pradesh/Maharashtra) are well-known protected areas, but none of them was the first to get satellite phones. Kaziranga, on the south bank of the Brahmaputra in Golaghat and Nagaon districts, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site (1985) and is home to about two-thirds of the world's great one-horned rhinoceros population, which is why poaching control there is a priority. It was declared a national park in 1974 and a tiger reserve in 2006. Exam tip: link Kaziranga with rhinos, floods of the Brahmaputra and 'firsts' in anti-poaching technology.

Q 4 | cur | National
Consider the following statements regarding 'SonChiraiya' and select the correct one/s :
1. SonChiraiya was launched for marketing of urban self help group products.
2. It was launched by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry.
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 1
~ 'SonChiraiya' is a single brand and logo launched by the Union government in August 2021 for the marketing of products made by urban self-help groups (SHGs), so statement 1 is correct. It was launched by the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs (MoHUA) under the Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana – National Urban Livelihoods Mission (DAY-NULM), not by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry, so statement 2 is wrong. Wrong-ministry statements are a common trap in such two-statement questions.

Q 5 | cur | National
India's highest herbal park has been inaugurated at
(1) Arunachal Pradesh
(2) Sikkim
(3) Uttarakhand
(4) Himachal Pradesh
= 3
~ India's highest herbal park was inaugurated in August 2021 at Mana village in Chamoli district of Uttarakhand, close to the India–China border near Badrinath. It lies at a height of about 11,000 feet above sea level and was developed by the research wing of the Uttarakhand Forest Department over about three acres of land, with funding under the Centre's CAMPA scheme. Exam tip: for 'highest' or 'first' parks in 2021 current affairs, Uttarakhand features often (Mana herbal park, Ranikhet fern garden, Haldwani grass conservatory).

Q 7 | pol | Laws, Police & Society
In India, the foreign grants received by NGO's are regulated by
(1) Foreign Exchange Management Act
(2) Foreign Exchange Contribution Act
(3) Foreign Contribution Regulation Act
(4) Foreign Exchange Regulation Act
= 3
~ Foreign donations to NGOs, associations and individuals in India are regulated by the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act (FCRA), administered by the Union Ministry of Home Affairs. The first FCRA was passed in 1976; it was replaced by FCRA 2010, which was amended in 2020. The 2020 amendment made it mandatory to receive foreign contributions only in a designated FCRA account at the State Bank of India, New Delhi Main Branch, barred transfer of such funds to other organisations, cut the limit on administrative expenses from 50% to 20%, and made Aadhaar mandatory for office bearers. FEMA 1999 deals with foreign exchange transactions and trade, not donations; FERA 1973 was the older, stricter law replaced by FEMA; 'Foreign Exchange Contribution Act' does not exist. Exam tip: FCRA = donations (Home Ministry); FEMA = foreign exchange (Finance Ministry / RBI, enforced by the Enforcement Directorate).

Q 8 | cur | International & Sports
Which neighbouring nation has first adopted India's BHIM UPI payment systems ?
(1) Sri Lanka
(2) Bhutan
(3) Myanmar
(4) Bangladesh
= 2
~ Bhutan became the first neighbouring country to adopt India's BHIM UPI (Unified Payments Interface) QR-based payments. Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman and Bhutan's Finance Minister Lyonpo Namgay Tshering launched the service virtually on 13 July 2021, through a partnership between NPCI International Payments Ltd (NIPL) and the Royal Monetary Authority of Bhutan. Q 9 | geo | Karnataka Geography
Jog Falls is in :
(1) Ghataprabha
(2) Bhima
(3) Cauvery
(4) Sharavati
= 4
~ Jog Falls is formed by the Sharavati river, which plunges about 253 metres (830 ft) on the border of Shivamogga and Uttara Kannada districts in Karnataka. It is one of the highest plunge waterfalls in India and falls in four streams named Raja, Rani, Roarer and Rocket. The Linganamakki dam upstream and the Mahatma Gandhi hydro-electric station use the Sharavati's water, which is why the falls are fullest during the monsoon. The other rivers have different falls: Gokak Falls is on the Ghataprabha river (Belagavi district); Shivanasamudra (Gaganachukki and Bharachukki) is on the Cauvery; the Bhima flows mainly through Maharashtra and north Karnataka and is not linked to Jog. The Sharavati rises at Ambutirtha near Thirthahalli and flows west into the Arabian Sea at Honnavar. Exam tip: remember river–waterfall pairs of Karnataka – Sharavati: Jog; Ghataprabha: Gokak; Cauvery: Shivanasamudra; Aghanashini: Unchalli; Kali: Lalguli.

Q 10 | eco | Money, Banking & Budget
RBI extended the RBIA system for housing finance companies. What is RBIA ?
(1) Risk Based Internal Audit
(2) Reserve Bank Internal Assessment
(3) Reserve Bank of India Audit
(4) None of these
= 1
~ RBIA stands for Risk Based Internal Audit. Under this system, the internal audit of a financial institution concentrates on the areas of highest risk, assessing both the risk management and the internal controls, instead of checking every transaction equally. RBI had made RBIA mandatory for scheduled commercial banks earlier; in February 2021 it extended the requirement to large non-banking financial companies (deposit-taking NBFCs and non-deposit NBFCs with assets of Rs 5,000 crore and above), and in June 2021 to housing finance companies on similar lines, to be implemented in a phased manner. 'Reserve Bank Internal Assessment' and 'Reserve Bank of India Audit' are not real terms. Since August 2019 HFCs have been regulated by the RBI (taken over from the National Housing Bank), which is why RBI issued these instructions. Exam tip: RBIA = Risk Based Internal Audit; for banks the related term is RBS, Risk Based Supervision.

Q 11 | sci | Chemistry
Which among the following is "White Vitriol" ?
(1) Zinc oxide
(2) Zinc Sulphate
(3) Zinc Iodide
(4) Zinc Phosphate
= 2
~ White vitriol is the common name of zinc sulphate, usually as the heptahydrate ZnSO4·7H2O, a colourless (white) crystalline solid. 'Vitriol' is the old name for sulphates (and oil of vitriol for sulphuric acid). The other vitriols are: blue vitriol – copper sulphate pentahydrate (CuSO4·5H2O), and green vitriol – ferrous sulphate heptahydrate (FeSO4·7H2O). Zinc oxide (ZnO) is called 'Chinese white' or philosopher's wool and is used in ointments and paints; zinc iodide and zinc phosphate have no such common names (zinc phosphate is used as a dental cement and anti-rust coating). Zinc sulphate is used in medicine to treat zinc deficiency and diarrhoea (with ORS), in agriculture as a micronutrient fertiliser, in rayon manufacture and as a mordant in dyeing. Exam tip: remember the three vitriols by colour – Blue: copper, Green: iron, White: zinc – all three are sulphates.

Q 12 | sci | Physics
Which among the following is responsible for the energy released in an atom bomb ?
(1) Nuclear fusion
(2) Gamma decay
(3) Nuclear fission
(4) None of these
= 3
~ An atom bomb (atomic bomb) gets its energy from nuclear fission – the splitting of heavy nuclei such as uranium-235 or plutonium-239 when struck by neutrons. Each fission releases about 200 MeV of energy and two or three fresh neutrons, which split more nuclei, setting off an uncontrolled chain reaction once the supercritical mass is reached. The energy comes from the mass defect, according to Einstein's E = mc². Nuclear fusion (joining of light nuclei like hydrogen isotopes) is the source of energy in the hydrogen bomb and in the Sun and stars, not the atom bomb; in fact a fission bomb is used as the trigger of a hydrogen bomb. Gamma decay is only the emission of gamma rays by an excited nucleus and cannot produce an explosion. Exam tip: atom bomb and nuclear reactors = fission (controlled in reactors with moderators and control rods); hydrogen bomb and the Sun = fusion.

Q 13 | his | Ancient India
In which language is Kalhana's Rajatarangini written ?
(1) Sanskrit
(2) Persian
(3) Prakrit
(4) Pali
= 1
~ Rajatarangini ('River of Kings') was written in Sanskrit verse by the Kashmiri poet-historian Kalhana in 1148–49 CE, during the reign of King Jayasimha of the Lohara dynasty. It contains about 7,800 verses in eight books (tarangas) and narrates the history of Kashmir's kings from legendary times to the 12th century. It is regarded as the first true historical work of India because Kalhana examined inscriptions, coins and earlier records and tried to be impartial. Persian is wrong – the work was later translated into Persian in the Mughal period (on Akbar's orders) and continued in Sanskrit by Jonaraja and Srivara; Prakrit and Pali were the languages of Jain and Buddhist texts respectively. Kalhana's father Champaka was a minister of King Harsha of Kashmir. Exam tip: Rajatarangini – Kalhana – Sanskrit – Kashmir – 12th century; it is the standard example of early Indian historiography.

Q 14 | his | Ancient India
In which edict Ashoka refers himself as King of Magadha ?
(1) Maski edict
(2) Rummindei edict
(3) Kausambi edict
(4) Bhabru edict
= 4
~ In the Bhabru (Bairat–Calcutta) Minor Rock Edict, found near Bairat in Rajasthan, Ashoka calls himself 'Priyadasi Laja Magadhe' – Priyadarshi, King of Magadha – while addressing the Buddhist Sangha. It is also notable for declaring his faith in the Buddha, Dhamma and Sangha and for listing seven Buddhist texts that monks and lay followers should study. The other edicts do not use this title: the Maski edict (Raichur district, Karnataka, discovered in 1915) is famous because it was the first to mention the name 'Ashoka' (Devanampiya Asoka); the Rummindei pillar records Ashoka's visit to Lumbini, the Buddha's birthplace, and his reduction of taxes there; the Kausambi (Allahabad) pillar contains the Queen's edict about his queen Karuvaki. Exam tip: Maski, Gujarra, Nittur and Udegolam give the name Ashoka; Bhabru gives 'King of Magadha'; Rummindei refers to Lumbini.

Q 15 | geo | Indian Geography
Which is the oldest mountain range of India ?
(1) Himalayas
(2) Aravalli
(3) Jaintia
(4) Satpura
= 2
~ The Aravalli range is the oldest fold mountain range of India, formed in the Precambrian era, about 1.5–2 billion years ago (Proterozoic). It runs about 690–700 km from near Delhi through Haryana and Rajasthan to Gujarat. Being so old, it has been heavily eroded into residual (relict) mountains; its highest peak is Guru Shikhar (about 1,722 m) on Mount Abu. The Himalayas are the youngest fold mountains, formed in the Tertiary period by the collision of the Indian and Eurasian plates, and are still rising. The Jaintia hills are part of the Meghalaya plateau, an extension of the peninsular block, and the Satpura is a block mountain range between the Narmada and Tapi rivers – both are younger than the Aravallis. The Aravallis act as a barrier against the spread of the Thar desert eastward. Exam tip: Aravalli = oldest fold mountains; Himalaya = youngest fold mountains.

Q 16 | geo | Environment & Ecology
Where is India's only private sanctuary - SAI sanctuary located ?
(1) Chikamagaluru
(2) Shivamogga
(3) Kodagu
(4) Uttara Kannada
= 3
~ The SAI (Save Animals Initiative) Sanctuary is a private wildlife sanctuary in Kodagu (Coorg) district of Karnataka, near the Brahmagiri hills, adjoining the Brahmagiri Wildlife Sanctuary. It was created by Anil Malhotra and his wife Pamela Gale Malhotra, who began buying abandoned coffee and cardamom plantations and paddy land from 1991 and let them regenerate into natural rainforest. It now covers about 300 acres and is home to elephants, tigers, leopards, Indian bison, deer, hornbills and many species of birds and reptiles, and a river runs through it. It is often described as India's first and only private wildlife sanctuary. Chikkamagaluru (Bhadra), Shivamogga (Shettihalli) and Uttara Kannada (Dandeli) have government-notified sanctuaries, not the SAI sanctuary. Exam tip: link Kodagu with Nagarahole, Brahmagiri, Pushpagiri and Talacauvery sanctuaries – and the privately created SAI sanctuary.

Q 17 | eco | Money, Banking & Budget
Who sets the inflation target in India ?
(1) Reserve Bank of India
(2) SEBI
(3) Government of India
(4) Both (a) and (c)
= 4
~ Under Section 45ZA of the RBI Act, 1934 (inserted by the Finance Act, 2016), the Government of India, in consultation with the Reserve Bank of India, sets the inflation target once every five years. For 2016–2021 the target was 4% CPI inflation with a tolerance band of 2% to 6%, and in March 2021 the government retained the same target for 2021–2026. The RBI's six-member Monetary Policy Committee (three from RBI, including the Governor as chairman, and three appointed by the government) then fixes the policy repo rate to achieve this target; if inflation stays outside the band for three consecutive quarters, the RBI must report the reasons to the government. Strictly, the legal power to set the target lies with the Government of India (option c), but because the target is fixed in consultation with the RBI and the RBI is responsible for achieving it, KSP's key gives 'Both (a) and (c)'. SEBI is the capital-market regulator and has no role in inflation targeting. Exam tip: flexible inflation targeting began with the Monetary Policy Framework Agreement of February 2015 (Urjit Patel committee recommendation).

Q 19 | comp | Computer Fundamentals
A groupware is a :
(1) Software
(2) Hardware
(3) Anchor
(4) Network
= 1
~ Groupware is a kind of application software that helps groups of people work together on a common task, even when they are in different places. It is also called collaborative software. Typical features are shared calendars and scheduling, e-mail, messaging, document sharing and joint editing, discussion forums, video conferencing and workflow management. Examples include Lotus Notes, Microsoft Teams, Microsoft Exchange/Outlook, Google Workspace, Slack and Zoom. Groupware is not hardware (physical parts like the CPU or printer), not an anchor (a hyperlink marker in HTML), and not a network itself – though it usually runs over a network such as a LAN or the Internet, which is the trap in option (4). The study of how people use such tools is called CSCW (Computer Supported Cooperative Work). Exam tip: any word ending in '-ware' (groupware, firmware, malware, shareware, freeware) is a type of software, except hardware.

Q 20 | his | Art & Culture
Togalu Gombeyata' is :
(1) Puppetry
(2) Folk dance
(3) Painting
(4) Mural
= 1
~ Togalu Gombeyata is the traditional leather shadow puppetry of Karnataka. 'Togalu' means leather and 'gombe-aata' means doll play. The puppets are cut from goat or deer skin, painted in bright colours and made translucent; they are held against a white screen lit from behind by a lamp, so the audience sees their coloured shadows. Stories are usually taken from the Ramayana, Mahabharata and Puranas and are accompanied by music and narration in Kannada. Togalu Gombeyata is not a folk dance (such as Dollu Kunitha or Yakshagana), a painting style (like Mysuru painting) or a mural tradition. Similar shadow-puppet forms in other states are Tholu Bommalata (Andhra Pradesh), Tholpavakoothu (Kerala), Ravanchhaya (Odisha) and Chamadyache Bahulya (Maharashtra). Exam tip: 'Togalu' = leather in Kannada – the word itself tells you it is leather puppetry.

Q 21 | pol | Judiciary & Constitutional Bodies
Which among the following Writs examines the functioning of subordinate Courts ?
(1) Mandamus
(2) Quo Warranto
(3) Certiorari
(4) Habeas Corpus
= 3
~ Certiorari, meaning 'to be certified' or 'to be informed', is issued by a higher court (the Supreme Court under Article 32 or a High Court under Article 226) to a lower court or tribunal, transferring a pending case to itself or quashing an order already passed. It is used when the lower court acts without or in excess of jurisdiction, violates natural justice or commits an error of law apparent on the record – in other words, it examines the functioning of subordinate courts. Prohibition is the related writ issued to stop a lower court before it passes an order. Mandamus ('we command') directs a public official or body to perform a public duty; Quo Warranto ('by what authority') questions a person's right to hold a public office; Habeas Corpus ('to have the body') orders the production of a detained person. Exam tip: Certiorari is corrective (after the order), Prohibition is preventive (before the order); both are issued only against judicial or quasi-judicial bodies.

Q 22 | geo | World Geography
The Horn of Africa denotes the region containing :
1. Djibouti    2. Somalia
3. Kenya    4. Eritrea
Select the correct answer using the code below.
(1) 1 and 3
(2) 1, 2 and 3
(3) 1, 2 and 4
(4) 1, 2, 3 and 4
= 3
~ The Horn of Africa is the peninsula in north-east Africa that juts into the Arabian Sea, lying along the southern side of the Gulf of Aden and the Red Sea. It conventionally consists of four countries: Djibouti, Eritrea, Ethiopia and Somalia. Of the listed countries, Djibouti (1), Somalia (2) and Eritrea (4) are in the Horn, while Kenya (3) lies further south in East Africa and is not usually counted in the Horn (though it is a member of the wider regional body IGAD). Hence the answer is 1, 2 and 4. The region is strategically important because the Bab-el-Mandeb strait between Djibouti/Eritrea and Yemen links the Red Sea with the Gulf of Aden, a key route for oil and trade through the Suez Canal; many countries, including China, the USA and France, have military bases in Djibouti. Somali piracy in the Gulf of Aden also made the region well known. Exam tip: Horn of Africa = DEES – Djibouti, Eritrea, Ethiopia, Somalia.

Q 25 | cur | National
National handloom day is celebrated on :
(1) July 7
(2) August 7
(3) September 7
(4) November 7
= 2
~ National Handloom Day is observed every year on 7 August. The date commemorates the Swadeshi Movement, launched on 7 August 1905 at a meeting in the Calcutta Town Hall to protest against the partition of Bengal; it promoted the boycott of foreign goods and the revival of domestic products, especially handloom cloth. The day was first celebrated in 2015, when Prime Minister Narendra Modi inaugurated it in Chennai, and it is organised by the Union Ministry of Textiles to honour handloom weavers and raise awareness about the sector's contribution to the economy. 7 August 2021 was the seventh National Handloom Day. July 7, September 7 and November 7 are not linked with this day. The handloom sector is one of the largest sources of rural employment after agriculture. Exam tip: 7 August = National Handloom Day (Swadeshi Movement, 1905); remember the 'India Handloom' brand was also launched on 7 August 2015.

Q 26 | cur | International & Sports
India's first Olympic gold medal in track & field events was in :
(1) Hammer throw
(2) Discuss throw
(3) Javelin throw
(4) Shot put
= 3
~ India's first ever Olympic gold medal in athletics (track and field) came in the javelin throw. Neeraj Chopra won the men's javelin final at the Tokyo 2020 Olympics on 7 August 2021 with a best throw of 87.58 m, India's first Olympic athletics medal since Norman Pritchard's two silvers (200 m and 200 m hurdles) at Paris 1900. It was also only India's second individual Olympic gold after Abhinav Bindra's shooting gold at Beijing 2008. India has never won an Olympic gold in the hammer throw, discus throw or shot put, so options (1), (2) and (4) are wrong. To honour the feat, the Athletics Federation of India declared 7 August as National Javelin Day. Neeraj Chopra, then a Subedar in the Indian Army from Panipat (Haryana), later also won silver at Paris 2024. Exam tip: remember the pair "Bindra 2008 shooting, Chopra 2021 javelin" for India's individual Olympic golds.

Q 27 | geo | Indian Geography
Which of the following tribes are found in Andaman & Nicobar islands ?
1. Sentinelese
2. Ulladan
3. Jarawa
4. Bakarwal
Choose the correct option.
(1) 1 and 3
(2) 2 and 4
(3) 1, 2, 3
(4) 1, 2, 3 and 4

(3) Oxbow lake

= 2
~ The word 'aeolian' comes from Aeolus, the Greek god of the winds, and refers to landforms made by wind. Q 44 | geo | World Geography
The migration of magnetic poles over earth's surface through geologic time is called :
(1) Celestial Pole
(2) Aurora Polaris
(3) Axial rotation
(4) Polar Wandering
= 4
~ Polar wandering is the apparent movement of the Earth's magnetic poles over geological time. When volcanic rocks cool, magnetic minerals in them align with the Earth's magnetic field of that time and record the position of the poles (palaeomagnetism). Rocks of different ages from the same continent point to different pole positions, and when these positions are joined they form an "apparent polar wandering path". Since paths from different continents do not match, scientists concluded that it is the continents that moved, giving strong support to the theory of continental drift and plate tectonics. So option (4) is correct. A celestial pole is the point in the sky directly above the Earth's geographic pole; aurora polaris is the display of polar lights caused by solar particles; axial rotation is the Earth's spin on its axis. None of these refers to pole migration. Exam tip: palaeomagnetism and sea-floor spreading are key evidences of plate tectonics.

(2) Hypermetropia
(3) Presbyopia

= 1

Q 53 | sci | Physics
If a candle burns in zero gravity condition.
(1) The candle will not burn
(2) The flame of candle will become spherical
(3) Candle will burn for shorter time
(4) None of these
= 2
~ On Earth a candle flame is long and tear-shaped because of convection: hot, less dense gases rise (buoyancy), and fresh, cooler air carrying oxygen flows in from below. In zero gravity (microgravity, as on a space station) there is no buoyancy, so hot gases do not rise and there is no convection current. Oxygen reaches the wick only by slow diffusion, equally from all sides, so the flame becomes spherical (and dim and bluish), which makes option (2) correct. The candle does burn, so option (1) is wrong; because oxygen supply is slower, the flame is weaker and the candle actually burns more slowly, not for a shorter time, so option (3) is wrong. Such spherical flames have been studied in NASA microgravity experiments. Q 56 | sci | Physics
Who discovered electrons?
(1) James Chandwick
(2) Goldstein
(3) J.J. Thomson
(4) Ernest Rutherford
= 3
~ The electron was discovered by the British physicist J.J. Thomson in 1897 through his cathode ray tube experiments. He showed that cathode rays were made of tiny negatively charged particles much lighter than atoms, measured their charge-to-mass ratio (e/m), and later proposed the 'plum pudding' model of the atom. Q 75 | his | Ancient India

(2) 104
(3) 106

= 4
~ The traditional answer is 108 Upanishads, as listed in the Muktika Upanishad, where Lord Rama tells Hanuman the names of 108 Upanishads; so option (4) is the key's answer. Q 77 | his | Ancient India

(1) Rig Veda
(2) Yajur Veda
(3) Sama Veda
(4) Atharva Veda
= 1
~ The Gayatri Mantra is found in the Rig Veda, Mandala III, hymn 62, verse 10 (RV 3.62.10). The Yajur Veda deals with sacrificial formulas, the Sama Veda with melodies for chanting (mostly drawn from the Rig Veda), and the Atharva Veda with charms, spells and everyday life. Q 81 | sci | Biology
Plants growing in marshy areas are called :
(1) Helophytes
(2) Xerophytes
(3) Eremophytes
(4) Psammophytes
= 1
~ Helophytes (from the Greek "helos", marsh) are plants that grow in marshy or swampy places, with their roots and lower parts in waterlogged soil or shallow water and their shoots above the water, for example reeds, cattails (Typha) and bulrushes. The other terms describe plants of dry or special habitats: xerophytes are plants adapted to dry, arid conditions (cactus, Opuntia, Calotropis) with features such as thick cuticle, sunken stomata and succulent tissues; eremophytes are desert plants (from "eremos", desert); psammophytes are plants growing on sand or sandy soil, such as sand dune vegetation. Related terms often asked are hydrophytes (water plants such as Hydrilla and lotus), halophytes (plants of saline soils such as mangroves, with pneumatophores), mesophytes (plants of moderate conditions) and lithophytes (plants growing on rocks). Exam tip: learn these ecological plant groups by their Greek roots: helo = marsh, xero = dry, halo = salt, psammo = sand, litho = rock.

Q 85 | sci | Chemistry
What causes 'Blue Baby Syndrome' ?
(1) Fluoride
(2) Chloride
(3) Nitrate
(4) Sulphate
= 3
~ Blue baby syndrome (methaemoglobinaemia) is caused by excess nitrate in drinking water. When infants drink water or formula prepared with nitrate-rich water, the nitrate is converted to nitrite in the body; nitrite changes haemoglobin into methaemoglobin, which cannot carry oxygen. The baby's skin, lips and nails turn bluish because of oxygen shortage, hence the name. Infants below six months are most at risk. Nitrate enters groundwater mainly from excessive use of nitrogenous fertilisers, sewage and animal waste. The BIS (IS 10500) acceptable limit for nitrate in drinking water is 45 mg per litre. The other options cause different problems: excess fluoride causes dental and skeletal fluorosis; excess chloride gives a salty taste and corrosion; excess sulphate causes a laxative effect. Exam tip: remember the pairs nitrate – blue baby, fluoride – fluorosis, arsenic – blackfoot disease, mercury – Minamata, cadmium – Itai-itai.

Q 89 | geo | World Geography
Seasonal contrasts are maximum in :
(1) Equator
(2) Low Latitudes
(3) Mid Latitudes
(4) High Latitudes
= 3
~ Seasonal contrasts are greatest in the mid latitudes. The mid latitudes (roughly 30° to 60°, the temperate zone) are the region of the four distinct seasons, spring, summer, autumn and winter, because the sun's angle and the length of day change a lot during the year and because the region lies in the path of moving air masses, fronts and temperate cyclones. Near the equator and in low latitudes the sun is high all year and days are almost equal, so temperature hardly changes and there are no marked seasons (only wet and dry spells). In high latitudes there are mainly two seasons, a long cold winter and a short cool summer. So the mid latitudes, with four well-marked seasons and the largest change of weather types through the year, show the maximum seasonal contrast; note, though, that the largest annual range of temperature is found in high-latitude continental interiors such as Verkhoyansk in Siberia. Exam tip: annual range of temperature increases from the equator towards the poles and from the coast to the interior.

Q 90 | geo | Indian Geography
The map showing the specialty of the surface of any area is called :
(1) Area map
(2) Relief map
(3) Thematic map
(4) Cadastral map
= 2
~ A relief map shows the surface features (relief) of an area, that is, the variations in height and shape of the land, such as mountains, plateaus, plains, valleys and river basins. Relief is shown by methods such as contour lines, layer tints (colours like green for lowlands and brown for highlands), hachures, hill shading and spot heights; physical maps in an atlas are mostly relief maps. Q 92 | geo | Environment & Ecology
Which among the following types of vegetation exhibits maximum bio diversity ?
(1) Tropical rain forest
(2) Dry deciduous forest
(3) Moist deciduous forest
(4) Alpine forest
= 1
~ Tropical rain forests (tropical evergreen forests) have the highest biodiversity of all vegetation types. They lie near the equator, where high temperature throughout the year, heavy rainfall (over 200 cm) and high humidity allow plants to grow all year round. They have a multi-layered canopy, with very tall trees, climbers, epiphytes and shrubs, which creates many niches for animals, birds and insects. Although they cover only about 6-7% of the earth's land surface, they are believed to hold more than half of the world's plant and animal species. In India they are found in the Western Ghats, the North-East and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands. Dry and moist deciduous forests shed leaves in the dry season and have fewer species, while alpine forests at high altitudes have a short growing season, low temperatures and the lowest diversity among the options. Exam tip: biodiversity generally decreases from the equator to the poles and with increasing altitude.
