Q 1 | geo | India: People and Economy
According to the chapter, India's share in the total volume of world trade is about:
(1) 1 per cent
(2) 5 per cent
(3) 10 per cent
(4) 17 per cent
= 1
~ Although India's share is small, it plays a significant role in the world economy.

Q 2 | geo | India: People and Economy
Which of the following is NOT given as a reason for the sharp rise in India's foreign trade?
(1) Momentum picked up by the manufacturing sector
(2) Liberal policies of the government
(3) Diversification of markets
(4) Ban on all imports
= 4
~ India's external trade rose from Rs 1,214 crore in 1950-51 to over Rs 77 lakh crore in 2020-21.

Q 3 | geo | India: People and Economy
Consider the following statements about India's balance of trade:
1. The value of India's imports has continued to be higher than that of its exports.
2. The trade deficit has been compounded by the rising price of crude petroleum, a major import.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 3
~ Despite growth in both imports and exports, India has an increasing trade deficit.

Q 4 | geo | India: People and Economy
Which category formed the largest share of India's exports in 2021-22, according to the chapter's table?
(1) Agriculture and allied products
(2) Ores and minerals
(3) Manufactured goods
(4) Crude and petroleum products
= 3
~ Manufactured goods made up 67.8 per cent of exports in 2021-22; engineering goods and gems and jewellery are important items.

Q 5 | geo | India: People and Economy
Among agricultural exports, the chapter notes a great decline in traditional items. Which of the following is one of them?
(1) Floricultural products
(2) Coffee, spices and tea
(3) Fresh fruits
(4) Marine products
= 2
~ Tough international competition has reduced exports of traditional items like coffee, spices, tea and pulses, while floriculture, fresh fruits, marine products and sugar have grown.

Q 7 | geo | India: People and Economy
Consider the following statements about changes in India's imports:
1. In the 1950s and 1960s, food grains were a major item of import.
2. After the 1970s, food grain imports were discontinued because of the success of the Green Revolution.
3. The energy crisis of 1973 pushed up the prices of petroleum and the import budget.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ Food grain imports were replaced by fertilisers and petroleum. Machinery, special steel, edible oils and chemicals largely make up the import basket.

Q 11 | geo | India: People and Economy
Which two important ports did India lose at Partition in 1947?
(1) Karachi and Chittagong
(2) Karachi and Kandla
(3) Chittagong and Haldia
(4) Mumbai and Kolkata
= 1
~ Karachi went to Pakistan and Chittagong to East Pakistan (now Bangladesh). To compensate, ports like Kandla and Diamond Harbour were developed.

Q 12 | geo | India: People and Economy
Kandla port (now Deendayal Port), developed to reduce pressure on Mumbai port, is located at:
(1) The head of the Vembanad Kayal
(2) The mouth of the Zuari estuary
(3) The head of the Gulf of Kachchh
(4) The Mahanadi delta
= 3
~ It is designed for large quantities of petroleum, petroleum products and fertiliser; an offshore terminal at Vadinar reduces pressure on it.

Q 13 | geo | India: People and Economy
Which port is described as a natural harbour and the biggest port of the country, with the country's largest oil terminal?
(1) Kochi
(2) Visakhapatnam
(3) Chennai
(4) Mumbai
= 4
~ Mumbai lies close to the routes from the Middle East, Mediterranean countries, North Africa, North America and Europe. Its hinterland includes Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Uttar Pradesh and parts of Rajasthan.

Q 14 | geo | India: People and Economy

Q 15 | geo | India: People and Economy
Marmagao port, at the entrance of the Zuari estuary in Goa, gained significance after its remodelling in 1961 to handle:
(1) Crude oil imports
(2) Iron ore exports to Japan
(3) Tea exports
(4) Container traffic only

~ The Konkan Railway has considerably extended its hinterland, which covers Karnataka, Goa and southern Maharashtra.

Q 18 | geo | India: People and Economy
Which port, situated in the Mahanadi delta about 100 km from Cuttack, has the deepest harbour, especially suited for very large vessels?
(1) Haldia
(2) Ennore
(3) Visakhapatnam
(4) Paradip
= 4
~ Paradip was developed mainly for large-scale export of iron ore; Odisha, Chhattisgarh and Jharkhand form its hinterland.

Q 19 | geo | India: People and Economy
Which port is a land-locked harbour connected to the sea by a channel cut through solid rock and sand?
(1) Chennai
(2) Visakhapatnam
(3) Tuticorin
(4) Mumbai
= 2
~ A breakwater protects the channel from rough sea, and an outer harbour handles iron ore, petroleum and general cargo.

Q 20 | geo | India: People and Economy
Chennai port, one of the oldest on the eastern coast, is:
(1) A natural harbour with very deep water
(2) An artificial harbour built in 1859, not very suitable for large ships because of shallow water near the coast
(3) An inland port on a river
(4) A satellite port of Visakhapatnam
= 2
~ Ennore, about 25 km north of Chennai, and V.O. Chidambaranar (Tuticorin) were developed to relieve pressure on Chennai.
