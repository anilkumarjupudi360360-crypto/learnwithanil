Q 1 | geo | India: People and Economy
Planning that aims to reduce imbalances in development between different areas is called:
(1) Sectoral planning
(2) Regional planning
(3) Target group planning
(4) Rolling planning
= 2
~ Sectoral planning focuses on sectors such as agriculture, industry and transport; regional planning adds a spatial perspective to reduce regional imbalance.

Q 2 | geo | India: People and Economy
NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission on:
(1) 15 August 2014
(2) 1 January 2015
(3) 26 January 2016
(4) 1 April 2017
= 2
~ NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India) provides strategic and technical advice to the government.

Q 3 | geo | India: People and Economy
Which of the following is an example of a target group programme, rather than a target area programme?
(1) Command Area Development Programme
(2) Drought Prone Area Programme
(3) Small Farmers Development Agency
(4) Hill Area Development Programme
= 3
~ The Small Farmers Development Agency and Marginal Farmers Development Agency target groups of people; the others target areas.

Q 4 | geo | India: People and Economy

(1) Third
(2) Fourth
(3) Fifth
(4) Eighth
= 3
~ It covered 15 districts, including the hill districts of present Uttarakhand, the Mikir and North Cachar hills of Assam, Darjeeling district and Nilgiri district.

Q 6 | geo | India: People and Economy
The Drought Prone Area Programme was initiated during which Five Year Plan?
(1) Second
(2) Tenth
(3) Sixth
(4) Fourth
= 4
~ It aimed to provide employment in drought-prone areas and create productive assets, later shifting to irrigation, land development, afforestation and rural infrastructure.

(1) 1 and 2 only

Q 14 | geo | India: People and Economy
The Indira Gandhi Canal originates at:
(1) Hirakud dam in Odisha
(2) Bhakra dam in Himachal Pradesh
(3) Harike barrage in Punjab
(4) Farakka barrage in West Bengal
= 3
~ The canal was conceived by Kanwar Sain in 1948 and launched on 31 March 1958. It runs parallel to the Pakistan border through the Thar desert.

Q 15 | geo | India: People and Economy
Consider the following statements about the Indira Gandhi Canal command area:
1. The total planned length of the system is about 9,060 km.
2. Stage-I covers Ganganagar, Hanumangarh and northern Bikaner, while Stage-II covers districts like Jaisalmer, Barmer, Jodhpur and Churu.
3. Irrigation in Stage-II began earlier than in Stage-I.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1
~ Irrigation in Stage-I began in the early 1960s and in Stage-II in the mid-1980s.

Q 16 | geo | India: People and Economy
In the Indira Gandhi Canal system, water is lifted up to make it flow against the slope of the land in:
(1) Flow channels on the right bank
(2) Lift canals, which originate on the left bank of the main canal
(3) The Harike barrage only
(4) Natural streams
= 2
~ All lift canals start from the left bank of the main canal, while all canals on the right bank are flow channels. About 70 per cent of the command area was planned for flow irrigation.

Q 17 | geo | India: People and Economy
Canal irrigation in the Indira Gandhi Canal command area replaced traditional crops like gram, bajra and jowar with:
(1) Wheat, cotton, groundnut and rice
(2) Tea and coffee
(3) Jute and sugarcane only
(4) Ragi and millets only
= 1
~ This brought big gains in agricultural and livestock productivity.

Q 18 | geo | India: People and Economy
Which twin environmental problems have emerged from intensive irrigation in the Indira Gandhi Canal command area?
(1) Flooding and landslides
(2) Desertification and drought
(3) Waterlogging and soil salinity
(4) Glacier melt and avalanches
= 3
~ On the positive side, longer soil moisture and afforestation and pasture programmes have greened the land and reduced wind erosion.

Q 19 | geo | India: People and Economy
The "warabandi" system, recommended for the Indira Gandhi Canal command area, means:
(1) Equal distribution of canal water in the command area of an outlet
(2) A ban on all irrigation
(3) Growing only water-intensive crops
(4) A tax on groundwater use
= 1
~ Along with lining of water courses and land levelling, warabandi helps reduce the conveyance loss of water.

Q 20 | geo | India: People and Economy
Which of the following is NOT among the measures proposed in the chapter for sustainable development in the Indira Gandhi Canal command area?
(1) Strict implementation of the water management policy
(2) Encouraging plantation crops such as citrus fruits instead of water-intensive crops
(3) Eco-development through afforestation, shelterbelts and pasture development
(4) Expanding the area under water-intensive crops like rice and sugarcane
= 4
~ The cropping pattern should not include water-intensive crops. Five of the seven measures proposed aim to restore ecological balance.
