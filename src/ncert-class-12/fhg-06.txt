# NCERT Class 12 Geography, Fundamentals of Human Geography, Chapter 6: Tertiary and Quaternary Activities
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). November 2026.

Q 1 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
What is the main difference between secondary and tertiary activities, according to the chapter?
(1) Tertiary activities rely more on specialised skills, experience and knowledge of workers than on production techniques and machinery
(2) Tertiary activities produce physical goods in factories
(3) Secondary activities do not use any machines
(4) There is no difference between them
= 1
~ Tertiary activities provide services; their output is measured indirectly through wages and salaries.

Q 2 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Consider the following statements about the trend in employment:
1. In the initial stage of economic development, a larger share of people work in the primary sector.
2. In developed economies, the majority of workers are employed in tertiary activities.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 3
~ As economies develop, employment in services rises while it stays the same or falls in primary and secondary activities.

Q 3 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Periodic markets in rural areas are found where:
(1) There are large shopping malls
(2) There are no regular markets; they may be held weekly or bi-weekly on specified dates
(3) Only wholesale trade happens
(4) Goods are sold only through the internet
= 2
~ Shopkeepers move from one periodic market to another on different days, so they remain busy every day while serving a large area.

Q 4 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Which of the following is an example of non-store retail trading?
(1) Departmental stores
(2) Chain stores
(3) Mail-order, street peddling and automatic vending machines
(4) Consumer co-operative shops
= 3
~ Non-store retailing also includes handcarts, trucks, door-to-door sales, telephone and internet sales.

Q 5 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
According to the chapter, which were the first to introduce large-scale innovations in retailing?
(1) Chain stores
(2) Consumer co-operatives
(3) Departmental stores
(4) Mail-order companies
= 2
~ Departmental stores delegate purchasing to departmental heads, and chain stores buy merchandise economically and can test ideas in one store before applying them to many.

Q 6 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
In transport geography, lines drawn on a map to join places that are equal in terms of the time taken to reach them are called:
(1) Isobars
(2) Contours
(3) Isotherms
(4) Isochronic lines
= 4
~ Distance can be measured in kilometres, in time or in cost; isochronic lines show time-distance.

Q 7 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
In a transport network, the meeting point of two or more routes, or a sizeable town along a route, is called a:
(1) Link
(2) Corridor
(3) Hinterland
(4) Node
= 4
~ Every road joining two nodes is a link. A developed network has many links, which means places are well connected.

Q 8 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Radio and television are termed "mass media" because:
(1) They are owned only by the government
(2) They relay news, pictures and calls to vast audiences
(3) They use only written messages
(4) They work only in cities
= 2
~ Telecommunications have made messages travel in minutes instead of weeks; telegraph, Morse code and telex have almost become things of the past.

Q 9 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Consider the following statements about services:
1. Low-order services such as grocery shops and laundries are more common and widespread than high-order services.
2. Specialised services such as those of accountants, consultants and physicians are high-order services.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2

~ Services occur at many levels: some are geared to industry, some to people, and some to both.

Q 14 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Quaternary activities centre around:
(1) Extraction of natural resources
(2) Research, development and services requiring specialised knowledge, technical skills and administrative competence
(3) Manufacturing of heavy machinery
(4) Shifting cultivation
= 2
~ Personnel in office buildings, schools, universities, hospitals, theatres, accounting and brokerage firms belong to this category.

Q 15 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Consider the following statements about quaternary activities:
1. Over half of all workers in developed economies are in the "knowledge sector".
2. Like some tertiary functions, quaternary activities can also be outsourced.
3. They are tied to resources and are always localised by the market.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1
~ Quaternary activities are not tied to resources, not affected by the environment and not necessarily localised by the market.

Q 16 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Quinary activities, which focus on the creation, re-arrangement and interpretation of new and existing ideas, are often called:
(1) Red-collar professions
(2) Blue-collar professions
(3) Gold-collar professions
(4) Pink-collar professions
= 3
~ They represent the special and highly paid skills of senior business executives, government officials, research scientists, and financial and legal consultants.

Q 17 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
When outsourcing involves transferring work to overseas locations, it is described as:
(1) Trans-shipment
(2) Dumping
(3) Barter
(4) Off-shoring
= 4
~ Outsourcing (contracting out) means giving work to an outside agency to improve efficiency and reduce costs.

Q 18 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Knowledge Processing Outsourcing (KPO) differs from Business Process Outsourcing (BPO) because KPO:
(1) Involves only call centres
(2) Involves highly skilled workers and information-driven knowledge work, such as R&D, e-learning and legal services
(3) Does not use computers
(4) Is limited to manufacturing
= 2
~ KPO examples given include research and development, e-learning, business research, intellectual property research, the legal profession and banking.

Q 19 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
The unequal distribution of opportunities from information and communication technology across and within countries is called the:
(1) Demographic dividend
(2) Brain drain
(3) Trade deficit
(4) Digital divide
= 4
~ Developed countries have generally surged ahead. Within large countries like India, metropolitan centres have better connectivity than peripheral rural areas.
