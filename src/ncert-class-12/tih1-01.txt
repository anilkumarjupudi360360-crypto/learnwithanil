Q 1 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
The Harappan civilisation is named after Harappa because:
(1) It was the capital of the civilisation
(2) It was the largest Harappan city
(3) It was the first site where this civilisation was discovered
(4) It is mentioned in the Rigveda
= 3
~ Archaeologists name a culture after the place where it was first found. The Mature Harappan culture is dated to about 2600-1900 BCE.

Q 2 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Which of the following crops was NOT among the main grains found at Harappan sites?
(1) Wheat
(2) Barley
(3) Maize
(4) Lentil and chickpea
= 3
~ Grains found include wheat, barley, lentil, chickpea and sesame; millets in Gujarat; rice is relatively rare. Maize reached India much later from the Americas.

Q 3 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Evidence of a ploughed field, with two sets of furrows at right angles to each other, has been found at:
(1) Mohenjodaro
(2) Kalibangan
(3) Lothal
(4) Dholavira
= 2
~ The pattern suggests two different crops were grown together. Terracotta models of the plough have been found at Banawali (Haryana) and in Cholistan.

Q 4 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Mohenjodaro was divided into two sections. These were:
(1) The Royal Palace and the Temple
(2) The Port and the Market
(3) The Citadel, a smaller but higher section, and the larger Lower Town
(4) The East Town and the West Town, separated by a river
= 3
~ Buildings on the Citadel stood on mud-brick platforms. The Lower Town had residential buildings.

Q 5 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
The Great Bath, a large rectangular tank surrounded by corridors, was found in:
(1) The Citadel of Mohenjodaro
(2) The Lower Town of Harappa
(3) Lothal's dockyard
(4) Kalibangan
= 1
~ The tank was made watertight with bricks and a layer of bitumen. It may have been used for a special ritual bath.

Q 6 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Which of the following was a feature of houses in the Lower Town of Mohenjodaro?
1. Many houses were built around a courtyard.
2. There were no windows in the walls along the ground level facing the street.
3. Every house had its own bathroom paved with bricks, with drains connected to the street drain.
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ The planning shows concern for privacy. Some houses also had stairs to a second storey or the roof, and many had their own wells.

Q 7 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Bricks of standard ratio were used in Harappan settlements. What was the ratio of length to breadth to height?
(1) 3:2:1
(2) 2:2:1
(3) 4:2:1
(4) 5:3:1
= 3
~ The same ratio was used in all Harappan settlements, whether sun-dried or baked bricks, which points to a common system.

Q 8 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Chanhudaro, a tiny settlement, is known for:
(1) Its Great Bath
(2) Being almost entirely devoted to craft production, such as bead-making, shell-cutting, metal-working, seal-making and weight-making
(3) Its dockyard
(4) Its water reservoirs
= 2
~ Craftspeople there used a range of materials: carnelian, jasper, crystal, quartz, steatite, copper, bronze, gold, shell and faience.

Q 9 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Harappan weights were usually made of chert and cubical. Their lower denominations followed which system?
(1) Decimal (1, 10, 100)
(2) Duodecimal
(3) Binary (1, 2, 4, 8, 16 ...)
(4) Random
= 3
~ The lower denominations were binary and the higher ones followed the decimal system. Small weights were probably used to weigh jewellery and beads.

Q 10 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Which of the following is correctly matched with the material it supplied to the Harappans?
(1) Khetri (Rajasthan) - copper
(2) Shortughai (Afghanistan) - shell
(3) Nageshwar (Gujarat) - lapis lazuli
(4) Lothal - gold
= 1
~ Shortughai was near the source of lapis lazuli. Nageshwar and Balakot were near the coast and worked shell. Lothal was near sources of carnelian.

Q 11 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Mesopotamian texts mention contact with a region called "Meluhha", possibly the Harappan region. Which other places mentioned in those texts are linked to Oman and Bahrain?
(1) Kish and Ur
(2) Mari and Babylon
(3) Persepolis and Susa
(4) Magan (probably Oman) and Dilmun (probably Bahrain)
= 4
~ Products from Meluhha mentioned in Mesopotamian texts include carnelian, lapis lazuli, copper, gold and kinds of wood.

Q 12 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
What is true of the Harappan script?
(1) It has been deciphered as an early form of Brahmi
(2) It was alphabetic, with about 26 signs
(3) It was written only on pottery
(4) It remains undeciphered; it had many signs (about 375-400) and was mostly written from right to left
= 4
~ Because it had so many signs, it was not alphabetic. Most inscriptions are short, found on seals, copper tools, rims of jars and even a sign board at Dholavira.

Q 13 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Harappan seals were mostly made of:
(1) Steatite
(2) Gold
(3) Iron
(4) Ivory
= 1
~ Seals usually show an animal motif and a few signs. They were probably used to seal bags or packages of goods to ensure they reached safely.

Q 14 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Who was the first Director-General of the Archaeological Survey of India, who visited Harappa but did not realise how old it was?
(1) John Marshall
(2) Mortimer Wheeler
(3) Daya Ram Sahni
(4) Alexander Cunningham
= 4
~ Cunningham's main interest was in the history of the early historic period, of which Harappa did not seem to be a part. He failed to see Harappa's significance.

Q 15 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
In 1924, the discovery of a new civilisation in the Indus valley was announced to the world by:
(1) John Marshall, Director-General of the ASI
(2) Alexander Cunningham
(3) R.E.M. Wheeler
(4) James Prinsep
= 1
~ Seals had been found at Harappa by Daya Ram Sahni and at Mohenjodaro by Rakhal Das Banerji. Marshall's announcement changed the understanding of Indian history.

Q 16 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Mortimer Wheeler, who became Director-General of the ASI in 1944, introduced:
(1) The method of digging along regular horizontal units ignoring layers
(2) The practice of following the stratigraphy (layers) of the mound while excavating
(3) Radiocarbon dating
(4) Decipherment of the script
= 2
~ Earlier excavators often dug in uniform horizontal units, which mixed up finds from different layers. Wheeler corrected this.

Q 17 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Which of the following Harappan finds has been interpreted, though not with certainty, as a "proto-Shiva"?
(1) A bronze dancing girl
(2) A seal showing a figure seated cross-legged in a yogic posture, surrounded by animals
(3) A stone bust of a "priest-king"
(4) A terracotta toy cart
= 2
~ Early archaeologists also saw terracotta female figurines as mother goddesses. Such interpretations remain uncertain because the script cannot be read.

Q 18 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Which of the following Harappan cities, in Kutch, is known for its elaborate water reservoirs and for being fortified as a whole?
(1) Dholavira
(2) Harappa
(3) Mohenjodaro
(4) Kalibangan
= 1
~ At Dholavira and Lothal the entire settlement was fortified, and sections within the town were also separated by walls. Dholavira is famous for its water-harvesting system.

Q 19 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Which of the following explanations have been given for the end of the Mature Harappan phase around 1900 BCE?
1. Climatic change, deforestation and excessive floods
2. Shifting or drying up of rivers
3. Overuse of the landscape
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ None of these alone explains the decline everywhere. The disappearance of a strong unifying element, possibly the Harappan state, is also suggested.

Q 20 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Wheeler's idea that Mohenjodaro was destroyed by invading Aryans was questioned by later archaeologists because:
(1) There were no skeletons at all
(2) The skeletons found were not all from the same period, so there is no evidence of a single massacre
(3) The Aryans lived in Mohenjodaro
(4) The city was never abandoned
= 2
~ The archaeologist George Dales wrote of "the mythical massacre at Mohenjodaro". The evidence does not show a sudden attack.
