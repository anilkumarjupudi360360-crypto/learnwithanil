# NCERT Class 12 Economics, Introductory Macroeconomics, Chapter 6: Open Economy Macroeconomics
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
The balance of payments (BoP) of a country is:
(1) The government's annual budget
(2) The record of only exports and imports of goods
(3) A record of the transactions in goods, services and assets between residents of a country and the rest of the world over a period, usually a year
(4) The total foreign debt of a country
= 3
~ The BoP records all economic transactions with the rest of the world: trade in goods and services, transfers and flows of assets.

Q 2 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
The two main accounts of the balance of payments are:
(1) The current account and the capital account
(2) The revenue account and the capital account
(3) The savings account and the current account
(4) The trade account and the budget account
= 1
~ The current account records trade in goods and services and transfer payments; the capital account records transactions in assets.

Q 3 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
Which of the following is recorded in the current account of India's BoP?
(1) Foreign direct investment into India
(2) Remittances sent home by Indians working abroad
(3) External commercial borrowing by an Indian firm
(4) Purchase of Indian shares by foreign investors
= 2
~ Remittances are transfers and are recorded in the current account. Investment and borrowing are transactions in assets and go to the capital account.

Q 4 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
Which of the following is recorded in the capital account of the BoP?
(1) Export of software services
(2) Import of crude oil
(3) Gifts received from relatives abroad
(4) Foreign direct investment
= 4
~ The capital account records international purchases and sales of assets such as shares, bonds, land and direct investment, and borrowing and lending.

Q 5 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
The 'balance of trade' refers to:
(1) The balance on the whole current account
(2) The balance on the capital account
(3) The change in foreign exchange reserves
(4) The difference between the value of exports and imports of goods only
= 4
~ Trade in goods is called visible trade, and the gap between exports and imports of goods is the balance of trade. The current account also includes invisibles.

Q 6 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
'Invisibles' in the current account of the BoP include:
(1) Only exports of goods
(2) Trade in services, factor income and transfers
(3) Foreign direct investment
(4) Changes in gold reserves
= 2
~ Invisibles are items that are not visible goods: services such as banking, insurance and software, income from investment and work abroad, and transfers such as gifts and remittances.

Q 7 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
A country's exports of goods are 500, its imports of goods are 600 and its net invisibles are +60 (in crore dollars). Its current account balance is:
(1) A deficit of 40
(2) A surplus of 40
(3) A deficit of 100
(4) A surplus of 160
= 1
~ Trade balance = 500 − 600 = −100. Current account balance = trade balance + net invisibles = −100 + 60 = −40, a deficit of 40.

Q 8 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
'Autonomous' transactions in the BoP are:
(1) Transactions undertaken only by the central bank
(2) Transactions made to cover a deficit
(3) Transactions undertaken for their own sake, such as for profit, independent of the state of the BoP
(4) Transactions that are never recorded
= 3
~ Exports, imports and investments are made because they are profitable or useful, not to balance the BoP. These are autonomous, or 'above the line', transactions.

Q 9 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
'Accommodating' transactions in the BoP are:
(1) Transactions by tourists
(2) Exports of services
(3) Transactions undertaken to cover the gap left by autonomous transactions, such as official reserve transactions of the central bank
(4) Gifts sent by migrants
= 3
~ When autonomous receipts and payments do not match, the gap is filled by accommodating transactions, mainly changes in the central bank's foreign exchange reserves.

Q 10 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
How is a BoP deficit usually financed?
(1) By the central bank selling foreign exchange from its reserves
(2) By printing more domestic currency
(3) By raising income tax
(4) By increasing exports immediately
= 1
~ A BoP deficit means autonomous payments exceed receipts. The central bank meets the gap by selling foreign exchange, so official reserves decline.

Q 11 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
The foreign exchange rate is:
(1) The interest rate on foreign loans
(2) The price of one currency in terms of another
(3) The rate of growth of exports
(4) The tax on imports
= 2
~ The exchange rate tells how many units of domestic currency are needed to buy one unit of a foreign currency, for example ₹83 per US dollar.

Q 12 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
Under a flexible (floating) exchange rate system, the exchange rate is determined by:
(1) The government alone
(2) The IMF
(3) The price of gold
(4) Market forces of demand for and supply of foreign exchange
= 4
~ In a flexible system the central bank does not fix the rate; it moves with demand and supply in the foreign exchange market.

Q 13 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
If the exchange rate changes from ₹80 to ₹84 per US dollar under a flexible system, the rupee has:
(1) Depreciated
(2) Appreciated
(3) Been revalued
(4) Not changed in value
= 1
~ More rupees are now needed to buy one dollar, so the rupee has lost value: it has depreciated, and the dollar has appreciated.

Q 14 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
What is the likely effect of a depreciation of the rupee?
(1) Imports become cheaper for Indians
(2) Indian goods become costlier for foreigners
(3) Indian exports become cheaper for foreigners and imports become costlier for Indians
(4) There is no effect on trade
= 3
~ After depreciation, foreigners need fewer units of their currency to buy Indian goods, so exports rise; Indians need more rupees to buy foreign goods, so imports tend to fall.

Q 15 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
'Devaluation' of a currency refers to:
(1) A fall in the currency's value due to market forces
(2) A rise in the value of the currency
(3) Removal of the currency from circulation
(4) A deliberate increase in the price of foreign currency by the government under a fixed exchange rate system, reducing the value of the domestic currency
= 4
~ Depreciation happens through the market in a flexible system; devaluation is an official decision in a fixed system. The opposite of devaluation is revaluation.

Q 16 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
A 'managed floating' exchange rate system is one in which:
(1) The government fixes the rate permanently
(2) The rate is largely market-determined, but the central bank buys and sells foreign currency to moderate its movements
(3) Only gold is used for payments
(4) There is no foreign exchange market
= 2
~ Managed floating, also called dirty floating, mixes the flexible and fixed systems. The central bank intervenes when it thinks movements are excessive.

Q 17 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
Which of the following adds to the supply of foreign exchange in India?
(1) Indians travelling abroad
(2) Indian firms importing machinery
(3) Exports by Indian firms, foreign investment into India, spending by foreign tourists and remittances from abroad
(4) Indian students paying fees to foreign universities
= 3
~ Foreign exchange flows in when foreigners buy Indian goods and services or assets, and when Indians abroad send money home. The other options create demand for foreign exchange.

Q 18 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
Which of the following creates demand for US dollars in India?
(1) An Indian company importing machinery from the USA
(2) An American tourist spending money in Goa
(3) A US company setting up a factory in India
(4) An Indian software firm exporting services to the USA
= 1
~ To pay for imports, the Indian firm must buy dollars. The other three bring dollars into India and add to their supply.

Q 19 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
Under a flexible exchange rate, if Indians' demand for US dollars rises while supply is unchanged, then:
(1) The rupee appreciates
(2) The exchange rate stays the same
(3) The dollar depreciates
(4) The dollar appreciates and the rupee depreciates
= 4
~ Higher demand raises the price of the dollar in rupees. More rupees are needed per dollar, so the rupee depreciates.

Q 20 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
The real exchange rate is:
(1) The exchange rate fixed by the government
(2) The relative price of foreign goods in terms of domestic goods, that is, eP(f) ÷ P
(3) The nominal exchange rate in the base year
(4) The interest rate in foreign countries
= 2
~ The real exchange rate adjusts the nominal rate e for the price levels abroad (Pf) and at home (P). It measures a country's international competitiveness.
