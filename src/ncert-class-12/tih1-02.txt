Q 1 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Who deciphered the Brahmi and Kharosthi scripts in the 1830s, making it possible to read the Ashokan inscriptions?
(1) James Prinsep
(2) Alexander Cunningham
(3) William Jones
(4) John Marshall
= 1
~ Prinsep was an officer in the mint of the East India Company. His work showed that the king called "Piyadassi" in the inscriptions was Ashoka.

Q 2 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
In the inscriptions, Ashoka often referred to himself as:
(1) Maharajadhiraja
(2) Devanampiya Piyadassi, "beloved of the gods" and "pleasant to behold"
(3) Chakravartin
(4) Devaputra
= 2
~ Only a few inscriptions mention his name, Asoka. Most use these titles.

Q 3 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
The sixth century BCE is seen as a major turning point in early Indian history because:
(1) It saw early states, cities, the growing use of iron, the development of coinage and new systems of thought like Buddhism and Jainism
(2) The Harappan cities were built then
(3) The Vedas were first composed then
(4) The Mughals arrived
= 1
~ Buddhist and Jaina texts mention sixteen states known as mahajanapadas, such as Vajji, Magadha, Koshala, Kuru, Panchala, Gandhara and Avanti.

Q 4 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Which of the following reasons have been given for Magadha becoming the most powerful mahajanapada?
1. Fertile land and high agricultural productivity
2. Access to iron mines in present-day Jharkhand
3. Elephants from the forests, used in the army
4. Rivers like the Ganga and its tributaries for cheap communication
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 1, 3 and 4 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2, 3 and 4
= 4
~ Early Buddhist and Jaina writers also credited the policies of ambitious kings like Bimbisara, Ajatasattu and Mahapadma Nanda, and their ministers.

Q 5 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
The early capital of Magadha, Rajagaha, was a fortified settlement among hills. In the fourth century BCE, the capital was shifted to:
(1) Pataliputra
(2) Vaishali
(3) Ujjayini
(4) Taxila
= 1
~ Pataliputra (present-day Patna) commanded routes along the Ganga, which helped Magadha control trade.

Q 6 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I

(2) An ambassador of the Greek ruler Seleucus Nicator at the court of Chandragupta Maurya
(3) A minister of Ashoka
(4) A Roman merchant

~ Megasthenes described a committee with six sub-committees to coordinate military activity, among other things.

Q 7 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
The Arthashastra, an important source for Mauryan administration, is traditionally attributed to:
(1) Banabhatta
(2) Harisena
(3) Kalidasa
(4) Kautilya (Chanakya)
= 4
~ Parts of it are believed to go back to the time of Chandragupta Maurya. Other sources include Buddhist, Jaina and Puranic texts and Ashoka's inscriptions.

Q 8 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Which of the following were the five major political centres of the Mauryan empire?
(1) Pataliputra, Mathura, Kanchi, Madurai and Ujjayini
(2) Pataliputra, Taxila, Ujjayini, Tosali and Suvarnagiri
(3) Rajagaha, Vaishali, Shravasti, Kosambi and Champa
(4) Taxila, Peshawar, Kabul, Kandahar and Herat
= 2
~ Taxila and Ujjayini were on important long-distance trade routes. Suvarnagiri was important for gold mines in Karnataka.

Q 9 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Ashoka appointed special officers known as dhamma mahamattas to:
(1) Collect land revenue
(2) Command the army
(3) Spread the message of dhamma, his principles of conduct
(4) Supervise foreign trade
= 3
~ Ashoka's dhamma included respect towards elders, generosity to Brahmanas and those who renounced worldly life, treating slaves and servants kindly, and respect for other religions.

Q 10 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
The Kushanas, who ruled over a vast kingdom from Central Asia to north-west India (c. first century BCE to first century CE), adopted the title:
(1) Devaputra, "son of god"
(2) Devanampiya
(3) Samrat
(4) Maharaja of Kosala
= 1
~ Colossal statues of Kushana rulers have been found in a shrine at Mat near Mathura. Many historians feel they wanted to present themselves as god-like.

Q 11 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
The Prayaga Prashasti, a eulogy of the Gupta ruler Samudragupta, was composed by:
(1) Kalidasa
(2) Harisena, his court poet
(3) Kautilya
(4) Banabhatta
= 2
~ It was inscribed on the Allahabad Pillar. Prashastis were written in praise of rulers and must be read with care.

Q 12 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
The Tamil Sangam texts mention chiefs of three ruling lineages in Tamilakam. They were:
(1) The Satavahanas, Shakas and Kushanas
(2) The Pallavas, Chalukyas and Rashtrakutas
(3) The Cholas, Cheras and Pandyas

Q 13 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Which of the following agricultural changes helped increase production in this period?
1. The use of the iron ploughshare
2. Transplantation of paddy in the Ganga valley
3. Irrigation through wells, tanks and canals
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ The benefits were not shared equally. Land owners became richer while landless labourers did not.

Q 14 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
The term "gahapati" in Pali texts referred to:
(1) A priest
(2) The owner, master or head of a household, who controlled the land, animals and people in it
(3) A soldier
(4) A king's minister
= 2
~ The term was also used for rich people in towns, such as merchants. In Tamil texts, large landowners were called vellalar.

Q 15 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
The earliest coins of the subcontinent, used from about the sixth century BCE, were:
(1) Gold coins of the Guptas
(2) Roman gold coins
(3) Kushana gold coins
(4) Punch-marked coins made of silver and copper
= 4
~ The first gold coins were issued around the first century CE by the Kushanas. The Indo-Greeks issued the first coins bearing names and images of rulers.

Q 16 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Which of the following is true about the Yaudheyas?
(1) They were Greek rulers
(2) They were Kushana officials
(3) They were tribal republics of Punjab and Haryana who issued thousands of copper coins
(4) They were Chinese traders
= 3
~ The Yaudheya coins show that these groups were active in trade, even though they were not ruled by kings.

Q 17 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Prabhavati Gupta, whose land grant is studied in the chapter, was:
(1) The daughter of the Gupta ruler Chandragupta II, married into the Vakataka family
(2) A queen of the Mauryas
(3) A Buddhist nun
(4) A Kushana princess
= 1
~ According to the Dharmashastras, women were not supposed to have independent access to land. Her grant shows that such rules were not always followed.

Q 18 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Land grants recorded in inscriptions were usually given to:
(1) Foreign traders
(2) Soldiers only
(3) Slaves
(4) Religious institutions or Brahmanas
= 4
~ Some historians see land grants as part of a strategy to extend agriculture to new areas. Others see them as a sign of weakening royal power.

Q 19 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Which of the following is a limitation of inscriptions as a source of history, as discussed in the chapter?
(1) Inscriptions are always written in English
(2) Inscriptions record everything about ordinary people
(3) Letters are very faintly engraved, some are damaged, and inscriptions mostly record what their makers wanted to say, often about kings

Q 20 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
"Shrenis" mentioned in sources of the period were:
(1) Priests
(2) Royal officials
(3) Guilds or associations of craft producers and merchants
(4) Military commanders

~ Guilds probably procured raw materials, regulated production and marketed finished goods. Some also gave gifts to religious institutions.
