# NCERT Class 12 Political Science, Politics in India since Independence, Chapter 3: Politics of Planned Development

Q 1 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The Planning Commission of India was set up in 1950 by:
(1) An Act of Parliament
(2) A constitutional amendment
(3) A simple resolution of the Government of India
(4) An order of the Supreme Court
= 3
~ The Planning Commission was not created by the Constitution or a law; it was set up by a government resolution in March 1950, with the Prime Minister as its chairperson.

Q 2 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
In 1944 a group of big industrialists drew up a plan for setting up a planned economy in India, in which the state would make major investments in industry. This is known as the:
(1) Bombay Plan
(2) Gandhian Plan
(3) People's Plan
(4) Harrod-Domar Plan
= 1
~ The Bombay Plan shows that there was broad agreement, even among industrialists, that the state should play a big role in economic development.

Q 3 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The First Five Year Plan (1951–1956) gave the highest priority to:
(1) Heavy industries
(2) Space research
(3) Defence production
(4) Agriculture, including dams and irrigation
= 4
~ Drafted under economist K.N. Raj's influence, the First Plan focused on agriculture, which had suffered from Partition, and invested in dams and irrigation such as the Bhakra Nangal project.

Q 4 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The Second Five Year Plan, which stressed heavy industries, was drafted under the leadership of:
(1) K.N. Raj
(2) P.C. Mahalanobis
(3) J.C. Kumarappa
(4) Chaudhary Charan Singh
= 2
~ The statistician P.C. Mahalanobis designed the Second Plan (1956–61), which aimed at quick structural change through heavy industry, with the public sector in the lead.

Q 5 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which of the following was a key debate in India's early planning, according to the chapter?
(1) Whether to have elections or not
(2) Agriculture versus industry, and the role of the public versus the private sector
(3) Whether to adopt a monarchy
(4) Whether to abolish the Constitution
= 2
~ Critics argued that the Second Plan neglected agriculture. There was also a debate about how much of the economy the state should control and how much should be left to private enterprise.

Q 6 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
A Gandhian economist, J.C. Kumarappa, proposed an alternative model of development that emphasised:
(1) Heavy industries in the public sector
(2) Rapid urbanisation
(3) Foreign investment
(4) Rural industrialisation and a decentralised, village-centred economy
= 4
~ Kumarappa's ideas, in works like Economy of Permanence, favoured small-scale and village industries, in line with Gandhi's vision of self-reliant villages.

Q 7 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which leader, who left the Congress, argued that planning was urban and industry-centred and neglected agriculture and farmers?
(1) Chaudhary Charan Singh
(2) P.C. Mahalanobis
(3) Jawaharlal Nehru
(4) K.N. Raj
= 1
~ Charan Singh was a strong voice for the interests of farmers and argued that agriculture should be at the centre of planning.

Q 8 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
India's economic model in the early decades, with both a public and a private sector, is called:
(1) A capitalist economy
(2) A communist economy
(3) A mixed economy
(4) A barter economy
= 3
~ India combined features of capitalism and socialism: the private sector worked alongside a large public sector. Critics argued that it combined the disadvantages of both.

Q 9 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which of the following was a criticism of India's development strategy mentioned in the chapter?
(1) It spent too little on industry
(2) It abolished all private property
(3) It gave too much power to state governments
(4) Too many controls and licences hampered the private sector, while the public sector often became inefficient, and the poor did not benefit enough
= 4
~ The "licence-permit raj" restricted private enterprise; public sector units protected from competition often made losses; and inequalities persisted. These criticisms later led to economic reforms.

Q 10 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The abolition of zamindari and land reforms in the early years of independence aimed at:
(1) Ending the system of large landowners who did not cultivate the land, and giving land to cultivators
(2) Increasing the power of landlords
(3) Selling land to foreign companies
(4) Ending agriculture altogether
= 1
~ Zamindari abolition was carried out in most states, but land ceilings and tenancy reforms were less successful because landowners resisted them.

Q 11 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The "Green Revolution" of the 1960s mainly involved:
(1) Planting trees in cities
(2) Banning chemical fertilisers
(3) High-yielding varieties of seeds, fertilisers and irrigation, which raised the production of wheat and rice
(4) Nationalising all farms
= 3
~ The Green Revolution greatly increased food grain production, especially in Punjab, Haryana and western Uttar Pradesh. The chapter notes it also increased regional and class inequalities.

Q 12 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which of the following was a negative effect of the Green Revolution mentioned in the chapter?
(1) India became more dependent on food imports
(2) It benefited richer peasants and some regions more than others, increasing inequalities
(3) It reduced wheat production
(4) It ended irrigation
= 2
~ Farmers who could afford inputs and irrigation gained most. The rise of a class of middle and rich farmers also changed politics in some states.

Q 13 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
"Operation Flood", the cooperative dairy movement that made India a leading milk producer, is linked with:
(1) Verghese Kurien and Amul in Anand, Gujarat
(2) M.S. Swaminathan in Punjab
(3) Sardar Patel in Kerala
(4) C. Rajagopalachari in Madras
= 1
~ Operation Flood, launched in 1970, spread the Anand model of milk cooperatives across the country. It is often called the White Revolution.

Q 14 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
In 2015 the Planning Commission was replaced by:
(1) The Finance Commission
(2) The Inter-State Council
(3) The National Development Council
(4) NITI Aayog
= 4
~ NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India) was set up on 1 January 2015 as a policy think tank, replacing the Planning Commission.

Q 15 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The chapter discusses how decisions about development are also political decisions. Which example shows this?
(1) The weather in Delhi
(2) The rules of cricket
(3) Disputes over mining iron ore in tribal areas, where development projects affect the land and livelihood of local people
(4) The colour of currency notes
= 3
~ Decisions like where to mine or build dams involve trade-offs between growth, the environment and people's livelihoods. Who gains and who loses is a political question.

Q 16 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which of the following was a broad consensus among India's leaders at independence, according to the chapter?
(1) That development should be left entirely to the market
(2) That the government had a key role in planning development, which should include economic growth and social and economic justice
(3) That industry should be banned
(4) That India should copy the Soviet model exactly
= 2
~ Leaders differed on details, but most agreed that the state should guide development to reduce poverty and inequality, rather than leave it all to the market.

Q 17 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The Kerala model of development, mentioned in the chapter, is known for:
(1) The highest industrial output in India
(2) Large heavy industries
(3) Low literacy
(4) Achievements in education, health, land reforms and poverty reduction despite modest economic growth
= 4
~ Kerala focused on human development and decentralised planning. The chapter notes it as an alternative path, while also mentioning its limits.

Q 18 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Why was the Second Five Year Plan criticised for ignoring agriculture?
(1) Because it banned farming
(2) Because its focus on heavy industry left fewer resources for agriculture, and the country faced food shortages later
(3) Because it was written by farmers
(4) Because it gave too much land to industry for free
= 2
~ Critics argued that agriculture needed more investment and that the neglect contributed to food shortages in the mid-1960s, which led to the Green Revolution strategy.

Q 19 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which of the following statements about the public sector, as discussed in the chapter, is correct?
(1) It produced nothing
(2) It was set up only after 1991
(3) It helped build basic industries and infrastructure, though many units became inefficient and loss-making over time
(4) It was run by private companies
= 3
~ The public sector created steel, power and heavy industries that private capital could not build. Its later inefficiency was one reason for the economic reforms.

Q 20 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which country's planning experience influenced the idea of five year plans in India?
(1) The Soviet Union
(2) The United States
(3) Japan
(4) The United Kingdom
= 1
~ The USSR had pioneered five year plans. India adopted the idea of planning but combined it with democracy and a private sector, unlike the Soviet system.
