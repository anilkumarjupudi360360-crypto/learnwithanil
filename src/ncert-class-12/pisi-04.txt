Q 1 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The central idea of India's foreign policy under Jawaharlal Nehru was:
(1) Non-alignment: keeping away from the two Cold War military blocs led by the USA and the USSR
(2) A military alliance with the USA
(3) A military alliance with the USSR
(4) Isolation from world affairs
= 1
~ Non-alignment allowed India to take independent positions on world issues, to get help from both blocs and to support decolonisation, without joining either military alliance.

Q 2 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which article of the Indian Constitution, in the Directive Principles, asks the state to promote international peace and security?
(1) Article 14
(2) Article 32
(3) Article 51
(4) Article 370
= 3
~ Article 51 directs the state to promote international peace, maintain just and honourable relations between nations, respect international law and settle disputes by arbitration.

Q 3 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The Panchsheel, the five principles of peaceful coexistence, was signed in 1954 between India and:
(1) Pakistan
(2) China
(3) Nepal
(4) The USSR
= 2
~ Nehru and Chinese Premier Zhou Enlai signed it. It included mutual respect for each other's territorial integrity and sovereignty, non-aggression, non-interference, equality and peaceful coexistence.

Q 4 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The Afro-Asian conference of 1955, which strengthened solidarity among newly independent nations, was held at:
(1) Belgrade
(2) Cairo
(3) Colombo
(4) Bandung
= 4
~ The Bandung Conference in Indonesia was an important step towards the Non-Aligned Movement, whose first summit followed in 1961.

Q 5 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The first summit of the Non-Aligned Movement was held in 1961 at:
(1) New Delhi
(2) Jakarta
(3) Havana
(4) Belgrade
= 4
~ The Belgrade summit was hosted by Yugoslavia. Nehru, Tito (Yugoslavia), Nasser (Egypt), Sukarno (Indonesia) and Nkrumah (Ghana) were among its founders.

Q 6 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
In which year did the war between India and China take place?
(1) 1962
(2) 1965
(3) 1971
(4) 1999
= 1
~ In October 1962 China launched attacks in the eastern (NEFA, now Arunachal Pradesh) and western (Aksai Chin, Ladakh) sectors. India's defeat hurt its image and Nehru's standing.

Q 7 | pol | Politics in India since Independence

(1) Y.B. Chavan
(2) Jagjivan Ram
(3) V.K. Krishna Menon
(4) Swaran Singh
= 3
~ Krishna Menon had to leave the cabinet. The war also led to a no-confidence motion against the Nehru government in the Lok Sabha.

Q 8 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
In 1959 the Dalai Lama crossed into India and was given asylum. This added to tension between India and China mainly because:
(1) India refused to trade with China
(2) China objected to India giving asylum to the Tibetan leader and his followers
(3) The Dalai Lama was an Indian citizen
(4) India had annexed Tibet
= 2
~ After a failed uprising in Tibet, the Dalai Lama fled to India. China accused India of allowing anti-China activities, adding to the border dispute.

Q 9 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The 1965 war between India and Pakistan ended with the Tashkent Agreement, signed by:
(1) Indira Gandhi and Z.A. Bhutto
(2) Lal Bahadur Shastri and Ayub Khan
(3) Jawaharlal Nehru and Liaquat Ali Khan
(4) Atal Bihari Vajpayee and Nawaz Sharif

Q 10 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
In August 1971, before the war that led to the creation of Bangladesh, India signed a twenty-year treaty with:
(1) The United States
(2) China
(3) The United Kingdom
(4) The Soviet Union
= 4
~ The Indo-Soviet Treaty of Peace and Friendship assured India of Soviet support, at a time when the USA and China were moving closer to Pakistan.

Q 11 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The agreement signed between Indira Gandhi and Z.A. Bhutto in 1972, after the 1971 war, is known as the:
(1) Shimla Agreement
(2) Tashkent Agreement
(3) Lahore Declaration
(4) Indus Waters Treaty
= 1
~ The Shimla Agreement of July 1972 formalised the return of peace and committed both countries to settle differences bilaterally.

Q 12 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The 1971 war led to the creation of Bangladesh. What triggered the crisis in East Pakistan?
(1) A border dispute with China
(2) A dispute over river waters with India
(3) The refusal of the Pakistani rulers to accept the election verdict won by the Awami League led by Sheikh Mujib-ur Rahman, followed by repression in East Pakistan
(4) A famine in West Pakistan
= 3
~ The Pakistani army cracked down on the people of East Pakistan, and a huge number of refugees came to India. India supported the liberation struggle, and Bangladesh became independent in December 1971.

Q 13 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
India's first nuclear explosion, described as a "peaceful nuclear explosion", was conducted in:
(1) 1962
(2) 1965
(3) 1998
(4) 1974

~ The test was conducted at Pokhran in May 1974. India conducted a series of nuclear tests in May 1998 and declared itself a nuclear weapons state.

Q 14 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Why did India refuse to sign the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (NPT) of 1968?
(1) Because India did not want nuclear energy
(2) Because India considered it discriminatory, as it allowed five countries to keep nuclear weapons while forbidding others

Q 16 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which of the following best describes non-alignment, as explained in the chapter?
(1) Taking an independent stand on world issues on the merits of each case, rather than following either bloc
(2) Neutrality in every issue, with no opinion at all
(3) Joining whichever bloc gave more aid
(4) Keeping no relations with any country
= 1
~ Non-alignment was not neutrality: India actively took positions, for example on decolonisation and disarmament, but decided them independently.

Q 17 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which of the following was a criticism of India's non-aligned policy mentioned in the chapter?
(1) That India joined NATO
(2) That India refused to talk to any country
(3) That it was unprincipled, and that India in practice sided with one bloc at times, for example with the Soviet Union in 1971
(4) That India had no foreign policy
= 3
~ Critics said India's pursuit of national interest sometimes looked like a tilt, especially after the 1971 Indo-Soviet treaty. Supporters reply that national interest guided its choices.

Q 18 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The policy of non-alignment helped India in which way, according to the chapter?
(1) It isolated India completely
(2) It made India a member of a military alliance
(3) It ended all wars on India's borders
(4) It allowed India to get economic and military help from both blocs and to play a role in world affairs larger than its power
= 4
~ By staying out of military blocs, India could deal with both superpowers and lead newly independent countries on issues like decolonisation and disarmament.

Q 19 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which of the following statements about India–China relations in the early 1950s is correct?
(1) India and China fought a war in 1950
(2) Relations were friendly, summed up in the slogan "Hindi-Chini bhai-bhai", and India recognised China's control over Tibet
(3) India refused to recognise the People's Republic of China
(4) China supported India in the 1962 war
= 2
~ India was among the first countries to recognise the Communist government in China. Friendship gave way to tension after disputes over Tibet and the border.

Q 20 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Why did India want to keep its foreign policy independent soon after independence, according to the chapter?
(1) Because it had just won freedom from colonial rule and wanted to protect its sovereignty, pursue its own development and support other colonised countries
(2) Because it wanted to become a colonial power
(3) Because it had no army
(4) Because Britain asked it to
= 1
~ A newly free country did not want to be dragged into the conflicts of others. An independent foreign policy served India's interests in peace and development.
