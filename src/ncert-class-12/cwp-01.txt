# NCERT Class 12 Political Science, Contemporary World Politics, Chapter 1: The End of Bipolarity
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The Berlin Wall, which symbolised the division between the capitalist and communist worlds, was built in 1961 and brought down by people in:
(1) 1985
(2) November 1989
(3) December 1991
(4) 2001
= 2
~ The fall of the Wall in November 1989 was followed by the unification of Germany and the collapse of communist regimes in eastern Europe. The Soviet Union itself broke up in 1991.

Q 2 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The Soviet Union came into being after which revolution?
(1) The French Revolution of 1789
(2) The Chinese Revolution of 1949
(3) The socialist revolution in Russia in 1917
(4) The Iranian Revolution of 1979
= 3
~ The Bolshevik revolution of 1917 was inspired by socialist ideals. The USSR became a superpower after the Second World War, leading the "second world" of communist countries.

Q 3 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following was a feature of the Soviet system, as described in the chapter?
(1) A centrally planned economy, state ownership and control, and the dominance of the Communist Party
(2) A free market with many competing parties
(3) Private ownership of all industries
(4) Regular change of governments through elections between parties
= 1
~ The Soviet system gave citizens basic needs like health care, education and jobs, but it was bureaucratic and authoritarian, with one party and little freedom.

Q 4 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Mikhail Gorbachev's reforms in the Soviet Union in the late 1980s were called:
(1) Shock therapy and privatisation
(2) Glasnost and détente
(3) New Deal and Marshall Plan
(4) Perestroika (restructuring) and glasnost (openness)
= 4
~ Gorbachev tried to reform the economy and open up the system. The reforms released forces he could not control, and the Soviet Union broke up in 1991.

Q 5 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
In which year did the Soviet Union disintegrate?
(1) 1985
(2) 1989
(3) 1990
(4) 1991
= 4
~ In December 1991 Russia, Ukraine and Belarus announced the end of the USSR, and Gorbachev resigned. Fifteen independent countries emerged.

Q 6 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
After the disintegration of the USSR, which grouping was formed by most of the former Soviet republics?
(1) The European Union
(2) The Commonwealth of Independent States (CIS)
(3) NATO
(4) ASEAN
= 2
~ The CIS was formed in December 1991. The Baltic states (Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania) chose to stay out of it.

Q 7 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which republics were the first to declare independence from the Soviet Union, in the early 1990s?
(1) Ukraine, Belarus and Russia
(2) Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan and Tajikistan
(3) Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania
(4) Georgia, Armenia and Azerbaijan
= 3
~ The three Baltic republics, which had been forcibly incorporated into the USSR, led the move for independence. Lithuania declared independence in March 1990.

Q 8 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which country was accepted as the successor state of the Soviet Union and inherited its seat in the UN Security Council?
(1) Russia
(2) Ukraine
(3) Kazakhstan
(4) Belarus
= 1
~ Russia took over the USSR's permanent seat in the Security Council and its international commitments, including treaties.

Q 9 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The rapid transition from communism to capitalism in the former Soviet countries, promoted by the World Bank and the IMF, was called:
(1) Shock therapy
(2) Perestroika
(3) Glasnost
(4) Non-alignment
= 1
~ Shock therapy meant a sudden shift to private ownership, free trade and market prices. It caused great economic hardship and inequality in the 1990s.

Q 10 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The sale of valuable state-owned industries in Russia at very low prices during shock therapy has been described as:
(1) The great leap forward
(2) The iron curtain
(3) The largest garage sale in history
(4) The new economic policy
= 3
~ State assets were sold off cheaply, often to well-connected people. The rouble lost value, inflation soared and many people lost their savings.

Q 11 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following was a consequence of the disintegration of the Soviet Union, according to the chapter?
(1) The start of the Cold War
(2) The end of the Cold War and of the bipolar world, with the USA emerging as the sole superpower
(3) The strengthening of the Warsaw Pact
(4) The return of colonial empires
= 2
~ The end of the USSR ended the ideological contest between communism and capitalism. Many new countries emerged, and some joined the European Union and NATO.

Q 12 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following was a reason for the disintegration of the USSR mentioned in the chapter?
(1) Military defeat by the USA
(2) Too much democracy and freedom in the Soviet Union
(3) Excessive economic growth
(4) Economic stagnation, the burden of the arms race, the dominance of one party and the rise of nationalism in the republics
= 4
~ The economy had stagnated, huge resources went into the arms race and Afghanistan, and people in the republics resented domination. Gorbachev's reforms raised expectations they could not meet.

Q 13 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Who was the first elected President of Russia, who played a key role in the break-up of the Soviet Union?
(1) Mikhail Gorbachev
(2) Joseph Stalin
(3) Boris Yeltsin
(4) Vladimir Putin
= 3
~ Boris Yeltsin resisted the coup of August 1991 against Gorbachev and then led Russia out of the Soviet Union.

Q 14 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
In which of the following regions did conflicts and civil wars break out after the break-up of the USSR, as mentioned in the chapter?
(1) Chechnya, Tajikistan and the Caucasus region
(2) Scandinavia
(3) The Iberian Peninsula
(4) North America
= 1
~ Russia faced a violent secessionist movement in Chechnya, and Tajikistan had a civil war that lasted for about ten years, until 2001.

Q 15 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The term "second world" during the Cold War referred to:
(1) The developing countries of Asia and Africa
(2) The countries of western Europe
(3) The non-aligned countries
(4) The communist countries of eastern Europe, led by the Soviet Union
= 4
~ After the Second World War, the countries of eastern Europe that the Soviet army had liberated came under Soviet control and formed the socialist bloc, the "second world".

Q 16 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
How did India's relations with Russia develop after the end of the Soviet Union?
(1) India broke off all relations with Russia
(2) India and Russia continued their close relationship, with cooperation in defence, energy, trade and a shared vision of a multipolar world
(3) Russia joined India's rivals in a military alliance
(4) India stopped buying any defence equipment
= 2
~ Russia has remained an important partner of India, especially for military hardware and energy. Both favour a multipolar world order.

Q 17 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following statements about the Soviet economy is supported by the chapter?
(1) It had no industry at all
(2) It ensured a minimum standard of living for all citizens and had a vast industrial base, but it fell behind the West, especially in technology and consumer goods
(3) It was a fully market-based economy
(4) It had no planning
= 2
~ The Soviet state provided jobs, health care, education and subsidised necessities. But the system was rigid, lacked innovation and failed to keep up with the West.

Q 18 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The Soviet intervention that drained its resources and is mentioned among the causes of its weakening took place in:
(1) Cuba in 1962
(2) Vietnam in 1965
(3) Korea in 1950
(4) Afghanistan in 1979
= 4
~ The war in Afghanistan, which lasted until 1989, weakened the Soviet Union militarily and economically and added to public discontent.

Q 19 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Why does the chapter say the end of the Soviet Union mattered for countries like India?
(1) Because the end of bipolarity changed the international system, the balance of power and the options available to developing countries
(2) Because India was part of the Soviet Union
(3) Because India joined the Warsaw Pact
(4) Because India stopped trading with all countries
= 1
~ India had relied on the USSR for support and defence supplies. The new world order led India to rethink its foreign and economic policies.

Q 20 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following was a political consequence of shock therapy in Russia, as discussed in the chapter?
(1) A stable multi-party democracy was immediately established everywhere
(2) Collective farms were strengthened
(3) The creation of a mafia and the rise of a class of very rich oligarchs, with weak democratic institutions
(4) The Communist Party returned to sole power
= 3
~ Shock therapy did not create a working market economy overnight. Instead, a few people grabbed state assets, inequality grew and democratic institutions remained weak.
