# NCERT Class 12 Political Science, Contemporary World Politics, Chapter 2: Contemporary Centres of Power
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The treaty signed in 1992 that established the European Union is known as the:
(1) Treaty of Rome
(2) Treaty of Versailles
(3) Maastricht Treaty
(4) Treaty of Paris
= 3
~ The Maastricht Treaty created the European Union with common foreign and security policies, cooperation on justice and home affairs, and the path to a single currency.

Q 2 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The American programme of economic aid for the reconstruction of Europe after the Second World War was called the:
(1) Marshall Plan
(2) Monroe Doctrine
(3) Truman Doctrine
(4) New Deal
= 1
~ Under the Marshall Plan the USA helped rebuild Europe's economy. The Organisation for European Economic Cooperation (OEEC) was set up in 1948 to channel the aid.

Q 3 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The European Economic Community, which preceded the EU, was created in 1957 by the:
(1) Maastricht Treaty
(2) Treaty of Lisbon
(3) Treaty of Westphalia
(4) Treaty of Rome
= 4
~ The Treaty of Rome created the EEC and the European Atomic Energy Community. The process of integration later grew into the European Union.

Q 4 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The common currency of many European Union countries is the:
(1) Pound
(2) Euro
(3) Franc
(4) Mark
= 2
~ The euro was introduced in 1999 (notes and coins in 2002). Some EU members, such as Denmark and Sweden, chose not to adopt it.

Q 5 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following shows that the European Union has political and military influence, not only economic power, according to the chapter?
(1) It has no army
(2) Two of its members held permanent seats in the UN Security Council, and its members have large defence budgets and nuclear weapons
(3) It has never taken any position on world affairs
(4) It has only one member
= 2
~ France (and the United Kingdom, until it left the EU) are permanent Security Council members. The EU also influences world affairs through diplomacy, trade and aid.

Q 6 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
In a referendum in 2016, the people of which country voted to leave the European Union?
(1) France
(2) Germany
(3) Italy
(4) The United Kingdom
= 4
~ The vote is known as Brexit. The United Kingdom formally left the EU in January 2020.

Q 7 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
ASEAN was established in 1967 by the Bangkok Declaration. Which of the following was a founding member?
(1) Indonesia
(2) Vietnam
(3) Myanmar
(4) Cambodia
= 1
~ The five founders were Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore and Thailand. Brunei, Vietnam, Laos, Myanmar and Cambodia joined later.

Q 8 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The "ASEAN Way", as described in the chapter, refers to:
(1) A military alliance with the USA
(2) A single currency
(3) Informal, non-confrontational and cooperative interaction that respects national sovereignty
(4) Free movement of armies across borders
= 3
~ ASEAN works by consultation and consensus rather than binding rules, respecting the sovereignty of each member.

Q 9 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The ASEAN Community is built on three pillars. Which of the following is NOT one of them?
(1) ASEAN Security Community
(2) ASEAN Economic Community
(3) ASEAN Military Command
(4) ASEAN Socio-Cultural Community
= 3
~ ASEAN's three pillars are security, economic and socio-cultural communities. It has no joint military command.

Q 10 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The ASEAN Regional Forum (ARF), set up in 1994, deals mainly with:
(1) Political and security dialogue in the Asia-Pacific region
(2) Sports competitions
(3) Fixing oil prices
(4) A common currency for Asia
= 1
~ The ARF brings together ASEAN members and other countries, including India, China and the USA, to discuss security issues and build confidence.

Q 11 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
India's policy of closer engagement with South-East Asian countries, begun in the 1990s, was first called the "Look East" policy. It was later renamed:
(1) Neighbourhood First
(2) Connect Central Asia
(3) Panchsheel
(4) Act East Policy
= 4
~ India has signed free trade agreements with ASEAN and expanded connectivity and security cooperation with the region under these policies.

Q 12 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
China's economic reforms and "open door" policy began in 1978 under the leadership of:
(1) Mao Zedong
(2) Deng Xiaoping
(3) Zhou Enlai
(4) Xi Jinping
= 2
~ Deng Xiaoping opened China to foreign trade and investment, allowed private enterprise in steps and set up Special Economic Zones. China grew rapidly afterwards.

Q 13 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
China joined the World Trade Organization in:
(1) 2001
(2) 1978
(3) 1991
(4) 2010
= 1
~ WTO membership deepened China's integration into the world economy. It became the world's leading manufacturing and trading nation.

Q 14 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following is mentioned in the chapter as a negative aspect of China's economic growth?
(1) It has no industry
(2) Its exports have fallen
(3) Unemployment, inequality between rural and urban areas and between regions, environmental damage and corruption
(4) It has no foreign investment
= 3
~ Not everyone benefited equally. Coastal and urban areas grew richer than rural and interior areas, and pollution became serious.

Q 15 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following statements about Japan is correct, according to the chapter?
(1) Japan is not a member of the G-7
(2) Japan has the largest army in Asia
(3) Japan contributes nothing to the UN budget
(4) Japan has few natural resources and imports most raw materials, yet became one of the world's largest economies; its constitution renounces war
= 4
~ Japan was the only Asian member of the G-7 and a major contributor to the UN budget. Article 9 of its constitution renounces war as a means of settling disputes.

Q 16 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
South Korea's rapid economic growth from the 1960s to the 1980s is often called:
(1) The Great Leap Forward
(2) The Miracle on the Han River
(3) The Green Revolution
(4) The Asian Spring
= 2
~ South Korea grew into a major industrial economy through land reform, education, exports and large industrial groups. It became a member of the OECD in 1996.

Q 17 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Why does the chapter discuss "alternative centres of power"?
(1) To show that the USA has no power
(2) To show that only military power matters
(3) To show that the UN is the only power in the world
(4) To show that after the Cold War, regional groupings and rising economies like the EU, ASEAN and China could counter the dominance of the USA
= 4
~ Regional organisations and emerging powers offer other ways of exercising influence, through economic strength and cooperation, alongside the US-led order.

Q 18 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following is a reason given in the chapter for European countries coming together after the Second World War?
(1) The need to rebuild their economies and to avoid the wars that had devastated Europe
(2) A wish to colonise Asia again
(3) A plan to abolish all national governments
(4) A dispute with Japan
= 1
~ Two world wars had begun in Europe. Cooperation in trade and economic matters, encouraged by the USA during the Cold War, was seen as a way to secure peace and prosperity.

Q 19 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
India–China relations, as described in the chapter, include which of the following?
(1) No trade at all
(2) Border disputes and rivalry, but also growing trade and cooperation on some international issues
(3) A military alliance
(4) A common currency
= 2
~ After the 1962 war, relations improved gradually from the late 1970s and especially after Rajiv Gandhi's visit in 1988. Trade grew rapidly, though border tensions continue.

Q 20 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The ASEAN Free Trade Area (AFTA) aims at:
(1) A common army for the region
(2) A single parliament for the region
(3) Reducing tariffs and trade barriers among member countries
(4) Abolishing national borders
= 3
~ AFTA lowers tariffs within the region. ASEAN also aims at a single market and production base through the ASEAN Economic Community.
