Q 2 | geo | India: People and Economy

(1) Agriculture
(2) Industry
(3) Pilgrimage and fairs
(4) Natural erosion
= 2
~ Industries discharge wastes, polluted water, chemicals and heavy metals into water bodies, destroying their bio-systems.

Q 4 | geo | India: People and Economy

(1) One-tenth
(2) One-fourth
(3) One-half

~ Diarrhoea, intestinal worms and hepatitis are common diseases caused by contaminated water.

Q 5 | geo | India: People and Economy
The Namami Gange Programme, approved as a "Flagship Programme" by the Union Government in June 2014, aims at:
(1) Effective abatement of pollution and conservation and rejuvenation of the Ganga
(2) Building dams on the Ganga for power
(3) Interlinking the Ganga with peninsular rivers
(4) Increasing navigation only
= 1
~ Its objectives include sewerage treatment in towns, monitoring industrial effluents, river front development, afforestation, cleaning the river surface, developing "Ganga Grams" and creating public awareness.

(3) Rajasthan and Gujarat
(4) Odisha and Andhra Pradesh
= 2
~ These are the states through which the Ganga flows.

Q 7 | geo | India: People and Economy

(1) Combustion of fossil fuels
(2) Mining
(3) Industries
(4) Rainwater harvesting
= 4
~ These sources release oxides of sulphur and nitrogen, hydrocarbons, carbon dioxide, carbon monoxide, lead and asbestos.

Q 8 | geo | India: People and Economy
Consider the following statements about air pollution:
1. Smoky fog over cities, called urban smog, is caused by atmospheric pollution.
2. Air pollution can cause acid rain.
3. Rainwater analysis shows that the pH of the first rain after summer is always higher than that of later rains.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1
~ The pH of the first rain after summer is always lower (more acidic) than that of later rains, because it washes out the pollutants that have collected in the air.

Q 10 | geo | India: People and Economy

(1) Loudspeakers at festivals
(2) Traffic-generated noise
(3) Construction work
(4) Sirens

~ The intensity of traffic noise depends on the type of vehicle, aircraft or train and on the condition of the road and vehicle. In sea traffic, noise is confined to harbours.

Q 12 | geo | India: People and Economy
The Daurala case study near Meerut, discussed in the chapter, illustrates:
(1) The success of shifting cultivation
(2) A dam built to stop floods
(3) The "polluter pays" principle in action, with an NGO helping clean contaminated groundwater
(4) A new airport project
= 3
~ Untreated industrial waste water had contaminated groundwater with heavy metals; extra pipelines, desilting, rainwater harvesting and tree planting helped improve the situation.

Q 13 | geo | India: People and Economy
According to the chapter, about what share of India's urban growth after 1961 is attributed to rural-urban migration?
(1) About 5 per cent
(2) About 60 per cent
(3) About 90 per cent
(4) About 29 per cent
= 4
~ About 60 per cent of urban growth is due to natural increase. Migrants are pushed by low job opportunities in villages and pulled by the demand for labour in cities.

Q 14 | geo | India: People and Economy
The chapter's case study of Ramesh, a migrant welder from Odisha who worked in several cities and sends money home every year, mainly illustrates:
(1) Noise pollution
(2) Shifting cultivation
(3) Watershed management
(4) Rural-urban migration and the role of remittances
= 4
~ Ramesh's story, with jobs in places like Ludhiana and Surat, shows why people migrate from villages to cities and how their earnings support families back home.

Q 15 | geo | India: People and Economy
Dharavi, described in the chapter as Asia's largest slum, is located in:
(1) Delhi
(2) Kolkata
(3) Mumbai
(4) Chennai
= 3
~ Many Dharavi residents work in activities like leather tanning, pottery, embroidery, zari work, metalwork and furniture making.

Q 16 | geo | India: People and Economy
Which of the following is NOT a typical feature of slums described in the chapter?
(1) Dilapidated houses and poor ventilation
(2) Lack of basic amenities like drinking water, light and toilets
(3) Congested areas with narrow streets prone to fire hazards
(4) Spacious, well-planned houses with modern facilities
= 4
~ Slum dwellers mostly work in low-paid, high-risk, unorganised sectors and are vulnerable to poor health, drug abuse, crime and social exclusion.

Q 17 | geo | India: People and Economy
Which of the following types of wasteland is caused by natural agents, according to the chapter?
(1) Degraded shifting cultivation area
(2) Mining and industrial wastelands
(3) Gullied or ravinous land
(4) Degraded pastures
= 3
~ Natural wastelands include gullied/ravinous land, desert or coastal sands, barren rocky areas, steep slopes and glacial areas. The other three are human-induced.
