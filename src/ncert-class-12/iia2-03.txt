# NCERT Class 12 Fine Arts (Art & Culture), An Introduction to Indian Art Part II, Chapter 3: The Mughal School of Miniature Painting
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Babur greatly admired which painter, a master of the Herat school?
(1) Bihzad
(2) Mir Sayyid Ali
(3) Abd us Samad
(4) Mansur
= 1
~ Babur admired Bihzad of Herat and praised Shah Muzaffar for his hairstyles. His memoir, the Baburnama, began the Mughal tradition of memoirs.

Q 2 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
After his defeat by Sher Shah, Humayun took refuge at the court of:
(1) The Ottoman Sultan
(2) The Safavid ruler Shah Tahmasp of Persia
(3) The Uzbek Khan of Bukhara
(4) The Sultan of Gujarat
= 2
~ At Shah Tahmasp's court Humayun saw Persian miniature painting and manuscript production; he set up his court in Kabul in 1545.

Q 3 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Which two master artists did Humayun bring back to India from Persia?
(1) Basawan and Daswant
(2) Bihzad and Shah Muzaffar
(3) Mir Sayyid Ali and Abd us Samad
(4) Mansur and Abul Hasan
= 3
~ Humayun invited Mir Sayyid Ali and Abd us Samad, both noted for portraiture; they later supervised Akbar's Hamzanama.

Q 4 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The painting workshop founded by Humayun as a part of his library was called the:
(1) Suratkhana
(2) Mandi
(3) Karkhana
(4) Nigaar Khana
= 4
~ Humayun founded the Nigaar Khana and started the illustration of the Hamzanama, which Akbar continued.

Q 5 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Consider the following statements about the Hamzanama:
1. It is an illustrated account of the deeds of Hamza, the uncle of Prophet Muhammad.
2. It was painted on cloth, with paper backing that carried the text for the narrator.
3. It had about 14 volumes with about 1,400 illustrations.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ All three are given in the chapter. The large-format Hamzanama was painted in gouache (opaque water-based colours).

Q 6 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The Hamzanama took about fifteen years to complete and is dated:
(1) 1530–1545
(2) 1556–1570
(3) 1567–1582
(4) 1589–1604
= 3
~ It is dated 1567–1582 and was supervised by Mir Sayyid Ali and Abd us Samad. Its pages mix Persian floor patterns with Indian trees and creepers.

Q 7 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The Razmnama, produced under Akbar, is the Persian translation and illustration of:
(1) The Ramayana
(2) The Mahabharata
(3) The Panchatantra
(4) The Harivamsa
= 2
~ The Razmnama is the Persian Mahabharata. The Ramayana was translated and illustrated around the same time.

Q 8 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The Razmnama, with 169 paintings, was completed in 1589 under the supervision of which master artist?
(1) Daswant
(2) Miskin
(3) Govardhan
(4) Bichitra
= 1
~ Daswant supervised the Razmnama, completed in 1589 in ornate calligraphy with 169 paintings.

Q 9 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
According to Abul Fazl, how many artists worked in Akbar's royal atelier?
(1) More than 50
(2) More than 100
(3) More than 500
(4) About 20
= 2
~ Abul Fazl recorded that more than 100 artists worked in Akbar's atelier, which also had calligraphers, gilders and binders.

Q 10 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
In the making of a Mughal painting, the stage called 'rangamizi' refers to:
(1) Colouring
(2) The first composition
(3) Drawing the portraits
(4) Burnishing the finished work
= 1
~ The stages were tarh (composition), chiharanama (portraits and outlines) and rangamizi (colouring). The work was often done by a team.

Q 11 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Jahangir's interest in the scientific accuracy of depicting plants and animals is recorded in which memoir?
(1) Baburnama
(2) Akbarnama
(3) Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri
(4) Padshahnama
= 3
~ The Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri records it. Jahangir preferred fewer, finer works by single master artists to mass production.

Q 12 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Ustad Mansur, famous for his paintings of birds and animals, was given which title?
(1) Nadir al Zaman
(2) Jahangir-dad
(3) Shirin Qalam
(4) Nadir ul Asr
= 4
~ Mansur was called Nadir ul Asr, 'Wonder of the Age'. His works include a falcon on a bird rest and a zebra presented at Nowruz in 1621.

Q 13 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Abul Hasan, son of Aqa Riza, received which title from Jahangir?
(1) Nadir ul Asr
(2) Ustad
(3) Raja
(4) Nadir al Zaman
= 4
~ Abul Hasan was titled Nadir al Zaman. He painted Jahangir in Darbar (with Manohar) and Jahangir's Dream.

Q 14 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The 'muraqqa', which became popular under Jahangir, was:
(1) A royal chronicle
(2) A large wall hanging
(3) An album of individually mounted paintings with gold-illuminated margins
(4) A map of the empire
= 3
~ Muraqqas were albums of individual paintings whose margins were illuminated in gold with flora, fauna and figures.

Q 15 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
In Bichitra's painting 'Jahangir Enthroned on an Hourglass' (1625), the Persian calligraphy says that Jahangir prefers:
(1) Dervishes (Sufi saints) to kings
(2) Hunting to ruling
(3) Poetry to painting
(4) Persia to India
= 1
~ The painting shows Jahangir offering a book to Shaikh Husain of the Chishti shrine rather than to the kings who wait, among them King James I of England.

Q 16 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
In Abul Hasan's 'Jahangir's Dream', Jahangir is shown embracing:
(1) Sir Thomas Roe
(2) Shah Abbas of Persia
(3) King James I
(4) Prince Khurram
= 2
~ Jahangir embraces Shah Abbas; the lion and sheep are symbols, and angels hold a halo of the sun and moon.

Q 17 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The Padshahnama (Chronicles of the King) was one of the most ambitious painting projects of:
(1) Shah Jahan
(2) Akbar
(3) Jahangir
(4) Aurangzeb
= 1
~ Shah Jahan preferred idealisation, jewel-like colours and fine lines; the Padshahnama was his great painting project.

Q 18 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
In Mughal painting, the pigment ultramarine was obtained from:
(1) Cinnabar
(2) Orpiment
(3) Lapis lazuli
(4) Lampblack
= 3
~ Ultramarine came from lapis lazuli, vermilion from cinnabar, bright yellow from orpiment, white from ground shells and black from lampblack.

Q 19 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Mughal artists' brushes were made of:
(1) Camel hair
(2) Squirrel or kitten hair
(3) Goat hair
(4) Plant fibre
= 2
~ Brushes were made of squirrel or kitten hair, and finished paintings were burnished with agate.

Q 20 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The last Mughal ruler, also a poet and scholar, who was exiled to Burma by the British after the Revolt of 1857, was:
(1) Shah Alam II
(2) Muhammad Shah Rangila
(3) Akbar II
(4) Bahadur Shah Zafar
= 4
~ Bahadur Shah Zafar was the last Mughal ruler. Works made under Muhammad Shah Rangila, Shah Alam II and Bahadur Shah Zafar were the 'last flickers' of the style.
