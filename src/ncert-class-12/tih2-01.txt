Q 1 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Al-Biruni, the author of Kitab-ul-Hind, was born in 973 in:
(1) Tangier, Morocco
(2) Baghdad
(3) Herat
(4) Khwarizm, in present-day Uzbekistan
= 4
~ Al-Biruni was well versed in several languages, including Syriac, Arabic, Persian and Hebrew. He came to India after Sultan Mahmud of Ghazni invaded Khwarizm and took scholars to Ghazni.

Q 2 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Al-Biruni's Kitab-ul-Hind was written in:
(1) Persian
(2) Sanskrit
(3) Turkish
(4) Arabic
= 4
~ It is divided into 80 chapters on subjects such as religion, philosophy, festivals, astronomy, alchemy, social life, weights and measures, laws and metrology.

Q 3 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Al-Biruni identified several barriers to understanding India. Which of the following was among them?
1. Language, since Sanskrit was very different from Arabic and Persian
2. Differences in religious beliefs and practices
3. The self-absorption and insularity of the local population
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ To overcome these, he relied heavily on Sanskrit texts, especially Brahmanical works like the Vedas, the Puranas, the Bhagavad Gita, the works of Patanjali and the Manusmriti.

Q 4 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
In describing the caste system, Al-Biruni:
(1) Accepted the idea of pollution completely
(2) Compared it with the four social categories of ancient Persia, and rejected the notion of pollution as contrary to the laws of nature
(3) Said there was no caste system in India
(4) Praised untouchability
= 2
~ He tried to explain the caste system by looking for parallels in other societies, showing that social divisions were not unique to India.

Q 5 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Ibn Battuta, whose travel account is called the Rihla, was born in:
(1) Tangier, Morocco
(2) Samarqand
(3) Cairo
(4) Venice
= 1
~ Born in 1304 into a family of Islamic legal scholars, Ibn Battuta considered travel an important source of knowledge. He reached Sind in 1333.

Q 6 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Which Sultan of Delhi appointed Ibn Battuta as the qazi (judge) of Delhi?
(1) Alauddin Khalji
(2) Iltutmish
(3) Muhammad bin Tughlaq
(4) Firuz Shah Tughlaq
= 3
~ The Sultan was impressed by his scholarship. In 1342, Ibn Battuta was sent as the Sultan's envoy to the Mongol ruler of China.

Q 7 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Ibn Battuta described two strange things he saw in India in detail. They were:
(1) The coconut and the paan (betel leaf)
(2) The elephant and the tiger
(3) The temple and the mosque
(4) Tea and coffee
= 1
~ His descriptions of these unfamiliar things to readers in Morocco show how travellers tried to make the unknown understandable.

Q 8 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
According to Ibn Battuta, the postal system in India had two kinds. The horse post was called "uluq". The foot post, with stations at every third of a mile, was called:
(1) Barid
(2) Dawa
(3) Yam
(4) Sarai
= 2
~ The postal system was very efficient. Letters reached their destinations quickly, and even fruit could be sent from Khurasan.

Q 9 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Ibn Battuta wrote that Indian cities were:
(1) Empty and poor
(2) All in ruins
(3) Densely populated and prosperous, full of exciting opportunities for those with the necessary drive and resources
(4) Without markets
= 3
~ He described Delhi as a vast city with a great population, and Daulatabad as being no less. Markets were also places of social and economic activity.

Q 10 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Francois Bernier, who was in India from 1656 to 1668, was a:
(1) French physician, political philosopher and historian
(2) Portuguese sailor
(3) Italian Jesuit priest
(4) Dutch trader
= 1
~ He was closely associated with the Mughal court, first as physician to Prince Dara Shukoh and later as an intellectual and scientist with an Armenian Mughal noble, Danishmand Khan.

Q 11 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Bernier's account, "Travels in the Mogul Empire", was dedicated to:
(1) Louis XIV, the king of France
(2) Shah Jahan
(3) The Pope
(4) Queen Elizabeth
= 1
~ Bernier constantly compared what he saw in India with the situation in Europe, generally France, and often presented India as inferior.

Q 12 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Bernier claimed that a fundamental difference between Mughal India and Europe was:
(1) That India had no cities
(2) The lack of private property in land in Mughal India, because the emperor owned all land
(3) That India had no army
(4) That India had more private property than Europe
= 2
~ Bernier felt that crown ownership of land was harmful for both the state and its people. Historians note that Mughal sources do not show the emperor as the sole owner of land.

Q 13 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Bernier's ideas about crown ownership of land influenced which later European thinkers?
(1) Rousseau and Voltaire
(2) Copernicus and Galileo
(3) Montesquieu, who developed the idea of "oriental despotism", and Karl Marx, who wrote of the "Asiatic mode of production"
(4) Burckhardt and Ranke
= 3
~ Such ideas presented Asian societies as unchanging and despotic. Historians today question this picture.

Q 14 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Which of the following travellers is correctly matched with the place he came from?
(1) Abdur Razzaq Samarqandi - Herat, a diplomat who visited south India in the 1440s
(2) Jean-Baptiste Tavernier - Morocco
(3) Ibn Battuta - France

Q 15 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Bernier's account of sati describes:
(1) A happy festival
(2) A rare event he never saw
(3) A law passed by the Mughals to encourage it
(4) The case of a young widow forced to be burnt, which he found deeply disturbing
= 4
~ European travellers wrote about the practice of sati as a sign of the position of women in India. Bernier also noted that women's lives involved much more than sati.

Q 16 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Bernier wrote about the karkhanas in Mughal cities. These were:
(1) Temples
(2) Inns for travellers
(3) Workshops where artisans worked under the supervision of officials
(4) Prisons
= 3
~ Bernier felt that artisans had no incentive to improve their work because the profits went to the state. Yet he also noted that the Mughal empire produced fine goods for the world.

Q 17 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Al-Biruni's understanding of the caste system was based mainly on:
(1) His own travels in villages
(2) Conversations with traders
(3) Persian court records
(4) Sanskrit texts laying down the rules of the system
= 4
~ In reality, the system was not as rigid as the texts suggested. Social categories like the antyaja were expected to provide cheap labour.

Q 18 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Ibn Battuta's Rihla is a valuable source for:
(1) The Harappan civilisation
(2) The Mauryan empire
(3) The social and cultural life of the Delhi Sultanate in the fourteenth century
(4) British rule
= 3
~ Ibn Battuta returned to Morocco in 1354. His account was written down by a scribe from his oral narration.

Q 19 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Slaves in the Delhi Sultanate, according to Ibn Battuta, were:
(1) Never seen
(2) Openly sold in markets and given as gifts; some were used as domestic labour, and some skilled female slaves were used in the Sultan's service
(3) Only men who worked in mines
(4) Freed by law
= 2
~ Ibn Battuta himself bought slaves. Slavery was part of the social order of the time.

Q 20 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Why do historians use travellers' accounts with care?
(1) Because travellers always wrote in Sanskrit
(2) Because they often compared what they saw with their own societies, and their views were shaped by what they found unusual or by their own interests
(3) Because travellers never visited India
(4) Because their accounts were always written by kings
= 2
~ Travellers' accounts give rich details about social life. But they need to be compared with other sources, such as court chronicles and inscriptions.
