# NCERT Class 12 History, Themes in Indian History Part II, Chapter 3: An Imperial Capital: Vijayanagara
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
The Vijayanagara empire was founded in 1336 by:
(1) Krishnadeva Raya
(2) Aliya Rama Raya
(3) Deva Raya
(4) Harihara and Bukka
= 4
~ Vijayanagara means "city of victory". The empire stretched from the river Krishna to the extreme south of the peninsula.

Q 2 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
The ruins of Vijayanagara at Hampi were brought to light in 1800 by:
(1) Colin Mackenzie, an engineer and antiquarian who later became the first Surveyor General of India
(2) Alexander Cunningham
(3) John Marshall
(4) James Prinsep
= 1
~ Mackenzie prepared the first survey map of the site. The name Hampi comes from Pampadevi, the local mother goddess.

Q 3 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Which of the following is the correct order of the dynasties that ruled Vijayanagara?
(1) Saluva, Sangama, Aravidu, Tuluva
(2) Tuluva, Sangama, Saluva, Aravidu
(3) Aravidu, Tuluva, Saluva, Sangama
(4) Sangama, Saluva, Tuluva, Aravidu
= 4
~ Krishnadeva Raya belonged to the Tuluva dynasty. The Aravidu dynasty ruled after the battle of 1565 from places further south.

Q 4 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Krishnadeva Raya (ruled 1509-29) is known for which of the following?
1. Acquiring the land between the Tungabhadra and Krishna rivers (the Raichur doab) in 1512
2. Writing a work on statecraft in Telugu, the Amuktamalyada
3. Building fine temples and adding impressive gopurams to many important south Indian temples
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ He also subdued the rulers of Orissa and defeated the Sultan of Bijapur. He founded a suburb near Vijayanagara called Nagalapuram after his mother.

Q 5 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
In 1565, at the battle of Rakshasi-Tangadi (Talikota), the Vijayanagara army led by the chief minister Rama Raya was defeated by:
(1) The Mughals
(2) The combined forces of the Sultans of Bijapur, Ahmadnagar and Golconda
(3) The Portuguese
(4) The Marathas
= 2
~ The victorious armies sacked the city, and within a few years it was abandoned.

Q 6 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
The Vijayanagara kings imported horses mainly from:
(1) China
(2) Bengal
(3) Sri Lanka
(4) Arabia and Central Asia, through traders called kudirai chettis
= 4
~ Horses were very important for the cavalry. This trade was initially controlled by Arab traders and later by the Portuguese.

Q 7 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
The "amara-nayaka" system was a major political innovation of the Vijayanagara empire. It drew on features of the:
(1) Mughal mansabdari system
(2) Roman provinces
(3) Iqta system of the Delhi Sultanate
(4) Mauryan districts
= 3
~ The amara-nayakas were military commanders who were given territories to govern by the raya. They collected taxes, maintained horses and elephants and paid tribute to the king.

Q 8 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
The great water tank built in the early fifteenth century, which supplied water to the "royal centre" through a channel, was the:
(1) Kamalapuram tank
(2) Hussain Sagar
(3) Sudarshana lake
(4) Hauz Khas
= 1
~ Water from the Tungabhadra was also brought through the Hiriya canal. Vijayanagara's natural setting, with the river and hills, shaped its water management.

Q 9 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Abdur Razzaq, an ambassador sent to Calicut in the fifteenth century, was greatly impressed by:
(1) The Vijayanagara navy
(2) The temple paintings
(3) The fortifications, describing seven lines of forts that encircled not only the city but its agricultural hinterland and forests
(4) The size of the royal library
= 3
~ The walls also enclosed fields, so the city could withstand a siege. The fortifications were made of stone with no mortar.

Q 10 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
The "Mahanavami dibba" in the royal centre of Vijayanagara was:
(1) A large platform, one of the highest points in the city, associated with the Mahanavami festival celebrated in September-October
(2) A temple of Virupaksha
(3) An elephant stable
(4) A market
= 1
~ Rituals on the platform included worship of the image, the state horse, and sacrifice of buffaloes and other animals, with dances, wrestling and processions.

Q 11 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
The "Lotus Mahal" in the royal centre was given its name by:
(1) Krishnadeva Raya
(2) British travellers in the nineteenth century
(3) Abdur Razzaq
(4) The Sultans of Bijapur
= 2
~ Historians are not sure what the building was used for. One suggestion is that it was a council chamber where the king met his advisers.

Q 12 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
The temple in the royal centre whose walls are carved with scenes from the Ramayana, and which was probably meant for the king and his family, is the:
(1) Virupaksha temple
(2) Vitthala temple
(3) Hazara Rama temple
(4) Brihadeshvara temple
= 3
~ The central shrine of this temple is empty. The sculpted panels show scenes from the Ramayana.

Q 13 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
The guardian deity of the Vijayanagara kingdom, whose temple was the main religious centre of the city, was:
(1) Vitthala
(2) Virupaksha, a form of Shiva
(3) Venkateshvara
(4) Pampadevi only
= 2
~ Rulers issued orders on behalf of Shri Virupaksha. Krishnadeva Raya built a hall in front of the main shrine and an eastern gopuram.

Q 14 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
The Vitthala temple at Vijayanagara is dedicated to a form of Vishnu. Vitthala was generally worshipped in:
(1) Bengal
(2) Punjab
(3) Maharashtra
(4) Kashmir
= 3
~ The worship of Vitthala in Karnataka shows how the rulers drew on different traditions. The temple is famous for its chariot-shaped shrine.

Q 15 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Gopurams, a feature of temples during the Vijayanagara period, were:
(1) Royal gateways, often towering over the central shrines, signalling the presence of the temple from far away
(2) Water tanks
(3) Pillared halls
(4) Platforms for festivals
= 1
~ Gopurams were also reminders of the power of kings who could command the resources needed to build them.

Q 16 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Which Portuguese traveller wrote about Krishnadeva Raya, describing him as a well-built king of medium height?
(1) Domingo Paes
(2) Duarte Barbosa
(3) Abdur Razzaq
(4) Afanasii Nikitin
= 1
~ Paes visited the city in the sixteenth century. Travellers' accounts are important sources on Vijayanagara.

Q 17 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Vijayanagara rulers sometimes called themselves "Hindu Suratrana", a Sanskritised form of "Hindu Sultan". This shows that:
(1) There was no contact between them
(2) The Sultans paid tribute to Vijayanagara
(3) Rulers of the time borrowed ideas of kingship and military practices from one another, even though they were rivals
(4) Vijayanagara was a Sultanate
= 3
~ Rulers of the period borrowed titles, military practices and architectural forms, such as arches and domes, from one another.

Q 18 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Which of the following rivers flowed past the city of Vijayanagara?
(1) Kaveri
(2) Tungabhadra
(3) Godavari
(4) Krishna
= 2
~ The river flows in a north-easterly direction through a natural basin formed by granite hills. Many streams flow into it from these hills.

Q 19 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
The main rival of the Vijayanagara kings for control of the fertile river valleys and trade was:
(1) The Mughals
(2) The Deccan Sultans, earlier the Bahmani kingdom
(3) The Ahom kings
(4) The Cholas
= 2
~ Conflicts often concerned the Raichur doab. Yet there was also trade and contact between the two sides.

Q 20 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Which of the following is a reason historians can know so much about Vijayanagara?
(1) The city was never abandoned
(2) Only inscriptions survive
(3) It was described only by the British
(4) The ruins, inscriptions, literature in Sanskrit, Telugu, Kannada and Tamil, and accounts of foreign travellers together give a detailed picture
= 4
~ After Mackenzie, photographers and archaeologists recorded the monuments. Hampi was declared a site of national importance and later a World Heritage Site.
