Q 1 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The members of the Constituent Assembly were:
(1) Directly elected by all adult citizens in 1946
(2) Elected in 1946 by the members of the Provincial Legislatures, not by universal franchise
(3) Nominated by the British Viceroy
(4) Chosen by the princely states only
= 2
~ The Congress dominated the Assembly, winning a large majority of the general seats. The Muslim League chose to boycott the Assembly.

Q 2 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The Constituent Assembly first met on:
(1) 15 August 1947
(2) 26 January 1950
(3) 9 December 1946
(4) 26 November 1949
= 3
~ The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 and came into effect on 26 January 1950.

Q 3 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The Constituent Assembly's sessions were spread over three years, with how many sittings in all?
(1) Eleven sessions, with sittings spread over 165 days
(2) Two sessions of 30 days
(3) One session of 365 days
(4) Fifty sessions
= 1
~ Between the sessions, committees revised the drafts. The debates were published and discussed widely in the press.

Q 4 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Who moved the "Objectives Resolution" in the Constituent Assembly on 13 December 1946?
(1) B.R. Ambedkar
(2) Rajendra Prasad
(3) Vallabhbhai Patel
(4) Jawaharlal Nehru
= 4
~ The resolution defined the ideals of the Constitution: an independent sovereign republic, guaranteeing justice, equality and freedom, and safeguards for minorities, backward and tribal areas, and depressed classes.

Q 5 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Who was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Constitution?
(1) B.N. Rau
(2) B.R. Ambedkar
(3) K.M. Munshi
(4) Rajendra Prasad
= 2
~ Rajendra Prasad was the President of the Constituent Assembly. B.N. Rau was the constitutional adviser and S.N. Mukherjee the chief draughtsman.

Q 6 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
B. Pocker Bahadur of Madras argued in the Constituent Assembly for:
(1) A unitary state
(2) Hindi as the national language
(3) Continuing separate electorates for Muslims
(4) Abolishing reservations
= 3
~ He felt minorities needed a voice in the political system. Most nationalists, such as G.B. Pant, argued that separate electorates had divided the country and would isolate minorities.

Q 7 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
N.G. Ranga, a socialist leader of the peasant movement, said that the real minorities were:
(1) The poor and downtrodden, who could not enjoy their rights without economic means
(2) Religious groups only
(3) The princes
(4) The English-educated
= 1
~ He urged that the "masses" be given the economic means to make use of the rights the Constitution would grant them.

Q 8 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Jaipal Singh, in the Constituent Assembly, spoke eloquently for:
(1) The rights of industrialists
(2) The tribes (Adivasis), who had been marginalised and needed protection
(3) The Muslim League
(4) A strong Centre only
= 2
~ He argued that tribal people needed policies to bring them into the mainstream while respecting their ways of life.

Q 9 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Which of the following was a provision of the Constitution regarding the depressed castes, as finally adopted?
1. Untouchability was abolished.
2. Hindu temples were thrown open to all castes.
3. Seats in legislatures and jobs in government offices were reserved for the lowest castes.
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 3 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ Many members believed that the social disabilities of the depressed castes could not be solved only through constitutional safeguards; the attitudes of society also had to change.

Q 10 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The Constitution provided three lists of subjects to divide powers. These were:
(1) Union, State and Concurrent lists
(2) Central, Provincial and Local lists
(3) National, Regional and Municipal lists
(4) Federal, Unitary and Mixed lists
= 1
~ Subjects in the Union List were for the Centre, those in the State List for the states, and the Concurrent List was shared. More items were placed under exclusive Union control.

Q 11 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
K. Santhanam from Madras, speaking on Centre-state relations, argued that:
(1) States should be abolished
(2) The Centre should have all taxes
(3) There was no need for a strong Centre
(4) Not only the states but the Centre also needed to be strengthened, but an over-burdened Centre would not function well, so the states needed adequate finances
= 4
~ He was concerned that the allocation of finances would make the provinces dependent on the Centre.

Q 12 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
After much debate, what did the Constitution provide about the official language?
(1) English alone would be the official language forever
(2) Hindustani in both scripts would be the national language
(3) Hindi in Devanagari script would be the official language, with English continuing to be used for official purposes for fifteen years
(4) Each state would have its own national language
= 3
~ Gandhi had favoured Hindustani, a blend of Hindi and Urdu. After Partition, the debate became heated, and a compromise was reached.

Q 13 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
R.V. Dhulekar, a Congressman from the United Provinces, argued in the Assembly:
(1) For Hindi to be used as the language of constitution-making and as the national language
(2) For English as the sole language
(3) For separate electorates
(4) Against reservations
= 1
~ Many members from south India opposed the imposition of Hindi. Shrimati G. Durgabai of Madras spoke about the fears it raised among non-Hindi speakers.

Q 14 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The Constitution adopted universal adult franchise. This meant:
(1) Only property owners could vote
(2) Every adult citizen got the right to vote, irrespective of caste, class, gender or religion
(3) Only educated people could vote
(4) Only men could vote
= 2
~ This was a bold step, given that most Indians were poor and many could not read. It showed faith in the people.

Q 17 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The partition of India strengthened the argument in the Constituent Assembly for:
(1) A strong Centre, to maintain order and plan for the economic welfare of the country
(2) A weak Centre
(3) Separate electorates
(4) Abolishing the Union List
= 1
~ Before Partition, the Congress had agreed to give considerable autonomy to provinces. After it, there was a strong push for centralisation.

Q 18 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The Indian Constitution is notable for having been framed through:
(1) A secret process with no public discussion
(2) Copying the British constitution word by word
(3) Intense public debate inside and outside the Assembly, with the debates reported in newspapers and public opinion influencing the members
(4) A decree of the Viceroy
= 3
~ The Assembly also invited suggestions from the public. This gave the Constitution wide legitimacy.

Q 19 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Somnath Lahiri, a Communist member, warned in the debate on the Objectives Resolution about:
(1) The dangers of Hindi
(2) The rights of princes
(3) The hand of British imperialism in the framing of the Constitution, urging members to free themselves from imperial influence
(4) The need for a monarchy
= 3
~ He felt that the Assembly was British-made and that the British had a role in its working.

Q 20 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Begum Aizaz Rasul, a member of the Constituent Assembly, held the view that:
(1) Separate electorates must continue
(2) Separate electorates were self-destructive, as they isolated minorities from the majority
(3) Muslims should not take part in elections
(4) Only Urdu should be the official language
= 2
~ By 1949, most Muslim members agreed that separate electorates were against the interests of the minorities.
