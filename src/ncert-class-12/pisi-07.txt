# NCERT Class 12 Political Science, Politics in India since Independence, Chapter 7: Regional Aspirations
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
According to the chapter, how has the Indian approach dealt with regional aspirations?
(1) By suppressing every regional demand with force
(2) By accepting that regional aspirations are a part of democratic politics and accommodating them through negotiation, while keeping the country united
(3) By allowing any region to leave the country
(4) By abolishing all states
= 2
~ The chapter argues that regional demands are not anti-national in themselves. Democracy allows them to be expressed and, where possible, accommodated through federal arrangements.

Q 2 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The Anandpur Sahib Resolution of 1973, which called for regional autonomy and a redefinition of Centre–state relations, was passed by:
(1) The Akali Dal
(2) The DMK
(3) The Mizo National Front
(4) The Asom Gana Parishad
= 1
~ The resolution asserted the regional autonomy of Punjab and the aspirations of the Sikh community. Some later used it to justify demands for a separate country.

Q 3 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The army operation of June 1984 to flush out militants from the Golden Temple in Amritsar was called:
(1) Operation Vijay
(2) Operation Polo
(3) Operation Flood
(4) Operation Blue Star
= 4
~ The operation removed the militants but damaged the shrine and hurt the feelings of many Sikhs. Prime Minister Indira Gandhi was assassinated in October 1984.

Q 4 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The agreement of July 1985 that tried to bring normalcy to Punjab was signed between Rajiv Gandhi and:
(1) Laldenga
(2) Prafulla Kumar Mahanta
(3) Harchand Singh Longowal
(4) Sheikh Abdullah
= 3
~ The Punjab Accord, also called the Rajiv Gandhi–Longowal Accord, addressed issues like Chandigarh and river waters. Longowal was assassinated soon after, and violence continued for several years.

Q 5 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The Mizo National Front, which launched an armed struggle for independence in 1966, was led by:
(1) Laldenga
(2) A.Z. Phizo
(3) Zoramthanga
(4) Rajiv Gandhi
= 1
~ Anger over the government's poor response to a famine in 1959 led to the MNF's rebellion. In 1986 Laldenga signed a peace accord with Rajiv Gandhi and became Chief Minister of the new state of Mizoram.

Q 6 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Mizoram became a full state of the Indian Union in:
(1) 1963
(2) 1972
(3) 1975
(4) 1987
= 4
~ After the 1986 accord, Mizoram became a state in 1987. It is often cited as one of the most successful examples of accommodating a regional movement.

Q 7 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The Assam movement of 1979–1985 was mainly directed against:
(1) The Hindi language
(2) The presence of "outsiders", especially illegal migrants from Bangladesh
(3) The construction of dams
(4) The Panchayati Raj system
= 2
~ The movement, led by the All Assam Students' Union (AASU), demanded the detection and deportation of illegal migrants. It ended with the Assam Accord in 1985.

Q 8 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
After the Assam Accord of 1985, which party was formed by the leaders of the Assam movement and won the elections in the state?
(1) Akali Dal
(2) DMK
(3) Asom Gana Parishad (AGP)
(4) National Conference
= 3
~ The AGP came to power in 1985 promising to resolve the foreigners issue and to build a "golden Assam". The problem of migration, however, remained unresolved.

Q 9 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The Naga National Council, which demanded independence for the Naga people, was led by:
(1) Laldenga
(2) Sheikh Abdullah
(3) A.Z. Phizo
(4) Master Tara Singh
= 3
~ Phizo declared Naga independence in 1951. Nagaland became a state in 1963, but sections of the movement continued their struggle for many years.

Q 10 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Sikkim became the twenty-second state of India in:
(1) 1975
(2) 1961
(3) 1947
(4) 1987
= 1
~ Sikkim was a protectorate of India after 1947. In 1975, after a referendum and the 36th Amendment, it became a full state.

Q 11 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Goa, which remained a Portuguese colony after 1947, became part of India in:
(1) 1947
(2) 1950
(3) 1956
(4) 1961
= 4
~ The Indian army moved in in December 1961 and Goa was liberated. In a special opinion poll in 1967, people chose to remain a union territory instead of merging with Maharashtra; Goa became a state in 1987.

Q 12 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
In the "opinion poll" held in Goa in January 1967, people voted on whether Goa should:
(1) Go back to Portugal
(2) Merge with Maharashtra or remain separate
(3) Become independent
(4) Join Karnataka
= 2
~ The Maharashtrawadi Gomantak Party wanted merger with Maharashtra, but most voters chose to keep Goa separate.

Q 13 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The movement in Tamil Nadu that opposed the imposition of Hindi, culminating in a major agitation in 1965, was led by:
(1) The Akali Dal
(2) The Asom Gana Parishad
(3) The Mizo National Front
(4) The Dravidian parties, especially the DMK
= 4
~ The Dravidian movement asserted Tamil identity and opposed Hindi dominance. The DMK came to power in Madras state in 1967, an early example of regional politics winning through democratic means.

Q 14 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Sheikh Mohammad Abdullah, mentioned in the chapter, was the leader of which party?
(1) The Akali Dal
(2) The DMK
(3) The National Conference of Jammu and Kashmir
(4) The Asom Gana Parishad
= 3
~ Sheikh Abdullah led the National Conference, which opposed the Maharaja's rule and supported accession to India on the basis of autonomy. He became Prime Minister and later Chief Minister of Jammu and Kashmir.

Q 15 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
In August 2019, Parliament changed the constitutional status of Jammu and Kashmir. What did these changes do?
(1) Created two new states
(2) Ended the special status under Article 370 and reorganised the state into two union territories, Jammu and Kashmir, and Ladakh
(3) Merged it with Himachal Pradesh
(4) Gave it more autonomy than before
= 2
~ The provisions of Article 370 that gave the state special status ceased to apply, and the Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019, created two union territories.

Q 16 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which of the following is a general lesson that the chapter draws from the history of regional movements in India?
(1) Regional aspirations are part of democratic politics, and their accommodation through dialogue and federal arrangements has often helped resolve conflicts
(2) Regional movements must always be suppressed by force
(3) Federalism weakens the country
(4) Only economic issues lead to regional movements
= 1
~ Accords in Punjab, Mizoram and Assam show the value of negotiation. The chapter also notes that delays and the use of force alone have often made problems worse.

Q 17 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
In the north-east, the chapter identifies three kinds of issues behind regional politics. Which of the following is one of them?
(1) Demands for a monarchy
(2) Demands for autonomy, movements for secession, and opposition to "outsiders"
(3) Demands to abolish elections
(4) Demands to join China
= 2
~ The region saw demands for separate states or autonomy, secessionist movements in places like Nagaland and Mizoram, and movements against migrants, as in Assam.

Q 18 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Prime Minister Indira Gandhi was assassinated in October 1984. What followed in Delhi and other parts of north India?
(1) Peaceful elections only
(2) An economic boom
(3) A war with Pakistan
(4) Violence against the Sikh community in which thousands were killed
= 4
~ More than two thousand Sikhs were killed in Delhi alone. In 2005 the Prime Minister expressed regret in Parliament for the killings. Rajiv Gandhi became Prime Minister after the assassination.

Q 19 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which of the following best explains why the north-eastern region has special political importance, according to the chapter?
(1) It is connected to the rest of India by a narrow corridor and shares long borders with other countries, and has great social and cultural diversity
(2) It has the largest population in India
(3) It has no international borders
(4) It was never part of British India
= 1
~ Isolation, international borders, economic backwardness and diverse communities have shaped the region's politics and its relations with the Centre.

Q 20 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
In which year was the Punjab Accord (Rajiv Gandhi–Longowal Accord) signed?
(1) 1966
(2) 1973
(3) 1985
(4) 1992
= 3
~ The accord was signed in July 1985. Normalcy returned to Punjab only by the mid-1990s, after years of militancy and counter-insurgency.
