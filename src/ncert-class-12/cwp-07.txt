# NCERT Class 12 Political Science, Contemporary World Politics, Chapter 7: Globalisation
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Globalisation, as defined in the chapter, is fundamentally about:
(1) Flows of ideas, capital, commodities and people across different parts of the world
(2) Only the movement of goods
(3) Only the movement of armies
(4) The creation of a world government
= 1
~ Globalisation involves many kinds of flows that create worldwide interconnectedness. It has political, economic and cultural dimensions.

Q 2 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following is the most important cause of globalisation, according to the chapter?
(1) The end of all wars
(2) The growth of agriculture
(3) Technology, especially advances in telegraph, telephone, the internet and transport
(4) Population decline
= 3
~ Technology has made it possible to move ideas, capital, goods and people faster and more cheaply than ever before.

Q 3 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which statement about the movement of people in a globalised world is correct, according to the chapter?
(1) People move across borders as freely as capital and goods
(2) Developed countries have guarded their borders carefully, so the movement of people is much more restricted than that of capital and goods
(3) Migration has stopped completely
(4) Only developing countries restrict migration
= 2
~ Visa and immigration rules limit the movement of people. The chapter notes this uneven nature of globalisation.

Q 4 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
How has globalisation affected the capacity of the state, according to the chapter?
(1) It has abolished states
(2) It has only increased the state's power in every respect
(3) It has had no effect
(4) It has led to a decline in the welfare functions of some states, but the state remains important and technology has also increased its capacity
= 4
~ States have withdrawn from some welfare roles and the market has become more important, but states still maintain law and order and national security, and have new tools to gather information.

Q 5 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The term "McDonaldisation" in the debate on cultural globalisation refers to:
(1) Restaurants opening in every city
(2) The spread of fast food only
(3) Diversity of world cultures
(4) The fear that the spread of Western, especially American, culture will make cultures across the world uniform
= 4
~ Critics fear cultural homogenisation, where the dominant culture of powerful countries spreads everywhere. The chapter also discusses how cultures can mix and enrich each other.

Q 6 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The positive cultural effect of globalisation, where outside influences enlarge our choices and mix with local traditions, is called:
(1) Cultural homogenisation
(2) Cultural heterogenisation
(3) Cultural imperialism
(4) Cultural isolation
= 2
~ The example of blue jeans worn with a khadi kurta shows how cultures combine. Globalisation can broaden cultural choices, not only make them uniform.

Q 7 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
India began its programme of economic reforms, opening up to globalisation, in:
(1) 1991
(2) 1971
(3) 1985
(4) 2001
= 1
~ Facing a financial crisis in 1991, India reduced controls, opened up to foreign investment and trade, and gave more room to the private sector.

Q 8 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The World Social Forum, a platform for those opposed to neo-liberal globalisation, held its first meeting in 2001 at:
(1) Mumbai
(2) Seattle
(3) Porto Alegre, Brazil
(4) Davos
= 3
~ The WSF brings together human rights activists, environmentalists, labour, youth and women's groups. Its fourth meeting was held in Mumbai in 2004.

Q 9 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Large protests against unfair trading practices at a WTO ministerial meeting took place in 1999 in:
(1) Geneva
(2) Seattle
(3) Davos
(4) Doha
= 2
~ The Seattle protests criticised the power of rich countries in the WTO and the effects of economic globalisation on workers and developing countries.

Q 10 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following is a criticism of economic globalisation discussed in the chapter?
(1) It has increased the welfare spending of every state
(2) It has ended all inequality
(3) It has stopped trade between countries
(4) It has benefited a small section of the population and may increase inequality, while reducing protection for the poor
= 4
~ Critics argue that the gains are unevenly shared. Supporters say it creates growth and opportunities. The chapter asks students to weigh both views.

Q 11 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following statements about globalisation in India is supported by the chapter?
(1) India had no contact with the outside world before 1991
(2) Globalisation in India began only in the twenty-first century
(3) India has had long links with other parts of the world through trade and migration, but its policies of protectionism after independence limited them until the reforms of 1991
(4) India rejected all foreign investment after 1991
= 3
~ The chapter notes India's historical role in global trade and the period after independence when it chose protectionism to build its own economy, before opening up again in 1991.

Q 12 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Economic globalisation has involved greater roles for which international institutions, according to the chapter?
(1) The IMF and the WTO
(2) SAARC and ASEAN
(3) The League of Nations
(4) The Warsaw Pact
= 1
~ The IMF and the WTO influence economic policies across countries, and multinational companies have become more powerful as barriers to trade and investment fell.

Q 13 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of these groups in India have resisted aspects of globalisation, according to the chapter?
(1) Left-wing groups, trade unions, farmers' groups and some right-wing groups opposed to foreign cultural influence
(2) Only multinational companies
(3) Only the government
(4) No group has ever resisted
= 1
~ Resistance has come from different directions: left groups opposed economic policies, unions and farmers' groups opposed the entry of multinationals, and some groups objected to Western cultural influence.

Q 14 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
"Neo-liberal globalisation", criticised by many movements, refers to:
(1) A return to colonial rule
(2) A ban on international trade
(3) A form of globalisation based on free markets, reduced state intervention and opening economies to foreign capital
(4) A socialist world order
= 3
~ Critics say this form of globalisation favours the rich and powerful countries and companies, and reduces the state's role in protecting the poor.

Q 15 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following is an example of the "cultural" consequences of globalisation discussed in the chapter?
(1) Changes in interest rates
(2) The fall of the Berlin Wall
(3) The Kyoto Protocol
(4) Changes in what people eat, wear and watch, with both the mixing of cultures and fears of uniformity
= 4
~ Globalisation affects tastes, food habits, clothing and entertainment. The chapter discusses both homogenisation and heterogenisation.

Q 16 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Why does the chapter say globalisation is "multi-dimensional"?
(1) Because it has only an economic aspect
(2) Because it has political, economic and cultural dimensions, which must be considered together
(3) Because it is measured in three dimensions
(4) Because it applies only to rich countries
= 2
~ Focusing only on economics misses its effects on the state's role, on culture and on people's identities. Its effects also differ across countries and groups.

Q 17 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
According to the chapter, does globalisation necessarily reduce the importance of the state?
(1) Yes, states have disappeared
(2) Yes, multinational companies now make all laws
(3) No; while some functions have declined, the state remains the main actor in many areas, and technology has even increased some of its capacities
(4) No, because globalisation has stopped
= 3
~ Political communities remain organised as states, which continue to control law and order, security and many economic decisions.

Q 18 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following is an example of resistance in India to the effects of economic globalisation?
(1) The Champaran Satyagraha of 1917
(2) Protests by trade unions and farmers' groups, for example against the patenting of plants like neem by multinational companies
(3) The Non-Cooperation Movement
(4) The Quit India Movement
= 2
~ Farmers' groups and activists opposed the patenting of traditional knowledge and crops, and unions opposed the effects of liberalisation on jobs.

Q 19 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which statement best reflects the chapter's view of globalisation?
(1) Its effects are mixed: it creates opportunities and growth for some while creating hardship and inequality for others, so its consequences must be judged carefully
(2) It is wholly good for everyone
(3) It is wholly harmful for everyone
(4) It has no effects on ordinary people
= 1
~ The chapter presents arguments for and against. It encourages students to examine who gains and who loses from globalisation in particular situations.

Q 20 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The World Social Forum's fourth meeting, mentioned in the chapter, was held in 2004 in:
(1) Porto Alegre
(2) New York
(3) Beijing
(4) Mumbai
= 4
~ The Mumbai meeting brought together a wide range of groups opposed to neo-liberal globalisation, under the slogan "Another world is possible".
