Q 1 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
The Jon Beel Mela, where the barter system is still practised, is held every January at:
(1) Jagiroad, about 35 km from Guwahati
(2) Pushkar, Rajasthan
(3) Sonepur, Bihar
(4) Shillong, Meghalaya
= 1
~ In barter, goods are exchanged directly without money; the difficulties of barter were later overcome by money.

Q 2 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
The word "salary" comes from the Latin "salarium", meaning payment by:
(1) Gold
(2) Salt
(3) Silver
(4) Cattle
= 2
~ Before coins and paper money, rare objects with high value such as flintstones, cowrie shells, salt, copper, silver and gold served as money.

Q 3 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
The Silk Route, an early example of long-distance trade, connected Rome to China along a route of about:
(1) 600 km
(2) 6,000 km
(3) 16,000 km
(4) 60,000 km
= 2
~ Traders carried Chinese silk, Roman wool, precious metals and other high-value goods from intermediate points in India, Persia and Central Asia.

Q 4 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Arrange the following countries in the order in which they abolished the slave trade, as given in the chapter:
1. Great Britain
2. United States
3. Denmark
Select the correct answer:
(1) 2 – 1 – 3
(2) 1 – 2 – 3
(3) 3 – 1 – 2
(4) 3 – 2 – 1
= 3
~ Denmark abolished it in 1792, Great Britain in 1807 and the United States in 1808.

Q 5 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
International trade is based on the principle of:
(1) Comparative advantage, complementarity and transferability of goods and services
(2) Absolute self-sufficiency of every country
(3) Equal production of all goods by all countries
(4) Barter only
= 1
~ International trade results from specialisation in production and should, in principle, be mutually beneficial to trading partners.

Q 6 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography

A. China — 1. Carpets

C. North Africa — 3. Porcelain and brocades

Select the correct answer:

Q 7 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Why do densely populated countries often have a large volume of internal trade but little external trade?
(1) They have no transport
(2) They are not allowed to export
(3) Most of their agricultural and industrial production is consumed in local markets
(4) They have no natural resources
= 3
~ Size of population and standard of living affect the type and volume of goods traded.

Q 8 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Which three aspects of international trade are discussed in the chapter?
(1) Tariffs, quotas and subsidies
(2) Ports, airports and railways
(3) Volume, sectoral composition and direction of trade
(4) Imports, exports and smuggling
= 3
~ Since services cannot be measured in tonnage, the total value of goods and services traded is taken as the volume of trade.

Q 9 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
A country has a negative or unfavourable balance of trade when:
(1) The value of its exports is more than the value of its imports
(2) The value of its imports is more than the value of its exports
(3) It trades with only one country
(4) It has no ports
= 2
~ A persistently negative balance means a country spends more on buying goods than it earns by selling, which can exhaust its financial reserves.

Q 10 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Trade between two countries that agree to trade specified commodities with each other is called:
(1) Multilateral trade
(2) Barter trade
(3) Entrepot trade
(4) Bilateral trade
= 4
~ Multilateral trade is conducted with many countries; a country may also grant some partners "Most Favoured Nation" status.

Q 11 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Selling a commodity in two countries at prices that differ for reasons not related to costs is called:
(1) Protectionism
(2) Off-shoring
(3) Barter
(4) Dumping
= 4
~ Dumped goods at cheap prices can harm domestic producers, so countries need to be cautious along with free trade.

Q 12 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Consider the following statements about the World Trade Organisation (WTO):
1. GATT was formed in 1948 and was transformed into the WTO from 1 January 1995.
2. The WTO's headquarters is in Geneva, Switzerland.
3. India joined the WTO only in 2005.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1
~ India is one of the founder members of the WTO. The WTO is the only international organisation dealing with global rules of trade between nations.

Q 13 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Which of the following is a criticism of the WTO mentioned in the chapter?
(1) It has made all countries equally rich
(2) Free trade is widening the gulf between rich and poor, and many developed countries have not fully opened their markets to developing countries
(3) It deals only with agricultural trade
(4) It has no rules for settling disputes
= 2
~ Critics also argue that issues of health, workers' rights, child labour and the environment are ignored.

Q 14 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
According to the chapter, 120 regional trade blocs generate about what share of world trade?
(1) 12 per cent
(2) 25 per cent
(3) 52 per cent
(4) 90 per cent
= 3
~ Regional trade blocs encourage trade between countries with geographical proximity and complementary products.

Q 15 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Which of the following is an example of an inland port mentioned in the chapter?
(1) Aden
(2) Piraeus in Greece
(3) Dover in England
(4) Kolkata, located on the river Hooghly
= 4
~ Inland ports are linked to the sea through a river or canal; Manchester (canal), Memphis (Mississippi), and Mannheim and Duisburg (Rhine) are other examples.

Q 16 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Piraeus, which serves Athens in Greece by receiving ships too large to approach the parent port, is an example of:
(1) An entrepot port
(2) An out port
(3) A packet station
(4) A naval port
= 2
~ Out ports are deep-water ports built away from the actual port to serve it.

Q 17 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Dover in England and Calais in France, which face each other across the English Channel, are examples of:
(1) Inland ports
(2) Oil ports
(3) Entrepot ports
(4) Packet stations (ferry ports)
= 4
~ Packet stations carry passengers and mail across short stretches of water and occur in pairs facing each other.

Q 18 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Which of the following is correctly matched as an entrepot port?
(1) Singapore — for Asia
(2) Maracaibo — for South America
(3) Abadan — for Europe
(4) Kochi — for Africa
= 1
~ Entrepot ports are collection centres where goods are brought from different countries for export: Singapore for Asia, Rotterdam for Europe and Copenhagen for the Baltic region.

Q 19 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Kochi and Karwar in India are given in the chapter as examples of:
(1) Ports of call
(2) Tanker ports
(3) Packet stations
(4) Naval ports
= 4
~ Naval ports have only strategic importance; they serve warships and have repair workshops for them.

Q 20 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Aden, Honolulu and Singapore originally developed as calling points on main sea routes where ships anchored for refuelling, water and food. Such ports are called:
(1) Ports of call
(2) Out ports
(3) Industrial ports
(4) Inland ports
= 1
~ Ports of call later developed into commercial ports. Maracaibo, Esskhira and Tripoli are tanker ports, and Abadan is a refinery port.
