# NCERT Class 12 Fine Arts (Art & Culture), An Introduction to Indian Art Part II, Chapter 2: The Rajasthani Schools of Painting
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Who coined the term 'Rajput Paintings' in 1916 to distinguish these works from the Mughal School?
(1) E.B. Havell
(2) Percy Brown
(3) Ananda Coomaraswamy
(4) B.N. Goswamy
= 3
~ Ananda Coomaraswamy coined the term. Scholars now treat 'Rajput Schools' as obsolete and speak of Rajasthani and Pahari schools instead.

Q 2 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Rajasthani paintings were made on 'wasli'. What is wasli?
(1) Thin sheets of handmade paper glued together to the desired thickness
(2) A cotton cloth primed with lime
(3) A palm leaf treated with oil
(4) A wooden board
= 1
~ Wasli is layered handmade paper. Outlines were drawn in black or brown and filled with pigments from minerals and precious metals, bound with glue.

Q 3 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
With what were finished Rajasthani paintings burnished to give them a uniform sheen?
(1) A conch shell
(2) A silver coin
(3) Gold leaf
(4) Agate
= 4
~ The finished painting was burnished with agate. Brushes were made of camel and squirrel hair, and the work was done by a team led by a master.

Q 4 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Which of the following is NOT one of the six main ragas of Ragamala painting named in the chapter?
(1) Bhairava
(2) Yaman
(3) Hindol
(4) Megha
= 2
~ The six main ragas are Bhairava, Malkos, Hindol, Dipak, Megha and Shri.

Q 5 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
A Ragamala set is usually arranged as:
(1) 12 folios, one for each month
(2) 36 or 42 folios in families, each with a male raga and his consorts (raginis)
(3) 108 folios, one for each shrine
(4) 700 folios, one for each verse
= 2
~ Ragamala albums have 36 or 42 folios; each family has a male raga and six raginis.

Q 6 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Match the text with its author:
A. Gita Govinda — 1. Keshav Das
B. Rasamanjari — 2. Bihari Lal
C. Rasikapriya — 3. Jayadeva
D. Satsai — 4. Bhanu Datta
Select the correct answer:
(1) A-3, B-1, C-4, D-2
(2) A-4, B-3, C-1, D-2
(3) A-3, B-4, C-2, D-1
(4) A-3, B-4, C-1, D-2
= 4
~ Jayadeva wrote the twelfth-century Gita Govinda, Bhanu Datta the Rasamanjari, Keshav Das the Rasikapriya (1591), and Bihari Lal the Satsai of 700 verses.

Q 7 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Keshav Das, author of the Rasikapriya (1591), was the court poet of:
(1) Raja Madhukar Shah of Orchha
(2) Mirza Raja Jai Singh of Jaipur
(3) Rana Pratap of Mewar
(4) Lakshmana Sen of Bengal
= 1
~ Keshav Das wrote the Rasikapriya in Brajbhasha at the court of Raja Madhukar Shah of Orchha. Jayadeva is said to have been the court poet of Lakshmana Sen.

Q 8 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The Baramasa theme in painting describes:
(1) The eight heroines (ashta nayikas)
(2) The ten incarnations of Vishnu
(3) The twelve months of the year
(4) The six main ragas
= 3
~ Baramasa describes the twelve months; Keshav Das treats it in the tenth chapter of his Kavipriya.

Q 9 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The emergence of the Mewar School is linked to a dated Ragamala set painted at Chawand in 1605 by:
(1) Sahibdin
(2) Manohar
(3) Nihal Chand
(4) Nisardin
= 4
~ The Chawand Ragamala of 1605 by Nisardin is identified by its colophon. Long wars with the Mughals destroyed most earlier Mewar works.

Q 10 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
In the Yuddha Kanda of the Jagat Singh Ramayana (1652), Sahibdin introduced which new pictorial device for the large scale of war scenes?
(1) Linear vanishing-point perspective
(2) Light and shade (chiaroscuro)
(3) Oblique aerial perspective
(4) Gold-leaf backgrounds
= 3
~ Sahibdin used an oblique aerial perspective to give credibility to the vast scale of battle scenes. Under Jagat Singh I (1628–1652), Sahibdin and Manohar reshaped the Mewar style.

Q 11 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The Bundi Ragamala of 1591 was painted at Chunar during the reign of:
(1) Rao Bhoj Singh
(2) Umed Singh
(3) Bishen Singh
(4) Ram Singh
= 1
~ It was painted at Chunar under the Hada ruler Bhoj Singh; its artists Shaykh Hasan, Shaykh Ali and Shaykh Hatim were pupils of Mir Sayyid Ali and Abdus Samad.

Q 12 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The Kota School of painting excels in the depiction of:
(1) Portraits of saints
(2) Hunting scenes and animal combat
(3) Jain manuscripts
(4) Mughal court ceremonies
= 2
~ Kota, separated from Bundi in 1625, became famous for hunting scenes. Its artists were the first to make landscape the main subject of a composition.

Q 13 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Ruknuddin, from a family of Mughal court artists, was the master artist at Bikaner under which ruler?
(1) Karan Singh
(2) Rao Bika
(3) Sawai Jai Singh
(4) Anup Singh
= 4
~ Ruknuddin worked under Anup Singh (1669–1698), who set up a library of manuscripts and paintings. Karan Singh had earlier employed Ustad Ali Raza from Delhi.

Q 14 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
At Bikaner, the painting studios supervised by master artists were called:
(1) Mandi
(2) Karkhana
(3) Suratkhana
(4) Nigar Khana
= 1
~ The studios were called mandi; gudarayi meant the master's finishing touches, and marammat meant repairing or copying older works. Bikaner is one of the best-documented schools.

Q 15 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The famous 'Bani Thani' and its distinctive facial type belong to which school?
(1) Bundi
(2) Mewar
(3) Kishangarh
(4) Jodhpur
= 3
~ Nihal Chand, who worked for Sawant Singh of Kishangarh between 1735 and 1757, is credited with the Kishangarh face inspired by the singer and poetess Bani Thani.

Q 16 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Sawant Singh of Kishangarh wrote devotional Brajbhasha poetry on Radha and Krishna under which name?
(1) Bihari
(2) Nagari Das
(3) Sur Das
(4) Ras Khan
= 2
~ Sawant Singh wrote as Nagari Das. He abdicated in 1757 and retired to Vrindavan with Bani Thani.

Q 17 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Consider the following features:
1. Arched eyebrows
2. Lotus-petal eyes tinged with pink, with drooping eyelids
3. A broad, snub nose
Which of the above are features of the Kishangarh facial type?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 2 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 3
~ The Kishangarh face has arched eyebrows, pink-tinged lotus-petal eyes with drooping lids, a slender nose and thin lips. Elongated figures, green tones and panoramic landscapes are other traits.

Q 18 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
One of the earliest sets of the Marwar (Jodhpur) School is a Ragamala painted at Pali by the artist Virji in:
(1) 1591
(2) 1605
(3) 1652
(4) 1623
= 4
~ Virji's Pali Ragamala is dated 1623. Jaswant Singh (1638–1678) later started documentary painting and portraiture at Jodhpur.

Q 19 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Maharaja Man Singh of Jodhpur (1803–1843) commissioned the Nath Charita because he was a follower of:
(1) The Nath Sampradaya
(2) The Pushtimarg of Vallabhacharya
(3) The Ramanandi sect
(4) The Dadu Panth
= 1
~ Man Singh followed the Nath Sampradaya and commissioned the Nath Charita (1824); his reign was the last innovative phase of Jodhpur painting.

Q 20 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Who founded the city of Jaipur in 1727 and reorganised the Suratkhana (painting workshop)?
(1) Pratap Singh
(2) Sawai Jai Singh
(3) Raja Bharmal
(4) Madho Singh
= 2
~ Sawai Jai Singh (1699–1743) founded Jaipur, brought Mughal painters from Delhi and employed the portrait painter Sahibram. Pratap Singh (1779–1803) later employed about 50 artists.
