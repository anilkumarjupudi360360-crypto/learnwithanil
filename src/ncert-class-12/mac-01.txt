# NCERT Class 12 Economics, Introductory Macroeconomics, Chapter 1: Introduction
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
Macroeconomics deals with:
(1) The behaviour of a single consumer
(2) The pricing decisions of one firm
(3) Aggregate economic variables of the economy as a whole, such as total output, employment and the general price level
(4) The demand for one particular good
= 3
~ Macroeconomics studies the economy as a whole. Microeconomics studies individual consumers, firms and markets.

Q 2 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
Which of the following is a macroeconomic question?
(1) How does a household decide how many apples to buy?
(2) Why did the unemployment rate of the economy rise this year?
(3) How much should a firm produce to maximise profit?
(4) What determines the price of cotton shirts in a market?
= 2
~ The unemployment rate is an aggregate for the whole economy. The other questions concern individual households, firms or markets, which are studied in microeconomics.

Q 3 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
Which book is credited with establishing macroeconomics as a separate branch of economics?
(1) The Wealth of Nations by Adam Smith
(2) Das Kapital by Karl Marx
(3) Principles of Economics by Alfred Marshall
(4) The General Theory of Employment, Interest and Money by John Maynard Keynes
= 4
~ Macroeconomics emerged as a separate subject after Keynes published The General Theory, which tried to explain the mass unemployment of the Great Depression.

Q 4 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
Keynes's The General Theory of Employment, Interest and Money was published in:
(1) 1936
(2) 1929
(3) 1945
(4) 1776
= 1
~ The General Theory appeared in 1936, during the aftermath of the Great Depression that began in 1929.

Q 5 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
The Great Depression, which led to the emergence of macroeconomics, began in:
(1) 1914
(2) 1929
(3) 1939
(4) 1947
= 2
~ The Great Depression began in 1929. Output and employment fell sharply in Europe and North America and in the rest of the world.

Q 6 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
During the Great Depression, the unemployment rate in the USA rose from about 3 per cent in 1929 to about:
(1) 5 per cent
(2) 10 per cent
(3) 15 per cent
(4) 25 per cent
= 4
~ From 1929 to 1933 the unemployment rate in the USA rose from 3 per cent to 25 per cent, and aggregate output fell by about 33 per cent.

Q 7 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
Before Keynes, the classical economists generally believed that:
(1) Unemployment would always be very high
(2) Governments should own all factories
(3) All labourers ready to work would find work, and the economy would produce at the full employment level
(4) Prices never change
= 3
~ The classical tradition held that markets would ensure full employment of labour and other resources. The Great Depression, with its mass unemployment, challenged this view.

Q 8 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
Which of the following are features of a capitalist economy described in the chapter?
1. Private ownership of the means of production
2. Production of goods for sale in the market
3. Sale and purchase of labour services at a wage rate
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
(1) 1, 2 and 3
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1 and 3 only
= 1
~ In a capitalist economy, the means of production are privately owned, production is meant for the market, and labour is hired for wages. Most economic activity is organised this way.

Q 9 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
In a capitalist economy, the entrepreneur is the person who:
(1) Organises production, hires the other factors of production, bears the risks and earns profit
(2) Only supplies labour for wages
(3) Only lends money and earns interest
(4) Collects taxes
= 1
~ The entrepreneur brings land, labour and capital together, takes the risk of the business and receives profit as reward.

Q 10 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
Match the factors of production with their rewards:
A. Land | 1. Profit
B. Labour | 2. Interest
C. Capital | 3. Wages
D. Entrepreneurship | 4. Rent
Select the correct code:
(1) A-1, B-2, C-3, D-4
(2) A-4, B-2, C-3, D-1
(3) A-4, B-3, C-2, D-1
(4) A-3, B-4, C-1, D-2
= 3
~ Land earns rent, labour earns wages, capital earns interest and the entrepreneur earns profit.

Q 11 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
Investment expenditure, in macroeconomics, means:
(1) Buying shares on the stock market
(2) Depositing money in a bank
(3) A household buying food
(4) Expenditure by firms on capital goods, such as machines and buildings, that adds to the stock of capital
= 4
~ In macroeconomics, investment means adding to the stock of physical capital. Buying shares merely transfers ownership of existing assets.

Q 12 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
The four major sectors used in macroeconomic analysis are:
(1) Agriculture, industry, services and banking
(2) Households, firms, government and the external sector
(3) Public, private, joint and co-operative sectors
(4) Rural, urban, formal and informal sectors
= 2
~ Firms produce, households consume and supply factors, the government taxes and spends, and the external sector links the economy with the rest of the world.

Q 13 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
The external sector in macroeconomic analysis refers to:
(1) Firms located outside cities
(2) The informal sector
(3) Households living abroad only
(4) The economy's dealings with the rest of the world, such as exports, imports and flows of capital
= 4
~ An economy trades goods and services with other countries and also receives and sends capital. These transactions form the external sector.

Q 14 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
What role does the government play in the economy, as described in the chapter?
(1) It imposes taxes and spends on things such as public infrastructure, schools, health and law and order
(2) It only consumes goods
(3) It only supplies labour
(4) It has no economic role
= 1
~ The state frames laws, imposes taxes and spends money on public goods and services such as infrastructure, education, health and administration.

Q 15 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
In macroeconomic analysis, households:
(1) Only produce goods for sale
(2) Only collect taxes
(3) Consume goods and services, and own the factors of production, which they supply to firms in return for factor incomes
(4) Only trade with other countries
= 3
~ Households are both consumers and suppliers of land, labour, capital and entrepreneurship. Their factor incomes pay for their consumption.

Q 16 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
Which idea is associated with Keynes?
(1) The economy always reaches full employment on its own
(2) Aggregate demand can fall short, so an economy may suffer from prolonged unemployment and the government may need to act
(3) Money has no role in the economy
(4) Unemployment is always voluntary
= 2
~ Keynes argued that when total demand is low, output and employment can stay low for a long time. This explained the Great Depression and gave a role to government policy.

Q 17 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
Which of the following is a macroeconomic policy concern?
(1) Controlling the general price level (inflation) of the whole economy
(2) Fixing the price of a single brand of soap
(3) Deciding the wages of one shop's workers
(4) Choosing the location of one factory
= 1
~ Inflation, unemployment and the growth of total output are concerns for the economy as a whole, and governments and central banks frame policies to deal with them.

Q 18 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
The study of how the price of a particular good is determined in its market belongs to:
(1) Macroeconomics
(2) National income accounting
(3) Public finance
(4) Microeconomics
= 4
~ Price determination in an individual market is a microeconomic topic. Macroeconomics looks at the general price level instead.

Q 19 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
Why does macroeconomics combine all goods and services into aggregates such as total output?
(1) Because individual goods have no prices
(2) It simplifies the study of the economy as a whole, so questions about overall growth, inflation and employment can be answered
(3) Because microeconomics is wrong
(4) Because the government requires it by law
= 2
~ Treating all goods and services together, for instance as GDP, makes it possible to study how the whole economy is doing, which is the subject matter of macroeconomics.

Q 20 | eco | Introductory Macroeconomics
Which of the following is NOT a macroeconomic variable?
(1) Gross domestic product
(2) General price level
(3) The price of a kilogram of tomatoes in a local market
(4) Unemployment rate
= 3
~ The price of one good in one market is a microeconomic variable. GDP, the general price level and the unemployment rate describe the whole economy.
