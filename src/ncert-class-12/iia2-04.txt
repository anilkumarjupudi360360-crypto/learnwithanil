# NCERT Class 12 Fine Arts (Art & Culture), An Introduction to Indian Art Part II, Chapter 4: The Deccani Schools of Painting
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The history of Deccani painting can largely be traced from the late sixteenth century until the 1680s. What happened in the 1680s?
(1) The British took over the Deccan
(2) The Marathas crowned Shivaji
(3) The Vijayanagara empire fell
(4) The Mughals conquered the Deccan
= 4
~ The Deccani style flourished under the Deccan Sultans until the Mughal conquest in the 1680s.

Q 2 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
After the Mughal conquest, the Deccani style continued into the nineteenth century under:
(1) The Marathas of Tanjore
(2) The Wodeyars of Mysore
(3) The Asafiya dynasty and the courts under the Nizam in the State of Hyderabad
(4) The Portuguese of Goa
= 3
~ The style lived on under the Asafiya dynasty and in the provincial courts of Rajas and Nawabs under the Nizam in Hyderabad State.

Q 3 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The earliest examples of Deccani painting are found in a volume of poems celebrating the reign of:
(1) Ibrahim Adil Shah II of Bijapur
(2) Hussain Nizam Shah I of Ahmadnagar
(3) Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah of Golconda
(4) Ali Adil Shah I of Bijapur
= 2
~ The volume celebrates Hussain Nizam Shah I of Ahmadnagar (1553–1565). Of its 12 miniatures, those of the queen and her marriage are admired for their colours and lines.

Q 4 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The high circular horizon and the gold sky seen in Deccani paintings show the influence of:
(1) Persia
(2) Europe
(3) China
(4) The Mughal court
= 1
~ The chapter says all the Deccani kingdoms owe their landscape idiom, with its high circular horizon and gold sky, to Persia.

Q 5 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
In the Ahmadnagar paintings, a long scarf worn below the hips is a southern fashion also seen in which murals?
(1) Lepakshi
(2) Ajanta
(3) Badami
(4) Sittanavasal
= 1
~ The scarf passing round the body below the hips is a southern fashion seen in the Lepakshi frescoes; the women also wear a choli and long braided pigtails ending in a tassel.

Q 6 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The Nujum al-Ulum (1570) of Bijapur is:
(1) A volume of poems with 12 miniatures
(2) An encyclopaedia with 876 miniatures of weapons, utensils, constellations and more
(3) A Ragamala set of 36 folios
(4) A biography of the Sultan
= 2
~ The Nujum al-Ulum is an encyclopaedia with 876 miniatures; its women are tall and slender and wear south Indian dress.

Q 7 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Ibrahim Adil Shah II of Bijapur, an expert in Indian music, wrote a book on music called the:
(1) Kitab-i-Hind
(2) Tarif-i-Hussain Shahi
(3) Nauras-nama
(4) Ragadarpan
= 3
~ Ibrahim II (1580–1627) wrote the Nauras-nama. He owned the Nujum al-Ulum and may have commissioned the Ragamala series of the 1590s.

Q 8 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Which two rulers of Bijapur are named in the chapter as great patrons of art and literature?
(1) Yusuf Adil Shah and Ismail Adil Shah
(2) Muhammad Adil Shah and Ali Adil Shah II
(3) Hussain Nizam Shah I and Burhan Nizam Shah
(4) Ali Adil Shah I and Ibrahim Adil Shah II
= 4
~ Ali Adil Shah I (1558–1580) and his successor Ibrahim II (1580–1627) were the great patrons of art and literature at Bijapur.

Q 9 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The 'Throne of Prosperity' in the Nujum al-Ulum is a symbolic diagram of an auspicious throne of how many stages?
(1) Three
(2) Five
(3) Seven
(4) Nine
= 3
~ The seven-stage throne is supported by elephants, tigers, palm trees, peacocks and primitive tribes; its arabesques and deep blue sky follow the Persian tradition.

Q 10 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The astronomical illustrations of the Nujum al-Ulum may derive from manuscripts of which country, with which Bijapur had close ties?
(1) Portugal
(2) China
(3) Egypt
(4) Turkey
= 4
~ Bijapur had close ties with Turkey, and the astronomical illustrations may come from Ottoman Turkish manuscripts.

Q 11 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The seventeenth-century painting 'Yogini', now in the Chester Beatty Library, with a myna bird and a vertical composition, is associated with which Deccani school?
(1) Bijapur
(2) Golconda
(3) Ahmadnagar
(4) Hyderabad
= 1
~ The chapter discusses the Yogini among the Bijapur paintings; its artist is unknown.

Q 12 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Golconda became an independent kingdom in:
(1) 1490
(2) 1512
(3) 1565
(4) 1687
= 2
~ Golconda became independent in 1512 and by the end of the sixteenth century was the wealthiest of the Deccan kingdoms.

Q 13 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Consider the following statements about the wealth of Golconda:
1. It came from trade through east-coast ports, from where iron and cotton goods went to South East Asia.
2. It also came from trade with Persia.
3. Its diamonds were discovered in the fourteenth century.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 2
~ Statement 3 is wrong: diamonds were discovered early in the seventeenth century. Golconda paintings show the gold jewellery worn by both women and men.

Q 14 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
In the late seventeenth century, which merchants carried portraits of the Golconda sultans to Europe?
(1) Dutch
(2) Portuguese
(3) French
(4) English
= 1
~ Dutch merchants carried such portraits to Europe; they were probably made for the bazaar.

Q 15 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Earlier Golconda paintings of 1635–1650, as large as eight feet high, were used as:
(1) Book covers
(2) Temple banners
(3) Carpets
(4) Wall hangings
= 4
~ These large paintings served as wall hangings.

Q 16 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The 'Composite Horse' of the early seventeenth century, a galloping horse made up of human figures with cranes, lions and Chinese clouds, belongs to which school?
(1) Bijapur
(2) Ahmadnagar
(3) Golconda
(4) Hyderabad
= 3
~ The Composite Horse is a Golconda painting; its palette is mainly brown and blue.

Q 17 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The painting 'Chand Bibi Playing Polo' shows the queen who resisted the attempts of which emperor to take her state?
(1) Babur
(2) Humayun
(3) Aurangzeb
(4) Akbar
= 4
~ Chand Bibi resisted Akbar's attempts to take her state. The painting itself is a provincial work of a much later period.

Q 18 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The painting 'Hazrat Nizamuddin Auliya and Amir Khusrau', showing the thirteenth-century Sufi saint listening to his disciple, is a provincial painting from:
(1) Hyderabad, Deccan
(2) Lucknow
(3) Bikaner
(4) Murshidabad
= 1
~ It is a provincial painting from Hyderabad in the Deccan, now in the National Museum, New Delhi.

Q 19 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The dark outlines of the Ragamala painting 'Ragini Pathamsika of Raga Hindola' (c. 1590–95), possibly from Bijapur, are linked in the chapter to:
(1) Mughal portraits
(2) The Ajanta murals
(3) Persian carpets
(4) European engravings
= 2
~ The chapter links its dark outlines to the Ajanta murals; the painting also shows a veena and Devanagari script.

Q 20 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Which of the following is NOT a feature of Deccani painting described in the chapter?
(1) Intense colours
(2) Dense compositions and an air of romance
(3) Strict optical realism with European perspective
(4) Affinity with regional aesthetics
= 3
~ Deccani painting is known for sensuality, intense colours, dense compositions, romance and regional affinities, not European-style realism.
