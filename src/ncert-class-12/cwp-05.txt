# NCERT Class 12 Political Science, Contemporary World Politics, Chapter 5: Security in the Contemporary World
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
According to the traditional notion of security, the greatest danger to a country comes from:
(1) Disease and epidemics
(2) Military threats, which endanger its sovereignty, independence and territorial integrity
(3) Climate change
(4) Migration
= 2
~ The traditional view focuses on the use or threat of military force, mainly from other countries. Its responses are deterrence, defence, balance of power and alliances.

Q 2 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Preventing war by raising the costs of an attack so high that the enemy is discouraged from attacking is called:
(1) Disarmament
(2) Surrender
(3) Arms control
(4) Deterrence
= 4
~ Deterrence aims to prevent war; defence aims to limit or end it once it starts. Nuclear deterrence was central to the Cold War.

Q 3 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
A coalition of states that coordinate their actions to deter or defend against military attack is called:
(1) An alliance
(2) A cartel
(3) A federation
(4) A confederation of NGOs
= 1
~ Alliances like NATO are formed to increase the combined power of members. Countries form alliances based on interests, which can change over time.

Q 4 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following is a component of traditional security policy discussed in the chapter?
(1) Fighting HIV/AIDS
(2) Reducing poverty
(3) Balance of power
(4) Environmental protection
= 3
~ Traditional security includes deterrence, defence, balance of power and alliance building. The other options belong to non-traditional security.

Q 5 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The Biological Weapons Convention (1972) and the Chemical Weapons Convention (1992) are examples of:
(1) Military alliances
(2) Arms control only
(3) Disarmament, banning the making and possession of entire categories of weapons
(4) Trade agreements
= 3
~ Disarmament requires states to give up certain kinds of weapons. More than 155 states acceded to the BWC and 181 to the CWC, including all great powers.

Q 6 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Agreements that regulate the acquisition or development of weapons, like the Anti-Ballistic Missile Treaty of 1972 between the USA and the USSR, are examples of:
(1) Arms control
(2) Disarmament
(3) Deterrence
(4) Balance of power
= 1
~ Arms control limits the number or kinds of weapons rather than banning them altogether. SALT I, SALT II and START are other examples.

Q 7 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (NPT) of 1968 has been criticised, including by India, because:
(1) It banned all nuclear energy
(2) It was signed by every country
(3) It required all countries to give up nuclear weapons at once
(4) It allowed countries that had tested nuclear weapons before 1967 to keep them, while forbidding others to acquire them
= 4
~ The NPT treats the five nuclear weapon states differently from the rest. India sees this as discriminatory.

Q 8 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The non-traditional notion of security, according to the chapter, includes threats to:
(1) Only the territory of states
(2) Human beings and their well-being, such as threats from poverty, disease, environmental damage and terrorism
(3) Only the armed forces
(4) Only the government
= 2
~ Non-traditional security covers a wider range of threats and sees individuals and humanity, not only states, as the object of security.

Q 9 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
"Human security", as discussed in the chapter, puts the main emphasis on:
(1) The security of borders only
(2) The security of the army
(3) The security of governments in power
(4) The protection of people more than the protection of states
= 4
~ Supporters of human security argue that states exist to protect people. The narrow view focuses on freedom from violence; broader views include freedom from want and freedom from fear.

Q 10 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following is an example of a "global threat" in the non-traditional sense?
(1) A border dispute between two states
(2) Global warming, which threatens people everywhere
(3) A civil war in one province
(4) A coup in a small country
= 2
~ Threats like climate change and epidemics cross borders and cannot be handled by one country alone, so they need global cooperation.

Q 11 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following epidemics are mentioned in the chapter as examples of health threats that spread across borders?
(1) HIV/AIDS, bird flu and SARS
(2) Only malaria
(3) Only polio
(4) None
= 1
~ The chapter notes how diseases spread through travel and trade. Such threats require cooperation among countries.

Q 12 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Terrorism, according to the chapter, refers to:
(1) Wars between states declared by governments
(2) Peaceful protest
(3) Political violence that targets civilians deliberately and indiscriminately, to create fear
(4) A military alliance
= 3
~ Terrorists use violence against ordinary people to force governments or societies to change policy. Cases include hijacking, bombings and the attacks of 11 September 2001.

Q 13 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Why is cooperation between countries needed for many non-traditional threats?
(1) Because only one country can solve them
(2) Because problems like terrorism, epidemics, migration and climate change cross borders and cannot be solved by any single country
(3) Because countries have no armies
(4) Because the UN forbids national action
= 2
~ Cooperative security may involve international organisations, NGOs, businesses and civil society. Military force may still be needed, but only as a last resort.

Q 14 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
India's security strategy, as outlined in the chapter, has four broad components. Which of the following is NOT one of them?
(1) Strengthening military capabilities
(2) Strengthening international norms and institutions
(3) Joining a military bloc led by a superpower
(4) Meeting internal security challenges and developing the economy to lift people out of poverty
= 3
~ India has relied on its own military strength, support for international institutions and disarmament, dealing with internal challenges and economic development. It has not joined a military bloc.

Q 15 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Why did India conduct nuclear tests in 1998, according to the chapter?
(1) To start a war
(2) To join NATO
(3) To sell weapons
(4) To safeguard national security in a region with nuclear-armed neighbours, while declaring a policy of no first use
= 4
~ India justified the tests on security grounds. It committed to "no first use" and credible minimum deterrence, and continued to support universal disarmament.

Q 16 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
"Internal security" in the traditional notion refers to:
(1) Threats from within a country, such as secessionist movements and internal violence
(2) Threats from outer space
(3) Climate change
(4) Global epidemics
= 1
~ After the Second World War, many new countries faced internal conflicts and secessionist movements, so internal security became a major concern alongside external threats.

Q 17 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following is an example of a non-traditional threat from the movement of people?
(1) Flows of refugees and migrants fleeing war, persecution or poverty, which raise issues of rights and security
(2) An army crossing a border
(3) A missile test
(4) A naval blockade
= 1
~ Refugees and internally displaced people need protection. Migration also creates tensions in host areas, which governments must manage humanely.

Q 18 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following treaties banned the testing of nuclear weapons in the atmosphere, outer space and under water, but not underground?
(1) NPT, 1968
(2) CWC, 1992
(3) BWC, 1972
(4) Partial Test Ban Treaty, 1963
= 4
~ The Partial (Limited) Test Ban Treaty of 1963 was an early arms control step. Later agreements tried to limit nuclear arsenals further.

Q 19 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Global poverty is a security threat in the non-traditional sense because:
(1) Poor countries have no armies
(2) Rich countries cannot trade
(3) It harms the well-being of large numbers of people and can lead to migration, conflict and instability
(4) It affects only individuals and never societies
= 3
~ Much of the world's population growth is in poorer countries, and poverty can drive migration and conflict. Reducing poverty is therefore part of security.

Q 20 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
According to the chapter, a "cooperative" approach to security means:
(1) Only military alliances
(2) Working together across states and with international organisations, NGOs and civil society to deal with shared threats, with force only as a last resort
(3) Each country acting alone
(4) Abolishing all armies immediately
= 2
~ Many modern threats require cooperation rather than unilateral military action. Cooperative security brings many actors together to address them.
