# NCERT Class 12 Geography, India: People and Economy, Chapter 3: Land Resources and Agriculture
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). November 2026.

Q 1 | geo | India: People and Economy
In land revenue records, land left uncultivated for more than five years is classified as:
(1) Current fallow
(2) Fallow other than current fallow
(3) Culturable wasteland
(4) Barren and wasteland
= 3
~ Culturable wasteland can be brought under cultivation after reclamation. Current fallow is left uncultivated for one year or less, and other fallow for one to five years.

Q 2 | geo | India: People and Economy
Land under orchards and fruit trees is included in which land-use category?
(1) Net area sown
(2) Area under miscellaneous tree crops and groves
(3) Forests
(4) Permanent pastures
= 2
~ This land is not included in the net sown area and much of it is privately owned.

Q 3 | geo | India: People and Economy
Which land-use category shows the highest rate of increase in India since 1950-51, according to the chapter?
(1) Barren and wasteland
(2) Area under non-agricultural uses
(3) Culturable wasteland
(4) Permanent pastures
= 2
~ Growth of settlements, infrastructure and industries has increased the land under non-agricultural uses. Barren and culturable wastelands have declined.

Q 4 | geo | India: People and Economy
Common property resources (CPRs) are especially important for which groups in rural areas?
(1) Large landowners only
(2) Landless people, marginal farmers and women, who collect fodder and fuel from them
(3) Industrialists
(4) Urban residents
= 2
~ CPRs such as community forests, pastures and village water bodies are owned by the state for community use; everyone has a right of access with specified obligations.

Q 5 | geo | India: People and Economy
Cropping intensity is calculated as:
(1) (Net sown area ÷ gross cropped area) × 100
(2) (Irrigated area ÷ total area) × 100
(3) (Gross cropped area ÷ net sown area) × 100
(4) (Fallow land ÷ net sown area) × 100
= 3
~ A higher cropping intensity means the same land is cropped more than once in a year.

Q 6 | geo | India: People and Economy
Match the cropping seasons with the crops grown in northern India:
A. Kharif — 1. Wheat, gram, rapeseed and mustard, barley
B. Rabi — 2. Vegetables, fruits and fodder
C. Zaid — 3. Rice, cotton, bajra, maize, jowar and tur
Select the correct answer:
(1) A-3, B-1, C-2
(2) A-1, B-3, C-2
(3) A-2, B-1, C-3
(4) A-3, B-2, C-1
= 1
~ Kharif coincides with the south-west monsoon, rabi begins with the onset of winter, and zaid is a short summer season on irrigated land.

Q 7 | geo | India: People and Economy
Why can the same crops be grown up to three times a year in southern India, unlike in the north?
(1) Southern India gets snowfall
(2) There is no monsoon in the south
(3) Temperatures are high enough to grow tropical crops in any period, provided soil moisture is available
(4) The soils of the south are always waterlogged
= 3
~ The clear distinction between kharif, rabi and zaid does not exist in southern India.

Q 8 | geo | India: People and Economy
Consider the following statements about types of farming:
1. Protective irrigation aims to protect crops from soil moisture deficiency, covering as much area as possible.
2. Dryland farming is largely confined to regions with annual rainfall less than 75 cm.
3. Wetland farming regions grow hardy crops like ragi, bajra and guar.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1
~ Ragi, bajra, moong, gram and guar are dryland crops. Wetland regions grow water-intensive crops such as rice, jute and sugarcane.

Q 9 | geo | India: People and Economy
In West Bengal, farmers grow three crops of rice in a year. They are called:
(1) Kharif, rabi and zaid
(2) Arhar, moong and urad
(3) Jhum, milpa and ladang
(4) Aus, aman and boro
= 4
~ In the Himalayas and north-western parts, rice is grown as a kharif crop during the south-west monsoon.

Q 10 | geo | India: People and Economy
Rice cultivation was introduced in the irrigated areas of Punjab and Haryana in which decade, following the Green Revolution?
(1) 1950s
(2) 1970s
(3) 1990s
(4) 2000s
= 2
~ Improved seeds, high use of fertilisers and pesticides, and lower pest attack in the dry climate give high rice yields there.

Q 11 | geo | India: People and Economy
About 85 per cent of the total area under wheat is concentrated in:
(1) The southern plateau and coastal plains
(2) The Western Ghats
(3) The north-eastern states
(4) The north and central regions, i.e. the Indo-Gangetic plain, Malwa plateau and Himalayas up to 2,700 m
= 4
~ Wheat is a rabi crop of the temperate zone, mostly grown under irrigation; yields exceed 4,000 kg per hectare in Punjab and Haryana.

Q 12 | geo | India: People and Economy
Which state alone produces more than half of India's jowar?
(1) Rajasthan
(2) Uttar Pradesh
(3) Karnataka
(4) Maharashtra
= 4
~ Jowar is the main food crop of the semi-arid areas of central and southern India; Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana are other producers.

Q 13 | geo | India: People and Economy
Bajra, a hardy crop that resists frequent dry spells and drought, is grown mainly in:
(1) The north-western and western parts of the country
(2) The north-eastern states
(3) The delta regions of the east coast
(4) The Western Ghats
= 1
~ Rajasthan, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Uttar Pradesh and Haryana are the leading producers.

Q 14 | geo | India: People and Economy
Consider the following statements about pulses:
1. Pulses are legume crops that increase the natural fertility of soils through nitrogen fixation.
2. Gram has been displaced from much of the cropped area of Haryana, Punjab and northern Rajasthan after the Green Revolution.
3. Maharashtra alone contributes about one-third of India's tur production.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ Pulses occupy about 11 per cent of the cropped area. Being rainfed crops of drylands, their yields are low and fluctuate.

Q 15 | geo | India: People and Economy
Which two states together produce about 90 per cent of India's soyabean?
(1) Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra

(4) Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh

Q 16 | geo | India: People and Economy
Long staple (American) cotton grown in the north-western parts of India is called:
(1) Aman
(2) Tur
(3) Boro
(4) Narma
= 4
~ India grows both short staple (Indian) and long staple cotton. Cotton needs a clear sky during flowering.

Q 17 | geo | India: People and Economy
Which state accounts for about three-fourths of India's jute production?
(1) Assam
(2) Bihar
(3) West Bengal
(4) Odisha
= 3
~ India lost large jute-growing areas to East Pakistan (Bangladesh) during Partition. India now produces about three-fifths of the world's jute.

Q 18 | geo | India: People and Economy
Tea plantations in India started in the 1840s in:
(1) The Nilgiri hills
(2) The Brahmaputra valley of Assam
(3) Darjeeling
(4) The Cardamom hills
= 2
~ Assam is still the major tea-growing area. Plantations later spread to Darjeeling, Jalpaiguri and Cooch Behar, and to the Nilgiri and Cardamom hills.

Q 19 | geo | India: People and Economy
High-yielding varieties (HYVs) of wheat and rice introduced in India in the mid-1960s came from:
(1) Wheat from Mexico and rice from the Philippines
(2) Wheat from the USA and rice from China
(3) Wheat from Australia and rice from Japan
(4) Both from Mexico
= 1
~ The HYV package with chemical fertilisers and irrigation in Punjab, Haryana, western UP, Andhra Pradesh and Gujarat led to the Green Revolution.

Q 20 | geo | India: People and Economy
Among the three revenue systems of the British period — Mahalwari, Ryotwari and Zamindari — which one does the chapter describe as the most exploitative for peasants?
(1) Mahalwari
(2) Ryotwari
(3) Zamindari
(4) All were equally fair
= 3
~ Lack of effective land reforms after Independence continued the unequal distribution of land, one of the problems of Indian agriculture.
