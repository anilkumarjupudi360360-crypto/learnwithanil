Q 1 | geo | India: People and Economy
Which of the following is NOT listed as a factor responsible for different types of rural settlements in India?
(1) Physical features such as terrain, altitude, climate and water availability
(2) Cultural and ethnic factors such as social structure, caste and religion
(3) Security factors such as defence against thefts and robberies
(4) Number of factories in the village
= 4
~ Rural settlement types are shaped by physical, cultural and ethnic, and security factors.

Q 2 | geo | India: People and Economy
Rural settlements in India are broadly classified into four types. Which of the following is NOT one of them?
(1) Clustered
(2) Semi-clustered
(3) Hamleted
(4) Metropolitan
= 4
~ The four types are clustered (agglomerated or nucleated), semi-clustered (fragmented), hamleted, and dispersed (isolated).

Q 3 | geo | India: People and Economy
In Rajasthan, compact (clustered) settlements have developed mainly because of:
(1) Security from wild animals
(2) Scarcity of water, requiring maximum use of available water
(3) Fertile alluvial soil
(4) Heavy rainfall
= 2
~ Clustered settlements are also found in fertile alluvial plains and north-eastern states, and for security reasons in Bundelkhand and Nagaland.

Q 4 | geo | India: People and Economy
Semi-clustered settlements, where the land-owning community occupies the centre and lower strata live on the outer flanks, are widespread in:
(1) The Gujarat plain and parts of Rajasthan
(2) Meghalaya and Kerala
(3) The Himalayan valleys only
(4) The Thar desert only
= 1
~ They may result from fragmentation of a large compact village or from clustering in a restricted area.

Q 5 | geo | India: People and Economy
Units of a fragmented village bearing a common name, locally called panna, para, palli, nagla or dhani, are characteristic of:
(1) Clustered settlements
(2) Hamleted settlements
(3) Dispersed settlements
(4) Urban agglomerations
= 2
~ Hamleted settlements, often motivated by social and ethnic factors, are found in the middle and lower Ganga plain, Chhattisgarh and lower Himalayan valleys.

Q 6 | geo | India: People and Economy
Dispersed or isolated settlements, caused by extremely fragmented terrain, are found in many areas of:
(1) Meghalaya, Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh and Kerala
(2) Punjab and Haryana plains
(3) The Gujarat plain
(4) The middle Ganga plain
= 1
~ They appear as isolated huts or small hamlets in remote jungles or on small hills, with farms or pastures on the slopes.

Q 8 | geo | India: People and Economy

(2) Durgapur, Bhilai, Sindri and Barauni

Q 9 | geo | India: People and Economy

(1) An ancient religious town

~ The British and other Europeans first developed trading ports like Surat, Daman, Goa and Pondicherry, then consolidated around Mumbai, Chennai and Kolkata.

Q 10 | geo | India: People and Economy
Which group of towns developed after Independence as administrative headquarters?
(1) Ghaziabad, Rohtak and Gurugram
(2) Durgapur, Bhilai, Sindri and Barauni
(3) Chandigarh, Bhubaneswar, Gandhinagar and Dispur
(4) Varanasi, Prayag and Madurai
= 3
~ Durgapur, Bhilai, Sindri and Barauni developed as industrial centres, and Ghaziabad, Rohtak and Gurugram as satellite towns around Delhi.

Q 11 | geo | India: People and Economy
What was the level of urbanisation in India in 2011?
(1) 10.84 per cent
(2) 27.78 per cent
(3) 31.16 per cent
(4) 50.00 per cent
= 3
~ Urbanisation is measured as the percentage of urban population to total population. India's urban population grew about eleven-fold in the twentieth century.

Q 12 | geo | India: People and Economy
