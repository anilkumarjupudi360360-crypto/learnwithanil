(1) V.S. Sukthankar

Q 2 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
What did scholars working on the critical edition of the Mahabharata find?
(1) That all manuscripts were exactly the same
(2) That the epic was written in a single year
(3) That the epic was composed in Tamil
(4) That there were common elements across manuscripts from all over the subcontinent, as well as many regional variations
= 4
~ The variations reflect the complex processes that shaped social histories in different regions over centuries.

Q 3 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Patriliny means:
(1) Tracing descent from father to son, grandson and so on
(2) Tracing descent through the mother
(3) A system where the eldest daughter inherits
(4) Marriage within the same clan
= 1
~ The Mahabharata is a story of a feud over land and power between two groups of cousins, the Kauravas and the Pandavas, who belonged to the Kuru lineage.

Q 4 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
"Exogamy" refers to:
(1) Marriage within the same caste
(2) Marriage of one woman to several men
(3) Marriage of one man to several women
(4) Marriage outside a unit, such as the gotra
= 4
~ Brahmanical rules forbade marriage within the same gotra. Polygyny and polyandry are terms for plural marriage.

Q 5 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
The Dharmasutras and Dharmashastras recognised how many forms of marriage?
(1) Four
(2) Eight
(3) Six
(4) Ten
= 2
~ The first four were considered "good", while the rest were condemned. These forms may have been practised by people who did not accept Brahmanical norms.

Q 6 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Inscriptions of the Satavahana rulers show that:
(1) They followed the rules of gotra exogamy strictly
(2) Their queens kept names derived from their fathers' gotras, and some queens were from the same gotra as their husbands
(3) They had no queens
(4) Their kings took the gotra of their mothers' husbands only
= 2
~ The Satavahana evidence shows that Brahmanical norms were not always followed. Kings were also identified through metronymics, names derived from the mother, such as Gotami-puta Siri-Satakani.

Q 7 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Draupadi's marriage to the five Pandavas is an example of:
(1) Polygyny
(2) Endogamy
(3) Polyandry
(4) Exogamy only
= 3
~ Polyandry, a woman having several husbands, was rare in Brahmanical tradition. Historians have suggested that it may have existed among some ruling groups.

Q 8 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
According to the Brahmanical texts, the ideal occupations of the four varnas were assigned. Which of the following is correctly matched?
(1) Shudras - serve the three "higher" varnas
(2) Kshatriyas - study and teach the Vedas, perform sacrifices
(3) Vaishyas - engage in warfare and protect people
(4) Shudras - agriculture and trade
= 1
~ Brahmanas were to study and teach the Vedas, Kshatriyas to fight and protect, and Vaishyas to engage in agriculture, pastoralism and trade.

Q 9 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Which hymn of the Rigveda describes the origin of the four varnas from the body of a primeval man?
(1) Gayatri mantra
(2) Purusha sukta
(3) Nasadiya sukta
(4) Agni sukta
= 2
~ According to it, the Brahmana came from his mouth, the Kshatriya from his arms, the Vaishya from his thighs and the Shudra from his feet. Brahmanas used this to claim a divine origin for the varna order.

Q 10 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
The Shakas, who came from Central Asia, were regarded by Brahmanas as "mlechchhas" (barbarians, outsiders). Yet the Shaka ruler Rudradaman is known for:
(1) Repairing the Sudarshana lake, as recorded in a Sanskrit inscription
(2) Building the Great Stupa at Sanchi
(3) Writing the Arthashastra
(4) Founding Pataliputra
= 1
~ The case shows that rulers from outside the varna order could become powerful and patronise Sanskrit traditions.

Q 11 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Which of the following is true of the Chandalas, according to the Manusmriti?
(1) They were to perform sacrifices
(2) They were to live in the centre of the village
(3) They were to be kings
(4) They had to live outside the village, use discarded utensils, wear clothes of the dead and iron ornaments, and dispose of corpses and dead animals
= 4
~ Brahmanas regarded certain occupations as "polluting". The Chinese pilgrim Fa Xian wrote that "untouchables" had to strike a stick to warn others of their approach.

Q 12 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
"Stridhana" in the Manusmriti refers to:
(1) The dowry paid by the bride's father to the king
(2) The wealth a woman received as gifts at her marriage, which she could keep and her children could inherit
(3) A tax on women
(4) The share of land given to daughters
= 2
~ Manusmriti also said that the paternal estate was to be divided equally among sons after the death of the parents. Women could not claim a share of the paternal estate.

Q 13 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
The inscription from Mandasor (Madhya Pradesh) records the history of a guild of:
(1) Potters
(2) Iron smiths
(3) Silk weavers who migrated from Lata (Gujarat) to Dashapura (Mandasor) and built a temple to the sun god
(4) Merchants from Rome
= 3
~ The inscription shows that guild members took up other occupations and shared interests beyond their craft. Guilds were not just bound by caste.

Q 14 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
In the Mahabharata, who is said to have given the advice to Duryodhana not to go to war, saying that the best way to rule is by self-control?
(1) Kunti
(2) Draupadi
(3) Satyavati
(4) Gandhari, his mother
= 4
~ Duryodhana did not listen, and the war took place. The episode shows a mother's voice in the epic.

Q 15 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
The Mahabharata is traditionally believed to have been composed by:
(1) Valmiki
(2) Kalidasa
(3) The sage Vyasa, who is said to have dictated it to the god Ganesha
(4) Panini
= 3
~ Historians suggest that it was composed over a long period, roughly from about 500 BCE onwards, and continued to grow over centuries.

Q 16 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Ekalavya's story in the Mahabharata, in which Drona asked for his thumb, is often used to discuss:
(1) Rules of marriage
(2) Trade practices
(3) Gotra rules
(4) How social norms tried to restrict learning and status for those outside the privileged groups
= 4
~ Ekalavya was a forest-dwelling nishada. Drona refused to take him as a pupil and later demanded his right thumb as a fee.

Q 17 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Which of the following statements about the Mahabharata is correct?
(1) It is a story of a feud over land and power between the Kauravas and Pandavas, and it contains narrative and didactic sections
(2) It was composed in Persian
(3) It has no didactic sections
(4) It is a book of laws only
= 1
~ Didactic sections give norms of conduct. The narrative sections tell stories, such as the dicing game and the war.

Q 18 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
In early Tamil society, according to the Sangam texts, which groups are mentioned?
(1) Only kings and priests
(2) Only slaves
(3) Large landowners (vellalar), ploughmen (uzhavar) and slaves (adimai)
(4) Only merchants
= 3
~ These categories show that differences in wealth and access to land existed in early Tamil society.

Q 19 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
In the Brahmanical tradition, after marriage, women were expected to:
(1) Keep their father's gotra always
(2) Give up their father's gotra and adopt their husband's gotra
(3) Have no gotra at all
(4) Take their mother's gotra
= 2
~ The Satavahana evidence shows that this rule was not always followed. Many queens kept their fathers' gotra names.

Q 20 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Which of the following best describes the chapter's view of the varna order?
(1) It was followed exactly by everyone
(2) It existed only in theory with no effect
(3) It was a set of Brahmanical norms, and actual social practice often differed, as many communities and rulers did not fit neatly into it
(4) It was created by the British

~ Historians compare the prescriptions of the Dharmasutras with other sources to understand what actually happened in society.
