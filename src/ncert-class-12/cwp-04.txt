# NCERT Class 12 Political Science, Contemporary World Politics, Chapter 4: International Organisations
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The United Nations was founded on:
(1) 24 October 1945
(2) 15 August 1947
(3) 26 June 1919
(4) 10 December 1948
= 1
~ The UN came into being on 24 October 1945, after the UN Charter signed at San Francisco in June 1945 was ratified. 24 October is celebrated as UN Day.

Q 2 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The United Nations replaced which earlier international organisation that had failed to prevent the Second World War?
(1) The Warsaw Pact
(2) NATO
(3) The League of Nations
(4) The Commonwealth
= 3
~ The League of Nations was set up after the First World War. Its failure to stop aggression in the 1930s led to the creation of the UN with stronger machinery.

Q 3 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
How many founding member states did the UN have in 1945?
(1) 15
(2) 193
(3) 100
(4) 51
= 4
~ The UN started with 51 members. With decolonisation and new states, the membership has grown to 193.

Q 4 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which five countries are the permanent members of the UN Security Council?
(1) USA, UK, France, Germany and Japan
(2) USA, Russia, UK, France and China
(3) USA, Russia, India, China and Brazil
(4) UK, France, Italy, Japan and China
= 2
~ The five permanent members were the main victors of the Second World War. Each has a veto over Security Council decisions.

Q 5 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Non-permanent members of the UN Security Council are elected for a term of:
(1) One year
(2) Five years
(3) Two years
(4) Life
= 3
~ There are ten non-permanent members, elected by the General Assembly for two-year terms. They do not have the veto.

Q 6 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The "veto" power in the UN Security Council means that:
(1) Any member of the General Assembly can stop a resolution
(2) Any one permanent member can block a decision of the Security Council by voting against it
(3) The Secretary-General can cancel any decision
(4) Non-permanent members decide alone
= 2
~ A negative vote by a permanent member stops a substantive resolution. Critics argue the veto is undemocratic and reflects the world of 1945, not today's.

Q 7 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Who has been the Secretary-General of the United Nations since 2017?
(1) Kofi Annan
(2) Ban Ki-moon
(3) Boutros Boutros-Ghali
(4) António Guterres
= 4
~ António Guterres of Portugal became Secretary-General in January 2017. The first Secretary-General was Trygve Lie of Norway.

Q 8 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The International Monetary Fund (IMF) and the World Bank were set up after a conference held in 1944 at:
(1) Bretton Woods
(2) San Francisco
(3) Geneva
(4) Dumbarton Oaks
= 1
~ The Bretton Woods institutions were created to manage the world's financial system and to finance reconstruction and development.

Q 9 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The World Trade Organization (WTO) was set up in 1995 as the successor to:
(1) The IMF
(2) The General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT)
(3) The World Bank
(4) The League of Nations
= 2
~ The WTO sets rules for global trade. Developing countries complain that rich countries dominate its decision-making.

Q 10 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA), set up in 1957, works to:
(1) Build nuclear weapons for member states
(2) Fix oil prices
(3) Run elections
(4) Promote the peaceful use of nuclear energy and prevent its use for military purposes
= 4
~ IAEA inspectors check that civilian nuclear facilities are used for peaceful purposes.

Q 11 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Amnesty International and Human Rights Watch, mentioned in the chapter, are examples of:
(1) Non-governmental organisations (NGOs) working for human rights
(2) Military alliances
(3) UN specialised agencies
(4) Regional trade groups
= 1
~ These NGOs research and report on human rights abuses around the world and campaign for action, independently of governments.

Q 12 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following is a major demand in the debate on reforming the UN, as discussed in the chapter?
(1) Abolishing the General Assembly
(2) Moving the UN headquarters to Europe
(3) Reforming the Security Council to reflect present-day realities, including more permanent and non-permanent members from developing countries
(4) Ending the UN's work on development
= 3
~ The Security Council's structure reflects 1945. Many argue it should include more countries from Asia, Africa and Latin America.

Q 13 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
India's claim to a permanent seat in the UN Security Council rests on which of the following grounds, according to the chapter?
(1) India is the oldest country in the world
(2) India has the largest army
(3) India's large population, its economy, its contributions to UN peacekeeping and its democratic record
(4) India was a founder of the League of Nations
= 3
~ India argues that a reformed Council must include major democracies and developing countries. Some countries, especially Pakistan, oppose its claim.

Q 14 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The UN Charter was signed in June 1945 at:
(1) Geneva
(2) New York
(3) London
(4) San Francisco
= 4
~ Representatives of 50 countries signed the Charter at the San Francisco conference. Poland signed later, making it one of the 51 original members.

Q 15 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following is a function of the UN General Assembly?
(1) It alone can use military force
(2) All member states are represented in it with one vote each, and it discusses issues and passes resolutions
(3) It has only five members
(4) Its decisions are always binding on all countries
= 2
~ The General Assembly is the most representative body of the UN. Its resolutions are mostly recommendations; the Security Council's decisions on peace and security are binding.

Q 16 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Why are international organisations important, according to the chapter?
(1) They help countries cooperate on problems like war, disease and poverty, and provide forums to settle disputes peacefully
(2) They replace national governments
(3) They abolish all wars automatically
(4) They have no role in the modern world
= 1
~ International organisations help countries cooperate where no single country can act alone. Their success depends on the willingness of members, especially powerful ones.

Q 17 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following is a UN specialised agency dealing with health?
(1) UNICEF
(2) UNESCO
(3) ILO
(4) World Health Organization (WHO)
= 4
~ The WHO works on global health. UNICEF works for children, UNESCO for education, science and culture, and the ILO for labour standards.

Q 18 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
After the Cold War, the chapter notes that the UN faces the challenge of the dominance of a single superpower. Which country?
(1) China
(2) The United States
(3) Russia
(4) Japan
= 2
~ The USA is the most powerful country and the largest contributor to the UN budget, which gives it great influence. The UN can still provide a forum to discuss and limit unilateral actions.

Q 19 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The meeting in 1941 between US President Roosevelt and British Prime Minister Churchill, which set out principles that later shaped the UN, produced the:
(1) Atlantic Charter
(2) Marshall Plan
(3) Monroe Doctrine
(4) Treaty of Versailles
= 1
~ The Atlantic Charter of August 1941 outlined common goals for the post-war world. It was followed by the Declaration by United Nations in 1942.

Q 20 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
India's important contribution to the UN, highlighted in debates on its role, is its participation in:
(1) The veto
(2) The Warsaw Pact
(3) UN peacekeeping operations
(4) The OPEC cartel
= 3
~ India has been one of the largest contributors of troops to UN peacekeeping missions over the decades.
