Q 1 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The revolt of 1857 began with a mutiny of sepoys at:
(1) Barrackpore
(2) Meerut, on 10 May 1857
(3) Lucknow
(4) Kanpur
= 2
~ The sepoys freed their imprisoned comrades and then rode to Delhi, reaching it on the morning of 11 May.

Q 2 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
On reaching Delhi, the sepoys of Meerut went to the Red Fort and asked for the blessings of:
(1) Nana Sahib
(2) The Nawab of Awadh
(3) Bahadur Shah Zafar, the Mughal emperor
(4) The Peshwa
= 3
~ The emperor, old and with little power, agreed. The revolt thus acquired a legitimacy, as it could now be carried on in the name of the Mughal emperor.

Q 3 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The immediate cause of discontent among the sepoys was:
(1) Higher salaries
(2) A new uniform
(3) Transfer to Burma
(4) The new Enfield rifle, whose cartridges were rumoured to be greased with the fat of cows and pigs
= 4
~ Biting these cartridges would have offended both Hindu and Muslim sepoys. Rumours spread quickly among the troops.

Q 4 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Awadh was annexed by the British in 1856 by Lord Dalhousie on the grounds of:
(1) The Doctrine of Lapse
(2) The ruler being a minor
(3) Misgovernment
(4) The Subsidiary Alliance being broken by Awadh
= 3
~ The Nawab Wajid Ali Shah was dethroned and exiled to Calcutta. People in Awadh grieved for him, and the annexation became a major reason for the revolt there.

Q 5 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Which of the following leaders of 1857 is correctly matched with the place associated with them?
(1) Rani Lakshmibai - Jhansi
(2) Kunwar Singh - Kanpur
(3) Nana Sahib - Arrah
(4) Begum Hazrat Mahal - Delhi
= 1
~ Nana Sahib led the revolt in Kanpur, Kunwar Singh, a zamindar, in Arrah (Bihar), and Begum Hazrat Mahal in Lucknow.

Q 6 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
In Lucknow, the rebels proclaimed as their leader:
(1) Bahadur Shah Zafar
(2) Nana Sahib
(3) Birjis Qadr, the young son of the deposed Nawab Wajid Ali Shah
(4) Maulvi Ahmadullah Shah
= 3
~ His mother, Begum Hazrat Mahal, took an active part in organising the uprising against the British in Awadh.

Q 7 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The "subsidiary alliance", introduced by Lord Wellesley in 1798, required Indian rulers to:
(1) Pay the British army that was stationed in their territory, and not to enter into alliances with other rulers without British permission
(2) Give up their thrones
(3) Pay no taxes to the British
(4) Join the British army as officers
= 1
~ The British used the alliance to extend their control. Awadh was brought under it in 1801.

Q 8 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The "Summary Settlement" of 1856 in Awadh hit hard at:
(1) The sepoys
(2) The talukdars, many of whom lost their estates and forts
(3) The British officers
(4) The Mughal emperor
= 2
~ The British wanted to remove intermediaries and settle revenue directly with the peasants. The talukdars, dispossessed, joined the revolt.

Q 9 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Shah Mal, who led the revolt in his area, belonged to:
(1) The Mughal court
(2) The Bengal army as an officer
(3) Pargana Barout, in Uttar Pradesh, where he mobilised the villagers
(4) Jhansi
= 3
~ He occupied an engineer's bungalow, turned it into his "hall of justice" and settled disputes there. He was killed in battle in July 1857.

Q 10 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Maulvi Ahmadullah Shah, one of the many maulvis who played an important part in the revolt, was associated with:
(1) Faizabad, where he preached against the British and later fought at Lucknow
(2) Bengal
(3) Jhansi
(4) Delhi only
= 1
~ He was known as Danka Shah, the man with the drum. Many people believed he had magical powers.

Q 11 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The Azamgarh Proclamation of 25 August 1857 is an important rebel document. It:
(1) Supported British rule
(2) Was issued by the Governor-General
(3) Listed the grievances of various sections of society against the British and asked people to join the fight
(4) Announced the end of the revolt
= 3
~ It shows that the rebels wanted to restore the pre-British world. The proclamation appealed to zamindars, merchants, public servants, artisans and religious leaders.

Q 12 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Which of the following was a common feature of rebel actions, as discussed in the chapter?
1. They targeted symbols of British authority, such as treasuries, jails, telegraph offices and government records.
2. Communication between sepoys of different cantonments was evident.
3. The rebels issued proclamations in the name of the Mughal emperor.
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 3 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ The pattern of uprisings in different stations shows that there was planning and coordination among the sepoys.

Q 13 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The painting "Relief of Lucknow" (1859) by Thomas Jones Barker celebrates:
(1) The rebels' victory
(2) The capture of Delhi by the rebels
(3) The coronation of Bahadur Shah
(4) British heroes, such as Colin Campbell, Outram and Havelock, who rescued the British garrison
= 4
~ British paintings presented the British as heroes and saviours. Such images were used to arouse feelings of revenge and to reassure the public in Britain.

Q 14 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Which British commissioner of Awadh was killed in the siege of the Lucknow Residency in 1857?
(1) Henry Lawrence
(2) James Outram
(3) Colin Campbell
(4) Lord Canning
= 1
~ The British garrison was besieged in the Residency for months. It was finally relieved in November 1857.

Q 15 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The cartoon "Clemency Canning" in the British magazine Punch:
(1) Praised Canning for his strict punishment
(2) Mocked the Governor-General Canning, who had said that mild measures would win back the loyalty of the sepoys
(3) Showed Canning as a rebel
(4) Was about the Deccan riots
= 2
~ British public opinion demanded harsh revenge. Canning's call for leniency was ridiculed.

Q 16 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Which of the following explains why the British found it difficult to recapture Awadh?
(1) Awadh was an island
(2) The British army had no guns
(3) The Mughal army defended it
(4) The revolt there was popular, with talukdars, peasants and sepoys, many of whom came from Awadh, joining together
= 4
~ Many sepoys of the Bengal army were recruited from villages of Awadh. Their grievances and those of their families were linked.

Q 17 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Delhi was recaptured by the British in:
(1) July 1857
(2) June 1858
(3) January 1858
(4) September 1857
= 4
~ The British then moved to crush the revolt in other areas. Bahadur Shah was tried and exiled to Rangoon.

Q 18 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Which of the following is an Indian representation of 1857 that gave it a nationalist meaning in the twentieth century?
(1) Thomas Jones Barker's painting
(2) V.D. Savarkar's "The Indian War of Independence, 1857"
(3) Punch cartoons
(4) The Deccan Riots Commission report
= 2
~ Nationalist writers presented the revolt as a war of independence. Leaders like Rani Lakshmibai became heroic figures in poems and paintings.

(1) Tribal leader of the Kols of Singhbhum, who led the revolt in his area

Q 20 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Why is the revolt of 1857 studied through its "representations"?
(1) Because no records exist
(2) Because British and Indian writers, artists and later nationalists represented the events very differently, shaping how people remember them
(3) Because the revolt never happened
(4) Because only photographs survive
= 2
~ Official records, paintings, cartoons, popular songs and later nationalist writing all give different pictures of 1857.
