Q 1 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
The Alvars and Nayanars, whose bhakti movements began in Tamil Nadu from about the sixth century, were devotees of:
(1) Alvars - Shiva; Nayanars - Vishnu
(2) Both of Shiva
(3) Alvars - Vishnu; Nayanars - Shiva
(4) Both of the Buddha
= 3
~ They travelled from place to place singing hymns in Tamil in praise of their gods. Some of the places they visited became important pilgrimage centres.

Q 2 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
The compilation of the Alvars' compositions, often described as the "Tamil Veda", is called:
(1) Tevaram
(2) Tirumurai Kural
(3) Nalayira Divyaprabandham
(4) Bijak
= 3
~ The name means "Four Thousand Sacred Compositions". The Tevaram is the collection of the Nayanars' hymns.

Q 3 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Andal, whose devotional songs are widely sung even today, was a:
(1) Woman Nayanar
(2) Woman Alvar, devotee of Vishnu
(3) Sufi saint
(4) Rajput princess
= 2
~ Andal saw herself as the beloved of Vishnu. Karaikkal Ammaiyar, a devotee of Shiva, adopted extreme asceticism. Their compositions challenged patriarchal norms.

Q 4 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Which dynasty supported the Alvars and Nayanars, building splendid Shiva temples at Chidambaram, Thanjavur and Gangaikondacholapuram?
(1) The Pallavas
(2) The Chalukyas
(3) The Pandyas
(4) The Cholas
= 4
~ Chola rulers made land grants and also commissioned superb bronze sculptures of Shiva. Kings claimed divine support through such patronage.

Q 5 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
The Virashaiva (Lingayat) movement in Karnataka in the twelfth century was led by:
(1) Ramanuja
(2) Shankaracharya
(3) Chaitanya
(4) Basavanna (1106-68), a Brahmana who was initially a Jaina and served as a minister at a royal court
= 4
~ Lingayats worship Shiva in the form of a linga. They challenged the idea of caste and questioned the theory of rebirth.

Q 6 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Which of the following practices did the Lingayats encourage, contrary to the Dharmashastras?
(1) Child marriage
(2) Post-puberty marriage and the remarriage of widows
(3) Sati
(4) Animal sacrifice
= 2
~ Lingayats also bury their dead rather than cremate them. Most of what we know about them comes from vachanas, compositions in Kannada.

Q 7 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
In the bhakti tradition, "saguna" bhakti refers to the worship of:
(1) God with attributes, in specific forms such as Shiva, Vishnu and his avatars, and forms of the goddess
(2) An abstract, formless god
(3) The Buddha
(4) Ancestors
= 1
~ Nirguna bhakti was the worship of an abstract form of god. Kabir and Guru Nanak are examples of the nirguna tradition.

Q 8 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
The term "khanqah" in the Sufi tradition refers to:
(1) A hospice where a Sufi master (shaikh, pir) lived and taught his disciples (murids)
(2) The tomb of a Sufi saint
(3) A mosque
(4) A school of law
= 1
~ Khanqahs were centres of social life where people of all backgrounds came. The tomb-shrine of a Sufi was called a dargah.

Q 9 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
The word "urs", used for the annual celebration at a Sufi dargah on the death anniversary of the saint, literally means:
(1) Fasting
(2) Pilgrimage
(3) Wedding, signifying the union of the saint's soul with God
(4) Music
= 3
~ Pilgrimage to the tombs of Sufi saints is called ziyarat. People go there to seek the saint's spiritual grace (barakat).

Q 10 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
The most revered shrine of the Chishti order, which the Mughal emperor Akbar visited fourteen times, is the dargah of:
(1) Shaikh Nizamuddin Auliya in Delhi
(2) Khwaja Muinuddin Chishti in Ajmer
(3) Shaikh Salim Chishti in Fatehpur Sikri
(4) Baba Farid in Pakpattan
= 2
~ Shaikh Muinuddin came to India in the late twelfth century. His dargah attracted people of all backgrounds.

Q 11 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
"Sama", an important practice of the Chishti Sufis, was:
(1) A form of fasting
(2) A tax on pilgrims
(3) A pilgrimage to Mecca
(4) Listening to music, including the singing of mystical poetry, to evoke divine ecstasy
= 4
~ Sama shows the Chishtis' interaction with local devotional traditions. Amir Khusrau, a disciple of Shaikh Nizamuddin, gave a unique form to Chishti sama.

Q 12 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Kabir's poems have been preserved in which of the following traditions?
1. The Bijak, preserved by the Kabirpanth in Varanasi and elsewhere
2. The Kabir Granthavali, associated with the Dadupanth in Rajasthan
3. The Adi Granth Sahib
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ Kabir's poems are in several languages and dialects, some in a special language of nirguna poets called sant bhasha.

Q 13 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Guru Nanak (1469-1539) was born in:
(1) Amritsar
(2) A village near the river Ravi in present-day Pakistan (Nankana Sahib)
(3) Varanasi
(4) Anandpur
= 2
~ Guru Nanak advocated a form of nirguna bhakti and rejected sacrifices, ritual baths, image worship, austerities and the scriptures of both Hindus and Muslims.

Q 14 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
The compositions of Guru Nanak were compiled, along with those of his successors and other religious poets such as Baba Farid, Ravidas and Kabir, into the Adi Granth Sahib in 1604 by:
(1) Guru Arjan
(2) Guru Angad
(3) Guru Tegh Bahadur
(4) Guru Gobind Singh
= 1
~ Guru Gobind Singh later added the compositions of the ninth guru, Guru Tegh Bahadur. The scripture came to be called the Guru Granth Sahib.

Q 15 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Guru Gobind Singh laid the foundation of the Khalsa Panth (army of the pure) in:
(1) 1604
(2) 1539
(3) 1699
(4) 1765
= 3
~ He defined its five symbols. Under him, the community became a socio-religious and military force.

Q 16 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Mirabai, the best-known woman poet in the bhakti tradition, was:
(1) A Brahmana woman of Kashi
(2) A Sufi teacher
(3) A queen of Vijayanagara
(4) A Rajput princess from Merta in Marwar, who was married into the Sisodia clan of Mewar, and recognised Krishna as her only husband
= 4
~ Mirabai is said to have left the palace to live as a wandering singer. According to tradition, her guru was Raidas, a leather worker.

Q 17 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Amir Khusrau, the great poet and musician, was a disciple of:
(1) Shaikh Nizamuddin Auliya
(2) Khwaja Muinuddin Chishti
(3) Shaikh Salim Chishti
(4) Baba Farid
= 1
~ Amir Khusrau introduced the qaul, a hymn sung at the beginning or end of qawwali. He took pride in the Hindavi language.

Q 18 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Under Muslim rulers, non-Muslims who accepted Muslim rule and paid a tax called jizya were regarded as:
(1) Ulama
(2) Murids
(3) Zimmis, or protected subjects
(4) Pirs
= 3
~ In practice, rulers adopted flexible policies. Many gave land grants and tax exemptions to Hindu, Jaina, Zoroastrian, Christian and Jewish institutions.

Q 19 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
Which of the following is the most accurate summary of how historians understand the bhakti and Sufi traditions?
(1) They were complex traditions in which ordinary people, poets and saints shaped religious practice, often questioning caste and ritual, and drawing on the wider culture around them
(2) They had no effect on society
(3) They were limited to kings
(4) They opposed all forms of religion
= 1
~ Historians study the hymns, poems, hagiographies and biographies of saints, as well as the records of states, to understand these traditions.

Q 20 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part II
The term "silsila", in the Sufi tradition, means:
(1) A pilgrimage
(2) A chain or link, signifying the continuous line of succession from the Prophet through the masters to the disciples
(3) The tomb of a saint
(4) A Sufi hymn
= 2
~ Through the silsila, spiritual power and blessings were passed on. The Chishti silsila was one of the most influential in India.
