# NCERT Class 12 Geography, Fundamentals of Human Geography, Chapter 2: The World Population: Distribution, Density and Growth
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). November 2026.

Q 1 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
According to the chapter, about 90 per cent of the world's population lives in about what share of its land area?
(1) 10 per cent
(2) 30 per cent
(3) 50 per cent
(4) 70 per cent
= 1
~ World population is very unevenly distributed: about 90 per cent of people live on about 10 per cent of the land.

Q 2 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Consider the following statements:
1. The 10 most populous countries of the world contribute about 60 per cent of the world's population.
2. Of these 10 countries, six are located in Africa.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 1
~ Six of the 10 most populous countries are in Asia, not Africa.

Q 3 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Density of population is expressed as:
(1) Total population divided by the total number of houses
(2) Number of migrants per year
(3) Births minus deaths
(4) Number of persons per unit area
= 4
~ Density = population ÷ area. For example, 1,50,000 people in 100 sq km gives a density of 1,500 persons per sq km.

Q 4 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Which of the following regions are described in the chapter as very dense, with more than 200 persons per sq km?
(1) The north-eastern part of the USA, north-western Europe, and South, South-east and East Asia
(2) The polar regions and hot deserts
(3) Western China and Norway
(4) The Amazon basin and the Sahara
= 1
~ Areas near the poles, hot and cold deserts and high-rainfall equatorial zones are sparsely populated; western China, southern India, Norway and Sweden have medium density.

Q 5 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Which of the following is a geographical factor influencing the distribution of population?
(1) Urbanisation
(2) Industrialisation
(3) Religious significance of a place
(4) Availability of water
= 4
~ Geographical factors include availability of water, landforms, climate and soils. Urbanisation and industrialisation are economic factors, and religious significance is a social and cultural factor.

Q 6 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
The chapter contrasts two regions to show the effect of landforms on population. Which pair is it?
(1) Japan and Korea
(2) The Sahara and the Kalahari
(3) The Ganga plains, among the most densely populated areas, and the Himalayas, scarcely populated
(4) Siberia and Canada
= 3
~ People prefer flat plains and gentle slopes, which are suitable for farming, roads and industries.

Q 7 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
The Katanga and Zambia copper belt in Africa is given in the chapter as an example of population attracted by:
(1) Fertile soils
(2) Religious centres
(3) Pleasant climate
(4) Mineral deposits
= 4
~ Mining areas attract skilled and semi-skilled workers, making them densely populated.

Q 8 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
The Kobe-Osaka region of Japan is thickly populated mainly because of:
(1) Industrialisation
(2) Mineral resources
(3) Religious importance
(4) Fertile river valleys
= 1
~ Industrial belts provide jobs and attract large numbers of people, not only factory workers but also transport operators, shopkeepers and others.

Q 9 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Which formula correctly gives the actual growth of population of a region?
(1) Births – Deaths
(2) Births – Deaths + In-migration – Out-migration
(3) Births + Deaths – Migration
(4) In-migration – Out-migration only
= 2
~ Natural growth is births minus deaths; actual growth also adds in-migration and subtracts out-migration.

Q 10 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
The Crude Birth Rate (CBR) is expressed as the number of live births in a year per:
(1) 100 of the population
(2) 1,000 of the population
(3) 10,000 of the population
(4) Married women only
= 2
~ CBR = (live births in a year ÷ mid-year population) × 1,000. The Crude Death Rate is calculated the same way for deaths.

Q 11 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Which of the following are "pull" factors of migration?
(1) Unemployment and poor living conditions
(2) Political turmoil and epidemics
(3) Better job opportunities, peace and stability, and security of life and property
(4) Natural disasters and unpleasant climate
= 3
~ Push factors make a place of origin less attractive; pull factors make a destination more attractive.

Q 12 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
People who move into a new place are called:
(1) Emigrants
(2) Refugees
(3) Immigrants
(4) Nomads
= 3
~ Migrants who move into a new place are immigrants; those who move out of a place are emigrants.

Q 13 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
According to the chapter, by how much did the human population increase in the past 500 years?
(1) About twofold
(2) About hundredfold
(3) More than tenfold
(4) It remained constant
= 3
~ The chapter says population increased more than ten times in the past 500 years, and it grew fourfold in the twentieth century alone.

Q 14 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
In the first stage of the demographic transition theory:
(1) Both fertility and mortality are low
(2) Fertility and mortality are both high, and population growth is slow
(3) Fertility is low but mortality is high
(4) Population grows very rapidly
= 2
~ In the first stage people reproduce more to make up for deaths from epidemics and uncertain food supply. About 200 years ago all countries were in this stage.

Q 15 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
In the second stage of demographic transition, the net addition to population is high because:
(1) Both birth and death rates rise sharply
(2) Fertility remains high at first while mortality falls due to better sanitation and health
(3) Both birth and death rates fall equally
(4) Migration stops completely
= 2
~ The widening gap between births and deaths in this expanding stage causes rapid population growth.

Q 16 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Which of the following describes the last stage of the demographic transition?
(1) High fertility and high mortality in an agrarian society
(2) High fertility and falling mortality
(3) Low fertility and low mortality, with an urbanised, literate population that deliberately controls family size
(4) Rapidly rising mortality
= 3
~ In the last stage population is either stable or grows slowly.

Q 17 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Thomas Malthus, in his theory of 1798, argued that:
(1) Food supply would always grow faster than population
(2) Population would grow faster than food supply, leading to famine, disease and war
(3) Population would stop growing on its own
(4) Migration solves all population problems
= 2
~ Malthus predicted that population would outrun food supply, and argued that preventive checks are better than such physical checks.

Q 19 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Which of the following are listed in the chapter as population control measures?
1. Family planning and free availability of contraceptives
2. Tax disincentives
3. Active propaganda
Select the correct answer:
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ Family planning helps space and prevent births; governments have also used propaganda, free contraceptives and tax disincentives.

Q 20 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Consider the following statements about the factors affecting the distribution of population:
1. The Mediterranean regions were inhabited from early periods of history because of their pleasant climate.
2. Areas with very heavy rainfall or extreme climates have high populations.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 1
~ Areas with comfortable climates attract people, while very hot or cold deserts and very high-rainfall areas have low populations.
