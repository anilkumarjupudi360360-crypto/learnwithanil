# NCERT Class 12 Fine Arts (Art & Culture), An Introduction to Indian Art Part II, Chapter 5: The Pahari Schools of Painting
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The word 'Pahari' denotes:
(1) Royal or courtly
(2) Hilly or mountainous
(3) Religious or devotional
(4) Small or miniature
= 2
~ Pahari means 'hilly or mountainous'. The Pahari centres include Basohli, Guler, Kangra, Kullu, Chamba, Mankot, Nurpur, Mandi, Bilaspur and Jammu in the western Himalayas.

Q 2 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The Pahari centres emerged as centres of painting during which period?
(1) Thirteenth to fifteenth century
(2) Fifteenth to sixteenth century
(3) Sixteenth to seventeenth century
(4) Seventeenth to nineteenth century
= 4
~ The hill centres flourished from the seventeenth to the nineteenth century, moving from Basohli's bold style through the Guler phase to the refined Kangra style.

Q 3 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
According to the scholar B.N. Goswamy, the true basis of style in Pahari painting is:
(1) The family of artists rather than the region
(2) The religion of the patron
(3) The political boundaries of each hill state
(4) The paper and pigments used
= 1
~ Goswamy argued that families of artists, especially the family of Pandit Seu, shaped Pahari painting, and that regional labels can mislead because boundaries were fluid.

Q 4 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The Basohli style took its distinctive form under which ruler, who reigned from 1678 to 1695?
(1) Sansar Chand
(2) Govardhan Chand
(3) Kirpal Pal
(4) Balwant Singh
= 3
~ Raja Kirpal Pal gave Basohli its style: strong primary colours, warm yellow backgrounds, stylised vegetation and raised white paint for pearls.

Q 5 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The most notable feature of Basohli painting is:
(1) Small, shiny green particles of beetle wings used on jewellery to imitate emeralds
(2) Photographic landscapes
(3) Pale pastel colours with broad white areas
(4) European linear perspective
= 1
~ Beetle-wing casings, used to suggest emeralds, are the hallmark of Basohli painting.

Q 6 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The most popular theme of the Basohli painters was:
(1) The Bihari Satsai
(2) The Shahnama
(3) The Rasamanjari of Bhanu Datta
(4) The Hamzanama
= 3
~ The Rasamanjari was the favourite Basohli theme; the Bhagavata Purana, Ragamala and portraits were also popular.

Q 7 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The artist Devidasa, who painted a magnificent Rasamanjari series in 1694–95 for Kirpal Pal of Basohli, was a:
(1) Brahmin priest
(2) Tarkhan (carpenter-painter)
(3) Goldsmith
(4) Court poet
= 2
~ The chapter describes him as a tarkhan, a carpenter-painter.

Q 8 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The Shangri Ramayana, a notable set of the last quarter of the seventeenth century, comes from:
(1) Kangra
(2) Guler
(3) Basohli
(4) The Kullu valley
= 4
~ The Basohli style spread to Chamba and Kullu; the Shangri Ramayana from the Kullu valley varies in style, possibly because several artists worked on it.

Q 9 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Manaku's Gita Govinda set, painted at Guler in 1730, retains which element of the Basohli style?
(1) Photograph-like landscapes
(2) The straight-nosed Kangra face
(3) Lavish use of beetle-wing casings
(4) European perspective
= 3
~ The set keeps Basohli elements, most strikingly the beetle-wing casings, while pointing towards a softer style.

Q 10 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Nainsukh became the court painter of, and found his greatest patron in:
(1) Raja Balwant Singh of Jasrota
(2) Raja Sansar Chand of Kangra
(3) Raja Kirpal Pal of Basohli
(4) Raja Ram Singh of Kota
= 1
~ Nainsukh worked for Mian Zoravar Singh and then for Raja Balwant Singh of Jasrota, painting him at prayer, surveying a site and sitting in a camp in winter.

Q 11 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Pandit Seu's sons, credited with the shift to the new Guler style around 1730–40, were:
(1) Fattu and Purkhu
(2) Khushala and Gaudhu
(3) Devidasa and Molaram
(4) Manaku and Nainsukh
= 4
~ Pandit Seu and his sons Manak (Manaku) and Nainsukh shaped the Guler or pre-Kangra phase. Khushala, Fattu and Gaudhu were the next generation.

Q 12 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Nainsukh's palette is described as:
(1) Strong primary colours with yellow backgrounds
(2) Delicate pastels with broad areas of white or grey
(3) Dark browns and blacks
(4) Gold and lapis lazuli
= 2
~ Nainsukh used delicate pastels with broad areas of white or grey; the new style was more refined and subdued than Basohli's.

Q 13 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The Guler phase of Pahari painting appeared under which ruler?
(1) Raja Govardhan Chand (1744–1773)
(2) Raja Kirpal Pal
(3) Raja Sansar Chand
(4) Raja Ghamand Chand
= 1
~ The Guler phase came under Govardhan Chand, whose family Manaku painted; his successor Prakash Chand employed the sons of Manaku and Nainsukh.

Q 14 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Painting in the Kangra region blossomed under the patronage of:
(1) Raja Govardhan Chand
(2) Raja Balwant Singh
(3) Raja Kirpal Pal
(4) Raja Sansar Chand (1775–1823)
= 4
~ Sansar Chand of the Katoch dynasty, who came to power at the age of ten, presided over the blossoming of the Kangra style.

Q 15 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Under Sansar Chand, which place was the most prolific centre of Kangra painting?
(1) Alampur
(2) Tira Sujanpur
(3) Nadaun
(4) Haripur
= 2
~ Tira Sujanpur on the Beas, founded by his grandfather Ghamand Chand, was the most prolific centre; earlier work was done at Alampur and the most mature at Nadaun.

Q 16 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The distinctive female face of the Kangra style, which emerged around the 1790s, has:
(1) A receding chin and large round eyes
(2) Pink-tinged lotus eyes and arched brows
(3) A straight nose in line with the forehead
(4) A beaked nose and pellet eyes
= 3
~ The Kangra face, with the nose in line with the forehead, appeared around the 1790s. Kangra painting is known for delicate line, brilliant colour and lyrical serenity.

Q 17 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Around 1805, who besieged the Kangra fort, forcing Sansar Chand to flee to Tira Sujanpur?
(1) The Mughals
(2) The Sikhs
(3) The British
(4) The Gurkhas
= 4
~ The Gurkhas besieged the fort around 1805; in 1809 Ranjit Singh helped drive them out.

Q 18 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Which artist is associated with the Garhwal school, influenced by Sansar Chand's Kangra style, and signed several of his works?
(1) Molaram
(2) Purkhu
(3) Fattu
(4) Devidasa
= 1
~ Molaram is associated with Garhwal. Fattu, Purkhu and Khushala were important Kangra painters.

Q 19 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
A Baramasa series of Pahari painting consists of:
(1) 8 folios, one for each heroine
(2) 10 folios, one for each avatar
(3) 12 folios, one for each month
(4) 36 folios, one for each raga and ragini
= 3
~ A Baramasa series has 12 folios, one for each month; the theme is described in the tenth chapter of Keshav Das's Kavipriya.

Q 20 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The 'Ashta Nayikas', a popular Pahari theme, are:
(1) The eight incarnations of Devi
(2) Eight heroines shown in different emotional states, such as Utka, Vasakasajja and Abhisarika
(3) Eight queens of Krishna
(4) Eight seasons of the year
= 2
~ The Ashta Nayikas are eight heroines in various states of love. Abhisarika paintings show dark nights, lightning, clouds and snakes.
