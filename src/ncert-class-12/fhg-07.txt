Q 1 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
The first public railway line, mentioned in the chapter, was opened in 1825 between:
(1) London and Paris
(2) Stockton and Darlington in northern England
(3) Bombay and Thane
(4) New York and Chicago
= 2
~ Railways became the most popular and fastest form of transport in the nineteenth century, after the invention of the steam engine in the eighteenth century.

Q 2 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Which of the following pack animals is correctly matched with the region where it is used, according to the chapter?
(1) Mules — mountainous regions
(2) Camels — Siberia
(3) Reindeer — deserts
(4) Bullocks — North Europe
= 1
~ Dogs and reindeer draw sledges in North America, North Europe and Siberia, camels are used in deserts, and bullocks pull carts in India.

Q 5 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Roads laid out along international boundaries, which integrate remote people with major cities and help defence, are called:
(1) Highways
(2) Rural roads
(3) Expressways
(4) Border roads
= 4
~ Almost all countries build such roads to carry goods to border villages and military camps.

Q 6 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
According to the chapter, the standard gauge railway track used in the UK has a width of:
(1) More than 1.5 m
(2) 1.44 m
(3) 1 m
(4) 0.76 m
= 2
~ Railway gauges are broadly classed as broad (more than 1.5 m), standard (1.44 m), metre (1 m) and smaller gauges.

Q 7 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography

(1) Germany
(2) France
(3) Belgium
(4) United Kingdom
= 3
~ Europe has one of the densest rail networks in the world, about 4,40,000 km, mostly double or multiple tracked.

Q 12 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
The Orient Express ran from Paris to:
(1) Moscow
(2) Rome
(3) Berlin
(4) Istanbul
= 4
~ It passed through Strasbourg, Munich, Vienna, Budapest and Belgrade, cutting the London–Istanbul journey to 96 hours against 10 days by sea.

Q 15 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Consider the following statements about the Suez Canal:
1. It was constructed in 1869 between Port Said and Port Suez, linking the Mediterranean Sea and the Red Sea.
2. It is a sea-level canal without locks, about 160 km long.
3. It has a six-lock system.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1
~ The six-lock system belongs to the Panama Canal. About 100 ships use the Suez Canal daily, each taking 10–12 hours to cross.

Q 16 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
The Panama Canal shortens the sea distance between New York and San Francisco by about:
(1) 1,300 km
(2) 13,000 km
(3) 72 km
(4) 160 km
= 2
~ The canal is about 72 km long, runs between Panama City and Colon, and has six locks that lift ships about 26 m.

Q 18 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography

Q 19 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography

Q 20 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
India's first satellite, mentioned in the chapter's section on satellite communication, was:
(1) Aryabhatt
(2) Rohini
(3) Bhaskar-I
(4) APPLE
= 1
~ Aryabhatt was launched on 19 April 1975, followed by Bhaskar-I (1979), Rohini (1980) and APPLE (1981). Satellites make the cost of communication the same over 500 km as over 5,000 km.
