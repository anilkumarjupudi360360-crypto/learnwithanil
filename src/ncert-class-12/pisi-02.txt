Q 1 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Who was the first Chief Election Commissioner of India, who conducted the first general election?
(1) T.N. Seshan
(2) Sukumar Sen
(3) V.P. Menon
(4) K.M. Panikkar
= 2
~ Sukumar Sen headed the Election Commission set up in January 1950. He had to organise elections for an electorate of about 17 crore voters, most of whom could not read.

Q 2 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The first general election in India was held between:
(1) August 1947 and January 1948
(2) January 1950 and June 1950
(3) March 1955 and May 1955
(4) October 1951 and February 1952
= 4
~ The election had to be postponed twice and was held over several months from October 1951 to February 1952. It is often called the 1952 election, since most voting took place then.

Q 3 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
How did voters in the first general election, many of whom could not read, mark their choice?
(1) Each candidate had a separate ballot box with his or her election symbol, and voters put their ballot paper in the box of their choice
(2) Voters wrote the candidate's name
(3) Voters used electronic voting machines
(4) Voters raised their hands in public
= 1
~ Symbols made voting possible for people who could not read. Later, a ballot paper listing candidates with symbols came into use, and electronic voting machines were used across the country by 2004.

Q 4 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
In the first general election, the Congress won how many of the 489 Lok Sabha seats?
(1) 245
(2) 290
(3) 364
(4) 425
= 3
~ The Congress won 364 seats. The Communist Party of India came a distant second with 16 seats, so there was no strong opposition in the House.

Q 5 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
In 1957, a non-Congress party formed the government in a state for the first time in India, through elections. Which party and state was it?
(1) Jana Sangh in Rajasthan
(2) Swatantra Party in Gujarat
(3) Communist Party of India in Kerala
(4) Socialist Party in Bihar
= 3
~ The CPI formed the government in Kerala with E.M.S. Namboodiripad as Chief Minister. It was dismissed in 1959 under Article 356, a decision that remains controversial.

Q 6 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which country did the chapter mention as another example of one-party dominance, where one party ruled for about sixty years?
(1) Mexico, under the Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI)
(2) The United States
(3) The United Kingdom
(4) Switzerland
= 1
~ The PRI ruled Mexico for decades, but its dominance was achieved partly through undemocratic means. The chapter contrasts this with India, where Congress dominance came through free and fair elections.

Q 7 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
According to the chapter, which of the following explains the Congress's dominance in the early years?
(1) It banned other parties
(2) Elections were not free
(3) It was supported only by the princely rulers
(4) It inherited the legacy of the national movement, had an organisation across the country and was a broad social and ideological coalition
= 4
~ The Congress had led the freedom struggle, had the best-known leaders and the widest network, and brought together groups with different views and interests.

Q 8 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The chapter describes the Congress of this period as "a social and ideological coalition". What does this mean?
(1) It represented only the rich
(2) It accommodated people of different classes, castes, religions and languages, and of different ideologies, from left to right
(3) It had only one fixed ideology
(4) It was a coalition of foreign parties
= 2
~ Revolutionary and pacifist, conservative and radical, socialist and capitalist groups all had space in the Congress. This made it a broad platform rather than a narrow party.

Q 9 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The existence of organised groups with different views inside the Congress, which the party tolerated, is called:
(1) Factions
(2) Coalitions
(3) Fronts
(4) Alliances
= 1
~ Factions within the Congress acted as a check on its leadership. In a sense, the opposition of other parties was partly performed within the Congress itself.

Q 10 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which party was founded in 1951 with Syama Prasad Mookerjee as its founder-president?
(1) Swatantra Party
(2) Socialist Party
(3) Communist Party of India
(4) Bharatiya Jana Sangh
= 4
~ The Bharatiya Jana Sangh emphasised the idea of one country, one culture and one nation. It had limited electoral success in the early years.

Q 11 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The Swatantra Party, formed in 1959, was led by:
(1) Jayaprakash Narayan
(2) C. Rajagopalachari
(3) A.K. Gopalan
(4) Ram Manohar Lohia
= 2
~ Founded after the Congress's Nagpur resolution on cooperative farming, the Swatantra Party favoured a free market and opposed state intervention in the economy. K.M. Munshi, N.G. Ranga and Minoo Masani were also leaders.

Q 12 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The Socialist Party had its roots in a group formed within the Congress in 1934. What was it called?
(1) Forward Bloc
(2) Swaraj Party
(3) Congress Socialist Party
(4) Hindustan Republican Association
= 3
~ The Congress Socialist Party worked within the Congress. After independence, when dual membership was no longer allowed, the socialists formed a separate Socialist Party in 1948.

Q 13 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which leader is associated with the Socialist Party and later with the idea of "non-Congressism"?
(1) Syama Prasad Mookerjee
(2) C. Rajagopalachari
(3) Ram Manohar Lohia
(4) E.M.S. Namboodiripad
= 3
~ Lohia was a leading socialist thinker who argued that all opposition parties should come together to defeat the Congress, which shaped the politics of the 1960s.

Q 14 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which of the following parties favoured an economy with less government control and opposed land ceilings and cooperative farming?
(1) Communist Party of India
(2) Swatantra Party
(3) Socialist Party
(4) Bharatiya Jana Sangh
= 2
~ The Swatantra Party stood for a free economy and criticised the policies of central planning, nationalisation and the public sector.

Q 15 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Why does the chapter say the Congress's dominance in India was different from one-party rule in countries like the Soviet Union?
(1) Because India had no elections
(2) Because other parties were banned in India
(3) Because the Congress controlled the army
(4) Because it was achieved through free and fair elections in a multi-party system, while opposition parties existed and could criticise the government
= 4
~ In India the Congress kept winning elections that it could have lost. Opposition parties existed, criticised the government and influenced policy, which kept democracy alive.

Q 16 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
What role did opposition parties play during the era of Congress dominance, according to the chapter?
(1) They criticised the policies of the Congress, kept democratic politics alive and prepared leaders who would later matter
(2) They had no role at all
(3) They ran the government in most states
(4) They supported every policy of the Congress
= 1
~ Though small in numbers, the opposition offered sustained criticism and principled debate. Leaders like A.K. Gopalan, Lohia and Rajagopalachari were respected in Parliament.

Q 17 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
In the first elections, the Communist Party of India emerged as:
(1) The largest party in the Lok Sabha
(2) The second-largest party in the Lok Sabha, with 16 seats
(3) A party that did not contest
(4) The ruling party in every southern state
= 2
~ The CPI won 16 seats in 1952, making it the largest opposition party. Its support was concentrated in areas like Andhra Pradesh, West Bengal, Bihar and Kerala.

Q 18 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The CPI government in Kerala, formed in 1957, was dismissed in 1959 by the Centre under:
(1) Article 32
(2) Article 352
(3) Article 368
(4) Article 356
= 4
~ The Centre dismissed it after a "liberation struggle" against the government. Critics saw this as a misuse of Article 356, the provision for President's Rule.

Q 19 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Why was holding the first general election such a big challenge?
(1) Because only a few thousand people could vote
(2) Because the Constitution banned elections
(3) Because it involved drawing constituencies, preparing voters' lists for about 17 crore voters, training staff and enabling largely unlettered voters to vote
(4) Because no political parties existed
= 3
~ Many observers doubted that democratic elections could work in a poor, largely illiterate country. Their successful conduct proved the critics wrong.

Q 20 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
According to the chapter, how did the early Congress system serve Indian democracy?
(1) It accommodated different groups and allowed internal debate, so that conflicts were managed within one broad party while democratic norms were maintained
(2) It ended all opposition
(3) It abolished elections in the states
(4) It allowed only one leader to decide everything
= 1
~ The Congress system acted as a forum for negotiation among many groups. This helped Indian democracy settle down in its early years.
