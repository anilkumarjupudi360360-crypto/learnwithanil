Q 1 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Consider the following statements about growth and development:
1. Growth is quantitative and value neutral; it may have a positive or negative sign.
2. Development means a qualitative change that is always value positive.
3. Positive growth always leads to development.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1
~ Development cannot happen without some positive growth, but positive growth does not always lead to development.

Q 2 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
The concept of human development was introduced by:
(1) Amartya Sen
(2) Dr Mahbub-ul-Haq
(3) Griffith Taylor
(4) Thomas Malthus
= 2
~ Mahbub-ul-Haq described human development as development that enlarges people's choices and improves their lives, with people at the centre.

Q 3 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Which thinker saw an increase in freedom (or a decrease in unfreedom) as the main objective of development?
(1) Mahbub-ul-Haq
(2) Prof. Amartya Sen
(3) Ratzel
(4) Ellen Semple
= 2
~ Amartya Sen, a Nobel Laureate, linked development with expanding freedom; his capability approach is also discussed in the chapter.

Q 4 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Since which year has the UNDP been publishing the Human Development Report annually?
(1) 1975
(2) 1990
(3) 2000
(4) 2015
= 2
~ Mahbub-ul-Haq created the Human Development Index in 1990, and the UNDP has used his concept for its annual Human Development Report ever since.

Q 5 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Which three key areas of human development are identified in the chapter?
(1) Leading a long and healthy life, gaining knowledge, and having enough means to live a decent life
(2) Industrial output, exports and defence
(3) Population size, area and climate
(4) Urbanisation, migration and trade
= 1
~ Access to resources, health and education are the key areas of human development.

Q 6 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Match the pillars of human development with their meanings:
A. Equity — 1. Continuity in the availability of opportunities for every generation
B. Sustainability — 2. Constantly enriching human labour by building people's capabilities
C. Productivity — 3. Power to make choices, through increasing freedom and capability
D. Empowerment — 4. Equal access to opportunities for everybody
Select the correct answer:
(1) A-4, B-1, C-2, D-3
(2) A-1, B-4, C-3, D-2
(3) A-4, B-2, C-1, D-3
(4) A-3, B-1, C-2, D-4
= 1
~ Equity, sustainability, productivity and empowerment are the four pillars of human development.

Q 7 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Which approach to human development looks at human beings as beneficiaries of development and argues for higher government expenditure on education, health and amenities?
(1) Income approach
(2) Basic needs approach
(3) Welfare approach
(4) Capability approach
= 3
~ In the welfare approach people are passive recipients rather than participants, and the government maximises welfare spending.

Q 8 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
The basic needs approach, which identified six basic needs, was initially proposed by:
(1) The World Bank
(2) UNESCO
(3) The International Labour Organisation (ILO)
(4) The World Trade Organisation
= 3
~ The six needs were health, education, food, water supply, sanitation and housing; the question of human choices was ignored in this approach.

Q 9 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
The capability approach to human development is associated with:
(1) Mahbub-ul-Haq
(2) Ellen Semple
(3) Prof. Amartya Sen
(4) Griffith Taylor
= 3
~ The capability approach holds that building human capabilities in health, education and access to resources is the key to increasing human development.

Q 10 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Which approach is described in the chapter as the oldest, linking the level of income with the level of freedom a person enjoys?
(1) Income approach
(2) Welfare approach
(3) Basic needs approach
(4) Capability approach
= 1
~ The idea is that the higher the income, the higher the level of human development.

Q 11 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
In the Human Development Index, how much weight is given to each of the three dimensions — health, education and access to resources?
(1) Health 1/2, education 1/4, resources 1/4
(2) Each is given a weightage of 1/3
(3) Education 1/2, the others 1/4 each
(4) Only income is counted
= 2

Q 12 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography

Q 13 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography

(1) Purchasing power in US dollars

Q 14 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Which of the following is NOT one of the measures used in the Human Poverty Index?
(1) Probability of not surviving till the age of 40
(2) Adult illiteracy rate
(3) Number of people without access to clean water
(4) Number of mobile phones per family
= 4
~ The Human Poverty Index also counts the number of small children who are underweight. It measures the shortfall in human development and is a non-income measure.

Q 15 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography

(1) Nepal
(2) Sri Lanka
(3) Norway
(4) Bhutan

Q 16 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography

Q 18 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Consider the following statements based on the chapter's international comparisons:
1. Size of territory and per capita income are not directly related to human development.
2. Sri Lanka and Trinidad and Tobago rank higher than India in human development.
3. Within India, Kerala performs better than Punjab and Gujarat despite a lower per capita income.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
