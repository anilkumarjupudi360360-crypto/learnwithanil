# NCERT Class 12 Political Science, Contemporary World Politics, Chapter 3: Contemporary South Asia
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following countries is usually included in "South Asia", as described in the chapter?
(1) Myanmar
(2) Thailand
(3) Iran
(4) Bhutan
= 4
~ South Asia usually means Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, the Maldives, Nepal, Pakistan and Sri Lanka. Afghanistan is sometimes included and is a member of SAARC.

Q 2 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following military rulers took power in Pakistan through a coup in 1999?
(1) Ayub Khan
(2) Pervez Musharraf
(3) Yahya Khan
(4) Zia-ul-Haq
= 2
~ Pakistan has seen several military rulers: Ayub Khan, Yahya Khan, Zia-ul-Haq and Pervez Musharraf, who overthrew Nawaz Sharif's elected government in 1999.

Q 3 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following is mentioned in the chapter as a reason why democracy has not been stable in Pakistan?
(1) The dominance of the army, religious leaders and landowning aristocracy, and frequent conflict with India
(2) The absence of any political parties
(3) The country's small size
(4) A ban on elections in the constitution
= 1
~ The social dominance of the military, clergy and landlords, along with tensions with India and foreign support for military regimes, weakened democratic institutions.

Q 4 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Who led the struggle for the independence of Bangladesh and became its first leader?
(1) Ziaur Rahman
(2) Khaleda Zia
(3) Sheikh Mujib-ur Rahman
(4) H.M. Ershad
= 3
~ Sheikh Mujib-ur Rahman led the Awami League. After independence in 1971, he became Prime Minister and later President; he was assassinated in a military coup in 1975.

Q 5 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Nepal became a democratic republic, ending its monarchy, in:
(1) 2008
(2) 1990
(3) 1999
(4) 2015
= 1
~ After a people's movement in 2006, the monarchy was abolished in 2008. Nepal adopted a new constitution in 2015.

Q 6 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The ethnic conflict in Sri Lanka involved a militant organisation fighting for a separate state for the Tamils. It was called:
(1) Awami League
(2) Mukti Bahini
(3) Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam (LTTE)
(4) Maoists
= 3
~ The LTTE fought for "Tamil Eelam" in the north and east of Sri Lanka. The Sri Lankan army defeated it in 2009.

Q 7 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The Indian Peace Keeping Force (IPKF) was sent to Sri Lanka in 1987 under an agreement between India and Sri Lanka. What happened to it?
(1) It stayed permanently
(2) It got involved in fighting with the LTTE, became unpopular, and was withdrawn in 1989–90 without achieving its aim
(3) It took control of Sri Lanka
(4) It was never deployed
= 2
~ The IPKF was meant to help implement the accord, but it ended up fighting the LTTE. Many Sri Lankans saw it as interference, and it was withdrawn.

Q 8 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The Maldives became a republic in:
(1) 1947
(2) 1965
(3) 2005
(4) 1968
= 4
~ The Maldives was a sultanate until 1968, when it became a republic with a presidential system. Multi-party democracy was introduced in 2005.

Q 9 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation (SAARC) was set up in:
(1) 1975
(2) 1985
(3) 1995
(4) 2005
= 2
~ SAARC was founded in Dhaka in 1985 to promote cooperation among South Asian countries. Its secretariat is in Kathmandu.

Q 10 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The South Asian Free Trade Area (SAFTA) agreement was signed in 2004. Its aim is to:
(1) Create a common army
(2) Abolish national currencies
(3) Create a single parliament
(4) Lower trade tariffs among the SAARC countries
= 4
~ SAFTA came into effect in 2006. Some smaller neighbours have feared that it would let Indian businesses dominate their markets.

Q 11 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The Indus Waters Treaty between India and Pakistan was signed in 1960 with the help of:
(1) The United Nations
(2) The Soviet Union
(3) The World Bank
(4) The Commonwealth
= 3
~ The treaty, brokered by the World Bank, divided the use of the Indus river system's waters. It has survived several wars between the two countries.

Q 12 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following is an issue of disagreement between India and Bangladesh mentioned in the chapter?
(1) The sharing of the waters of the Ganga and Brahmaputra rivers
(2) The status of Kashmir
(3) The Siachen glacier
(4) Tamil Eelam
= 1
~ Water sharing, illegal migration, the border and transit have been issues. The two countries have also cooperated, for example on a water-sharing treaty and on economic links.

Q 13 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following is a source of conflict between India and Pakistan discussed in the chapter?
(1) The Teesta river
(2) The Farakka barrage
(3) The status of Bhutan
(4) The Kashmir issue and the control of the Siachen glacier
= 4
~ Kashmir has been at the centre of India–Pakistan conflict since 1947. The two countries have also disputed Siachen, Sir Creek and the sharing of river waters.

Q 14 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
In 2008 Bhutan moved from absolute monarchy to:
(1) A constitutional monarchy with multi-party democracy
(2) A military dictatorship
(3) A communist state
(4) A union with India
= 1
~ The King of Bhutan led the transition, and the country held its first parliamentary elections in 2008.

Q 15 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Why do smaller neighbours sometimes resent India, according to the chapter?
(1) Because India refuses to trade with them
(2) Because India has no army
(3) Because of India's size and power, they fear that India wants to dominate the region
(4) Because India is not a democracy
= 3
~ India is by far the largest country in the region. Its neighbours sometimes see its actions as interference, while India in turn worries about instability in neighbouring countries.

Q 16 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which outside power, according to the chapter, has played an important role in South Asia, especially in relation to India and Pakistan?
(1) Brazil
(2) The United States and China
(3) Australia
(4) South Africa
= 2
~ The USA has mediated between India and Pakistan and has close ties with both. China has close relations with Pakistan and growing trade with the region.

Q 17 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which agreement between India and Pakistan, signed after the 1971 war, the chapter mentions as a step towards settling issues bilaterally?
(1) The Shimla Agreement
(2) The Panchsheel Agreement
(3) The Lahore Bus Agreement
(4) The Tashkent Agreement
= 1
~ The Shimla Agreement of 1972 committed both countries to settle disputes bilaterally and peacefully. Tashkent followed the 1965 war.

Q 18 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Democracy in Bangladesh, after periods of military rule, was restored with a parliamentary system in:
(1) 1971
(2) 1975
(3) 1991
(4) 2008
= 3
~ After popular protests against the military ruler H.M. Ershad, elections were held and parliamentary democracy was restored in 1991.

Q 19 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The Maoist insurgency in Nepal, which ended in 2006, aimed at:
(1) Merging Nepal with China
(2) Restoring absolute monarchy
(3) Joining SAARC
(4) Overthrowing the monarchy and establishing a people's republic
= 4
~ The Maoists fought the monarchy for about ten years. They joined a people's movement with political parties in 2006, which led to the end of the monarchy.

Q 20 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
According to the chapter, why has cooperation through SAARC been limited?
(1) Because South Asia has only two countries
(2) Because of political differences, especially between India and Pakistan, which slow regional cooperation
(3) Because SAARC has no secretariat
(4) Because SAARC was dissolved in 1990
= 2
~ Persistent tensions, particularly between India and Pakistan, have prevented SAARC from becoming as effective as regional groupings like the EU or ASEAN.
