# NCERT Class 12 Fine Arts (Art & Culture), An Introduction to Indian Art Part II, Chapter 7: The Modern Indian Art
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Rabindranath Tagore, who turned to visual art late in life, developed his style from:
(1) Training in Paris
(2) The Japanese wash technique
(3) Kalighat pats
(4) Doodles and crossed-out words in his poetry manuscripts
= 4
~ While writing poems, Tagore turned doodles and crossed-out words into a calligraphic style of faces and landscapes, using a limited palette of black, yellow ochre, reds and browns.

Q 2 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Kala Bhavana, conceptualised by Rabindranath Tagore as part of Visva-Bharati University, was set up in:
(1) 1901
(2) 1919
(3) 1937
(4) 1947
= 2
~ Kala Bhavana was set up in 1919; Nandalal Bose joined it in 1921–22.

Q 3 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Consider the following statements about Amrita Sher-Gil:
1. She was half Hungarian and half Indian.
2. She trained in Paris and experienced Impressionism and Post-Impressionism at first hand.
3. She lived from 1913 to 1941.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 1, 2 and 3
(4) 2 and 3 only
= 3
~ All three are given in the chapter. She chose India as her base and died young; 'Haldi Grinder' (1940) is one of her works.

Q 4 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
'The Lives of Medieval Saints', a mural in Hindi Bhavana, Shantiniketan (1946–47), was painted by:
(1) Benode Behari Mukherjee
(2) Nandalal Bose
(3) Ramkinkar Baij
(4) K.G. Subramanyan
= 1
~ Benode Behari Mukherjee painted it despite poor eyesight, depicting saints such as Ramanuja, Kabir, Tulsidas and Surdas.

Q 5 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
In which technique was 'The Lives of Medieval Saints' painted?
(1) Tempera on cloth
(2) Fresco buono
(3) Oil on canvas
(4) Wash
= 2
~ It was done in fresco buono, covering about 23 metres of the upper half of three walls.

Q 6 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Ramkinkar Baij's 'Santhal Family' (1937), regarded as the first public modernist sculpture in India, is made of:
(1) Bronze
(2) Marble
(3) Wood
(4) Cement mixed with pebbles over a metal armature
= 4
~ Ramkinkar avoided marble, wood and stone and used cement with pebbles for this outdoor sculpture in the Kala Bhavana compound.

Q 7 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The Progressive Artists' Group of Bombay was led by:
(1) F.N. Souza
(2) Jamini Roy
(3) Prodosh Das Gupta
(4) K.G. Subramanyan
= 1
~ F.N. Souza led the Progressives; the other founders named are M.F. Husain, K.H. Ara, S.A. Bakre, H.A. Gade and S.H. Raza.

Q 8 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Which of the following was NOT a founder member of the Progressive Artists' Group?
(1) M.F. Husain
(2) S.H. Raza
(3) Tyeb Mehta
(4) K.H. Ara
= 3
~ The founders were F.N. Souza, M.F. Husain, K.H. Ara, S.A. Bakre, H.A. Gade and S.H. Raza. Tyeb Mehta is mentioned among artists who moved between abstraction and figuration.

Q 9 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The Calcutta Group, formed in 1943, was formed under:
(1) Nandalal Bose
(2) Chittoprasad
(3) Prodosh Das Gupta
(4) Abanindranath Tagore
= 3
~ Prodosh Das Gupta formed the Calcutta Group; its members included Nirode Mazumdar, Paritosh Sen, Gopal Ghose and Rathin Moitra.

Q 10 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
'Hungry Bengal' (1943), a series of sketches made during the Bengal famine and published as pamphlets, is by:
(1) Chittoprasad
(2) Somnath Hore
(3) Jamini Roy
(4) Ganesh Pyne
= 1
~ Chittoprasad made the Hungry Bengal sketches; he also worked in etchings, linocuts and lithographs.

Q 11 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
S.H. Raza, who moved towards abstraction, used which symbol from Indian philosophy in his paintings?
(1) The swastika
(2) The lotus
(3) The trident
(4) The bindu, as a symbol of oneness
= 4
~ Raza drew on mandala and yantra designs and used the bindu as a symbol of oneness; his painting 'Ma' dates from 1972.

Q 12 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Which of the following artists is associated with the neo-Tantric art of the 1960s?
(1) Amrita Sher-Gil
(2) G.R. Santosh
(3) Bhupen Khakhar
(4) Jamini Roy
= 2
~ Biren De and G.R. Santosh in Delhi and K.C.S. Paniker in Madras are named as neo-Tantric artists, part of a search for an Indian identity in modern art.

Q 13 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
K.C.S. Paniker later founded:
(1) Kala Bhavana, Shantiniketan
(2) The Baroda Art School
(3) The Progressive Artists' Group
(4) Cholamandalam, an artists' village near Madras
= 4
~ Paniker founded the Cholamandalam artists' village near Madras; his work 'The Dog' (1973) is in the NGMA.

Q 14 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
'Vanshri' (1994), made of hemp or jute fibre, is a work by:
(1) Meera Mukherjee
(2) Nalini Malani
(3) Mrinalini Mukherjee
(4) Arpita Singh
= 3
~ Mrinalini Mukherjee began experimenting with fibre in the early 1970s; Vanshri is one of her well-known works.

Q 15 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
'City for Sale' (1984), now in the Victoria and Albert Museum, London, is by:
(1) Bhupen Khakhar
(2) Gulam Mohammed Sheikh
(3) K.G. Subramanyan
(4) Arpita Singh
= 2
~ Gulam Mohammed Sheikh painted City for Sale; he is among the Baroda narrative painters.

Q 16 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
'Janata Watch Repairing' (1972) is a painting by:
(1) Bhupen Khakhar
(2) M.F. Husain
(3) Jogen Chowdhury
(4) Sudhir Patwardhan
= 1
~ Bhupen Khakhar painted Janata Watch Repairing, a work of the Baroda narrative trend that drew on everyday life.

Q 17 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Krishna Reddy's 'Whirlpool' (1963) was made using which technique, developed with Stanley William Hayter at Atelier 17?
(1) Viscosity printing
(2) Woodcut
(3) Lithography
(4) Screen printing
= 1
~ Whirlpool is a viscosity print, now in the Metropolitan Museum of Art. Somnath Hore, Jyoti Bhatt, Anupam Sud and Laxma Goud are other printmakers named in the chapter.

Q 18 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The bronze sculpture 'Triumph of Labour' (1959) on the Marina Beach in Chennai is by:
(1) Ramkinkar Baij
(2) P.V. Janakiram
(3) Amarnath Sahgal
(4) Debi Prasad Roy Chowdhury
= 4
~ Debi Prasad Roy Chowdhury made Triumph of Labour. P.V. Janakiram made a Ganesha in oxidised copper (1970).

Q 19 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
'Cries Unheard' (1958), a bronze about which Mulk Raj Anand wrote, is by:
(1) Prodosh Das Gupta

(3) Amarnath Sahgal

Q 20 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Which two artists are named in the chapter for installation art?
(1) Jamini Roy and Raja Ravi Varma
