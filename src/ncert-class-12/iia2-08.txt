# NCERT Class 12 Fine Arts (Art & Culture), An Introduction to Indian Art Part II, Chapter 8: The Living Art Traditions of India
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Mithila painting is named after Mithila, the ancient Videha and birthplace of Sita. It is also called Madhubani painting after the nearest district capital, in which state?
(1) Bihar
(2) Uttar Pradesh
(3) West Bengal
(4) Jharkhand
= 1
~ For centuries women of the Mithila region have painted the walls of their mud houses for ceremonies, especially weddings; local belief links its origin to the marriage of Sita and Rama.

Q 2 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
In Mithila painting, the 'kohbar' painted in the kohbar ghar is:
(1) A portrait of the bride's mother
(2) A map of the village
(3) A lotus with a stalk in full bloom, with metaphoric and tantric meaning
(4) A procession of elephants
= 3
~ The kohbar is a lotus in full bloom with its stalk, painted along with gods and goddesses in the inner room called the kohbar ghar.

Q 3 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The traditional colours of Mithila painting were obtained from:
(1) Imported chemical dyes
(2) Mineral stones and plants, such as phalsa and kusum flowers, bilwa leaves, kajal and turmeric
(3) Gold and silver powder
(4) Lapis lazuli and cinnabar only
= 2
~ The colours came from minerals and plants; brushes were bamboo twigs with cotton swabs, rice straw or fibre. Recent work is painted on fabric, paper and pots for sale.

Q 4 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The Warli community, known for Warli painting, has a large concentration in which district?
(1) Pune
(2) Nashik
(3) Raigad
(4) Thane
= 4
~ The Warlis live on the west coast of northern Maharashtra near the north Sahyadri range, mostly in Thane district.

Q 5 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The central figure of the Warli chowk painted by married women is:
(1) Ganesha
(2) Shiva
(3) Palaghat, the mother goddess
(4) Krishna
= 3
~ Palaghat, a fertility deity and corn goddess also called Kansari, is enclosed in a square frame with pointed chevrons representing Hariyali Deva, the god of plants.

Q 6 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Warli paintings are made with:
(1) Rice flour on earth-coloured walls, using a chewed bamboo stick as the brush
(2) Mineral colours on cloth with squirrel-hair brushes
(3) Oil paint on canvas
(4) Lacquer on wood
= 1
~ Warli painters use rice flour on earth-coloured walls; the paintings are meant to promote fertility, avert disease, appease the dead and satisfy spirits.

Q 7 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
In Warli painting, the 'Panch Sirya Devata' is:
(1) A five-armed goddess of rain
(2) The god of plants shown as chevrons
(3) A tiger deity
(4) A headless warrior with five corn shoots springing from his neck, who guards the fields as Khetrapal
= 4
~ The headless warrior escorting Palaghat, with five corn shoots from his neck, is the five-headed god who guards the fields.

Q 8 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Pithoro paintings are made by:
(1) The Gonds of Mandla
(2) The Rathva Bhils of Panchmahal in Gujarat and Jhabua in Madhya Pradesh
(3) The Warlis of Thane
(4) The Santhals of Jharkhand
= 2
~ The Rathva Bhils paint large Pithoro wall paintings on special or thanksgiving occasions, with rows of horse-riding deities.

Q 9 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
In a Pithoro painting, the lower section below the wavy line shows:
(1) Gods, heavenly bodies and mythical creatures
(2) The wedding procession of Pithoro
(3) Scenes from the Ramayana
(4) The village market
= 2
~ The upper section shows gods, heavenly bodies and mythical creatures; the lower one shows Pithoro's wedding procession with minor deities, kings, a farmer and domestic animals.

Q 10 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
In West Bengal, the performer who displays painted scrolls (pata) and sings their stories, as a hereditary profession, is called the patua or:
(1) Bhopa
(2) Joshi
(3) Ghadwa
(4) Chitrakar
= 4
~ The patua or chitrakar travels from village to village with his scrolls, usually narrating three or four stories in a performance, for alms in cash or kind.

Q 11 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
In the Puri patas of Odisha, the 'Ansara patti' is used:
(1) As a keepsake for pilgrims
(2) To decorate the temple chariot
(3) As a substitute for the icons in the garbhagriha while they are cleaned and repainted after Snanayatra
(4) As a calendar of festivals
= 3
~ The Ansara patti stands in for the icons of Jagannath, Balabhadra and Subhadra during their cleaning and repainting after Snanayatra.

Q 12 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
In Puri pata painting, the 'Jatri patti' is:
(1) A keepsake painting for pilgrims
(2) A palm-leaf manuscript
(3) A painted wooden mask
(4) A wall mural
= 1
~ Jatri pattis are keepsakes bought by pilgrims. Other themes include the veshas (attires) of the deities and the Kanchi Kaveri Pata.

Q 13 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
In Odisha patachitra, which natural pigment gives the white colour?
(1) Haritali
(2) Hingal stone
(3) Lamp black
(4) Powdered conch shell
= 4
~ Black comes from lamp black, yellow from haritali, red from hingal stone and white from powdered conch shell; finished works are lacquered over a charcoal fire.

Q 14 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The phads of Rajasthan are:
(1) Palm-leaf manuscripts of Jain texts
(2) Long horizontal cloth scrolls honouring folk deities of pastoral communities around Bhilwara
(3) Terracotta plaques
(4) Wall paintings in Rajput palaces
= 2
~ Phads honour the folk deities of pastoral communities around Bhilwara, for whom protecting livestock is central to myth and worship.

Q 15 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The itinerant bards who display phads at night-long performances, singing with a lamp held against the painting, are called:
(1) Bhopas
(2) Patuas
(3) Chitrakars
(4) Kathakars
= 1
~ Bhopas narrate and sing the phad stories, accompanied by the ravanahattha and veena.

Q 16 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The phads were painted by members of which caste, who were also court painters for the kings of Rajasthan?
(1) Rabari
(2) Meghwal
(3) Joshi
(4) Gujjar
= 3
~ The Joshi caste painted the phads. Rabaris, Gujjars, Meghwals and Regars are among the communities that follow the cattle heroes.

Q 17 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Cattle heroes such as Gogaji, Dev Narayan and Pabuji, who died protecting herds from robbers and are honoured in phads, are called:
(1) Jogis
(2) Bhopas
(3) Patuas
(4) Bhomias
= 4
~ These heroes are called bhomias; the chapter names Gogaji, Jejaji, Dev Narayan, Ramdevji and Pabuji.

Q 18 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The metal craftsmen of Bastar who make Dhokra castings are called:
(1) Patuas
(2) Bhopas
(3) Ghadwa
(4) Tarkhans
= 3
~ Bastar's metal craftsmen are called ghadwa, a name linked to shaping and creating. Dhokra is also made in parts of Madhya Pradesh, Odisha and Midnapore in West Bengal.

Q 19 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Dhokra casting uses which method?
(1) The lost-wax (cire perdue) method
(2) Sand casting
(3) Hammering sheets of metal
(4) Die stamping
= 1
~ Dhokra uses the lost-wax method; the mould is fired with saal wood or charcoal for two to three hours before the molten metal is poured in.

Q 20 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
In Dhokra casting, the fine threads and strips used for decorative details and eyes are made from:
(1) Beeswax mixed with clay
(2) Resin of the saal tree boiled with mustard oil
(3) Strips of bamboo
(4) Copper wire
= 2
~ Saal resin boiled with mustard oil and strained is shaped into threads and strips for decoration; the core is made of riverbank soil mixed with rice husk.
