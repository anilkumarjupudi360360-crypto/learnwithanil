Q 1 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The Permanent Settlement was introduced in Bengal in 1793 by:
(1) Warren Hastings
(2) Lord Wellesley
(3) Lord Dalhousie
(4) Lord Cornwallis
= 4
~ Under it, the revenue to be paid by each zamindar was fixed permanently. The Company hoped this would ensure a regular flow of revenue and encourage zamindars to invest in improving land.

Q 2 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Under the "sunset law" of the Permanent Settlement:
(1) Zamindars could collect revenue only in the evening
(2) Peasants could leave the village at sunset
(3) If a zamindar failed to pay the revenue by sunset of the specified date, his zamindari could be auctioned
(4) Courts closed at sunset
= 3
~ Many zamindaris were auctioned in the years after the settlement. The estates of the Raja of Burdwan were among those put up for auction in 1797.

Q 3 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
In late eighteenth-century north Bengal, the rich peasants who held large areas of land and controlled local trade and money-lending were called:
(1) Jotedars
(2) Ryots
(3) Paharias
(4) Sahukars
= 1
~ The jotedars' power was greater than that of the zamindars in the villages. Unlike zamindars, who lived in towns, jotedars lived in the villages.

Q 4 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The "Fifth Report", submitted to the British Parliament in 1813, dealt with:
(1) The revolt of 1857
(2) The Deccan riots
(3) The Partition of Bengal
(4) The administration and activities of the East India Company in India
= 4
~ It became the basis of intense debates in Britain about the Company's rule. It drew on petitions of zamindars and ryots and reports of collectors.

Q 6 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The Paharias were a group who lived around:
(1) The Nilgiri hills
(2) The Rajmahal hills
(3) The Western Ghats
(4) The Aravalli hills
= 2
~ The Paharias lived by shifting cultivation, hunting and gathering forest produce, and raided the plains. They saw the forest as their land.

Q 7 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Francis Buchanan, whose accounts are a source for this chapter, was:
(1) A missionary
(2) A governor-general
(3) A Santhal leader
(4) A physician who served the East India Company and travelled through areas such as the Rajmahal hills in about 1810, on survey
= 4
~ Buchanan's accounts reflect the Company's interest in what could be used to increase revenue, such as minerals and good land. They must be read with that perspective in mind.

Q 8 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
In 1832, a large area of land was demarcated as "Damin-i-Koh" for settlement by:
(1) The Santhals
(2) The Paharias
(3) British planters
(4) The jotedars
= 1
~ The Santhals had come to the area around the 1780s and cleared forests for settled agriculture. The Company encouraged them because they would pay revenue.

Q 9 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The Santhals rose in revolt (the "hul") in:
(1) 1855-56
(2) 1793
(3) 1832
(4) 1875
= 1
~ They revolted against zamindars, moneylenders and the colonial state because of rising revenue and debt. After the revolt, the Santhal Pargana was created with special laws.

Q 10 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The Deccan riots of 1875 began in the village of Supa, in Poona district. The ryots attacked:
(1) British soldiers
(2) The shops and houses of sahukars (moneylenders), burning their account books and debt bonds
(3) Zamindars' palaces
(4) Temples
= 2
~ The revolt spread to other villages. The government, fearing another 1857, sent police and troops.

Q 11 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The revenue system introduced in the Bombay Deccan in the 1820s, under which revenue was settled directly with the ryot, was the:
(1) Permanent Settlement
(2) Ryotwari settlement
(3) Mahalwari settlement
(4) Zabt system
= 2
~ The revenue was fixed for about 30 years, but the demand was very high. Many ryots fled, and others fell into debt.

Q 12 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The cotton boom in the Bombay Deccan in the 1860s was caused by:
(1) A new railway line
(2) A famine in Egypt
(3) The American Civil War, which cut off the supply of raw cotton from the USA to Britain
(4) The Suez Canal
= 3
~ British merchants gave advances to sahukars, who lent money to ryots to grow cotton. When the war ended in 1865, cotton exports fell and ryots found loans hard to get.

Q 13 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The Limitation Law of 1859, meant to protect ryots, stated that:
(1) Ryots could not take loans
(2) Moneylenders could charge only 5 per cent interest
(3) Loan bonds signed between moneylenders and ryots would be valid for only three years
(4) Land could not be sold
= 3
~ Moneylenders got around the law by making ryots sign a new bond every three years, adding the unpaid interest to the principal.

Q 14 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
After the Deccan riots, the government of Bombay set up a commission whose report was presented to the British Parliament in:
(1) 1878
(2) 1857
(3) 1905
(4) 1813
= 1
~ The Deccan Riots Commission enquired into the causes of the riots. Its report is an important source but reflects the government's own view of the events.

Q 15 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Which of the following is correct about the zamindars' "amlah" under the Permanent Settlement?
(1) They were peasant leaders
(2) They were British judges
(3) They were the zamindar's officers, who came to villages to collect rent
(4) They were Santhal chiefs
= 3
~ The relationship between the amlah and the ryots was often tense. Ryots sometimes delayed payments deliberately.

Q 16 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Why did the British prefer the Santhals to the Paharias as settlers in the Rajmahal area?
(1) The Santhals were hunters
(2) The Paharias were settled farmers
(3) The Santhals cleared forests and practised settled plough agriculture, which yielded revenue
(4) The Santhals refused to pay revenue
= 3
~ The Paharias were pushed deeper into the hills as the Santhals expanded. Conflicts between the two groups grew.

Q 17 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The ryots in the Deccan saw the moneylenders as:
(1) Devious and deceitful, violating the norms of the countryside, for example by refusing to give receipts and charging very high interest
(2) Generous friends
(3) Government officials
(4) Soldiers
= 1
~ The ryots' petitions to the government describe these grievances. Debt was at the heart of rural distress.

Q 19 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
What was a key aim of the Company in introducing the Permanent Settlement?
(1) To abolish zamindars
(2) To create a class of landlords who would invest in improving agriculture and pay a fixed, regular revenue
(3) To give land to the landless
(4) To stop all trade

~ The Company expected zamindars to be like English landlords. The results were very different.

Q 20 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The chapter title refers to "exploring official archives". Why is this important?
(1) Because colonial records are the only sources and are always correct
(2) Because official records, produced for the state's purposes, are rich in detail but must be read critically to understand the experiences of peasants and others
(3) Because peasants wrote all the archives
(4) Because the archives were destroyed
= 2
~ For example, the Deccan Riots Commission recorded the events from the government's point of view. Historians compare such records with other evidence.
