Q 1 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
People engaged in primary activities are called "red-collar workers" because:
(1) They wear red uniforms
(2) Their work is mostly outdoors
(3) They work in factories
(4) They are highly paid executives
= 2
~ Primary activities such as farming, fishing and mining depend directly on the environment and are done outdoors.

Q 2 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Which of the following is NOT listed among the low-latitude regions of hunting and gathering?
(1) Amazon basin
(2) Tropical Africa
(3) Northern fringe of Australia
(4) Northern Canada
= 4
~ Northern Canada, northern Eurasia and southern Chile are high-latitude gathering zones; the Amazon basin, tropical Africa, northern Australia and interior South-east Asia are low-latitude zones.

Q 3 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Chicle, a gathered product mentioned in the chapter, comes from:
(1) The milky juice of the zapota tree, used for making chewing gum
(2) The sap of the rubber tree
(3) The bark of the cork oak
(4) The cinchona tree
= 1
~ Gathered products include quinine, tannin extract, cork, chicle, rubber, balata, gums and resins.

Q 4 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Why does gathering have little chance of becoming important at the global level?
(1) Gathered products are too expensive to collect
(2) Its products cannot compete in the world market, and synthetic products have replaced many of them
(3) It is banned in all countries
(4) There are no forests left
= 2
~ Gathering has become market-oriented in some places, but many products now have synthetic substitutes of better quality and lower cost.

Q 5 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Which of the following is NOT one of the three important regions of nomadic herding mentioned in the chapter?
(1) From the Atlantic shores of North Africa eastwards to Mongolia and Central China
(2) The tundra region of Eurasia
(3) South-west Africa and the island of Madagascar
(4) The Pampas of Argentina

Q 6 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
The seasonal movement of herders from plains to mountain pastures in summer and back to the plains in winter is called:
(1) Shifting cultivation
(2) Ranching
(3) Transhumance
(4) Truck farming
= 3
~ In the Himalayas, the Gujjars, Bakarwals, Gaddis and Bhotiyas practise transhumance.

Q 7 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Consider the following statements about commercial livestock rearing:
1. It is organised and capital intensive, practised on permanent ranches divided into fenced parcels.
2. Usually many types of animals are reared together on the same ranch.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 1
~ Commercial livestock rearing is specialised: usually only one type of animal is reared. It is practised in New Zealand, Australia, Argentina, Uruguay and the USA.

Q 8 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Match the local names of shifting cultivation with their regions:
A. Jhuming — 1. Indonesia and Malaysia
B. Milpa — 2. North-eastern states of India
C. Ladang — 3. Central America and Mexico
Select the correct answer:
(1) A-2, B-3, C-1
(2) A-3, B-2, C-1
(3) A-1, B-3, C-2
(4) A-2, B-1, C-3
= 1
~ In shifting cultivation the forest is cleared by fire and the patch is cultivated for a few years before the farmer moves on.

Q 9 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
In primitive subsistence (shifting) agriculture, a patch is usually cultivated for how long before the farmer shifts to another patch?
(1) Only one season
(2) About 3 to 5 years
(3) About 20 years
(4) Permanently
= 2
~ After about 3 to 5 years the soil loses fertility, so the farmer clears another patch. A major problem today is that the jhum cycle keeps getting shorter.

Q 10 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Consider the following statements about intensive subsistence agriculture dominated by wet paddy:

3. Yield per unit area is high but productivity per labourer is low.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ This type of farming is found in densely populated monsoon Asia, with family labour and farmyard manure.

Q 11 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Which of the following correctly pairs a European power with the plantations it set up, as mentioned in the chapter?
(1) British — tea gardens in India and Sri Lanka
(2) French — rubber in Malaysia
(3) Dutch — coffee fazendas in Brazil
(4) Spanish — tea in Assam

~ The French set up cocoa and coffee in West Africa, the British tea in India and Sri Lanka and rubber in Malaysia, and the Dutch once had a monopoly over sugarcane in Indonesia.

Q 12 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Which of the following is a feature of plantation agriculture?
(1) Small farms with many crops
(2) Large estates, large capital investment, single-crop specialisation and cheap labour
(3) Cultivation by family labour only for self-consumption
(4) Shifting of fields every few years
= 2
~ Plantations also need scientific methods of cultivation and good transport links between the estates, factories and markets.

Q 13 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Extensive commercial grain cultivation is NOT practised in which of the following regions?
(1) Prairies of North America
(2) Pampas of Argentina
(3) Ganga plains of India
(4) Canterbury Plains of New Zealand
= 3
~ It is practised in the Eurasian steppes, Prairies, Pampas, Velds, Australian Downs and Canterbury Plains, where farms are very large and fully mechanised. Yield per acre is low but per person is high.

Q 14 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
In which type of farming is equal emphasis laid on crop cultivation and animal husbandry, with crop rotation and inter-cropping maintaining soil fertility?
(1) Dairy farming
(2) Plantation agriculture
(3) Mixed farming
(4) Nomadic herding
= 3
~ Mixed farming is found in highly developed parts of the world such as north-western Europe and eastern North America.

Q 15 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Which of the following is the largest region of commercial dairy farming?
(1) North-western Europe
(2) Canada
(3) South-eastern Australia
(4) Southern India
= 1
~ The three main regions are north-western Europe (the largest), Canada, and south-eastern Australia, New Zealand and Tasmania. Dairy farming is highly capital and labour intensive.

Q 16 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Viticulture, or grape cultivation, is a speciality of which type of agriculture?
(1) Plantation agriculture
(2) Mixed farming
(3) Mediterranean agriculture
(4) Truck farming
= 3
~ The Mediterranean region produces the best quality wines, and inferior grapes are dried into raisins and currants. It is also an important supplier of citrus fruits.

Q 17 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Farming in which farmers specialise only in vegetables, at a distance from the market that a truck can cover overnight, is called:
(1) Factory farming
(2) Truck farming
(3) Collective farming
(4) Mixed farming
= 2
~ Truck farming is part of market gardening and horticulture, which supply high-value crops to urban markets.

Q 18 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Consider the following statements about co-operative and collective farming:
1. In co-operative farming, individual farms remain intact, and farmers pool resources voluntarily through a co-operative society.
2. Collective farming, based on social ownership and collective labour, was introduced in the erstwhile Soviet Union as the Kolkhoz model.
3. The co-operative movement has been most successful in Denmark, where practically every farmer is a member.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ The co-operative movement also succeeded in the Netherlands, Belgium, Sweden and Italy. In the Kolkhoz model, farmers pooled land, livestock and labour and sold produce to the state at fixed prices.

Q 19 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Consider the following statements about methods of mining:
1. Open-cast mining is the easiest and cheapest way of mining minerals that occur close to the surface.
2. Shaft (underground) mining is used when the ore lies deep below the surface, and is risky.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 3
~ Underground mining needs lifts, drills, haulage vehicles and ventilation, and carries risks of poisonous gases, fires, floods and caving in.

Q 20 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Which of the following is NOT listed in the chapter as an economic factor affecting mining activity?
(1) Demand for the mineral
(2) Technology available and used
(3) Capital to develop infrastructure, and labour and transport costs
(4) Size and grade of the deposits
= 4
~ Size, grade and mode of occurrence of deposits are physical factors. Several countries of Africa, and some in South America and Asia, earn over half their export income from minerals.
