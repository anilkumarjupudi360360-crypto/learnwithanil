Q 1 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Mahatma Gandhi returned to India from South Africa in:
(1) August 1914
(2) January 1915
(3) April 1919
(4) December 1920
= 2
~ In South Africa, he had first used the techniques of non-violent protest known as satyagraha. His political mentor in India was Gopal Krishna Gokhale.

Q 2 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
At the opening of the Banaras Hindu University in February 1916, Gandhi:
(1) Praised the British government
(2) Spoke only about education
(3) Charged the Indian elite with a lack of concern for the labouring poor, and said Indian nationalism was an elite phenomenon
(4) Announced the Non-Cooperation Movement
= 3
~ He said there could be no salvation for India unless the wealthy and well-dressed gave up their jewels for the benefit of their countrymen.

Q 3 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Which of the following local movements did Gandhi lead in 1917-18?
1. Champaran, to secure better conditions for indigo cultivators
2. Ahmedabad, for better working conditions for textile mill workers
3. Kheda, asking the state to remit taxes for peasants after a crop failure
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 3 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ These movements made Gandhi known as a nationalist with a deep sympathy for the poor.

Q 4 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The Rowlatt Act of 1919, against which Gandhi called a countrywide campaign:
(1) Gave Indians the right to vote
(2) Abolished the salt tax
(3) Ended the First World War
(4) Allowed the government to imprison people without trial
= 4
~ The protests were strongest in Punjab. In April 1919, the Jallianwala Bagh massacre took place in Amritsar.

Q 5 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The Non-Cooperation Movement was combined with which campaign of Indian Muslims?
(1) The Swadeshi movement
(2) The Khilafat movement, seeking to restore the Caliphate
(3) The Home Rule movement
(4) The Pakistan movement
= 2
~ Gandhi hoped that by joining the two movements, Hindus and Muslims could together bring an end to colonial rule.

Q 6 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Gandhi called off the Non-Cooperation Movement in February 1922 because:
(1) Swaraj had been achieved
(2) A crowd at Chauri Chaura set fire to a police station, and several policemen died
(3) The British agreed to all demands
(4) He was arrested
= 2
~ Gandhi felt that the movement had turned violent. He was arrested in March 1922 and sentenced to six years in prison.

Q 7 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
At its annual session in Lahore in December 1929, the Congress:
(1) Committed itself to Purna Swaraj, or complete independence
(2) Accepted dominion status
(3) Decided to dissolve itself
(4) Joined the Round Table Conference
= 1
~ The session was presided over by Jawaharlal Nehru. As a result, "Independence Day" was observed on 26 January 1930.

Q 8 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Gandhi began the Salt March from Sabarmati Ashram on 12 March 1930 and reached Dandi on 6 April 1930. Why did he choose salt?
(1) Salt was a luxury item
(2) The state's monopoly on salt and the tax on it affected every household, and salt was an essential item of food
(3) Salt was made only in Britain
(4) Salt was free in India
= 2
~ The choice brought together people of all classes. Many people broke the salt law in other parts of the country.

Q 9 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The Gandhi-Irwin Pact of March 1931 resulted in:
(1) Independence for India
(2) A ban on the Congress
(3) The calling off of civil disobedience and the release of prisoners, and Gandhi's agreement to attend the second Round Table Conference
(4) Partition of India
= 3
~ The pact was criticised by radical nationalists because it did not secure a promise of independence. The second Round Table Conference in London failed to reach an agreement.

Q 10 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
At the second Round Table Conference in 1931, Gandhi's claim that the Congress represented all of India was challenged by:
(1) Only the British
(2) The Hindu Mahasabha alone
(3) The Muslim League, the princes and B.R. Ambedkar
(4) Nobody
= 3
~ The League claimed to represent Muslims, the princes claimed the Congress had no stake in their territories, and Ambedkar argued that Gandhi did not represent the lowest castes.

Q 11 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Under the Government of India Act of 1935, elections were held in 1937. In these elections:
(1) The Muslim League won most provinces
(2) No party won
(3) The elections were boycotted by all parties
(4) The Congress won and formed governments in eight of the eleven provinces
= 4
~ In 1939, the Congress ministries resigned in protest when the Viceroy declared India's entry into the Second World War without consulting Indians.

Q 12 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The Quit India Movement was launched in:
(1) August 1942
(2) March 1930
(3) January 1946
(4) February 1922
= 1
~ Gandhi gave the call "Do or die". Although the leaders were jailed at once, the movement spread across the country, with young activists organising strikes and sabotage.

Q 13 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The resolution of the Muslim League in 1940 demanding a measure of autonomy for Muslim-majority areas was passed at:
(1) Lahore
(2) Karachi
(3) Dhaka
(4) Aligarh
= 1
~ The demand grew stronger in the following years. In 1946, the League called for a "Direct Action Day" on 16 August.

Q 14 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
In the last months of his life, after independence in August 1947, Gandhi:
(1) Celebrated in Delhi
(2) Became Prime Minister
(3) Was in Calcutta and travelled to riot-torn areas such as Noakhali, working to restore communal harmony
(4) Went to England
= 3
~ He was assassinated on 30 January 1948 by Nathuram Godse. His death was widely mourned.

Q 16 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Which of the following ways did Gandhi try to connect with ordinary Indians?
1. He dressed simply, wearing a dhoti, and spoke in the language of the people.
2. He spent part of each day working on the charkha (spinning wheel).
3. He encouraged the boycott of foreign cloth.
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ The charkha became a symbol of self-reliance and of the dignity of labour. Gandhi was seen by many peasants as a saviour.

Q 17 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Rumours spread among peasants about Gandhi's "miraculous powers". Historians studying such rumours (as in Gorakhpur) find that they show:
(1) That Gandhi spread the rumours himself
(2) That the rumours were spread by the British
(3) How peasants saw Gandhi as a messiah-like figure who would rescue them from their suffering
(4) That peasants did not know who Gandhi was
= 3
~ Such rumours show how Gandhi's message reached and was interpreted by people in the villages, often in ways different from what he intended.

Q 18 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
The Poona Pact of 1932 was an agreement between Gandhi and B.R. Ambedkar on:
(1) Representation of the depressed classes, giving them reserved seats in a general electorate instead of separate electorates
(2) Partition
(3) Language policy
(4) The salt tax
= 1
~ Gandhi had gone on a fast against the British proposal of separate electorates for the depressed classes, which he felt would divide Hindu society.

(1) Motilal Nehru

Q 20 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part III
Why are police and government reports, as well as nationalist newspapers, used with care by historians studying Gandhi's movements?
(1) Each reflected the views and interests of its authors; police reports tried to show the government was in control, while nationalist papers might exaggerate
(2) They are always false
(3) They were written by Gandhi
(4) They do not exist
