# NCERT Class 12 Geography, Fundamentals of Human Geography, Chapter 1: Human Geography: Nature and Scope
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). November 2026.

Q 1 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
"Human geography is the synthetic study of relationship between human societies and earth's surface." This definition is given by:
(1) Ellen C. Semple
(2) Friedrich Ratzel
(3) Paul Vidal de la Blache
(4) Griffith Taylor
= 2
~ Ratzel's definition stresses synthesis. Semple stresses the dynamic relationship, and Vidal de la Blache speaks of synthetic knowledge of the physical laws and living beings.

Q 2 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Who described human geography as "the study of the changing relationship between the unresting man and the unstable earth"?
(1) Ellen C. Semple
(2) Ratzel
(3) Richard Hartshorne
(4) Amartya Sen
= 1
~ Semple's definition highlights the dynamism in the relationship between humans and the earth.

Q 3 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
The chapter's story of Benda, a tribal boy living in the Abujh Maad area of central India, is used to illustrate:
(1) Possibilism
(2) Environmental determinism, or the naturalisation of humans
(3) Neo-determinism
(4) Quantitative revolution
= 2
~ Benda's community depends directly on nature for food, medicine and shelter, and treats nature as a powerful force to be worshipped. This is the idea of the "naturalisation of humans".

Q 4 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
The view that people, with the help of technology, create possibilities from the resources of their environment, so that nature gets "humanised", is called:
(1) Determinism
(2) Post-modernism
(3) Neo-determinism
(4) Possibilism
= 4
~ Possibilism sees humans as active agents who leave their imprints on nature, such as health resorts, ports, fields and industries.

Q 5 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
The concept of "neo-determinism", also called "stop and go determinism", was put forward by:
(1) Ratzel
(2) Mahbub-ul-Haq
(3) Vidal de la Blache
(4) Griffith Taylor
= 4
~ Griffith Taylor took a middle path: humans can develop by following the laws of nature, much like traffic lights that tell us when to stop and when to go.

Q 6 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
The example of a university at Trondheim, Norway, where people enjoy bananas and kiwis flown in from warmer countries in winter, illustrates:
(1) Environmental determinism
(2) Shifting cultivation
(3) How technology lets humans overcome constraints of nature (possibilism)
(4) The quantitative revolution
= 3
~ The chapter uses Trondheim to show how technology frees humans from some constraints imposed by the physical environment.

Q 7 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Match the periods with the broad approaches of human geography:
A. Early colonial period — 1. Areal differentiation
B. Later colonial period — 2. Exploration and description
C. 1930s, inter-war period — 3. Spatial organisation
D. Late 1950s to late 1960s — 4. Regional analysis
Select the correct answer:
(1) A-2, B-4, C-1, D-3
(2) A-4, B-2, C-3, D-1
(3) A-2, B-1, C-4, D-3
(4) A-3, B-4, C-1, D-2
= 1
~ The chapter traces human geography from exploration and description to regional analysis, areal differentiation and then spatial organisation.

Q 8 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
The use of computers and sophisticated statistical tools and the application of laws of physics to map and analyse human phenomena marked which phase?
(1) Exploration and description
(2) Post-modernism
(3) Spatial organisation, also called the quantitative revolution
(4) Areal differentiation
= 3
~ This phase lasted from the late 1950s to the late 1960s. Discontent with its dehumanised approach led to new schools in the 1970s.

Q 9 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Which three schools of thought emerged in human geography in the 1970s, out of discontent with the quantitative revolution?
(1) Regional, systematic and areal differentiation
(2) Determinism, possibilism and neo-determinism
(3) Humanistic, radical and behavioural
(4) Post-modernism, Marxism and positivism
= 3
~ These schools brought human awareness, social inequality and lived experience back into the study of human geography.

Q 10 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Which school of human geography used Marxian theory to explain the basic causes of poverty, deprivation and social inequality?
(1) Humanistic school
(2) Regional analysis
(3) Behavioural school
(4) Radical school
= 4
~ The humanistic school stresses human awareness and agency; the behavioural school stresses lived experience and perception of space.

Q 11 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Which school laid emphasis on lived experience and the perception of space by social categories based on ethnicity, race, religion and so on?
(1) Radical school
(2) Exploration school
(3) Spatial organisation school
(4) Behavioural school
= 4
~ The behavioural school looks at how different groups experience and perceive space.

Q 12 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
In the 1990s, post-modernism in geography mainly:
(1) Promoted grand generalisations and universal theories
(2) Rejected the study of human beings
(3) Brought back environmental determinism
(4) Questioned grand generalisations and stressed understanding each local context
= 4
~ Post-modernism doubted the applicability of universal theories and emphasised the importance of local contexts.

Q 13 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Electoral geography, a sub-field of political geography, has a close interface with which sister discipline?
(1) Demography
(2) Psephology
(3) Epidemiology
(4) Anthropology
= 2
~ Psephology is the study of elections. Electoral geography studies the spatial patterns of elections and constituencies.

Q 15 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Cultural geography, a sub-field of social geography, has a close interface with:
(1) Resource economics
(2) Military science
(3) Anthropology
(4) Business studies

Q 16 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
The chapter notes that we often speak of the "face" of the earth, the "eye" of a storm, the "mouth" of a river and the "snout" of a glacier. These expressions show:
(1) That geography is a purely physical science
(2) That physical phenomena are described in metaphors using symbols from human anatomy
(3) That rivers and glaciers are living beings
(4) That human geography has no relation with physical geography
= 2
~ This use of human metaphors reflects the close interconnection between the physical and human worlds.

Q 17 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography

Q 19 | geo | Fundamentals of Human Geography
Which of the following is true about the relationship between humans and nature according to the chapter?
(1) Humans can completely ignore the physical environment
(2) Human activities create a cultural landscape, and the physical and human elements are closely intertwined
(3) Only the physical environment matters in human geography
(4) Human geography studies only cities
= 2
~ Human geography studies the interrelationship between the physical environment and the socio-cultural environment created by human beings.
