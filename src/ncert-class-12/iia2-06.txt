# NCERT Class 12 Fine Arts (Art & Culture), An Introduction to Indian Art Part II, Chapter 6: The Bengal School and Cultural Nationalism
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The 'Company School of Painting' refers to:
(1) Paintings by British artists who toured India
(2) The painting workshop of the Mughal emperors
(3) Paintings by local artists commissioned by English officers, mixing traditional ways with European close observation
(4) Posters made for the Indian National Congress
= 3
~ Around the eighteenth century, English officers commissioned local artists to paint people, plants, animals and places; the resulting mix of traditional and European styles is the Company School.

Q 2 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Many Company painters had migrated from the erstwhile courts of:
(1) Murshidabad, Lucknow and Delhi
(2) Mysore, Thanjavur and Madurai
(3) Udaipur, Jaipur and Bikaner
(4) Bijapur, Golconda and Ahmadnagar
= 1
~ The chapter names Murshidabad, Lucknow and Delhi. The paintings were largely on paper, and albums were in demand in India and Britain.

Q 3 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
What ended the popularity of the Company style of painting?
(1) The Revolt of 1857
(2) The rise of the Bengal School
(3) The end of the East India Company's trade monopoly
(4) The arrival of photography in India in the mid-nineteenth century
= 4
~ Once photography arrived in the mid-nineteenth century, the documentary Company paintings lost their appeal.

Q 4 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Raja Ravi Varma, a self-taught artist who mastered academic realism, belonged to which court?
(1) Mysore
(2) Travancore
(3) Baroda
(4) Hyderabad
= 2
~ Ravi Varma was an artist of the Travancore court in Kerala; he learnt by copying European paintings popular in Indian palaces.

Q 5 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Consider the following statements about Raja Ravi Varma:
1. He painted scenes from the Ramayana and Mahabharata in oil.
2. His paintings were reproduced as oleographs and used as calendar images in homes.
3. By the late nineteenth century, nationalists felt his academic style was too Western for Indian myths and history.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ All three are in the chapter. Ravi Varma was among the first Indian painters to use oil and lithographic reproduction for mythological subjects; 'Krishna as Envoy' (1906) is one of his works.

Q 6 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The Bengal School, associated with the Swadeshi movement, was spearheaded by:
(1) Raja Ravi Varma
(2) Abanindranath Tagore
(3) Jamini Roy
(4) Amrita Sher-Gil
= 2
~ Abanindranath Tagore (1871–1951) led the Bengal School, which began in Calcutta and sought an Indian style in both subject and manner.

Q 7 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
E.B. Havell, who supported Abanindranath Tagore, was:
(1) The principal of the Calcutta School of Art
(2) A Japanese painter
(3) The Viceroy of India
(4) The founder of Visva-Bharati
= 1
~ Havell, principal of the Calcutta School of Art, worked with Abanindranath from 1896 to bring Indian traditions into art education at the Government Art School, Calcutta.

Q 8 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
For the artists of the Bengal School, the most important sources of inspiration were:
(1) Company paintings
(2) The academic oil painting of colonial art schools
(3) Mughal and Pahari miniatures
(4) European Cubism
= 3
~ They turned to Mughal and Pahari miniatures, not to Company painting or academic realism, to create an Indian style.

Q 9 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Abanindranath Tagore is credited in the chapter with inventing which technique?
(1) The wash technique of painting
(2) Oleography
(3) Fresco buono
(4) Viscosity printing
= 1
~ The chapter credits Abanindranath with the wash painting technique, later taught at Shantiniketan by two Japanese artists.

Q 10 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Abanindranath Tagore's 'Journey's End' (1913) shows:
(1) The death of Shah Jahan
(2) A mother and child
(3) A farmer ploughing his field
(4) A collapsed camel against a red dusk
= 4
~ Journey's End, a watercolour, shows a camel collapsing under its load against a red dusk; it reflects the influence of Mughal and Pahari miniatures.

Q 11 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Abanindranath Tagore made a series of 45 paintings based on:
(1) Kalidasa's Meghaduta
(2) The Arabian Nights
(3) The Ramayana
(4) The Rubaiyat of Omar Khayyam
= 2
~ The chapter mentions his series of 45 paintings based on The Arabian Nights, besides works like The Forest and Mountain Traveller.

Q 12 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Which Japanese nationalist, visiting Rabindranath Tagore in Calcutta, joined Ananda Coomaraswamy in spreading the idea of Pan-Asianism?
(1) Yokoyama Taikan
(2) Hishida Shunso
(3) Kakuzo Okakura
(4) Tenshin Hara
= 3
~ Kakuzo Okakura's visit led to the Pan-Asian link; two Japanese artists later came to Calcutta and taught the wash technique.

Q 13 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Gaganendranath Tagore, brother of Abanindranath, used which modern style in works such as 'City in the Night' (1922)?
(1) Wash painting
(2) Kalighat pat
(3) Cubism
(4) Pop Art
= 3
~ Gaganendranath used Cubism; he also drew caricatures mocking wealthy Bengalis who blindly copied European lifestyles.

Q 14 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
The painting 'Radhika' is by:
(1) Kshitindranath Majumdar
(2) Nandalal Bose
(3) Jamini Roy
(4) Abdur Rahman Chughtai
= 4
~ M.A.R. Chughtai painted Radhika, using the wash technique with a calligraphic line from Mughal and Persian art. Kshitindranath Majumdar painted Rasa-Lila.

Q 15 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Kala Bhavana, described as India's first national art school, is part of:
(1) Jamia Millia Islamia
(2) Visva-Bharati University, Shantiniketan
(3) Calcutta University
(4) Banaras Hindu University
= 2
~ Rabindranath Tagore invited Nandalal Bose, a student of Abanindranath, to head the painting department at Kala Bhavana, part of Visva-Bharati at Shantiniketan.

Q 16 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Nandalal Bose painted the Haripura Posters for the Congress session at Haripura at the invitation of:
(1) Mahatma Gandhi
(2) Jawaharlal Nehru
(3) Subhas Chandra Bose
(4) Rabindranath Tagore
= 1
~ Gandhi invited Nandalal Bose; over 400 posters were prepared at Kala Bhavana under his supervision.

Q 17 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Nandalal Bose's 'Tiller of the Soil' (1938), showing a farmer ploughing, draws on:
(1) European Cubism
(2) The Ajanta wall paintings and patua scroll painting
(3) Mughal court portraits
(4) Company paintings
= 2
~ The Haripura panel shows the influence of Ajanta murals and of the patua scroll painters of Bengal.

Q 18 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Consider the following statements about Jamini Roy:
1. He rejected his academic training and turned to village folk painting, collecting pats in the Bengal countryside.
2. He made his own canvas from home-spun fabric.
3. He worked mainly in oil on imported canvas.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1
~ Statement 3 is wrong: Jamini Roy moved from monochrome brush drawings to seven basic colours in tempera, on canvas he made himself. His themes were women, children and rural life, as in 'Woman with Child' (1940).

Q 19 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
Under colonial art policy, students of the Bombay School of Art trained by Principal Gladstone Solomon got which commission?
(1) Decorating the Indian House in London
(2) Illustrating the Constitution of India
(3) Painting the Haripura Posters
(4) Murals for the buildings of Lutyens' Delhi
= 4
~ The Bombay students got the mural project for Lutyens' Delhi, while Bengal School artists decorated the Indian House in London under British supervision.

Q 20 | art | An Introduction to Indian Art, Part II
In 1922, an exhibition of Paul Klee, Kandinsky and other Bauhaus artists travelled to which Indian city?
(1) Bombay
(2) Madras
(3) Calcutta
(4) Lahore
= 3
~ The Bauhaus exhibition came to Calcutta in 1922; Gaganendranath Tagore's experiments with Cubism belong to the same moment.
