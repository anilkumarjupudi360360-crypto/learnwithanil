Q 1 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
According to the chapter, independent India faced three main challenges. Which of the following was NOT one of them?
(1) Shaping a nation that was united yet accommodated diversity
(2) Establishing democracy
(3) Ensuring development and well-being of the whole society
(4) Building a colonial empire abroad
= 4
~ The three challenges were national unity and integrity, establishing democracy, and ensuring development for all, especially the poor and the marginalised.

Q 2 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The "two-nation theory", on which the demand for Pakistan was based, held that:
(1) India had two official languages
(2) India consisted of two peoples, Hindus and Muslims, who should have separate countries
(3) India should have two capitals
(4) India and Britain were two equal nations
= 2
~ The Muslim League put forward this theory. The Congress opposed it, but Partition was finally based on religious majorities in different areas.

Q 3 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which of the following problems arose in drawing the boundaries of Partition?
1. Muslim-majority areas were in two places, the north-west and the east, with no single continuous territory
2. Not all Muslim-majority areas wanted to join Pakistan
3. The provinces of Punjab and Bengal had to be divided, though they had large populations of both communities
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ All three caused serious difficulties. Pakistan came to have two parts, East and West, more than a thousand miles apart, and large minorities were left on both sides of the new border.

Q 4 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan, who opposed the two-nation theory, was the leader of which region?
(1) North West Frontier Province
(2) East Bengal
(3) Sindh
(4) Baluchistan
= 1
~ Known as the "Frontier Gandhi", he was the undisputed leader of the NWFP. Despite his opposition, the NWFP was merged with Pakistan.

Q 5 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Approximately how many princely states existed in British India at the time of independence?
(1) About 100
(2) About 1,200
(3) About 50
(4) About 565
= 4
~ There were about 565 princely states. The British had announced that with the end of their rule, the paramountcy over these states would lapse and they would be free to join India, Pakistan or stay independent.

Q 6 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Who played the leading role, as Home Minister, in integrating the princely states into India?
(1) Jawaharlal Nehru
(2) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
(3) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
(4) Maulana Abul Kalam Azad
= 3
~ Patel used firm diplomacy, and force where needed, to bring the princely states in. Most rulers signed the Instrument of Accession before 15 August 1947.

Q 7 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The document by which a princely state agreed to become part of India was called the:
(1) Instrument of Accession
(2) Treaty of Merger
(3) Charter of Freedom
(4) Objectives Resolution
= 1
~ By signing the Instrument of Accession, rulers handed over control of defence, foreign affairs and communications to India; later merger agreements completed integration.

Q 8 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which of the following princely states was integrated into India through a military operation in September 1948, after its ruler tried to remain independent?
(1) Manipur
(2) Junagadh
(3) Mysore
(4) Hyderabad
= 4
~ The Nizam of Hyderabad wanted independence. Amid the Razakars' violence and a peasant struggle in the Telangana region, the Indian Army moved in in September 1948 and the Nizam surrendered.

Q 9 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which princely state held elections on the basis of universal adult franchise in June 1948, even before India did, and later merged with India in 1949?
(1) Hyderabad
(2) Manipur
(3) Travancore
(4) Kashmir
= 2
~ Maharaja Bodhachandra Singh of Manipur held elections and became a constitutional monarch. The merger agreement signed in September 1949, without consulting the elected assembly, caused resentment.

Q 10 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The movement against the Nizam that the chapter mentions, in which peasants of the region rose against oppression, took place in:
(1) Kerala
(2) Bengal
(3) Punjab
(4) Telangana
= 4
~ The Telangana peasant struggle, led largely by Communists, opposed the Nizam's rule and the landlords. The Razakars, a militia supporting the Nizam, attacked people in this period.

Q 11 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Potti Sriramulu's fast unto death, which led to the creation of the first linguistic state, was for the formation of:
(1) Kerala
(2) Karnataka
(3) Andhra
(4) Maharashtra
= 3
~ Potti Sriramulu died in December 1952 after a long fast demanding a Telugu-speaking state. Andhra state was formed on 1 October 1953.

Q 12 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The States Reorganisation Commission, appointed in 1953, recommended that state boundaries should reflect:
(1) Religious majorities
(2) The boundaries of the old princely states
(3) The boundaries of different languages
(4) The size of each state's army
= 3
~ The Commission's report led to the States Reorganisation Act of 1956, which created 14 states and 6 union territories, largely on linguistic lines.

Q 13 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Why were national leaders initially reluctant to reorganise states on linguistic lines?
(1) After Partition, they feared that dividing states by language might lead to further disintegration
(2) They wanted to keep the British boundaries forever
(3) They wanted only one language for the whole country
(4) The Constitution banned new states
= 1
~ Having seen Partition on religious lines, leaders worried that language-based states would weaken national unity. Popular movements finally convinced them, and the chapter notes that linguistic states actually strengthened unity.

Q 14 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which pair of states was created by dividing the bilingual Bombay State in 1960?
(1) Gujarat and Maharashtra
(2) Punjab and Haryana
(3) Assam and Meghalaya
(4) Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh
= 1
~ Bombay State was split into Maharashtra and Gujarat in 1960 after the Samyukta Maharashtra and Mahagujarat movements.

Q 15 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which of the following pairs of state and year of formation is correctly matched?
(1) Nagaland — 1953
(2) Haryana — 1966
(3) Telangana — 2000
(4) Jharkhand — 2014
= 2
~ Haryana was carved out of Punjab in 1966. Nagaland became a state in 1963, Jharkhand in 2000 and Telangana in 2014.

Q 16 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
According to the chapter, what was the human cost of Partition?
(1) It was peaceful, with very few people moving
(2) It caused one of the largest and most abrupt migrations in history, with lakhs killed and many women abducted and abused
(3) Only government officials were moved
(4) It affected only the princely states
= 2
~ Partition uprooted around eighty lakh people and led to communal killings in which lakhs died. The chapter describes the trauma that families on both sides carried for generations.

Q 17 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which statement reflects the approach of the Indian government towards the princely states, as described in the chapter?
(1) It used only force to integrate all states
(2) It allowed every state to remain independent
(3) It combined persuasion and negotiation with firmness, while being willing to give autonomy in some areas, and aimed for the integration of the whole territory
(4) It left the matter to the British
= 3
~ The government wanted a united India and also accepted the democratic wishes of people in the states. Most rulers joined peacefully; a few, like Hyderabad, were integrated after resistance.

Q 18 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Junagadh's ruler wanted to join Pakistan, though most of its people did not. How was the issue settled?
(1) By a war with Britain
(2) By a decision of the United Nations
(3) After popular protests, the matter was resolved and a plebiscite confirmed the people's wish to join India
(4) By the ruler's decision alone
= 3
~ After unrest among the people, Indian authority was established and a plebiscite in early 1948 showed overwhelming support for joining India.

Q 19 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The States Reorganisation Act, 1956, created how many states and union territories?
(1) 14 states and 6 union territories
(2) 28 states and 7 union territories
(3) 22 states and 9 union territories
(4) 10 states and 2 union territories
= 1
~ The Act implemented the Commission's recommendations. Many more states were created later, as other linguistic and regional demands were accepted.

Q 20 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
According to the chapter, how did the creation of linguistic states affect Indian democracy?
(1) It broke up the country
(2) It made the Hindi language compulsory everywhere
(3) It ended all regional demands permanently
(4) It made democracy more representative and accommodated diversity, while India remained united
= 4
~ Fears of disintegration proved unfounded. Recognising regional and linguistic identities strengthened the democratic system and national unity.
