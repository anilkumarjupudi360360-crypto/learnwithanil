# NCERT Class 12 Political Science, Contemporary World Politics, Chapter 6: Environment and Natural Resources
# Questions written by Learn with Anil from the chapter (current NCERT edition, Reprint 2026-27). 16 Nov 2026.

Q 1 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The UN Conference on Environment and Development, popularly called the Earth Summit, was held in 1992 at:
(1) Kyoto
(2) Stockholm
(3) Rio de Janeiro
(4) Paris
= 3
~ The Rio Earth Summit produced conventions on climate change and biodiversity, and Agenda 21, a list of development practices. It also brought out differences between the global North and South.

Q 2 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The book Limits to Growth (1972), which warned that the earth's resources are being depleted by rapid population growth, was published by:
(1) The Club of Rome
(2) The World Bank
(3) The United Nations Security Council
(4) Greenpeace
= 1
~ The Club of Rome's study raised early alarms about the effects of growth on resources. Environmental issues gained political importance from the 1960s onwards.

Q 3 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The 1987 report Our Common Future, which popularised the idea of sustainable development, is also known as the:
(1) Kyoto Report
(2) Stockholm Report
(3) Club of Rome Report
(4) Brundtland Report
= 4
~ The World Commission on Environment and Development, headed by Gro Harlem Brundtland, defined sustainable development as meeting present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet theirs.

Q 4 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Areas or regions outside the sovereign jurisdiction of any one state, which require common governance by the international community, are called:
(1) Exclusive economic zones
(2) Global commons
(3) Special economic zones
(4) Protected areas
= 2
~ The global commons include the earth's atmosphere, Antarctica, the ocean floor and outer space. Managing them requires international cooperation.

Q 5 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following is an example of an international agreement on the global commons mentioned in the chapter?
(1) The Shimla Agreement
(2) The Antarctic Treaty of 1959 and the Montreal Protocol of 1987
(3) The Treaty of Rome
(4) The Panchsheel Agreement
= 2
~ The Antarctic Treaty set aside Antarctica for peaceful and scientific purposes, and the Montreal Protocol limited substances that deplete the ozone layer.

Q 6 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The Montreal Protocol of 1987 was aimed at protecting:
(1) Tropical forests
(2) Whales
(3) Coral reefs
(4) The ozone layer
= 4
~ The protocol phased out chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) and other ozone-depleting substances. It is considered one of the most successful environmental agreements.

Q 7 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The principle of "common but differentiated responsibilities", adopted at Rio, means that:
(1) All countries share responsibility for protecting the environment, but developed countries should bear a greater burden because they caused most of the damage and have more resources
(2) Only developing countries are responsible for pollution
(3) No country has any responsibility
(4) All countries must cut emissions equally
= 1
~ Developed countries industrialised first and emitted most greenhouse gases historically. India and other developing countries have used this principle to argue that their development needs must be respected.

Q 8 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The Kyoto Protocol of 1997 set targets for reducing greenhouse gas emissions mainly for:
(1) All countries equally
(2) Only India and China
(3) Industrialised (developed) countries
(4) Only small island states
= 3
~ Under Kyoto, developed countries agreed to binding emission cuts. Developing countries like India, which ratified it in 2002, were exempt from binding targets.

Q 9 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) was adopted in:
(1) 1992
(2) 1972
(3) 1997
(4) 2015
= 1
~ The UNFCCC was adopted at the Rio Earth Summit. The Kyoto Protocol (1997) and the Paris Agreement (2015) were later agreements under it.

Q 10 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
How does the chapter describe India's position on climate change negotiations?
(1) India has refused to take any steps on the environment
(2) India wants developing countries to accept the same binding cuts as developed countries
(3) India supports the principle of common but differentiated responsibilities, pointing to its low per capita emissions and its development needs, while taking domestic action
(4) India has withdrawn from the UNFCCC
= 3
~ India argues that developed countries must lead, since their historical and per capita emissions are much higher. It has nonetheless taken steps like promoting renewable energy.

Q 11 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
"Resource geopolitics", as discussed in the chapter, is about:
(1) The sharing of sports facilities
(2) The study of maps only
(3) The conservation of wildlife only
(4) Who gets what natural resources, when and how, and the conflicts that arise over resources like oil and water
= 4
~ Competition for strategic resources like oil has shaped wars, foreign policy and alliances. Water scarcity is also emerging as a cause of conflict.

Q 12 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which region, mentioned in the chapter, has the largest proven reserves of petroleum and has been at the centre of resource geopolitics?
(1) South America
(2) The Persian Gulf (West Asia)
(3) South Asia
(4) Western Europe
= 2
~ The Gulf region holds a large share of the world's proven oil reserves. Its oil has made it a focus of great-power rivalry.

Q 13 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Indigenous peoples, discussed in the chapter, have raised which concerns about development?
(1) That they want all forests cut down
(2) That they want to move to cities only
(3) That they do not want any rights
(4) That their lands and resources are taken for development projects, threatening their livelihoods and ways of life
= 4
~ Indigenous communities' struggles concern their right to land, resources and culture. The chapter notes that in India many people displaced by development projects are tribal people.

Q 14 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Why does the chapter say the global North and South differ on environmental issues?
(1) Because the South has caused most of the pollution historically
(2) Because the North wants equal responsibility for all, while the South argues that developed countries caused most of the damage and must do more
(3) Because both agree completely
(4) Because the North has no industries
= 2
~ Developed countries stress shared responsibility for the future; developing countries stress historical responsibility and their right to develop.

Q 15 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Sacred groves, mentioned in the chapter, are an example of:
(1) Community-based conservation of forests, protected because of religious or cultural beliefs
(2) Government-owned plantations
(3) Industrial parks
(4) Mining areas
= 1
~ Many communities in India protect patches of forest as sacred. The chapter cites them as examples of traditional conservation.

Q 16 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
The Paris Agreement on climate change was adopted in:
(1) 1992
(2) 1997
(3) 2015
(4) 2005
= 3
~ Under the Paris Agreement, countries set their own targets (nationally determined contributions) to limit global warming to well below 2°C.

Q 17 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Why did environmental issues become a major part of global politics from the 1960s onwards, according to the chapter?
(1) Because the world's population fell
(2) Because all resources became unlimited
(3) Because the effects of environmental damage, such as pollution, deforestation and the loss of species, became widely visible and could not be handled by single countries
(4) Because wars stopped completely
= 3
~ Shrinking forests, polluted water, a thinning ozone layer and climate change drew attention, and governments began to negotiate international agreements.

Q 18 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Which of the following was produced at the Rio Earth Summit of 1992 as a list of development practices?
(1) Agenda 21
(2) The Kyoto Protocol
(3) The Montreal Protocol
(4) The Antarctic Treaty
= 1
~ Agenda 21 recommended practices for sustainable development. Critics said it emphasised economic growth more than ecological limits.

Q 19 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
Why is managing the global commons difficult, according to the chapter?
(1) Because they belong to one country
(2) Because nobody uses them
(3) Because they are very small
(4) Because of differences between countries about who should bear the costs, and because of uneven scientific knowledge and technology
= 4
~ The North–South divide and uncertainties about the environment make agreement hard. Advances in technology have also increased the possibility of exploiting areas like outer space and the ocean floor.

Q 20 | pol | Contemporary World Politics
India ratified the Kyoto Protocol in:
(1) 1997
(2) 2002
(3) 2005
(4) 2015
= 2
~ India ratified it in August 2002. As a developing country, India was not required to make binding emission cuts under the protocol.
