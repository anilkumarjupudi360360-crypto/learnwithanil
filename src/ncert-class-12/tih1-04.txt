Q 1 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
The Great Stupa at Sanchi survived and was preserved in the nineteenth century largely because of support from:
(1) The rulers of Gwalior
(2) The Nizam of Hyderabad
(3) The Begums of Bhopal, Shahjehan Begum and her successor Sultan Jehan Begum
(4) The Maharaja of Mysore
= 3
~ They gave money for its preservation. John Marshall dedicated his volumes on Sanchi to Sultan Jehan.

Q 2 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
The mid-first millennium BCE is often seen as a turning point in world history because thinkers emerged in many lands. Which of the following is correctly matched?
(1) Zarathustra - China
(2) Kong Zi - Iran
(3) Socrates, Plato and Aristotle - Greece
(4) Socrates - India
= 3
~ Zarathustra was in Iran, Kong Zi in China, and Mahavira and the Buddha in India. They tried to understand the mysteries of existence and the relationship between humans and the cosmic order.

Q 4 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
The most important idea in Jainism is that:
(1) The world is an illusion
(2) The entire world is animated; even stones, rocks and water have life, so non-injury (ahimsa) to all living beings is central
(3) Sacrifices bring salvation
(4) God created the world
= 2
~ The cycle of birth and rebirth is shaped by karma. Jaina monks and nuns took five vows, including not to kill and not to steal.

Q 5 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Which of the following sets of places in the Buddha's life is correctly matched?
(1) Birth - Lumbini; enlightenment - Bodh Gaya; first sermon - Sarnath; death - Kusinagara
(2) Birth - Bodh Gaya; first sermon - Lumbini

(4) Birth - Kusinagara; first sermon - Sanchi

Q 6 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
The Buddha's teachings were compiled after his death into three "baskets" (Tipitaka). Which of the following is NOT one of them?
(1) Vinaya Pitaka
(2) Jataka Pitaka
(3) Sutta Pitaka
(4) Abhidhamma Pitaka
= 2
~ The Vinaya Pitaka has rules for the sangha, the Sutta Pitaka the Buddha's teachings, and the Abhidhamma Pitaka philosophical matters. Jatakas are stories of the Buddha's previous births.

Q 7 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
The first woman to be ordained as a bhikkhuni (nun) was:
(1) Prabhavati Gupta
(2) Yashodhara
(3) Sanghamitta
(4) Mahapajapati Gotami, the Buddha's foster mother
= 4
~ Many women joined the sangha. The Therigatha, part of the Sutta Pitaka, is a collection of verses composed by bhikkhunis.

Q 8 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
In later Buddhist tradition, the Mahayana ("great vehicle") differed from older traditions because:
(1) It rejected the Buddha
(2) It saw the Buddha as a saviour and gave importance to Bodhisattas, compassionate beings who helped others attain nirvana
(3) It banned images
(4) It was practised only in Sri Lanka
= 2
~ Worship of images of the Buddha and Bodhisattas became important. Followers of Mahayana called the older tradition Hinayana; the older tradition is also called Theravada.

Q 9 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Which of the following is correctly matched with the parts of a stupa?
(1) Harmika - a balcony-like structure above the anda, representing the abode of the gods
(2) Anda - the railing around the stupa
(3) Harmika - the hemispherical mound
(4) Chhatri - the gateway
= 1
~ The anda is the hemispherical mound. A mast (yashti) rose from the harmika, topped by a chhatri, and a railing separated the sacred space.

Q 10 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
The stupa at Amaravati (Andhra Pradesh) did not survive like Sanchi because:
(1) It was destroyed by an earthquake
(2) Its sculpted slabs were removed in the nineteenth century, some taken to Madras, Calcutta and London
(3) It was never built
(4) It was submerged by a dam
= 2
~ Officials like Walter Elliot took slabs away. H.H. Cole, a curator, argued that copies should be made and the originals left where they were.

Q 11 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
The sculpture on the gateway at Sanchi of a woman swinging from the edge of the gateway, holding onto a tree, is identified as a:
(1) Shalabhanjika
(2) Tirthankara
(3) Bodhisatta
(4) Yakshini of Mathura
= 1
~ According to popular belief, a shalabhanjika was a woman whose touch caused trees to flower and bear fruit. It shows that people with other beliefs joined Buddhism.

Q 12 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Which of the following was a fatalist teacher who led the Ajivikas?
(1) Ajita Kesakambalin
(2) Nagarjuna
(3) Mahavira
(4) Makkhali Gosala

Q 13 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Which of the following is true of the Kailashanatha temple at Ellora (Maharashtra)?
(1) It was carved out of a single piece of rock, in the eighth century
(2) It was built with bricks
(3) It is a Buddhist stupa
(4) It was built by the Mauryas
= 1
~ The sculptor of the temple is said to have wondered how he could have made it. It shows great skill in rock-cut architecture.

Q 14 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
The paintings at Ajanta (Maharashtra) mainly depict:
(1) Stories from the Jatakas, and scenes of court life, processions and men and women at work
(2) Scenes of Mughal court life
(3) Battles of the Rajputs
(4) Landscapes of Europe
= 1
~ The paintings show the skill of artists in depicting human feelings. Because they are in caves, they have survived for centuries.

Q 15 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
In early Hindu temples, the small square room where the image of the chief deity was placed was called the:
(1) Mandapa
(2) Gopuram
(3) Shikhara
(4) Garbhagriha
= 4
~ Over time, a tall structure called the shikhara was built over the garbhagriha, and temple walls were decorated with sculpture.

Q 16 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
The idea of avatars, or incarnations, is associated mainly with which deity?
(1) Shiva
(2) Vishnu
(3) Indra
(4) Agni
= 2
~ The Vaishnava tradition held that Vishnu took different forms to save the world whenever it was threatened by evil. Shiva was often shown as a linga.

Q 17 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
The Sri Lankan chronicles that describe the history of Buddhism are:
(1) Rajatarangini and Harshacharita
(2) Jatakas and Therigatha
(3) Dipavamsa and Mahavamsa
(4) Puranas and Upanishads
= 3
~ Buddhist texts spread to other parts of Asia, such as Sri Lanka, China and South-East Asia, where they were preserved and translated.

Q 18 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
The Buddha's sangha was organised around which principle?
(1) All members were from the Brahmana varna
(2) Only men could join
(3) Members had to be rich
(4) Monks and nuns lived simply, possessing only essentials, and in theory all were equal, setting aside their social identities
= 4
~ Both men and women from different social backgrounds, such as kings, wealthy men, gahapatis and humbler people, joined the sangha.

Q 19 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Stupas were built over:
(1) The tombs of kings
(2) Temples of Vishnu
(3) Relics such as the bodily remains of the Buddha or objects he used, which were regarded as sacred
(4) Royal palaces
= 3
~ Inscriptions on stupa railings record donations by kings, guilds and ordinary men and women. Stupas thus became places of shared devotion.

Q 20 | his | Themes in Indian History, Part I
Why do historians face problems in understanding early sculpture?
(1) Sculpture was never made in India
(2) Images were always labelled
(3) Historians often had to interpret images by comparing them with texts, and some images, like those at Sanchi, could be understood only by those familiar with Buddhist stories
(4) All sculpture has been destroyed
= 3
~ Early European scholars sometimes misunderstood Indian art. Studying texts and traditions helps explain the meaning of sculpture.
