Q 1 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The national Emergency was declared on 25 June 1975 under Article 352 on the ground of:
(1) External aggression
(2) Financial crisis
(3) Internal disturbance
(4) Failure of the constitutional machinery in a state
= 3
~ The government said there was a threat of internal disturbance. After the Emergency, the 44th Amendment replaced "internal disturbance" with "armed rebellion" to prevent misuse.

Q 2 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
In June 1975, the Allahabad High Court declared Indira Gandhi's election to the Lok Sabha invalid. The case had been filed by:
(1) Raj Narain
(2) Jayaprakash Narayan
(3) Morarji Desai
(4) George Fernandes
= 1
~ Justice Jagmohan Lal Sinha ruled on 12 June 1975 on Raj Narain's petition, who had lost to her in Rae Bareli in 1971. The Supreme Court granted a partial stay, and the Emergency followed soon after.

Q 3 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which student-led movement in 1974 against price rise, corruption and the state government is mentioned in the chapter as part of the background to the Emergency?
(1) The Assam movement
(2) The Telangana movement
(3) The Chipko movement
(4) The Navnirman movement in Gujarat
= 4
~ Students in Gujarat protested against rising prices and corruption in January 1974, leading to President's Rule in the state. A similar movement soon began in Bihar.

Q 4 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Jayaprakash Narayan, who led the Bihar movement, gave a call for:
(1) Garibi Hatao
(2) Total Revolution (Sampoorna Kranti)
(3) Quit India
(4) Jai Jawan Jai Kisan
= 2
~ JP called for total revolution to establish what he saw as true democracy in social, economic and political spheres. He also demanded the dismissal of the Congress government in Bihar.

Q 5 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The nationwide railway strike of 1974, led by George Fernandes, was mainly about:
(1) The language policy
(2) Bonus and service conditions of railway employees
(3) Separate states
(4) Nuclear policy
= 2
~ The government declared the strike illegal and broke it, arresting many workers. The episode added to the climate of confrontation before the Emergency.

Q 6 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which of the following happened during the Emergency, according to the chapter?
1. Press censorship
2. Arrests of thousands of opposition leaders and workers without trial under preventive detention laws
3. Suspension of the right to move the courts for the enforcement of some Fundamental Rights
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ The Emergency suspended the federal distribution of powers, curtailed civil liberties, imposed censorship and detained opponents, often under MISA (Maintenance of Internal Security Act).

Q 7 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The supersession of three senior judges in 1973 to appoint the Chief Justice of India, mentioned in the chapter, was seen as:
(1) An attack on the independence of the judiciary
(2) A routine appointment
(3) Required by the Constitution
(4) A decision of the Supreme Court itself
= 1
~ The appointment of Justice A.N. Ray, superseding three senior judges, followed the Kesavananda Bharati judgment and became part of the conflict between the government and the judiciary.

Q 8 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
After the Emergency ended, a commission was set up in 1977 to inquire into the excesses committed during the Emergency. It was headed by:
(1) Justice R.S. Sarkaria
(2) Justice M.N. Venkatachaliah
(3) Justice J.C. Shah
(4) Justice P.N. Bhagwati
= 3
~ The Shah Commission found many cases of excesses, including forced sterilisations and demolitions, and arrests made without proper reasons.

Q 9 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
In the 1977 Lok Sabha elections held after the Emergency, the Congress was defeated by:
(1) The Janata Party
(2) The Swatantra Party
(3) The National Front
(4) The Communist Party of India
= 1
~ Opposition parties merged to form the Janata Party, which won a majority. Indira Gandhi and Sanjay Gandhi lost their own seats.

Q 10 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Who became the first non-Congress Prime Minister of India in 1977?
(1) Charan Singh
(2) Jagjivan Ram
(3) V.P. Singh
(4) Morarji Desai
= 4
~ Morarji Desai became Prime Minister. Rivalries within the Janata Party led to its collapse, and Charan Singh briefly became Prime Minister in 1979.

Q 11 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The Janata government did not complete its term. What happened in the 1980 Lok Sabha elections?
(1) The Janata Party won a bigger majority
(2) The Congress under Indira Gandhi returned to power with a large majority
(3) A national government of all parties was formed
(4) The elections were cancelled
= 2
~ Voters punished the Janata Party for its infighting. Indira Gandhi's Congress won a decisive victory in 1980.

Q 12 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which lesson about Indian democracy does the chapter draw from the Emergency?
(1) That democracy cannot survive in India
(2) That emergencies should be declared often
(3) That it is difficult to end democracy in India; people's commitment to it was shown in the 1977 elections, and safeguards were later strengthened
(4) That courts should be abolished
= 3
~ The 1977 elections showed that voters valued their freedoms. Amendments after the Emergency made it harder to misuse emergency powers, and awareness of civil liberties grew.

Q 13 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
After the Emergency, a national emergency under Article 352 can be declared only on the written recommendation of:
(1) The Prime Minister alone, orally
(2) The Chief Justice of India
(3) The Union Cabinet
(4) The Lok Sabha Speaker
= 3
~ The 44th Amendment requires the Cabinet's written advice to the President and approval by both Houses within a time limit, as safeguards against misuse.

Q 14 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which statement about the Emergency and the Constitution is correct?
(1) The Emergency was declared using a provision that was already in the Constitution
(2) The Constitution did not allow any emergency
(3) The Emergency was declared by the Supreme Court
(4) The Emergency suspended the Constitution completely
= 1
~ Article 352 already provided for emergencies. The debate was about whether the situation justified using it and about the excesses that followed.

Q 15 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
During the Emergency, which of the following was a major controversial programme associated with Sanjay Gandhi?
(1) Green Revolution
(2) Bank nationalisation
(3) Panchayati Raj
(4) Forced sterilisation as part of family planning, and demolition of slums in Delhi
= 4
~ The Shah Commission documented these excesses, which caused great anger, especially in north India, and contributed to the Congress's defeat in 1977.

Q 16 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which of the following leaders was among those arrested during the Emergency?
(1) Indira Gandhi
(2) Jayaprakash Narayan
(3) Sanjay Gandhi
(4) V.V. Giri
= 2
~ JP and most other opposition leaders were arrested soon after the Emergency was declared. Some went underground and organised resistance.

Q 17 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
The chapter notes that the Emergency had an effect on the relationship between civil liberties organisations and citizens. What happened after it?
(1) Interest in civil liberties disappeared
(2) Many civil liberties organisations emerged and people became more aware of their rights
(3) All rights organisations were banned
(4) Only the government could speak about rights
= 2
~ The experience of the Emergency made people value civil liberties. Organisations defending rights became more active, and courts later took a more active role in protecting them.

Q 18 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
Which of the following was part of the economic background that caused discontent before the Emergency?
(1) Rapid economic growth
(2) Falling prices
(3) Abundant food stocks
(4) Rising prices after the 1971 war and the 1973 oil crisis, drought and unemployment
= 4
~ The cost of the Bangladesh war, the oil price rise of 1973 and poor monsoons pushed prices up sharply, which fuelled protests in many states.

Q 19 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
In the 1977 elections, where did the Congress do particularly badly?
(1) In all the southern states
(2) In Kerala only
(3) In north India, where the effects of the Emergency's excesses were felt most
(4) In the north-eastern states only
= 3
~ The Congress was nearly wiped out in north India but held its ground in southern states such as Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Kerala and Tamil Nadu.

Q 20 | pol | Politics in India since Independence
How long did the Emergency of 1975 last?
(1) About 21 months, from June 1975 to March 1977
(2) Six months
(3) Five years
(4) One week
= 1
~ The Emergency was lifted in March 1977 after elections were held. It is remembered as the most controversial episode in India's democratic history.
