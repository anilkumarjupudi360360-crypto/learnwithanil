Q 1 | pls | Classification & Morphology
Who proposed the classification of organisms into three domains?
(1) CR Woese, O Kandler and ML Wheelis
(2) Ernst Haeckel
(3) Ernst Mayr
(4) Carl Linnaeus
= 1
~ In 1990 Carl Woese, Otto Kandler and Mark Wheelis proposed the three-domain system: Bacteria, Archaea and Eukarya, based mainly on ribosomal RNA sequences. Haeckel is known for the kingdom Protista and Mayr for the biological species concept, while Linnaeus gave the two-kingdom system and binomial nomenclature.

Q 2 | pls | Classification & Morphology
Bamboos belong to which plant family?
(1) Myrtaceae
(2) Moraceae
(3) Poaceae
(4) Fabaceae
= 3
~ Bamboos are woody, perennial grasses placed in the subfamily Bambusoideae of the grass family Poaceae (Gramineae). Myrtaceae includes eucalyptus and guava, Moraceae includes fig and mulberry, and Fabaceae is the legume family.

Q 3 | pls | Classification & Morphology
The International Code of Nomenclature for algae, fungi and plants adopted in the year 2017 is also known as:
(1) Shenzhen Code
(2) Melbourne Code
(3) Vienna Code
(4) Tokyo Code
= 1
~ The Shenzhen Code was adopted by the 19th International Botanical Congress held in Shenzhen, China, in July 2017 (published 2018). It replaced the Melbourne Code of 2011 (published 2012); the Vienna Code dates from 2005 and the Tokyo Code from 1993.

Q 4 | fpt | Pests & Diseases
Herbicides are used to kill:
(1) fungi
(2) insects
(3) undesirable plants or weeds
(4) bacteria
= 3
~ A herbicide (weedicide) is a chemical used to kill or suppress unwanted plants (weeds). Fungicides act on fungi, insecticides on insects and bactericides on bacteria.

Q 5 | ecw | Types of Ecosystems
Rhizophora species are characteristic of which kind of forest type?
(1) Grassland
(2) Alpine forest
(3) Mangrove
(4) Tropical rainforest
= 3
~ Rhizophora (e.g. R. mucronata, R. apiculata) is a typical mangrove genus of tidal coasts and estuaries, recognised by its prop (stilt) roots and viviparous seedlings. Mangroves are tidal halophytic forests, not rainforests, alpine forests or grasslands.

Q 6 | pls | Reproduction & Propagation
Flowers that are pollinated by bees are known as:
(1) Chiropterophily
(2) Melittophily
(3) Myophily
(4) Ornithophily
= 2
~ Pollination by bees is called melittophily (Greek melitta, bee). Chiropterophily is pollination by bats, myophily by flies and ornithophily by birds.

Q 7 | pls | Classification & Morphology
The scientific name of Asian rice is:
(1) Oryza sativa
(2) Oryza nivara
(3) Oryza glaberrima
(4) Oryza barthii
= 1
~ Asian cultivated rice is Oryza sativa. Oryza glaberrima is African rice, O. barthii is its wild African ancestor, and O. nivara is an annual wild relative from which Asian rice is thought to have been domesticated.

Q 8 | pls | Genetics & Plant Diseases
Transgenic Golden Rice has been hailed as a practical solution to the deficiency of Vitamin _____.
(1) A
(2) E
(3) D
(4) C
= 1
~ Golden Rice is genetically engineered to produce beta-carotene (provitamin A) in the endosperm, aimed at preventing vitamin A deficiency, a cause of night blindness and childhood blindness. Its yellow-orange colour comes from the beta-carotene.

Q 9 | pls | Classification & Morphology
Pusa Shymala, Pusa Purple Long, Pusa Purple Cluster are varieties of which vegetable?
(1) Brinjal
(2) Potato
(3) Cabbage
(4) Ladies finger
= 1
~ Pusa Purple Long, Pusa Purple Cluster and Pusa Shyamala are brinjal (eggplant, Solanum melongena) varieties released by ICAR-IARI, New Delhi (Pusa). Potato varieties from Shimla are named Kufri, not Pusa.

Q 10 | nrs | Mineral, Food & Energy Resources
How many agro-climatic zones are there in India?
(1) 10
(2) 13
(3) 15
(4) 20
= 3
~ The Planning Commission divided India into 15 agro-climatic regions in 1989 on the basis of soil type, rainfall, temperature and water resources, for regional agricultural planning. The ICAR's NARP had earlier delineated 127 agro-climatic zones within the country, so 15 is the standard "regional" figure.

Q 11 | nrs | Mineral, Food & Energy Resources
Bromelain is an enzyme found in which fruit?
(1) Banana
(2) Coconut
(3) Apple
(4) Pineapple
= 4
~ Bromelain is a mixture of protein-digesting (proteolytic) enzymes extracted from the stem and fruit of pineapple (Ananas comosus). It is why raw pineapple tenderises meat and prevents gelatin from setting.

Q 12 | pls | Reproduction & Propagation
The scientists at Assam Agriculture University have recently achieved a significant breakthrough in which of the following area?
(1) Development of hybrids in grain pearl millet and cotton
(2) Grafting the scion of Assam lemon on rough lemon rootstock
(3) Isolation and cloning drought stress responsive transcription factors 'TaCBF5' and 'TaCBF9' from drought tolerant wheat variety C 306
(4) Sequencing 6.7 million base pairs of the long-arm of chromosome 11 of rice
= 2
~ This is a current-affairs item. The keyed result is grafting of Assam lemon (kaji nemu) scions on rough lemon (Citrus jambhiri) rootstock, a citrus propagation technique and the only option about a crop of Assam. The other options (pearl millet and cotton hybrids, wheat drought-stress genes, rice chromosome 11 sequencing) are general crop-genetics topics that are not specific to Assam. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 13 | bdc | Biodiversity & Hotspots
How many biogeographic zones are there in India?
(1) 10
(2) 11
(3) 9
(4) 12
= 1
~ Rodgers and Panwar (WII) classified India into 10 biogeographic zones: Trans-Himalaya, Himalaya, Desert, Semi-Arid, Western Ghats, Deccan Peninsula, Gangetic Plain, North-East, Coasts and Islands. These are further divided into 25 biogeographic provinces.

Q 14 | bdc | Biodiversity & Hotspots
Which of the following is an example of ex-situ conservation?
(1) National park
(2) Zoo
(3) Wildlife sanctuary
(4) Sacred grove
= 2
~ Ex-situ conservation protects species outside their natural habitat, in zoos, botanical gardens, gene banks and seed banks. National parks, wildlife sanctuaries and sacred groves conserve species in their natural habitat (in-situ).

Q 15 | ecw | Wildlife & Protected Areas
The pangolins are among the most trafficked mammals in the world and killed for their:
(1) gall bladders
(2) horns
(3) furs
(4) scales
= 4
~ Pangolins are hunted mainly for their keratin scales, used in traditional medicine, and for meat. Gall bladders are traded from bears, and horns from rhinos and antelopes. Indian and Chinese pangolins are in Schedule I of the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972.

Q 16 | bdc | Biomes & Population Ecology
Suppose there are three communities — Community A, Community B and Community C.
Community A has 5 species and their abundances are 100, 10, 10, 10, 10, respectively;
Community B has 5 species and their abundances are 10, 10, 10, 10, 10, respectively and
Community C has 5 species and their abundances are 50, 10, 10, 10, 10, respectively.
Which community will have high species diversity?
(1) Community A
(2) Community B
(3) Community C
(4) Both Community A and C
= 2
~ All three communities have the same richness (5 species), so diversity depends on evenness. Community B has perfectly equal abundances, so its Shannon and Simpson diversity are highest, whereas A and C are dominated by one species (100 of 140 and 50 of 90 individuals respectively).

Q 17 | pls | Plant Physiology
Which of the following is NOT a plant growth promotor?
(1) Auxin
(2) Gibberellins
(3) Cytokinins
(4) Abscisic acid
= 4
~ Auxins, gibberellins and cytokinins promote cell division, elongation and growth. Abscisic acid (ABA) is a growth inhibitor, the stress hormone that induces seed and bud dormancy and stomatal closure.

Q 18 | anm | Economic Zoology
A process in which milk is treated with mild heat, usually to less than 100°C, to eliminate pathogens and extend shelf life is called:
(1) pasteurisation
(2) sterilisation
(3) neutering
(4) cauterisation
= 1
~ Pasteurisation (Louis Pasteur) heats milk to a temperature below boiling, e.g. 63°C for 30 minutes (holder method) or 72°C for 15 seconds (HTST), killing pathogens without cooking the milk. Sterilisation uses higher temperatures to destroy all microbes, neutering is removal of reproductive organs, and cauterisation is burning tissue.

Q 19 | pls | Plant Physiology
Which of the following is NOT a macronutrient?
(1) Nitrogen
(2) Phosphorus
(3) Magnesium
(4) Manganese
= 4
~ Plants need nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, calcium, magnesium and sulphur in large amounts, so these are macronutrients. Manganese is required only in trace amounts and is a micronutrient, along with iron, zinc, copper, boron, molybdenum and chlorine.

Q 20 | pls | Plant Physiology
Inhibition of primary root growth in plants is often associated with the deficiency of:
(1) Nitrogen
(2) Phosphorus
(3) Potassium
(4) Iron
= 2
~ Phosphate starvation is well known to arrest primary root elongation (as in Arabidopsis) while stimulating lateral roots and root hairs, which increases soil exploration for phosphorus. Iron deficiency can also stunt roots, but phosphorus is the nutrient classically linked to this response. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 21 | pls | Plant Physiology
The energy of sunlight is first absorbed by the pigments of the plant. All pigments active in photosynthesis are found in the:
(1) golgi apparatus
(2) mitochondria
(3) chloroplast
(4) amyloplast
= 3
~ Chlorophylls, carotenoids and the other photosynthetic pigments are embedded in the thylakoid membranes of the chloroplast. Mitochondria carry out respiration, and amyloplasts are colourless plastids that store starch.

Q 22 | nrs | Mineral, Food & Energy Resources
In 100 g of Banana, which mineral is found in the highest quantity?
(1) Potassium
(2) Manganese
(3) Zinc
(4) Iron
= 1
~ Banana is well known for potassium, about 350-360 mg per 100 g. Manganese is about 0.3 mg, zinc about 0.15 mg and iron about 0.3 mg per 100 g (USDA values), so potassium is by far the most abundant mineral.

Q 23 | pls | Reproduction & Propagation
The phenomenon in which the corolla of a flower opens to expose the stamens and the pistil is called:
(1) chasmogamy
(2) cleistogamy
(3) dichogamy
(4) geitonogamy
= 1
~ Chasmogamous flowers open at maturity and expose the anthers and stigma, allowing cross-pollination. In cleistogamy flowers remain closed and self-pollinate, dichogamy is the maturing of anthers and stigma at different times, and geitonogamy is pollen transfer between flowers of the same plant.

Q 24 | pls | Reproduction & Propagation
The transition from a flower to a developing fruit is termed as:
(1) flower set
(2) pollen set
(3) fruit set
(4) stigma set
= 3
~ Fruit set is the change of a pollinated, fertilised flower (ovary) into a developing fruit. "Flower set", "pollen set" and "stigma set" are not standard horticultural terms.

Q 25 | pls | Reproduction & Propagation
Papaya is propagated only through:
(1) root cutting
(2) stem cutting
(3) sexual method
(4) asexual method
= 3
~ Papaya is commercially raised from seed (sexual propagation), because the plant is a short-lived, mostly unbranched herbaceous tree on which cuttings and grafts establish poorly. Vegetative and tissue-culture methods are possible but not standard practice, and the word "only" makes the question debatable. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 26 | pls | Reproduction & Propagation
The method of vegetative plant propagation that encourages adventitious roots to form on stems while they remain attached to the parent or stock plant is called:
(1) layering
(2) grafting
(3) budding
(4) cloning
= 1
~ In layering, roots are induced on a stem while it is still attached to the mother plant, and the rooted shoot is then separated (e.g. air layering, mound layering). Grafting and budding join a scion to a rootstock, while cloning is the general term for any vegetative multiplication.

Q 27 | pls | Reproduction & Propagation
______ is the in vitro aseptic culture of whole plants under controlled nutritional and environmental conditions.
(1) Tissue culture
(2) Hydroponics
(3) Soilless culture
(4) Protected culture
= 1
~ Plant tissue culture is the aseptic culture of cells, tissues, organs or whole plants on a nutrient medium in vitro under controlled conditions. Hydroponics and soilless culture grow plants in nutrient solution or an inert medium without soil, but not aseptically in vitro, and protected culture means growing under greenhouses or net houses.

Q 28 | pls | Reproduction & Propagation
Which of the following is NOT a technique used to rejuvenate mature plants so that they are more amenable to clonal propagation?
(1) Hedging and stool bedding
(2) Etiolation and blanching
(3) Serial propagation and grafting
(4) Low temperature exposure
= 4
~ Mature trees root poorly, so they are rejuvenated before cloning by repeated pruning (hedging, stool beds), etiolation and blanching, serial grafting or serial propagation, and micropropagation. Low-temperature exposure is used for breaking dormancy (stratification) or chilling, not for rejuvenation.

Q 29 | slv | Nursery & Planting
Pre-germination seed treatment involves soaking hard and impermeable seeds in concentrated:
(1) sulphuric acid
(2) boric acid
(3) citric acid
(4) oxalic acid
= 1
~ Seeds with hard, water-impermeable coats (e.g. Acacia, Cassia, Albizia) are acid-scarified by soaking in concentrated sulphuric acid for a few minutes to hours, then washed thoroughly. Weak acids such as boric, citric and oxalic acid do not erode the seed coat in this way.

Q 30 | pls | Genetics & Plant Diseases
Which is NOT correct for chimeras?
(1) They are organisms that are composed of cells of one genotype.
(2) Chimeras arise from genetic changes in one or more of the layers in the apical meristem.
(3) Chimeras can be reliably propagated only from axillary buds.
(4) Florentine gardener Pietro Nati first described a chimera plant.
= 1
~ A chimera is an organism made of cells of two or more genetically different tissues, so statement 1 is wrong. Chimeras arise from mutations in one or more apical meristem layers and persist only if propagated through axillary buds or grafting, not from adventitious shoots. Pietro Nati, a Florentine gardener, described the Bizzarria, a graft chimera of citrus, in 1644.

Q 31 | pls | Classification & Morphology
Perigynous flowers are those in which:
(1) petals, sepals and stamens are attached at the base of the ovary
(2) petals, sepals and stamens are attached on the rim of the hypanthium
(3) petals, sepals and stamens are attached at the top of the ovary
(4) only sepals and stamens are attached at the base of the ovary
= 2
~ In a perigynous flower the sepals, petals and stamens are borne on the rim of a cup-shaped hypanthium (floral tube) that surrounds the ovary, and the ovary is superior (e.g. rose, plum). Attachment at the base of the ovary describes hypogynous flowers and attachment at the top describes epigynous flowers.

Q 33 | slv | Nursery & Planting
The Eden Project, the world’s largest greenhouse project, is located in which country?
(1) England
(2) The Netherlands
(3) France
(4) Germany
= 1
~ The Eden Project, with its giant biomes holding the world's largest indoor rainforest, is a botanical garden in a former clay pit near St Austell in Cornwall, England, opened in 2001. The Netherlands is the other country famous for greenhouses, but the Eden Project is not located there.

Q 34 | slv | Nursery & Planting
In greenhouses, the primary environmental parameter traditionally controlled is:
(1) temperature
(2) humidity
(3) wind
(4) precipitation
= 1
~ A greenhouse's basic function is to trap solar heat and keep the interior warmer than outside, so temperature (through heating, ventilation and shading) has traditionally been the first parameter controlled. Humidity, light and CO2 are controlled in modern greenhouses, but wind and rain are excluded by the structure itself.

Q 35 | slv | Nursery & Planting
The greenhouse industry in the Netherlands relies heavily on glass framed greenhouses in order to cope up with:
(1) cloudy conditions
(2) high precipitation
(3) high humidity
(4) extremely low temperature
= 1
~ The Dutch climate is cloudy with low winter light, so growers use glass-framed greenhouses, which transmit the maximum light to the crop (often with supplementary lighting). Glass is chosen mainly for light transmission, not to cope with rain, humidity or extreme cold.

Q 36 | slv | Nursery & Planting
Who designed the first modern greenhouse?
(1) Charles Lucien Bonaparte
(2) Russ Finch
(3) Tiberius
(4) Cardinal Lucien Bonaparte
= 1
~ The French naturalist Charles Lucien Bonaparte is credited in common sources with building the first practical modern greenhouse in Leiden, Netherlands, in the 1800s to grow tropical medicinal plants. Option 4 is a different member of the Bonaparte family, and Tiberius is the Roman emperor associated with early cucumber houses. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 37 | ssg | Soil Properties & Formation
World Soil Day is observed on:
(1) 5th December
(2) 6th December
(3) 5th June
(4) 2nd October
= 1
~ World Soil Day is observed every year on 5 December, to honour the birthday of King Bhumibol Adulyadej of Thailand and to raise awareness of soil. It was recognised by the FAO Conference in 2013 and the UN General Assembly in December 2013. 5 June is World Environment Day.

Q 38 | ssg | Soil Conservation & Reclamation
Sodic soil is characterised by a disproportionately high concentration of:
(1) Na+
(2) K+
(3) Ca2+
(4) Mg2+
= 1
~ Sodic (alkali) soils have excess exchangeable sodium, with an exchangeable sodium percentage (ESP) above 15, which disperses clay and gives a high pH. They are reclaimed by adding gypsum (calcium) so that Ca2+ replaces Na+ on the exchange complex.

Q 39 | ssg | Soil Properties & Formation
In soil classification systems, topsoil is known as the:
(1) O Horizon or A Horizon
(2) B Horizon
(3) C Horizon
(4) R Horizon
= 1
~ The topsoil is the uppermost layer, comprising the O horizon (organic matter) and the A horizon (mineral soil mixed with humus). The B horizon is the subsoil of accumulation, C is weathered parent material, and R is the underlying bedrock.

Q 40 | ssg | Soil Properties & Formation
_________ are the soils of grassland ecosystems.
(1) Oxisols
(2) Mollisols
(3) Ultisols
(4) Andisols
= 2
~ Mollisols (USDA Soil Taxonomy) are the dark, base-rich, humus-rich soils formed under grasslands such as the prairies and steppes, and are very fertile. Oxisols and Ultisols are highly weathered tropical and subtropical soils, and Andisols form from volcanic ash.

Q 41 | ssg | Soil Properties & Formation
Soil separates are the size groups of mineral particles that are:
(1) between 3 mm and 4 mm in diameter
(2) between 4 mm and 5 mm in diameter
(3) between 5 mm and 6 mm in diameter
(4) less than 2 mm in diameter
= 4
~ Soil separates (sand, silt and clay) are the size classes of mineral particles in the fine earth fraction, which is less than 2 mm in diameter. Particles larger than 2 mm are gravel and stones, which are not counted as soil separates.

Q 42 | ssg | Soil Properties & Formation
How many factors and their interactions are responsible for the formation of soil?
(1) 3
(2) 4
(3) 5
(4) 6
= 3
~ Jenny's model of soil formation names five factors: parent material, climate, organisms (biota), topography (relief) and time. Their interaction determines the soil profile that develops.

Q 43 | ssg | Soil Properties & Formation
Identify the correct sequence of six levels of classification proposed by the USDA soil taxonomy.
(1) Order → Suborder → Great Group → Subgroup → Family → Series
(2) Order → Suborder → Great Group → Subgroup → Series → Family
(3) Great Group → Subgroup → Order → Suborder → Family → Series
(4) Great Group → Subgroup → Order → Suborder → Series → Family
= 1
~ USDA Soil Taxonomy is hierarchical, running from the broadest category to the most specific: Order, Suborder, Great Group, Subgroup, Family and Series. Option 2 swaps the last two levels, and options 3 and 4 put Great Group and Subgroup ahead of Order and Suborder.

Q 44 | ssg | Soil Properties & Formation
______ measure the amount of tension or pull plant roots are exerting on the soil water and how tightly water is held by the soil.
(1) Time domain reflectometers
(2) Neutron probes
(3) Piezometer
(4) Tensiometers
= 4
~ A tensiometer is a water-filled tube with a porous ceramic cup and a vacuum gauge, and measures soil water tension (matric potential), that is, how tightly the soil holds water against the roots. Time domain reflectometers and neutron probes measure soil water content, and a piezometer measures water pressure in the saturated zone.

Q 45 | fse | Survey & Engineering
If a compass reading is 45°, in which direction are you looking at?
(1) North
(2) East
(3) South
(4) North-east
= 4
~ On a compass, bearings are measured clockwise from north: 0° is north, 90° east, 180° south and 270° west. A reading of 45° lies halfway between north and east, which is north-east.

Q 46 | ssg | Rocks & Minerals
The study of rock layers preserved in the geological record is called:
(1) Geochronology
(2) Stratigraphy
(3) Sedimentology
(4) Palaeontology
= 2
~ Stratigraphy is the branch of geology that studies rock layers (strata) and layering, including their order, correlation and age. Geochronology deals with dating rocks, sedimentology with sediments and sedimentary processes, and palaeontology with fossils.

Q 47 | ssg | Rocks & Minerals
What kind of unconformity occurs when the beds beneath an erosional surface are tilted and eroded?
(1) Paraconformities
(2) Non-conformities
(3) Angular unconformities
(4) Disconformities
= 3
~ An angular unconformity separates tilted, eroded older strata from younger, roughly horizontal strata above, so the two sets of beds meet at an angle (Hutton's Siccar Point). In a disconformity and a paraconformity the beds above and below are parallel, and in a nonconformity sediments overlie igneous or metamorphic rock.

Q 48 | ssg | Rocks & Minerals
Who proposed the Law of Fossil Assemblages?
(1) Nicolas Steno
(2) William Smith
(3) James Hutton
(4) Charles Lyell
= 2
~ William Smith, the English surveyor known as the father of English geology, observed that each rock layer contains a characteristic assemblage of fossils, so the strata can be identified and correlated by their fossils (the principle of faunal succession). Steno gave the laws of superposition and original horizontality, and Hutton and Lyell are associated with uniformitarianism.

Q 49 | fse | Survey & Engineering
What does 1:100000 scale map denotes?
(1) 1 cm on the map equals 1 km on the ground
(2) 10 cm on the map equals 1 km on the ground
(3) 1 cm on the map equals 10 km on the ground
(4) 1 cm on the map equals 100 km on the ground
= 1
~ A representative fraction of 1:100000 means 1 cm on the map represents 100000 cm on the ground. Since 100000 cm equals 1000 m, that is 1 km, so 1 cm = 1 km.

Q 50 | fse | Survey & Engineering
When two or more contour lines merge with each other, they represent features of vertical slopes such as
(1) Cliff
(2) Spur
(3) Valley
(4) Conical Hill
= 1
~ Contour lines join only where the ground is vertical or overhanging, so merging contours indicate a cliff. Spurs and valleys appear as V-shaped or U-shaped contours that stay separate, and a conical hill appears as roughly concentric closed contours.

Q 51 | ssg | Rocks & Minerals
Thrust fault is a fault in which ______.
(1) the hanging wall appears to have moved downwards relative to the footwall
(2) the hanging wall appears to have moved upwards relative to the footwall
(3) the fault plane is dipping at low angles (< 45)
(4) the movement is horizontal along the strike of the fault plane
= 3
~ A thrust fault is a reverse fault in which the fault plane dips at a low angle, generally less than 45 degrees, so older rocks are pushed over younger ones. Option 1 describes a normal fault and option 4 a strike-slip fault. Option 2 describes reverse faults in general (steep or shallow), so it does not single out a thrust. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 52 | ssg | Rocks & Minerals
Columnar joints are joints formed in:
(1) basalt
(2) mud
(3) sand
(4) granitic rocks
= 1,4
~ Columnar joints are the polygonal, usually hexagonal, prismatic columns formed when a lava flow or shallow igneous body contracts on cooling, as in the basalts of the Deccan Traps and the Giant's Causeway. Similar columns can also occur in other igneous bodies, including some granitic rocks, so APPSC found ambiguity and accepted both basalt and granitic rocks. Mud and sand form desiccation cracks or bedding, not columnar joints.

Q 53 | wsm | Water Resources
Cholera, a water-borne disease, is spread by Vibrio cholerae, which is a:
(1) nematode
(2) virus
(3) protozoan
(4) bacterium
= 4
~ Vibrio cholerae is a comma-shaped, Gram-negative bacterium that spreads through water or food contaminated with faeces and causes severe watery diarrhoea. Nematodes are worms, and protozoa such as Entamoeba cause amoebic dysentery rather than cholera.

Q 54 | fse | Survey & Engineering
Which of the following types of water distribution system is suitable for cities with rectangular layout and where the water mains and branches are laid in rectangles?
(1) Grid Iron System
(2) Radial System
(3) Dead End System
(4) Ring System
= 1
~ In the grid iron (reticulation) system, the mains, sub-mains and branches are laid in a rectangular grid, which suits towns with a rectangular street plan and gives water circulation from several directions. The dead end system is a tree-like layout, the ring system uses a main loop around the town, and the radial system supplies water outward from a central reservoir.

Q 55 | wsm | Water Resources
What is the safe limit for fluoride in drinking water fixed by the World Health Organization?
(1) 0.5 to 1.5 mg/L
(2) < 0.5 mg/L
(3) > 1.5 mg/L
(4) 0.5 to 1.5 g/L
= 1
~ The WHO guideline value for fluoride in drinking water is 1.5 mg/L, and the beneficial range for dental health is about 0.5 to 1.5 mg/L. Below 0.5 mg/L there is a risk of dental caries, and above 1.5 mg/L there is a risk of dental and skeletal fluorosis. The unit is mg/L, not g/L.

Q 56 | wsm | Water Resources
Water hardness is the amount of dissolved calcium and __________.
(1) magnesium
(2) iron
(3) potassium
(4) sodium
= 1
~ Hardness of water is caused mainly by dissolved calcium and magnesium salts (bicarbonates, chlorides and sulphates), and is expressed as mg/L of CaCO3. Iron, potassium and sodium are not the major hardness-causing ions.

Q 57 | wsm | Water Resources
Which of the following devices measures the pressure of groundwater?
(1) Barometer
(2) Piezometer
(3) Rheometer
(4) Manometer
= 2
~ A piezometer is a tube or well, open only at a measured depth, in which the water level shows the pore water pressure or hydraulic head of groundwater at that point. A barometer measures atmospheric pressure, a rheometer measures the flow behaviour of fluids, and a manometer is a general pressure gauge for fluids in pipes, not a groundwater device.

Q 58 | wsm | Water Resources
In bacteriological water analysis, m-Endo agar media is used in:
(1) multiple tube method
(2) ATP testing
(3) direct plate count
(4) membrane filtration
= 4
~ m-Endo agar (or broth) is the standard medium for detecting total coliform bacteria by the membrane filtration technique, where water is filtered through a 0.45 micron membrane that is then placed on the medium. Coliform colonies form a metallic green sheen. The multiple tube method uses lactose or lauryl tryptose broth instead.

Q 59 | wsm | Watershed & Water Harvesting
The Chauka system is a method for harvesting rainwater practiced in which Indian state?
(1) Rajasthan
(2) Gujrat
(3) Madhya Pradesh
(4) Bihar
= 1
~ The Chauka system is a traditional rainwater harvesting method of Rajasthan, especially the Jaipur region, in which rectangular, bunded pits (chaukas) are dug on sloping land to hold runoff and recharge groundwater.
