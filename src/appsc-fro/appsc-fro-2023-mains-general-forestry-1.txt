Q 1 | pls | Plant Physiology
Which among the micronutrient is essential for plant growth and metabolism.
(1) Potassium
(2) Calcium
(3) Manganese
(4) Magnesium
= 3
~ Manganese is a micronutrient (trace element): plants need it in very small amounts, e.g. for photosynthetic water splitting and enzyme activation. Potassium, calcium and magnesium are macronutrients required in large quantities, so they are the tempting wrong options.

Q 2 | bdc | Biomes & Population Ecology
The forests that predominantly have a limited number of conifer species beside few deciduous genera, and occupy around 17% of the Earth’s land surface in the circumpolar belt of the far Northern Hemisphere with long and very cold winters, are called _______ forests.
(1) semi-evergreen
(2) boreal
(3) cloud
(4) sub-alpine
= 2
~ Boreal forests (taiga) form the broad circumpolar belt south of the tundra in North America, Europe and Asia, dominated by a few conifers such as spruce, fir, pine and larch with some birch, aspen and poplar. Cloud and sub-alpine forests are mountain forests, and semi-evergreen forests are tropical.

Q 3 | ecw | Ecosystem Concepts
Match the following ecological processes that occur in forests.
Process | Organism 1 and Organism 2
(A) Commensalism | (1) Organism 1: +, Organism 2: -
(B) Amensalism | (2) Organism 1: +, Organism 2: +
(C) Predation | (3) Organism 1: +, Organism 2: 0
(D) Mutualism | (4) Organism 1: -, Organism 2: 0
(1) (A-1), (B-2), (C-3) and (D-4)
(2) (A-4), (B-3), (C-2) and (D-1)
(3) (A-1), (B-2), (C-4) and (D-3)
(4) (A-3), (B-4), (C-1) and (D-2)
= 4
~ In commensalism one species benefits and the other is unaffected (+/0). In amensalism one is harmed and the other unaffected (-/0), in predation the predator gains and the prey loses (+/-), and in mutualism both gain (+/+). These give A-3, B-4, C-1, D-2.

Q 4 | ecw | Ecosystem Concepts
Which of the following ecological pyramids is always upright in a forest?
(1) Pyramid of number of individuals
(2) Pyramid of number of species
(3) Pyramid of energy
(4) Pyramid if biomass
= 3
~ Energy is lost as heat at every trophic level (roughly 90% by the ten-per-cent law), so the pyramid of energy is always upright. Pyramids of numbers and of biomass can be inverted in a forest, for example when a single large tree supports thousands of insects.

As per the ‘Global Forest Resource Assessment’ (GFRA) report 2020 given by FAO, the top two countries with respect to forest area in the world are:
(1) Russia and Brazil
(2) Russia and Canada
(3) Canada and USA
(4) USA and Brazil
= 1

Q 6 | ecw | Ecosystem Concepts
Match the following authors and succession theories
Author | Theory
(A) Clements | (1) Polyclimax theory
(B) Tansley | (2) Climax pattern and vegetation gradation theory
(C) Aubreville | (3) Mosaic theory
(D) Whittaker | (4) Climatic climax theory
(1) (A-3), (B-2), (C-1), (D-4)
(2) (A-1), (B-2), (C-3), (D-4)
(3) (A-4), (B-1), (C-3), (D-2)

Q 7 | ecw | Ecosystem Concepts
‘Ecesis’ is process of succession that represents:
(1) migration of plants and animals to a new habitat
(2) reaction of plants and animals to a new habitat
(3) plants and animals refuse the new habitat
(4) establishment of plants and animals in a new habitat
= 4
~ In a succession (sere), ecesis is the stage in which migrated seeds or propagules germinate, grow and become established in the new habitat. Migration is the earlier stage of arrival, and reaction is the later stage in which the established species modify the habitat.

Q 8 | nrs | Forest & Water Resources
All trees present outside the recorded forest area (RFA) irrespective of their number and patch size, are termed as:
(1) forest cover
(2) trees outside forest
(3) tree cover
(4) agroforestry
= 2
~ The India State of Forest Report defines Trees Outside Forest (TOF) as all trees growing outside the recorded forest area, whatever the patch size or number. Tree cover is only the part of TOF occurring in patches smaller than 1 ha, and forest cover refers to tree-covered land of more than 1 ha with canopy density above 10%.

Q 9 | slv | Silvicultural Principles & Regeneration
A forest plant grown from seed, that attains a height of around six inches and has a few leaves, is called a _______.
(1) tree
(2) sapling
(3) seedling
(4) pole
= 3
~ A seedling is a young plant raised from seed in its earliest stage, with only a few leaves and a height of a few inches. A sapling is a larger young tree that has passed the seedling stage, and a pole is a still older young tree before it matures.

Q 10 | slv | Silvicultural Principles & Regeneration
In Pinus roxburghii forests, natural regeneration is encouraged by following ________ system.
(1) high forest
(2) clear-felling
(3) coppice
(4) Punjab shelterwood
= 4
~ The Punjab shelterwood system, a modified uniform system with regeneration fellings in a long regeneration period, was developed for chir pine (Pinus roxburghii) forests of the Himalayan foothills. Chir pine does not coppice, so the coppice system is unsuitable.

Q 12 | slv | Silvicultural Principles & Regeneration
The forest crop is renewed by which of the following?
A) Natural regeneration
B) Afforestation
C) Agroforestry
D) Reforestation
(1) A, C, and D
(2) B, C, and D
(3) A, B, and D
(4) A, B, C, and D
= 3
~ A forest crop is renewed either naturally (from seed, coppice or root suckers) or artificially by afforestation and reforestation (planting or sowing). Agroforestry is a land-use system that combines trees with crops or livestock and is not a method of renewing a forest crop.

Q 13 | slv | Silviculture of Species
Bamboo forests are regenerated by adopting a felling cycle of:
(1) 6-9 years
(2) 3-4 years
(3) 10-12 years
(4) 15-16 years
= 2
~ Bamboo clumps are worked on a short felling cycle, commonly 3 to 4 years, in which the mature culms are cut and the young culms are left so that the clump keeps regenerating. The longer cycles in the other options suit timber trees, not bamboo.

Q 14 | slv | Silvicultural Principles & Regeneration
Which of the following are tending operations often done in a forest?
A) Weeding
B) Improvement felling
C) Clear felling
D) Girdling
(1) C and D
(2) A, B, and C
(3) B, C, and D
(4) A and B
= 4
~ Tending operations are treatments given to a crop between regeneration and final harvest, such as weeding, cleaning, thinning, pruning and improvement felling. Clear felling is a regeneration (final) felling system, not a tending operation, so any option containing C is wrong. The key counts only weeding and improvement felling; girdling is a technique often used within improvement felling rather than a separate tending operation. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 15 | slv | Silvicultural Principles & Regeneration
Tending operations and maintenance of crops in a forest is carried out as per the procedure laid out in its working plan, which aims for:
(1) regular yield
(2) progressive yield
(3) sustained yield
(4) productive yield
= 3
~ The central objective of a working plan is sustained yield, that is, a continuous supply of produce of a similar kind and quantity from the forest without impairing its productive capacity. Regular, progressive and productive yield are not the standard terms for this aim.

Q 16 | slv | Silvicultural Principles & Regeneration
Select the correct statements with respect to weed control measures in forests.
A) Prevent the spread of weed seed by human activities, by keeping agricultural and forest machinery and objects clean.
B) Combat the spreading of weeds by using basic treatment such as ploughing, disking, tilling, etc.
C) Remove weeds by using flame and superheated steam.
D) Use any soils and sowing of any multi-crop seeds.
(1) Statements A, B and D are correct
(2) Statements A, D and C are correct
(3) Statements A, B and C are correct
(4) Statements B, C and D are correct
= 3
~ Weed control combines preventive measures (cleaning machinery so weed seed is not carried), mechanical methods (ploughing, disking, tilling) and thermal methods (flaming, superheated steam). Using any soil or any seed mixture, as in D, would carry weed seeds into the site, so it is not a control measure.

Q 17 | fpt | Pests & Diseases
In South India which insect is used to control Opuntia spp, a weed, in thorn forests?
(1) Orthezia insignis
(2) Cosmopterix pulchrimella
(3) Dactylopius coccus
(4) Estigmena chinensis
= 3
~ Prickly pear (Opuntia) is controlled biologically by cochineal scale insects of the genus Dactylopius, the classic Indian examples being D. ceylonicus and D. opuntiae; D. coccus is the species keyed here. Orthezia insignis and Cosmopterix pulchrimella are used against Lantana, and Estigmena chinensis is a bamboo pest. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 18 | slv | Silvicultural Principles & Regeneration
The interval in years between successive thinning are called _______.
(1) thinning process
(2) thinning intensity
(3) free thinning
(4) thinning cycle
= 4
~ The thinning cycle (thinning interval) is the number of years between two successive thinnings in the same stand. Thinning intensity is the amount of material removed at a thinning, not the time between them.

Q 19 | slv | Agroforestry
Taungya cultivation originated in ______.
(1) America
(2) India
(3) Burma
(4) Africa
= 3
~ The word taungya is Burmese (taung = hill, ya = cultivation). The system of raising forest plantations with farmers' food crops began in Burma in 1856 under Dietrich Brandis and was then adopted in India and elsewhere.

Q 20 | slv | Agroforestry
Which of the following is NOT the basis of classification of agroforestry systems as given by Nair (1987)?
(1) History of the system (land use in the past)
(2) The structure of the system (composition and arrangement of components)
(3) Function of the system (role and output of components)
(4) Ecological spread (ecological zone where system exists)
= 1
~ Nair's classification groups agroforestry systems by structure, function, socio-economic level of management and ecological spread. The past land-use history of a site is not one of these bases.

Q 21 | slv | Agroforestry
Alley cropping is also called _____.
(1) tree-grain cropping
(2) hedge-row intercropping
(3) relay cropping
(4) shifting cultivation
= 2
~ In alley cropping, food crops are grown in the alleys between hedgerows of fast-growing, usually leguminous trees such as Leucaena or Gliricidia, which are pruned to give mulch, green manure and fodder. Hence it is also called hedge-row intercropping. Relay cropping is a sequential cropping system of annual crops.

Basis of Classification | Principle
(A) Structural | (1) Level of input management (high or low inputs), commercial goals and, intensity and scale of management (subsistence, commercial etc.)

Q 29 | slv | Agroforestry
In an agroforestry system, soil productivity is enhanced due to:
A) Decreased soil organic matter
B) Addition of Nitrogen in the soil if leguminous plants are grown
C) Maintain soil organic matter
D) Promote more open nutrient cycling.
(1) A and B
(2) A and C
(3) B and D
(4) B and C
= 4
~ Trees in agroforestry maintain soil organic matter through litter fall and root turnover, and nitrogen-fixing legumes add nitrogen. Decreased organic matter (A) would reduce productivity, and agroforestry promotes closed or tighter nutrient cycling, not open cycling with losses (D).

Q 30 | slv | Agroforestry
Which of the following trees does NOT enhance nitrogen in soil, and thus soil productivity being a non-nitrogen fixing species?
(1) Caragana
(2) Luecaena
(3) Populus
(4) Faidherbia
= 3
~ Populus (poplar) is a non-leguminous tree that does not fix nitrogen. Caragana, Leucaena and Faidherbia (Acacia) albida are leguminous trees that fix atmospheric nitrogen through root nodules.

Q 31 | slv | Agroforestry
In an agroforestry system, soil health is maintained by which of the following?
A) Decreasing biological components of soil
B) Increasing or stabilising physical components of soil
C) Improve chemical components in the soil in favour of plants
(1) Only A
(2) Only B and C
(3) Only B
(4) Only A and C
= 2
~ Agroforestry improves soil health by improving or stabilising its physical properties (structure, porosity, infiltration) and its chemical properties (nutrient status, organic matter). It increases, not decreases, the biological components such as soil microbes and fauna, so A is wrong.

Q 32 | slv | Agroforestry

(3) B and C
(4) A and B

~ Trees improve nutrient availability and fertility (A) and raise soil organic carbon through litter and roots (C). Agroforestry stimulates rather than suppresses soil microbial activity, so B is wrong.

Q 36 | fel | Forest Policy
Which of the following is a/are basic objective(s) of National Forest Policy 1988?
A) Checking soil erosion and denudation in the catchment areas of rivers, lakes, reservoirs.
B) Increasing area under agriculture to mitigate food and oil shortage.
C) Meeting the requirements of fuel-wood, fodder, minor forest produce and small timber of the rural and tribal populations
D) Ignoring the productivity of forests to meet essential national needs.
(1) A and B
(2) A and C
(3) Only A
(4) Only D
= 2
~ The National Forest Policy 1988 gives priority to ecological stability, including checking soil erosion in catchments, and to meeting the fuelwood, fodder, minor forest produce and small timber needs of rural and tribal people. It does not aim to expand agriculture at the cost of forests (B) and calls for raising forest productivity, not ignoring it (D).

Elaeis guineensis is commonly known as:
(1) Coconut
(2) Areca nut
(3) African oil palm
(4) Cacao
= 3
~ Elaeis guineensis is the African oil palm, native to West Africa and the source of palm oil. Coconut is Cocos nucifera, areca nut is Areca catechu, and cacao is Theobroma cacao.
