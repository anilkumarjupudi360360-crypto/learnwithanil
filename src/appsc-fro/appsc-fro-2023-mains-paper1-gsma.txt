Q 1 | men | Reasoning
Seven people named P, Q, R, S, T, U and V are sitting around a circular table facing the centre. Q is second to the left of P. R is third to the right of Q. U is second to the left of T. V is third to the left of U. T is to the immediate right of S. Who is sitting third to left of V?
(1) T
(2) S
(3) R
(4) Q
= 2
~ Fix P at seat 0 and count each place to P's left as +1 (everyone faces the centre). Then Q = +2 and R = Q − 3 = −1, i.e. immediately right of P. T is just right of S, U = T + 2 and V = U + 3; the only fit in the free seats (+1, +3, +4, +5) is T = +3, S = +4, U = +5, V = +1. Third to the left of V is seat +4, which is S.

Q 2 | men | Reasoning
Each of K, L, M, N, O, P and S has an exam on a different day of a week starting from Monday and ending on Sunday of the same week. Only three people have exams before P. Only two people have exams between the exams of K and P. Only N has exam after L. K’s exam is immediately before S. O’s exam is on Friday. On which day is M’s exam?
(1) Monday
(2) Tuesday
(3) Wednesday
(4) Saturday
= 3
~ Three exams come before P, so P is on Thursday. Two exams lie between K and P, so K is on Monday or Sunday; S comes right after K, so K = Monday and S = Tuesday. Only N comes after L, so L = Saturday and N = Sunday, and O is on Friday. The only day left for M is Wednesday.

Q 3 | men | Reasoning
Eight people named A, B, C, D, E, F, G and H live on different floors of a building. The ground floor is number 1, the floor above it number 2 and so on till the top floor is number 8. E lives on floor 3. C lives on the floor immediately above H’s floor. B lives on the floor immediately above G’s floor. F lives on some floor below E’s floor. Only four people live above D’s floor. C does not live on floor 8. Only one person lives between the floors of D and A. Which of the following is a correct match of person and floor?
(1) B : Floor 7
(2) H : Floor 6
(3) A : Floor 5
(4) F : Floor 1
= 4
~ Four people live above D, so D is on floor 4, and A (one person in between) is on floor 2 or 6. F is below E (floor 3), so on floor 1 or 2. If A = 2, then F = 1 and floors 5–8 hold the pairs H–C and G–B; since C is not on 8, H = 5, C = 6, G = 7, B = 8. A = 6 leaves no room for two adjacent pairs, so only 'F : Floor 1' matches.

Q 4 | men | Reasoning
Read the given statements and conclusions carefully. Assuming that the information given in the statements is true, even if it appears to be at variance with commonly known facts, decide which of the given conclusions logically follow(s) from the statement(s).
Statements:
All pillows are blankets.
All blankets are beds.
Conclusions:

II. Some beds are blankets.

(2) Only conclusion II follows
(3) Both conclusions I and II follow
(4) Neither conclusion I nor II follow

Q 5 | men | Reasoning
Read the given statements and conclusions carefully. Assuming that the information given in the statements is true, even if it appears to be at variance with commonly known facts, decide which of the given conclusions logically follow(s) from the statement(s).
Statements:
All almonds are bananas.
All cashews are bananas.
All bananas are raspberries.
Conclusions:
I. All almonds are raspberries.
II. Some raspberries are cashews.
III. No cashew is a raspberry
(1) Only conclusion I follows
(2) Only conclusion II follows
(3) Conclusions I and II follow
(4) Conclusions I and III follow
= 3
~ Almonds lie inside bananas, which lie inside raspberries, so all almonds are raspberries (I follows). Cashews are also inside bananas and hence inside raspberries, so some raspberries are cashews (II follows), while 'No cashew is a raspberry' (III) is false. So conclusions I and II follow.

Q 6 | men | Reasoning

(2) 614181214

Q 7 | men | Reasoning
If H < F < T > R > L and S > T = M > Y < G, then which of the following is NOT definitely correct?
(1) L < T
(2) S > R
(3) T > G
(4) L < S
= 3
~ T > R > L gives L < T, and S > T > R gives S > R, so options 1 and 2 are definitely true; S > T > R > L also gives L < S. But T and G are linked only through Y (T = M > Y < G), so their relation cannot be fixed. Hence 'T > G' is not definitely correct.

In the letter clusters given below, if all the vowels are moved one letter forward and all consonants are moved one letter backward as per the English alphabet and then all the new letter clusters thus formed are arranged in the alphabetical order from left to right on the basis of their first letter, then which letter cluster will be second from the right end in the new arrangement?

Refer to the following number letter series and answer the question that follows.
(Left) F 2 B E 7 9 N 2 M A 3 L 5 4 P E 7 R 8 U 9 W 6 A D 7 5 A 8 W 4 L E H 5 T 3 U P 4 C 2 E 6 E S 7 A Y 8 U 9 N 3 E 3 L 2 5 A 7 9 M A 4 E 6 L (Right)

Select the option that is related to the fifth letter cluster in the same way as the first letter cluster is related to the second letter cluster and the third letter cluster is related to the fourth letter cluster.

Q 13 | men | Reasoning
Three of the following four letter-clusters are alike in a certain manner and one is different. Select the one that is different.
(1) JNSYF
(2) TXCIP
(3) CGLRY

= 4

Q 14 | men | Reasoning
Select the number from among the given options that will replace the question mark (?) in the following series.
19, 32, 46, 64, 91, ?
(1) 130
(2) 134
(3) 142
(4) 155
= 2
~ The differences are 13, 14, 18 and 27, and these themselves increase by 1, 4 and 9, i.e. 1², 2², 3². The next increase is 4² = 16, so the next difference is 27 + 16 = 43. The missing number is 91 + 43 = 134.

Q 15 | men | Reasoning
In a certain code language, if' ‘&’ means 'addition', '#' means 'multiplication', '@' means 'division' and '%' means 'subtraction', then find the value of
57 @ (27 % 8) & 5 # (74@37) % (125%25) @ 4 & 40.
(1) 15

(1) 1545 AD
(2) 1555 AD
(3) 1565 AD
(4) 1560 AD
= 3
~ In the Battle of Talikota (Rakkasa–Tangadi), fought on 23 January 1565, the combined armies of the Deccan Sultanates of Bijapur, Ahmadnagar, Golconda and Bidar defeated the Vijayanagara forces led by Aliya Rama Raya, who was killed.
