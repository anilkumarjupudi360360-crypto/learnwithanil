Q 1 | ecw | Ecosystem Concepts
Select the correct option based on the following assertion (A) and reason (R).
Assertion (A): The pyramid of energy in an ecosystem is always upright.
Reason (R): The flow of energy from one trophic level to the next is unidirectional.
(1) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(2) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(3) A is true, but R is false.
(4) Both A and R are false.
= 2
~ The pyramid of energy is always upright because a large part of the energy (roughly 90%) is lost as heat in respiration at each trophic transfer, so every higher level receives less. Energy flow is indeed unidirectional (it cannot be recycled), so R is true, but the narrowing of the pyramid is explained by the loss at each step, not by the direction of flow alone.

Q 2 | ecw | Ecosystem Concepts
In the forest ecosystem, maximum biomass accumulates at which trophic level?
(1) Producers
(2) Herbivores
(3) Primary consumers
(4) Secondary consumers
= 1
~ In a forest the producers (trees) form the first trophic level and hold the largest standing biomass, because only a fraction of the energy and matter is passed to each higher level. The pyramid of biomass in a forest is therefore upright, with producers at the base. Herbivores and carnivores carry far less biomass.

Q 3 | ecw | Types of Ecosystems
The anthropogenic activity that poses a threat to grassland ecosystems is:
(1) planting more trees to increase biodiversity
(2) promoting controlled grazing by herbivores
(3) overgrazing by livestock
(4) implementing conservation measures
= 3
~ Overgrazing by livestock removes the vegetative cover faster than it can regrow, compacts and erodes the soil, and lets unpalatable weeds replace good grasses, which degrades the grassland. Controlled grazing and conservation measures are management tools that help grasslands, and planting trees is not a typical threat in the sense asked here.

Q 4 | ecw | Types of Ecosystems
What is the primary factor responsible for the formation of grasslands?
(1) High annual precipitation
(2) Rich soil with high organic content
(3) Extreme temperature fluctuations
(4) Insufficient rainfall to support tree growth
= 4
~ Grasslands occur where rainfall is too low and too erratic (typically about 250-750 mm a year) for forests to establish, but high enough to prevent desert. High annual rainfall favours forests, so option 1 is wrong, and soil richness is a result of grassland rather than its cause.

Q 5 | ecw | Wildlife & Protected Areas
Which of the following national parks is a UNESCO World Heritage Site?
(1) Jim Corbett National Park
(2) Keoladeo National Park
(3) Namdapha National Park
(4) Bhitarkanika National Park
= 2
~ Keoladeo National Park (Bharatpur, Rajasthan) was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1985 for its wetland bird habitat. Jim Corbett, Namdapha and Bhitarkanika are important protected areas but are not inscribed World Heritage Sites (Bhitarkanika is a Ramsar site).

Q 6 | ecw | Wildlife & Protected Areas
Which sanctuary/national park is known for its population of the Great Indian bustard?
(1) Dachigam Wildlife Sanctuary
(2) Mudumalai Wildlife Sanctuary
(3) Desert National Park
(4) Ranthambore National Park
= 3
~ Desert National Park near Jaisalmer, Rajasthan, is the main stronghold of the critically endangered Great Indian Bustard (Ardeotis nigriceps), which prefers open arid grassland. Dachigam is known for the hangul, Mudumalai for elephants and tigers, and Ranthambore for tigers.

Q 7 | ecw | Wildlife & Protected Areas
Which of the following is the oldest national park in India?
(1) Bandipur National Park
(2) Kaziranga National Park
(3) Jim Corbett National Park
(4) Kanha National Park
= 3
~ Jim Corbett National Park in Uttarakhand was established in 1936 as Hailey National Park and is India's oldest national park. Kanha became a national park in 1955, while Kaziranga and Bandipur were notified as national parks in 1974.

Q 8 | ecw | Ecosystem Concepts
The ultradian rhythm refers to a cycle that lasts:
(1) less than 24 hours
(2) exactly 24 hours
(3) more than 24 hours
(4) varies in duration
= 1
~ Ultradian rhythms are biological cycles shorter than 24 hours and so repeat more than once a day, for example the heartbeat, breathing or the roughly 90-minute sleep cycle. A cycle of about 24 hours is circadian and one longer than 24 hours is infradian.

Q 9 | ecw | Ecosystem Concepts
The term ‘entrainment’ refers to the process of:
(1) synchronisation of biological rhythms to external cues
(2) disruption of biological rhythms due to jet lag
(3) altering the duration of the sleep-wake cycle
(4) regulating hormonal fluctuations during puberty
= 1
~ Entrainment is the synchronisation of an organism's internal biological clock to external time cues (zeitgebers) such as the light-dark cycle. Jet lag is the opposite situation, a temporary mismatch between the internal clock and the new local time.

Q 10 | ecw | Ecosystem Concepts
The menstrual cycle in monkeys is an example of:
(1) ultradian rhythm
(2) circadian rhythm
(3) infradian rhythm
(4) diurnal rhythm
= 3
~ The menstrual cycle of monkeys lasts about a month, much longer than 24 hours, so it is an infradian rhythm. Circadian rhythms last about 24 hours and ultradian rhythms are shorter than a day, while 'diurnal' describes day-time activity rather than a rhythm type.

Q 11 | ecw | Ecosystem Concepts
The term ‘free-running’ refers to a biological rhythm that:
(1) is unaffected by external cues
(2) is only present in animals
(3) oscillates at a fixed frequency
(4) is synchronised with the lunar cycle
= 1
~ A free-running rhythm is one that continues under constant conditions (for example constant darkness) with no external time cues, so it runs on the internal clock alone. Its period is usually slightly different from exactly 24 hours. Option 2 is wrong because free-running rhythms occur in plants and other organisms too, and option 3 misses the defining feature, which is the absence of external time cues rather than a fixed frequency.

Q 12 | ecw | Wildlife & Protected Areas
Which of the following is a critically endangered species of Indian vulture?
(1) Gyps bengalensis
(2) Gyps himalayensis
(3) Gyps indicus
(4) Neophron percnopterus
= 1
~ Gyps bengalensis (white-rumped vulture) is Critically Endangered on the IUCN Red List after a population crash of over 95% caused mainly by diclofenac poisoning. The Himalayan griffon (G. himalayensis) is Near Threatened and the Egyptian vulture (Neophron percnopterus) is Endangered. The Indian vulture (G. indicus) is also listed as Critically Endangered, so the question has more than one defensible answer. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 13 | ecw | Wildlife & Protected Areas
Which of the following is listed as vulnerable species of turtle found in the rivers of India?
(1) Lepidochelys olivacea
(2) Lissemys punctata
(3) Dermochelys coriacea
(4) Eretmochelys imbricata
= 2
~ Lissemys punctata, the Indian flapshell turtle, is a freshwater turtle of rivers, ponds and lakes and is listed as Vulnerable by the IUCN. The other three (olive ridley, leatherback and hawksbill) are sea turtles that nest on coasts rather than live in rivers.

Q 14 | ecw | Ecosystem Concepts
The ecosystem which has high carbon use efficiency will also have high:
(1) NPP to GPP ratio
(2) GPP to NPP ratio
(3) Respiration to GPP ratio
(4) GPP to biomass ratio
= 1
~ Carbon use efficiency is the fraction of the carbon fixed in photosynthesis (GPP) that remains as new biomass after plant respiration, that is CUE = NPP/GPP. A high CUE therefore means a high NPP to GPP ratio, and necessarily a low respiration to GPP ratio.

Q 15 | ecw | Ecosystem Concepts
Which of the following terrestrial ecosystems has highest GPP?
(1) Tropical savannah and grasslands
(2) Croplands
(3) Tropical forest
(4) Deserts
= 3
~ Tropical forests have the highest gross primary productivity among land ecosystems because warmth, abundant water and a large leaf area allow year-round photosynthesis; they account for roughly a third of global terrestrial GPP. Savannas, croplands and deserts have lower productivity per unit area.

Q 16 | fpt | Pests & Diseases
Which insect is known to attack and damage the bark of trees, leading to tree decline?
(1) Leaf miners
(2) Sawflies
(3) Bark beetles
(4) Fire ants
= 3
~ Bark beetles (Scolytinae, such as Ips and Dendroctonus) bore through the bark and feed on the phloem and cambium, girdling the tree and introducing fungi that lead to decline and death. Leaf miners and sawflies are foliage feeders, and fire ants are not bark pests.

Q 17 | fpt | Forest Fire
Which of the following is a natural method of preventing forest fires?
(1) Controlled burning
(2) Smokey bear campaigns
(3) Fire-fighting helicopters
(4) Fire-resistant tree species
= 1
~ Controlled (prescribed) burning imitates natural low-intensity fires and removes accumulated litter and dry fuel, so that a later accidental fire is less intense and spreads less. Smokey Bear campaigns are awareness efforts and helicopters are a suppression tool. Fire-resistant species could also be called natural, so the wording is open to debate. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 18 | fpt | Forest Fire
What is the most effective way to prevent human-caused forest fires?
(1) Enforcing strict penalties for arson
(2) Promoting fire safety education
(3) Increasing forest patrols
(4) Planting fire-resistant tree species
= 2
~ Most forest fires are started by people through carelessness, for example by campfires, cigarettes or clearing, so educating communities about fire safety removes the cause at its source. Penalties and patrols are enforcement measures that act only after or around the act, and fire-resistant species reduce damage but do not stop ignition.

Q 19 | fpt | Forest Fire
Which layer of the forest typically experiences the most intense burning during a fire?
(1) Canopy
(2) Understory
(3) Tree roots
(4) Forest floor
= 1
~ A crown fire, which burns through the canopy, is the most intense and destructive type of forest fire; it spreads rapidly, driven by wind, and is very hard to control. Ground fires smoulder in humus and roots, while surface fires burn litter and understory on the forest floor with lower intensity.

Q 20 | slv | Silvicultural Principles & Regeneration
What is the main objective of afforestation?
(1) Increase urban green spaces
(2) Improve soil fertility
(3) Restore and expand forest cover
(4) Provide timber for commercial purposes
= 3
~ Afforestation is the establishment of forest by planting or seeding on land that was not forest before (or has long lacked tree cover), so its main aim is to expand forest cover. Soil improvement, timber and urban greenery can be side benefits but are not its central objective.

Q 21 | slv | Silvicultural Principles & Regeneration
Afforestation is a long-term solution to:
(1) urbanisation
(2) desertification
(3) overfishing
(4) industrialisation
= 2
~ Desertification is land degradation in arid and semi-arid areas, and tree cover helps by binding soil, checking wind erosion, adding organic matter and improving the micro-climate (for example shelterbelts and green belts). Afforestation does not address urbanisation, overfishing or industrialisation.

Q 22 | slv | Nursery & Planting
Which type of trees is commonly planted in afforestation projects?
(1) Exotic and invasive species
(2) Endangered and rare species
(3) Native and indigenous species
(4) Fruit-bearing trees
= 3
~ Native (indigenous) species are the preferred choice because they are adapted to local climate and soil, support local wildlife and carry little risk of becoming invasive. Exotic and invasive species can damage the ecosystem, and rare species are planted only in special conservation programmes.

Q 23 | fpt | Grazing & Human Impacts
What is the primary benefit of rotational grazing?
(1) Increased forage production
(2) Increased animal reproduction
(3) Reduced infectious disease in animals
(4) Improved soil texture
= 1
~ In rotational grazing animals are moved between paddocks so that each pasture gets a rest period to regrow, which raises forage yield and quality over the season. Lower parasite load and better soil cover are secondary benefits.

Q 24 | fpt | Grazing & Human Impacts
The primary purpose of controlled grazing is to:
(1) improve livestock socialisation
(2) maximise forage utilisation
(3) minimise the need for feed supplement
(4) control invasive plant species
= 2
~ Controlled grazing regulates the number of animals and the timing and duration of grazing so that the available forage is used efficiently without damaging the pasture. Reduced supplementary feeding or weed control may follow, but the primary purpose is making the best use of forage.

Q 25 | fpt | Grazing & Human Impacts
Which of the following statements is true about rotational grazing?
(1) It requires large amount of land.
(2) It leads to overgrazing of certain areas.
(3) It can help control parasites in livestock.
(4) It is not suitable for maximising forage utilisation.
= 3
~ Moving animals from paddock to paddock breaks the life cycle of many internal parasites, because larvae on a resting pasture die before animals return, so parasite load in livestock falls. Rotational grazing is in fact designed to prevent patchy overgrazing and to improve forage utilisation, and it does not necessarily need more land.

Q 26 | nrs | Forest & Water Resources
The term ‘forest degradation’ refers to:
(1) the process of natural forest recovery after disturbance
(2) the reduction in forest area due to urbanisation
(3) the decline in forest health and productivity over time
(4) the intentional planting of fast-growing tree species
= 3
~ Forest degradation is the gradual reduction in a forest's density, structure, species composition and productivity, so that it supplies fewer goods and services while the land remains forest. Loss of forest area to urban use (option 2) is deforestation, and natural recovery is regeneration.

Q 27 | bdc | Biodiversity & Hotspots
The fragmentation of forest ecosystems due to human activities can lead to:
(1) increased species diversity
(2) enhanced gene flow between fragmented areas
(3) decreased habitat quality and loss of biodiversity
(4) improved ecosystem resilience and stability
= 3
~ Fragmentation splits continuous forest into small isolated patches, which increases edge effects, shrinks the interior habitat and isolates populations, causing inbreeding and local extinctions. Gene flow, species diversity and resilience therefore fall rather than rise.

Q 28 | fut | Logging & Wood Technology
The term ‘logging' refers to:
(1) planting new trees in a deforested area
(2) removing dead trees from a forest
(3) cutting down trees for commercial purposes
(4) using trees for landscaping in urban areas
= 3
~ Logging is the felling of trees and their conversion and removal from the forest, mainly for timber and other commercial use. Planting trees on cleared land is reforestation, and removal of dead trees is salvage or sanitation felling.

Q 29 | ens | Climate & Global Issues
What is the process by which forests help in carbon sequestration?
(1) Photosynthesis
(2) Respiration
(3) Combustion
(4) Decomposition
= 1
~ In photosynthesis trees absorb atmospheric carbon dioxide and fix it as sugars and then as wood, leaves and roots, thereby sequestering carbon. Respiration, combustion and decomposition all release carbon dioxide back to the atmosphere.

Q 30 | ens | Climate & Global Issues
Which type of forest tends to have the highest carbon sequestration potential?
(1) Young and rapidly growing forests
(2) Mature and stable forests
(3) Deforested areas
(4) Plantations with single-species trees
= 2
~ Mature forests hold the largest carbon stocks, in big trees, deadwood and soil, and keep it stored for a long time. Young forests take up carbon faster per year but hold much less in total, so some sources would choose option 1 if the question means rate of uptake. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 31 | ens | Climate & Global Issues
Which global initiative aims to reduce carbon emissions by incentivising forest conservation and restoration?
(1) Kyoto Protocol
(2) Paris Agreement
(3) Clean Development Mechanism
(4) REDD+
= 4
~ REDD+ (Reducing Emissions from Deforestation and forest Degradation, plus conservation, sustainable management and enhancement of forest carbon stocks) is the UNFCCC framework that gives results-based payments to developing countries for protecting and restoring forests. The Kyoto Protocol, Paris Agreement and CDM are broader climate instruments.

Q 32 | ens | Climate & Global Issues
Which is the most effective long-term strategy for carbon sequestration?
(1) Planting trees on a small scale
(2) Implementing large-scale reforestation projects
(3) Focusing on technological advancements
(4) Reducing overall carbon emissions
= 4
~ APPSC treats cutting emissions at the source as the most effective long-term strategy, because sequestration by forests can only offset emissions, is limited by available land and can be reversed by fire, pests or felling. Large-scale reforestation is the strongest sequestration option listed, so many sources would pick option 2. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 33 | fut | Logging & Wood Technology
The traditional Indian tool used for trimming branches and foliage from trees is:
(1) pole pruner
(2) loppers
(3) billhook
(4) hand saw
= 3
~ The billhook is a traditional hand tool with a curved or hooked blade and a short handle, widely used in India for lopping branches and cutting foliage, fuelwood and bushes. Pole pruners and loppers are modern pruning tools and a hand saw is for sawing.

Q 34 | fut | Logging & Wood Technology
Which Indian traditional tool is used for felling trees?
(1) Crosscut saw
(2) Chainsaw
(3) Broadaxe
(4) Handsaw
= 3
~ Felling by hand in India has traditionally been done with an axe, and the broadaxe is the only axe among the options. The chainsaw is a modern powered tool, while the crosscut saw and handsaw are saws, though a two-man crosscut saw is also widely used for felling. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 35 | fut | Logging & Wood Technology
The tool used for splitting logs into smaller pieces in Indian forestry is called:
(1) splitting maul
(2) wood splitting wedge
(3) log splitter
(4) wood mallet
= 1
~ A splitting maul is a heavy hammer-like tool with a wedge-shaped axe head that splits logs along the grain with one blow. A wedge needs to be driven by a hammer, a log splitter is a hydraulic machine and a mallet is only a striking tool.

Q 36 | fut | Logging & Wood Technology
The term ‘checking’ in wood refers to:
(1) the process of inspecting wood for defects
(2) the splitting or cracking of wood along its grain
(3) the process of removing moisture from wood
(4) the staining of wood due to exposure to sunlight
= 2
~ Checks are lengthwise cracks or splits along the grain, usually across the growth rings, caused by uneven drying and shrinkage, most often at the ends and surfaces of timber. Removal of moisture is seasoning, and staining is a discolouration defect.

Q 37 | fut | Logging & Wood Technology
In the process of kiln drying, what is the purpose of the conditioning phase?
(1) To accelerate the drying process
(2) To control the humidity within the kiln
(3) To enhance the colour and grain of the wood
(4) To prevent the growth of fungi and moulds
= 2
~ Conditioning is the final stage of a kiln schedule, in which steam or spray raises the humidity inside the kiln so that moisture differences and drying stresses (case hardening) in the timber are relieved. It is not meant to speed up drying, and it does not improve colour or grain.

Q 38 | fut | Logging & Wood Technology
Which type of wood typically requires longer seasoning periods due to its higher density?
(1) Softwood
(2) Medium-density fibreboard
(3) Particleboard
(4) Tropical hardwood
= 4
~ Dense tropical hardwoods have thick-walled cells and low permeability, so moisture moves out slowly and they need long, careful seasoning to avoid cracking and warping. Softwoods are less dense and dry faster, and MDF and particleboard are manufactured boards that are already dried.

Q 39 | fut | Logging & Wood Technology
Why is wood seasoning important?
(1) To make wood resistant to termites
(2) To increase the weight of the wood
(3) To enhance the colour of the wood
(4) To reduce moisture content and stabilise the wood
= 4
~ Seasoning lowers the moisture content of timber to a level suited to its use, which makes it dimensionally stable and reduces shrinkage, warping and decay by fungi. It makes the wood lighter, not heavier, and does not by itself make it termite-proof.

Q 40 | fut | Wood Properties
Which physical property of wood refers to its resistance to indentation?
(1) Hardness
(2) Elasticity
(3) Durability
(4) Grain pattern
= 1
~ Hardness is the resistance of wood to indentation and wear, and is measured by tests such as the Janka test, in which a steel ball is pressed into the surface. Elasticity is the ability to regain shape after loading and durability is resistance to decay.

Q 41 | fut | Wood Properties
The term ‘grain’ in wood refers to:
(1) the growth rings visible in cross-sections
(2) the weight of the wood
(3) the density of the wood
(4) the colour variations in the wood
= 1
~ In wood technology grain describes the arrangement and direction of the wood fibres, and the pattern it forms on a cut face is largely made visible by the growth rings seen in cross-section. This is the closest option, since weight, density and colour are separate properties. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 42 | fut | Wood Properties
The phenomenon of changing of the shape due to varying moisture content in wood is known as:
(1) warping
(2) fungus growth
(3) shrinkage
(4) expansion
= 1
~ Warping is the distortion of a piece of timber from its true shape (bow, cup, twist or crook) caused by uneven shrinkage as its moisture content changes. Shrinkage refers to the reduction in size, not the change of shape, and fungus growth is a biological defect.

Q 43 | fut | Wood Properties
Which physical property of wood determines its resistance to being split along its grain?
(1) Hardness
(2) Elasticity
(3) Compression strength
(4) Shear strength
= 4
~ Splitting along the grain happens when fibres slide past one another, so resistance to it is governed by the shear strength of wood parallel to the grain, which is low in all timbers. Hardness is resistance to indentation and compression strength is resistance to crushing.

Q 44 | fut | Wood Properties
The term ‘knots’ in wood refers to:
(1) portions of branches embedded in the wood
(2) colour variations in the wood
(3) the age of the tree
(4) the density of the wood
= 1
~ A knot is the base of a branch that has become enclosed in the stem as the tree grew, and it shows on sawn timber as a roughly round or oval patch with distorted grain. Knots are defects that reduce strength and affect appearance.

Q 45 | fut | Wood Properties
Which physical property of wood is responsible for its natural acoustic properties?
(1) Thermal conductivity
(2) Odour
(3) Density
(4) Colour
= 3
~ How fast sound travels through wood and how strongly it is damped depend mainly on its density together with its stiffness, which is why light, stiff woods are chosen for musical instruments. Thermal conductivity, odour and colour have no direct effect on sound.

Q 46 | anm | Snakes & Venomous Animals
Select the correct option based on the given statement and conclusions.
Statement: The antivenom most commonly used for the treatment of neurotoxic snake venom are antibodies.
Conclusions:
i) The most common treatment against snake venom is antivenom.
ii) Antivenom acts as antigen for the production of antibodies.
(1) Only conclusion (i) is correct
(2) Only conclusion (ii) is correct
(3) Both the conclusions (i) and (ii) are correct
(4) None of the conclusions are correct
= 3
~ Conclusion (i) is correct: antivenom, made of purified antibodies from immunised animals, is the standard treatment for snakebite envenoming. Conclusion (ii) is true only in the loose sense that antivenom is itself a foreign protein that can provoke an immune response in the patient; in making antivenom it is the venom that acts as the antigen. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 47 | anm | Snakes & Venomous Animals
Select the correct option based on the following assertion (A) and reason (R).
Assertion (A): Polyvalent snake antivenom is effective against more than one venomous snake species.
Reason (R): Polyvalent snake antivenom contains neutralizing antibodies raised against two or more species of snakes.
(1) Both A and R are correct, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(2) Both A and R are correct, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(3) A is true, but R is false.
(4) Both A and R are false.
= 1
~ Polyvalent antivenom is prepared by immunising horses with the venoms of several species (in India the 'big four': cobra, common krait, Russell's viper and saw-scaled viper), so it carries antibodies against each of them. That is exactly why it is effective against more than one species, so R explains A.

Q 48 | anm | Snakes & Venomous Animals
Select the correct option based on the following assertion (A) and reason (R).
Assertion (A): In India, available polyvalent anti-snake venom acts best in snakebites from the southern and western parts of India and not as well in the northern and eastern parts of the country.
Reason (R): The venoms of the same snake species have shown significant differences in toxic profiles.
(1) Both A and R are correct, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(2) Both A and R are correct, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(3) A is true, but R is false.
(4) Both A and R are false.
= 2
~ Both statements are accurate: Indian polyvalent antivenom is made from venoms collected mainly in southern India, and the venom of the same species varies from region to region. APPSC treats R as not the explanation of A, although geographic venom variation is the reason usually given for the weaker action in the north and east. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 49 | anm | Snakes & Venomous Animals
Select the correct option based on the following assertion (A) and reason (R).
Assertion (A): Lyophilised Anti-snake venom (ASV) is more useful than liquid ASV.
Reason (R): Liquid ASV requires a complete cold chain and refrigeration.
(1) Both A and R are correct, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(2) Both A and R are correct, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(3) A is true, but R is false.
(4) Both A and R are false.
= 1
~ Lyophilised (freeze-dried) ASV can be stored at ordinary temperatures and has a longer shelf life, which is valuable in rural primary health centres where refrigeration is unreliable. Liquid ASV must be kept at 2-8 °C throughout, so R is the reason A holds.

Q 50 | anm | Snakes & Venomous Animals
Which of the following is the specific antidote for neurotoxic snake venom?
(1) Antivenom
(2) Polyvalent antivenom
(3) Anticholinesterase agents
(4) Atropine
= 3
~ Neurotoxic venoms of cobras and kraits block acetylcholine receptors at the neuromuscular junction, and anticholinesterases such as neostigmine (given with atropine) reverse that block and are the receptor-level antidote. Antivenom neutralises circulating venom and many texts call it the specific antidote for snakebite, so the key is debatable. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 51 | fut | NTFPs & Medicinal Plants
Which NTFP is commonly used in the production of traditional Bidriware handicrafts in Andhra Pradesh?
(1) Wax
(2) Bamboo
(3) Sabai grass
(4) Bell metal
= 1
~ Of the four options, wax (beeswax and other natural waxes collected as NTFP) is the only forest produce used in metal-casting handicrafts, where wax patterns are used in the lost-wax method. Bamboo and sabai grass are used for baskets, mats and paper, and bell metal is an alloy, not a forest product. Bidriware itself is a zinc-copper alloy craft of Bidar in Karnataka, so the question is loosely framed. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 52 | fut | NTFPs & Medicinal Plants
Adda leaves collected from Bauhinia vahlii in Andhra Pradesh is used primarily for:
(1) bidi making
(2) medicinal purpose
(3) making eating plates
(4) green manuring
= 3
~ The large leaves of the climber Bauhinia vahlii (adda or maloo creeper) are stitched into leaf plates and cups, which are a major source of income for tribal collectors in Andhra Pradesh. Bidi wrappers come mainly from tendu (Diospyros melanoxylon) leaves.

Q 53 | fut | NTFPs & Medicinal Plants
Which NTFP is commonly used in the production of high-quality handmade paper in Andhra Pradesh?
(1) Sisal fibre
(2) Eucalyptus leaves
(3) Sabai grass
(4) Karaya gum
= 3
~ Sabai (babui) grass, Eulaliopsis binata, has long strong fibres and is a traditional raw material for high-quality paper, as well as ropes and mats. Karaya gum is an exudate used in food and pharmaceuticals, and eucalyptus leaves are not a pulp source for handmade paper.

Q 54 | fut | NTFPs & Medicinal Plants
Which of the following statements is true about Girijan Cooperative Corporation?
(1) It is the marketing wing of Andhra Pradesh Forest Department.
(2) It is the main purchaser of Bamboo from Andhra Pradesh Forest Department.
(3) It pays royalty to Andhra Pradesh Forest Department for collection and sale of non-timber forest product.
(4) It has full right to collect and sale non-timber forest products in Andhra Pradesh.
= 3
~ The Girijan Cooperative Corporation (GCC) is a separate State tribal-welfare undertaking that buys NTFP from tribal gatherers and markets it, and it pays royalty to the Forest Department for the produce it collects and sells. It is not the Forest Department's marketing wing, and its right to deal in NTFP is limited to what the State grants it, not 'full'.

Q 55 | anm | Animal Health & Nutrition
Which viral infection affects wild boars and other suid species in India, causing reproductive issues and high mortality rates?
(1) Hepatitis E
(2) Porcine circovirus
(3) Swine influenza
(4) Classical swine fever
= 1
~ APPSC keys Hepatitis E, a virus that circulates widely in pigs and wild boar. Systematic, sequential and stratified random sampling all follow a defined, objective selection procedure.

Q 148 | fim | Sampling & Inventory
The graphical representation of normal distribution of data is depicted by a curve which is:
(1) J shaped curve
(2) Bell shaped curve
(3) Sigmoid shaped curve
(4) Straight line
= 2
~ A normal distribution plots as a symmetrical bell-shaped curve with the mean, median and mode at the centre, which is why it is also called the Gaussian or bell curve. J-shaped and sigmoid curves describe other relationships, such as skewed distributions and cumulative growth, and the distribution is not a straight line.

Plane table surveying consists of:
(1) Compass, chain and Table
(2) Hypsometer, Chain and table

(4) Prismatic compass, sextant and rods
= 3

Q 150 | fse | Survey & Engineering
The most accurate and important category of levelling instruments is the Telescopic levels. It needs a set of two instruments which are:
(1) Compass & Chain
(2) Abney Level and rods
(3) The level and the levelling staff
(4) Tangent Clinometer & the Chain
= 3
~ A telescopic level (dumpy, tilting or automatic) gives a horizontal line of sight, and the levelling staff is read through it at each point to get differences in height. The set is therefore the level and the levelling staff; chains, compasses, the Abney level and clinometers are not used as the level-and-staff pair.
