Q 1 | men | Reasoning
A, B, C, D, L, M, N and O are sitting around a square table facing the centre of the table. Some of them are sitting at the corners while some are sitting at the exact centre of the sides. Only three people sit between D and A. D sits at the centre of one of the sides. N sits second to the left of A. O sits second to the right of C. B sits at one of the corners and is not an immediate neighbour of A. Only three people sit between O and L. B is not an immediate neighbour of M. Who sits fourth to the left of M?
(1) A
(2) C
(3) L
(4) N
= 4
~ Facing the centre, moving left means moving clockwise. D (a side centre) and A sit opposite each other; N is second to A's left, O is second to C's right, and B takes the corner not next to A or M. The only arrangement, clockwise, is B, D, O, M, C, A, L, N (B, O, C and L at the corners). Fourth to the left of M is the person directly opposite M, which is N.

Q 2 | men | Reasoning
J, K, L, S, T and U each scored different marks. Only two people have scored less than J. K has scored immediately more than S. Only two people have scored between S and T. L has scored more than U. No one has scored less than T. How many people have scored more than U?
(1) One
(2) Two
(3) Three
(4) Four
= 4
~ No one scored less than T, so T is 6th (lowest); with two people between S and T, S is 3rd from the top, and K, immediately above S, is 2nd. Only two scored less than J, so J is 4th; since L scored more than U, L is 1st and U is 5th. The order from the top is L, K, S, J, U, T, so four people (L, K, S, J) scored more than U.

Q 3 | men | Reasoning
In a certain code language,
A ¥ B means ‘A is the father of B’,
A ≠ B means ‘A is the brother of B’,
A ₹ B means ‘A is the daughter of B’,
A £ B means ‘A is the wife of B’.
Based on the above, how is T related to R if ‘T £ I ≠ G ₹ E ¥ R’?
(1) Father's sister
(2) Brother's wife
(3) Father's brother's wife
(4) Brother's wife's mother
= 2
~ T £ I means T is the wife of I, I ≠ G means I is the brother of G, G ₹ E means G is the daughter of E, and E ¥ R means E is the father of R. G and R are both children of E, so I, being G's brother, is also R's brother. T, the wife of I, is therefore R's brother's wife.

Q 4 | men | Reasoning
HOLD is related to KQOF in a certain way based on the English alphabetical order. In the same way, TWXL is related to WYAN. To which of the following options is CCGR related, following the same logic?
(1) FEJT
(2) FJFR
(3) FTRE
(4) DESW
= 1
~ The letters move forward alternately by 3 and 2 places: H+3 = K, O+2 = Q, L+3 = O, D+2 = F; likewise T+3 = W, W+2 = Y, X+3 = A, L+2 = N. Applying the same rule to CCGR: C+3 = F, C+2 = E, G+3 = J, R+2 = T, which gives FEJT.

Q 5 | men | Reasoning
Read the given statements and conclusions carefully. Assuming that the information given in the statements is true, even if it appears to be at variance with commonly known facts, decide which of the given conclusions logically follow(s) from the statements.
Statements:
All pencils are rods.
All pencils are trays.
All bats are trays.
Conclusions:
(I): All bats are rods.
(II): Some trays are rods.
(1) Only conclusion (I) follows
(2) Only conclusion (II) follows
(3) Neither conclusion (I) nor (II) follows
(4) Both conclusions (I) and (II) follow
= 2
~ Every pencil is both a rod and a tray, so the trays that are pencils are also rods; hence 'Some trays are rods' (II) follows. Bats are linked only to trays, and nothing connects bats with rods, so 'All bats are rods' (I) does not follow. Only conclusion II follows.

Q 6 | mth | Statistics & Probability
If the mode of a dataset is 85, and the mean is 55, then which of the following statements is true?
(1) The median of the dataset is 65, and the dataset is positively skewed.
(2) The median of the dataset is 65, and the dataset is negatively skewed.
(3) The median of the dataset is 45, and the dataset is negatively skewed.
(4) The median of the dataset is 45, and the dataset is positively skewed.
= 2
~ By the empirical relation Mode = 3 Median − 2 Mean: 85 = 3 Median − 110, so Median = 195 ÷ 3 = 65. Since Mean (55) < Median (65) < Mode (85), the longer tail lies on the left (lower values), so the distribution is negatively skewed.

Q 7 | men | Arithmetic
Considering the following statements, which of the given options is correct?
Statement I: If two buses leave a station at the same time and operate at different regular intervals (frequencies), the time when they will meet again is the LCM of their frequencies.
Statement II: The LCM of two or more numbers is always greater than the largest number in the set.
(1) Both Statements I and II are true.
(2) Both Statements I and II are false.
(3) Statement I is true, but Statement II is false.
(4) Statement I is false, but Statement II is true.
= 3
~ Statement I is true: buses that start together next leave together after a time equal to the LCM of their intervals (e.g., every 12 and 18 minutes → together again after 36 minutes). Statement II is false: the LCM can equal the largest number, e.g., LCM(4, 8) = 8, so it is not always greater.

Q 8 | mth | Statistics & Probability
A group of students conducted a survey of 22 households in a neighbourhood, which resulted in the following frequency table showing the number of family members per household.
Family Size | 1-3 | 3-5 | 5-7 | 7-9 | 9-11
Number of Families | 4 | 6 | 7 | 2 | 3
What is the mode of this data? ((Give your answer correct to 2 decimal places)
(1) 5.33
(2) 4.66
(3) 3.33
(4) 3.01
= 1
~ The modal class is 5-7 (highest frequency f₁ = 7), with f₀ = 6 before it, f₂ = 2 after it, lower limit l = 5 and width h = 2. Mode = l + [(f₁ − f₀) ÷ (2f₁ − f₀ − f₂)] × h = 5 + [1 ÷ (14 − 6 − 2)] × 2 = 5 + 2/6 = 5.33.

Q 9 | mth | Geometry & Mensuration
A cuboid has sides in the ratio 16 : 9 : 12. A cube has volume equal to the cuboid. The ratio of their surface areas is ______.
(1) 36 : 35
(2) 35 : 34
(3) 34 : 33
(4) 37 : 36
= 4
~ Let the cuboid be 16k × 9k × 12k; its volume is 1728k³, so the cube of equal volume has side 12k. Cuboid surface area = 2(144 + 108 + 192)k² = 888k². Cube surface area = 6 × 144k² = 864k². Ratio = 888 : 864 = 37 : 36.

Q 10 | mth | Geometry & Mensuration
A cone with volume 9π cubic units is made having curved surface area 9√2π square units. On the basis of the given information, which of the following statements is/are correct?
Statement 1: The flat surface area of the cone is less than 9π square units.
Statement 2: The diameter and the slant length are in the ratio √2 : 1.
(1) Only Statement 1 is correct.
(2) Only Statement 2 is correct.
(3) Both the Statements are correct.
(4) None of the Statements is correct.
= 2
~ The volume πr²h/3 = 9π gives r²h = 27 and the curved area πrl = 9√2π gives rl = 9√2; squaring and putting h = 27/r² gives r⁶ − 162r² + 729 = 0, whose root r² = 9 gives r = h = 3, l = 3√2. Then the flat (base) area πr² = 9π is not less than 9π (Statement 1 wrong), and diameter : slant = 6 : 3√2 = √2 : 1 (Statement 2 correct). (The other root, r² ≈ 5.56, would make only Statement 1 true; the key uses r = 3.)

Q 12 | mth | Geometry & Mensuration
A cylindrical water tank has an internal diameter of 2.8 metres and a height of 5 metres. The tank is filled with water up to 80% of its capacity. Due to a leak, water is being drained at a constant rate of 250 litres per hour.
Based on this, the following statements are made:
Statement I: The water currently stored in the tank is 24,640 litres.
Statement II: The time it takes to drain all the water from the tank is 98.56 hours.
(Consider π = 22/7)
Which of the following is true regarding these statements?
(1) Both Statements I and II are true.
(2) Both Statements I and II are false.
(3) Statement I is true, but Statement II is false.
(4) Statement I is false, but Statement II is true.
= 1
~ Radius = 1.4 m, so capacity = (22/7) × 1.4² × 5 = 30.8 m³ = 30,800 litres. At 80%, the water stored = 0.8 × 30,800 = 24,640 litres, so Statement I is true. Draining at 250 litres per hour takes 24,640 ÷ 250 = 98.56 hours, so Statement II is also true.

Q 13 | mth | Trigonometry
Find the value of the given expression.
[sin⁴(t) − cos⁴(t)] / [sin(t) × cos(t)] × tan(t) / [tan²(t) − 1]
(1) −1
(2) 1
(3) 0.5
(4) −0.5
= 2
~ sin⁴t − cos⁴t = (sin²t − cos²t)(sin²t + cos²t) = sin²t − cos²t. Also tan t / (tan²t − 1) = (sin t/cos t) × cos²t / (sin²t − cos²t) = sin t cos t / (sin²t − cos²t). Multiplying: [(sin²t − cos²t)/(sin t cos t)] × [sin t cos t/(sin²t − cos²t)] = 1.

Q 14 | mth | Algebra
The roots of the equation ax² − ax + b = 0 are a₁ and a₂. On the basis of the given information, which of the following statements is/are true?
Statement 1: The roots are equal if a = 12 and b = 3.
Statement 2: The roots are a₁ = 3 and a₂ = −3 if b = −9a.
(1) Only Statement 1 is true.
(2) Only Statement 2 is true.
(3) Both the Statements are true.
(4) None of the Statements is true.
= 1
~ The discriminant of ax² − ax + b = 0 is a² − 4ab. With a = 12 and b = 3 it is 144 − 144 = 0, so the roots are equal (both ½): Statement 1 is true. The sum of the roots is a/a = 1 for every a, but 3 + (−3) = 0; with b = −9a the equation is x² − x − 9 = 0, whose roots are (1 ± √37)/2. Statement 2 is false.

Q 22 | sci | Chemistry
Consider the following two statements and select the correct option.
Statement I: The Haber process is an industrial method used to produce ammonia by reacting nitrogen and hydrogen gases under high temperature and pressure in the presence of an iron catalyst.
Statement II: The Contact process is an important industrial method used to manufacture sulphuric acid, which involves the catalytic oxidation of sulphur dioxide to sulphur trioxide using vanadium pentoxide (V₂O₅) as a catalyst.
(1) Statement I is true, but statement II is false.
(2) Statement I is false, but statement II is true.
(3) Both statement I and statement II are true.
(4) Both statement I and statement II are false.
= 3
~ Haber process: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ at about 200 atm and 450 °C over an iron catalyst, so Statement I is true. Contact process: SO₂ is oxidised to SO₃ over vanadium pentoxide (V₂O₅); the SO₃ is absorbed in sulphuric acid to form oleum, which is diluted to give H₂SO₄, so Statement II is also true.

Q 23 | sci | Physics

(1) Statement I is true, but statement II is false.
(2) Statement I is false, but statement II is true.
(3) Both Statement I and statement II are true.
(4) Both Statement I and statement II are false.
