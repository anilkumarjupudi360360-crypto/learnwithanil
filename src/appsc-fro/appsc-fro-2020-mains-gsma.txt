Q 1 | sci | Chemistry
What is the result when we add salt in water?
(1) The boiling point decreases and freezing point increases.
(2) The boiling point increases and freezing point decreases.
(3) Both the boiling point and the freezing point increase.
(4) Both the boiling point and freezing point decrease.
= 2
~ Salt is a non-volatile solute: it lowers the vapour pressure of water, so the solution must be heated above 100 °C to boil (elevation of boiling point) and cooled below 0 °C to freeze (depression of freezing point). These are colligative properties, which is why sea water freezes near −2 °C and salt is spread to melt ice on roads.

Q 2 | sci | Chemistry
Tamarind contains:
(1) acetic acid
(2) citric acid
(3) methanoic acid
(4) tartaric acid
= 4
~ The sour taste of tamarind comes mainly from tartaric acid, which is also found in grapes. Acetic acid is the acid of vinegar, citric acid is found in lemons and oranges, and methanoic (formic) acid is present in ant stings and nettle leaves.

Q 3 | sci | Science & Technology
The Integrated Chip (IC) of a computer is usually made of:
(1) gold
(2) copper
(3) silicon
(4) tungsten
= 3
~ Integrated circuits are built on thin wafers of very pure silicon, a semiconductor whose conductivity can be controlled by doping to form transistors, diodes and resistors on a single chip. Gold and copper are used only for fine wiring and connections, and tungsten for filaments and contacts.

Q 4 | sci | Biology & Health
The longest fibre on the cell body of a neuron is called:
(1) dendrites
(2) axon
(3) cytoplasm
(4) sheath
= 2
~ A neuron has a cell body (cyton) with many short branched dendrites that bring impulses in, and one long fibre, the axon, that carries impulses away from the cell body to the next neuron or to a muscle. The myelin sheath is only a covering around the axon, and cytoplasm is the cell's fluid content.

Q 5 | sci | Chemistry
Which group of elements generally form coloured compounds?
(1) Alkali metals
(2) Transition metals
(3) Metalloids
(4) Non-metals
= 2
~ Transition (d-block) metal ions have partly filled d-orbitals; they absorb part of visible light when electrons jump between d-levels (d–d transitions), so their compounds look coloured, e.g. blue copper sulphate and purple potassium permanganate. Alkali metal compounds are generally white or colourless because their ions have no unpaired d-electrons.

Q 6 | sci | Science & Technology
Dactylography is the study of:
(1) fingerprints
(2) retina
(3) face recognition
(4) DNA
= 1
~ Dactylography (Greek daktylos, finger) is the study of fingerprints for identification. Fingerprint ridge patterns are unique to each person and do not change through life, so they are used in forensics and biometric systems such as Aadhaar. Retina scans, face recognition and DNA profiling are other identification methods.

Q 47 | sci | Chemistry
Which of the following noble gases is NOT present in the air?
(1) Argon
(2) Helium
(3) Radon
(4) Neon
= 3
~ Argon (about 0.93% of air), neon and helium are regular constituents of the atmosphere. Radon is a radioactive gas formed by the decay of radium in rocks and soil and decays with a half-life of about 3.8 days, so only minute traces seep into the air near the ground and it is not counted as a constituent of air. NCERT states that all noble gases except radon occur in the atmosphere.

Q 49 | geo | Environment & Ecology
The thickness of the ozone in a column of air from the ground to the top of the atmosphere is measured in terms of:
(1) Decible Unit (DU)
(2) Pascals Unit (PU)
(3) Dram Unit (DU)
(4) Dobson Unit (DU)
= 4
~ The total ozone in a column of air is measured in Dobson Units, named after G.M.B. Dobson. One DU equals a 0.01 mm thick layer of pure ozone at standard temperature and pressure; the global average is about 300 DU, and an area below 220 DU is called an ozone hole. Decibel measures sound and pascal measures pressure.

Q 50 | geo | Environment & Ecology
The natural aging of a lake by nutrient enrichment of its water is called:
(1) Biomagnification
(2) Eutrophication
(3) Sedimentation
(4) Biodegradation
= 2
~ Eutrophication is the natural ageing of a lake as nutrients such as nitrogen and phosphorus build up, causing algal blooms, loss of oxygen and gradual filling of the lake until it becomes land; sewage and fertiliser run-off speed it up (cultural eutrophication). Biomagnification is the build-up of toxins along a food chain, and biodegradation is breakdown by microbes.

(1) Eratosthenes
(2) Holmes
(3) Hipparchus
(4) Aristotle
= 1
~ Eratosthenes (c. 276–194 BCE), the Greek scholar at Alexandria, first used the term "geographika" and measured the Earth's circumference with remarkable accuracy, so he is called the father of geography. Hipparchus is known for latitude–longitude and trigonometry, Aristotle argued that the Earth is spherical, and Arthur Holmes proposed convection currents in the mantle.

Q 102 | geo | World Geography
Which planet is NOT an inner planet?
(1) Venus
(2) Jupiter
(3) Earth
(4) Mars
= 2
~ The inner or terrestrial planets are Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars, which lie between the Sun and the asteroid belt and are small, dense and rocky. Jupiter lies beyond the asteroid belt and is the largest of the outer, gaseous (Jovian) planets, so it is not an inner planet.
