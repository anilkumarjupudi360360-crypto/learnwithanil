Q 1 | geo | Physical Geography
Consider the following statements:
Statement-I: The atmosphere is heated more by incoming solar radiation than by terrestrial radiation.
Statement-II: Carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases in the atmosphere are good absorbers of long wave radiation.
Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?
(1) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II explains Statement-I
(2) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct, but Statement-II does not explain Statement-I
(3) Statement-I is correct, but Statement-II is incorrect
(4) Statement-I is incorrect, but Statement-II is correct
= 4
~ Statement-I is incorrect: the atmosphere is heated mainly from below, by the long-wave (terrestrial) radiation the Earth gives out after absorbing sunlight; incoming short-wave solar radiation passes through the air largely unabsorbed. Statement-II is correct: carbon dioxide, water vapour and other greenhouse gases are good absorbers of long-wave radiation, which is why terrestrial radiation warms the air. Hence (d).

Q 2 | geo | Physical Geography
Consider the following statements:
Statement-I: Thickness of the troposphere at the equator is much greater as compared to poles.
Statement-II: At the equator, heat is transported to great heights by strong convectional currents.
Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?
(1) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II explains Statement-I
(2) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct, but Statement-II does not explain Statement-I
(3) Statement-I is correct, but Statement-II is incorrect
(4) Statement-I is incorrect, but Statement-II is correct
= 1
~ The troposphere is about 18 km thick at the equator and about 8 km at the poles. The reason is the strong heating at the equator: powerful convection currents carry heat and air to great heights, lifting the tropopause. Both statements are correct and Statement-II explains Statement-I, so (a).

Q 3 | geo | Physical Geography
Consider the following:
1. Pyroclastic debris
2. Ash and dust
3. Nitrogen compounds
4. Sulphur compounds
How many of the above are products of volcanic eruptions?
(1) Only one
(2) Only two
(3) Only three
(4) All four
= 4
~ A volcanic eruption throws out solid, liquid and gaseous material: pyroclastic debris (fragments of rock and lava), volcanic ash and dust, and gases such as water vapour, carbon dioxide, sulphur compounds (sulphur dioxide, hydrogen sulphide) and nitrogen compounds. All four are products of eruptions, so (d).

Q 4 | geo | World Geography
Which of the following is/are correct inference/inferences from isothermal maps in the month of January?
1. The isotherms deviate to the north over the ocean and to the south over the continent.
2. The presence of cold ocean currents, Gulf Stream and North Atlantic Drift make the North Atlantic Ocean colder and the isotherms bend towards the north.
Select the answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 1
~ In January it is winter in the northern hemisphere: the oceans stay warmer than the land, so isotherms bend towards the north (poleward) over the oceans and towards the south over the continents. Statement 1 is correct. Statement 2 is wrong: the Gulf Stream and the North Atlantic Drift are warm currents; they make the North Atlantic warmer, which is why the isotherms bend northwards there. Hence (a).

(4) Madagascar and Mozambique

Q 6 | geo | Indian Geography
With reference to the Himalayan rivers joining the Ganga downstream of Prayagraj from West to East, which one of the following sequences is correct?
(1) Ghaghara – Gomati – Gandak – Kosi
(2) Gomati – Ghaghara – Gandak – Kosi
(3) Ghaghara – Gomati – Kosi – Gandak
(4) Gomati – Ghaghara – Kosi – Gandak
= 2
~ Below Prayagraj the Ganga receives, from west to east: the Gomati (joining near Kaithi, Varanasi–Ghazipur), the Ghaghara (near Chhapra in Bihar), the Gandak (opposite Patna, at Hajipur) and the Kosi (near Kursela, Katihar). The Gomati rises in the plains but is listed with these north-bank tributaries. The correct order is Gomati – Ghaghara – Gandak – Kosi, option (b).

Q 7 | geo | Physical Geography
Consider the following statements:
Statement-I: Rainfall is one of the reasons for weathering of rocks.
Statement-II: Rain water contains carbon dioxide in solution.
Statement-III: Rain water contains atmospheric oxygen.
Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?
(1) Both Statement-II and Statement-III are correct and both of them explain Statement-I
(2) Both Statement-II and Statement-III are correct, but only one of them explains Statement-I
(3) Only one of the Statements II and III is correct and that explains Statement-I
(4) Neither Statement-II nor Statement-III is correct
= 1
~ Rain is a major agent of chemical weathering. Rain water dissolves carbon dioxide and forms weak carbonic acid, which attacks rocks such as limestone (carbonation); it also carries dissolved oxygen, which reacts with iron-bearing minerals (oxidation, rusting). So both Statements II and III are correct and both explain why rainfall weathers rocks: (a).

Q 12 | geo | Physical Geography
With reference to "water vapour", which of the following statements is/are correct?

Select the answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only

Q 13 | geo | World Geography
Consider the following description:
1. Annual and daily range of temperatures is low.
2. Precipitation occurs throughout the year.
3. Precipitation varies between 50 cm – 250 cm.
What is this type of climate?
(1) Equatorial climate
(2) China type climate
(3) Humid subtropical climate
(4) Marine West coast climate
= 4
~ The Marine West Coast (cool temperate oceanic, or British) type has mild winters and cool summers, so a low annual and daily range of temperature, owing to the onshore westerlies and warm currents; rain falls throughout the year and varies from about 50 cm to 250 cm. Equatorial climate has much heavier rain and high temperatures; the China type has hot summers and a larger range. Hence (d).

Q 14 | geo | Physical Geography
With reference to "Coriolis force", which of the following statements is/are correct?
1. It increases with increase in wind velocity.
2. It is maximum at the poles and is absent at the equator.
Select the answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 3
~ The Coriolis force, caused by the Earth's rotation, is proportional to the speed of the moving air (2Ωv sin φ), so it increases as wind velocity increases. Because it depends on the sine of the latitude, it is greatest at the poles and zero at the equator. Both statements are correct: (c).

Q 15 | geo | Physical Geography
On June 21 every year, which of the following latitude(s) experience(s) a sunlight of more than 12 hours?
1. Equator
2. Tropic of Cancer
3. Tropic of Capricorn
4. Arctic Circle
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) 3 and 4
(4) 2 and 4
= 4
~ On 21 June (summer solstice) the sun is overhead at the Tropic of Cancer and every latitude north of the equator has a day longer than 12 hours: about 13.5 hours at the Tropic of Cancer and 24 hours at the Arctic Circle. The equator always has about 12 hours, and the Tropic of Capricorn has less than 12. Hence 2 and 4, (d).

Q 16 | env | Ecology & Ecosystems

(2) Congo Basin
(3) Kikori Basin
(4) Rio de la Plata Basin

Q 17 | env | Climate Change & Pollution
With reference to perfluoroalkyl and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) that are used in making many consumer products, consider the following statements:
1. PFAS are found to be widespread in drinking water, food and food packaging materials.
2. PFAS are not easily degraded in the environment.
3. Persistent exposure to PFAS can lead to bioaccumulation in animal bodies.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ PFAS (per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances), used in non-stick cookware, food packaging, waterproof fabrics and fire-fighting foams, are called 'forever chemicals'. They are found widely in drinking water, food and packaging; their strong carbon–fluorine bonds make them very hard to break down; and continued exposure leads to their build-up (bioaccumulation) in the bodies of animals and humans. All three statements are correct: (d).

Q 28 | env | Biodiversity & Conservation
Which one of the following shows a unique relationship with an insect that has coevolved with it and that is the only insect that can pollinate this tree?
(1) Fig
(2) Mahua
(3) Sandalwood
(4) Silk cotton
= 1
~ Fig trees (Ficus) and fig wasps show obligate mutualism: each fig species is pollinated only by its own species of tiny wasp, which breeds inside the fig's enclosed inflorescence (syconium), and the two have co-evolved over millions of years. Neither can complete its life cycle without the other. Hence (a).

Q 31 | sci | Space & Defence

Q 33 | sci | Biology & Health
Which one of the following is synthesised in human body that dilates blood vessels and increases blood flow?
(1) Nitric oxide
(2) Nitrous oxide
(3) Nitrogen dioxide
(4) Nitrogen pentoxide
= 1
~ Nitric oxide (NO) is made in the lining (endothelium) of blood vessels from the amino acid L-arginine. It relaxes the smooth muscle of the vessel walls, widening them (vasodilation) and increasing blood flow; drugs such as nitroglycerin work by releasing NO. Nitrous oxide is laughing gas, and nitrogen dioxide and pentoxide are pollutants. Hence (a).

Q 37 | sci | Physics & Chemistry

Q 38 | sci | IT, Computers & Emerging Technology
Recently, the term "pumped-storage hydropower" is actually and appropriately discussed in the context of which one of the following?
(1) Irrigation of terraced crop fields
(2) Lift irrigation of cereal crops
(3) Long duration energy storage
(4) Rainwater harvesting system
= 3

Q 39 | env | Climate Change & Pollution

Q 41 | eco | Poverty, Employment & Welfare
