Q 1 | rc | Passages
Read the following passage and answer the question that follows. Your answer should be based on the passage only.
We often hear about conflicts among different States in India over river waters. Of the 20 major river systems, 14 are already water-stressed; 75% of the population lives in water-stressed regions, a third of whom live in water-scarce areas. Climate change, the demands of rising population and the need for agriculture to keep pace, and increased rate of urbanization and industrialization will exacerbate water stress. According to the Constitution of India, water is a State subject and not that of the Union, except for regulation of inter-State rivers. Key to ensuring balance between competing demands of various stakeholders is a basin-based approach to allocate water amongst constituent regions and States. Allocating fair share of water to them requires assessments based on objective criteria, such as specificities of the river basin, size of dependent population, existing water use and demand, efficiency of use, projected future use, etc. while ensuring the environmental needs of the river and aquifers.
Which one of the following statements best reflects the most rational, practical and immediate action required to ensure fair and equitable allocation of water to different stakeholders?
(1) A national, pragmatic, legal and policy framework for water allocation should be made.
(2) All river systems of the country should be linked and huge aquifers created.
(3) Water channels between regions of water surplus and regions of water deficit should be created.
(4) To mitigate water crisis, water demand of sectors such as agriculture and industry should be reduced.
= 1
~ The passage says water is a State subject, conflicts are growing, and fair allocation needs a basin-based approach built on objective criteria (basin features, dependent population, use, efficiency, future demand, environmental needs). The immediate, rational step is therefore a national, pragmatic legal and policy framework for allocation. Linking all rivers or building channels are big engineering ideas the passage never proposes, and cutting sectoral demand ignores the allocation question. Hence (a).

Q 4 | qa | Permutations, Combinations & Probability
Raj has ten pairs of red, nine pairs of white and eight pairs of black shoes in a box. If he randomly picks shoes one by one (without replacement) from the box to get a red pair of shoes to wear, what is the maximum number of attempts he has to make?
(1) 27
(2) 36
(3) 44
(4) 45
= 4
~ Worst case: he first picks all 18 white and all 16 black shoes (34), then 10 red shoes of the same foot (say all left), which still gives no red pair: 44 picks. The 45th shoe must be a red right shoe and completes a red pair. Maximum attempts = 45, (d).

Q 5 | qa | Permutations, Combinations & Probability
In how many ways can a batsman score exactly 25 runs by scoring single runs, fours and sixes only, irrespective of the sequence of scoring shots?
(1) 18
(2) 19
(3) 20
(4) 21
= 2
~ Let sixes = a, fours = b; singles fill the rest, so we need 6a + 4b ≤ 25. a = 0: b = 0 to 6 (7 ways); a = 1: b = 0 to 4 (5); a = 2: b = 0 to 3 (4); a = 3: b = 0 or 1 (2); a = 4: b = 0 (1). Total 7 + 5 + 4 + 2 + 1 = 19, (b).

Q 6 | qa | Permutations, Combinations & Probability
There are four letters and four envelopes and exactly one letter is to be put in exactly one envelope with the correct address. If the letters are randomly inserted into the envelopes, then consider the following statements:
1. It is possible that exactly one letter goes into an incorrect envelope.
2. There are only six ways in which only two letters can go into the correct envelopes.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 2
~ Statement 1 is impossible: if three letters are in their correct envelopes, the only envelope left is the fourth letter's own, so it also goes in correctly. Statement 2: choose the two letters that go correctly in C(4,2) = 6 ways; the other two must then swap, in exactly 1 way. So there are exactly 6 ways. Only 2 is correct, (b).

Q 8 | qa | Number System
What is the unit digit in the expansion of (57242)^(9×7×5×3×1)?
(1) 2
(2) 4
(3) 6
(4) 8
= 1
~ The unit digit depends only on 2 raised to the power 9 × 7 × 5 × 3 × 1 = 945. Powers of 2 end in the cycle 2, 4, 8, 6 (length 4). 945 = 4 × 236 + 1, so the unit digit is the first in the cycle: 2, (a).

Q 9 | qa | Number System
If ABC and DEF are both 3-digit numbers such that A, B, C, D, E and F are distinct non-zero digits such that ABC + DEF = 1111, then what is the value of A + B + C + D + E + F?
(1) 28
(2) 29
(3) 30
(4) 31
= 4
~ Since all digits are non-zero, C + F cannot be 1, so C + F = 11 (carry 1). Then B + E + 1 must end in 1, so B + E = 10 (carry 1), and A + D + 1 = 11 gives A + D = 10. Total = 11 + 10 + 10 = 31, (d). (Example: 235 + 876 = 1111.)

Q 10 | qa | Number System
D is a 3-digit number such that the ratio of the number to the sum of its digits is least. What is the difference between the digit at the hundred's place and the digit at the unit's place of D?
(1) 0
(2) 7
(3) 8
(4) 9
= 3
~ To make number ÷ (sum of digits) smallest, keep the hundreds digit small and the other digits large. 199/19 ≈ 10.47 is less than 189/18 = 10.5, 198/18 = 11 and any number with hundreds digit 2 or more (e.g. 299/20 ≈ 14.95). So D = 199, and 9 − 1 = 8, (c).

Q 14 | qa | Number System
Three of the five positive integers p, q, r, s, t are even and two of them are odd (not necessarily in order). Consider the following:
1. p + q + r − s − t is definitely even.
2. 2p + q + 2r − 2s + t is definitely odd.
Which of the above statements is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 1
~ Subtracting a number has the same effect on parity as adding it, so p + q + r − s − t has the same parity as p + q + r + s + t = (three evens) + (two odds) = even. Statement 1 is always true. In 2p + q + 2r − 2s + t, the even terms 2p, 2r, 2s do not matter, so its parity is that of q + t, which is odd only if exactly one of q, t is odd; that is not certain. Only 1, (a).

Q 15 | qa | Number System
Consider the following in respect of prime number p and composite number c.
1. (p + c)/(p − c) can be even.
2. 2p + c can be odd.
3. pc can be odd.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ 1: take p = 2, c = 6: (2 + 6)/(2 − 6) = 8/(−4) = −2, an even number. 2: p = 2, c = 9: 2p + c = 13, odd. 3: p = 3, c = 9: pc = 27, odd. Each statement can hold, so all three are correct, (d).

Q 16 | qa | Number System
A 3-digit number ABC, on multiplication with D gives 37DD where A, B, C and D are different non-zero digits. What is the value of A + B + C?
(1) 18
(2) 16
(3) 15
(4) Cannot be determined due to insufficient data
= 1
~ ABC × D = 37DD = 3700 + 11D. Try D: D = 4 gives 3744 ÷ 4 = 936 (digits 9, 3, 6, 4 all different and non-zero). D = 5 gives 751, which repeats 5; D = 6, 7, 8, 9 do not divide evenly; D ≤ 3 makes ABC a 4-digit number. So ABC = 936 and A + B + C = 18, (a).

Q 20 | qa | Arithmetic
A, B, C working independently can do a piece of work in 8, 16 and 12 days respectively. A alone works on Monday, B alone works on Tuesday, C alone works on Wednesday; A alone, again works on Thursday and so on.
Consider the following statements:
1. The work will be finished on Thursday.
2. The work will be finished in 10 days.
Which of the above statements is/are correct?
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 1
~ Take the work as 48 units: A does 6, B 3 and C 4 units a day, so each 3-day round (A, B, C) does 13 units. After 3 rounds (9 days) 39 units are done; day 10 (A) brings it to 45 and day 11 (B) to 48. Day 1 is Monday, so day 8 is Monday and day 11 is Thursday. The work ends on Thursday, but in 11 days, not 10. Only 1, (a).
