Q 1 | eco | Money, Banking & Inflation
With reference to investments, consider the following:
I. Bonds
II. Hedge Funds
III. Stocks
IV. Venture Capital
How many of the above are treated as Alternative Investment Funds?
(1) Only one
(2) Only two
(3) Only three
(4) All the four
= 2
~ Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs) are privately pooled investment vehicles regulated by SEBI under the SEBI (Alternative Investment Funds) Regulations, 2012, which collect money from sophisticated investors and invest it under a defined policy. Category I AIFs include venture capital funds (with angel funds, SME funds, social venture funds and infrastructure funds), and Category III AIFs include hedge funds that use complex trading strategies and leverage. Bonds and stocks are traditional securities, the basic asset classes of conventional investing, not alternative investment funds. Hence only two (hedge funds and venture capital).

Q 2 | eco | Money, Banking & Inflation
Which of the following are the sources of income for the Reserve Bank of India?
I. Buying and selling Government bonds
II. Buying and selling foreign currency
III. Pension fund management
IV. Lending to private companies
V. Printing and distributing currency notes
Select the correct answer using the code given below.
(1) I and II only
(2) II, III and IV
(3) I, III, IV and V
(4) I, II and V
= 1
~ The RBI's income comes mainly from interest on the government securities it holds and profits on their sale (open market operations), and from its foreign currency assets: interest on foreign securities and deposits and gains on foreign-exchange transactions such as selling dollars from its reserves. So I and II are sources of income. The RBI does not manage pension funds (the NPS is regulated by the PFRDA) and does not lend to private companies; its lending is to banks and the government. Printing and distributing currency notes is an expenditure item in the RBI's accounts, not a source of income.

Q 3 | eco | Public Finance & Taxation
With reference to the Government of India, consider the following information:
Organization : Some of its functions : It works under
I. Directorate of Enforcement : Enforcement of the Fugitive Economic Offenders Act, 2018 : Internal Security Division–I, Ministry of Home Affairs
II. Directorate of Revenue Intelligence : Enforces the Provisions of the Customs Act, 1962 : Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance
III. Directorate General of Systems and Data Management : Carrying out big data analytics to assist tax officers for better policy and nabbing tax evaders : Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance
In how many of the above rows is the information correctly matched?
(1) Only one
(2) Only two
(3) All the three
(4) None
= 1
~ Row I is wrong: the Directorate of Enforcement does enforce the Fugitive Economic Offenders Act, 2018 (along with PMLA, 2002 and FEMA, 1999), but it works under the Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance, not the Ministry of Home Affairs. Row II is correct: the DRI, the apex anti-smuggling intelligence agency, enforces the Customs Act, 1962 under the CBIC, Department of Revenue. Row III is wrong: the Directorate General of Systems and Data Management does sit under CBIC in the Department of Revenue, but it is CBIC's IT and data wing, running the computerised customs and GST systems and compiling revenue statistics and budget forecasts. Big data analytics to aid policy and catch tax evaders is the mandate of the Directorate General of Analytics and Risk Management (DGARM), set up under the CBEC (now CBIC) on 1 July 2017. Hence only one row is correct.

Q 4 | eco | Money, Banking & Inflation
Consider the following statements:
I. The Reserve Bank of India mandates all the listed companies in India to submit a Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report (BRSR).
II. In India, a company submitting a BRSR makes disclosures in the report that are largely non-financial in nature.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) I only
(2) II only
(3) Both I and II
(4) Neither I nor II
= 2
~ Statement I is incorrect on two counts: the BRSR was introduced by SEBI (circular of May 2021, under the Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements Regulations), not the RBI, and it is mandatory only for the top 1,000 listed companies by market capitalisation from 2022-23, not for all listed companies. Statement II is correct: the BRSR replaced the older Business Responsibility Report and asks for disclosures on environmental, social and governance (ESG) matters such as energy and water use, emissions, waste, employee welfare and community engagement, which are largely non-financial in nature.

Q 5 | eco | Public Finance & Taxation
Consider the following statements:
Statement-I: In India, income from allied agricultural activities like poultry farming and wool rearing in rural areas is exempted from any tax.
Statement-II: In India, rural agricultural land is not considered a capital asset under the provisions of the Income-tax Act, 1961.
Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?
(1) Both Statement I and Statement II are correct and Statement II explains Statement I
(2) Both Statement I and Statement II are correct but Statement II does not explain Statement I
(3) Statement I is correct but Statement II is not correct
(4) Statement I is not correct but Statement II is correct
= 4
~ Section 10(1) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 exempts only "agricultural income" as defined in Section 2(1A): rent or revenue from agricultural land and income from agriculture carried on such land. Allied activities such as poultry farming, dairying, sheep and wool rearing, fisheries or bee-keeping are not agriculture in this sense, so their income is taxable as business income even in rural areas; Statement I is wrong. Statement II is correct: Section 2(14)(iii) excludes rural agricultural land (land outside municipal limits and the specified distance belts) from the definition of capital asset, so gains on its sale are not taxed as capital gains. The Income-tax Act, 2025, which replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026, carries both rules over.

Q 6 | eco | Agriculture & Industry
Consider the following statements:
I. India has joined the Minerals Security Partnership as a member.
II. India is a resource-rich country in all the 30 critical minerals that it has identified.
III. The Parliament in 2023 has amended the Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Act, 1957 empowering the Central Government to exclusively auction mining lease and composite license for certain critical minerals.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) I and II only
(2) II and III only
(3) I and III only
(4) I, II and III
= 3
~ Statement I is correct: India joined the US-led Minerals Security Partnership, which aims to secure diversified critical mineral supply chains, in June 2023. Statement II is wrong: the 30 critical minerals identified by the Ministry of Mines in 2023 were chosen largely because India has little or no domestic reserves or production of many of them, such as lithium, cobalt, nickel and several rare earths, and depends on imports. Q 7 | eco | Money, Banking & Inflation
Consider the following statements:
Statement-I: As regards returns from an investment in a company, generally, bondholders are considered to be relatively at lower risk than stockholders.
Statement-II: Bondholders are lenders to a company whereas stockholders are its owners.
Statement-III: For repayment purpose, bondholders are prioritized over stockholders by a company.
Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?
(1) Both Statement II and Statement III are correct and both of them explain Statement I
(2) Both Statement I and Statement II are correct and Statement I explains Statement II
(3) Only one of the Statements II and III is correct and that explains Statement I
(4) Neither Statement II nor Statement III is correct
= 1
~ Bondholders are creditors of a company: they lend money in return for a contractually fixed interest and the return of principal on maturity, whatever the company's profits. Stockholders are part-owners: their dividends are paid only out of profits at the board's discretion, and the value of their shares rises and falls with the company's fortunes, so Statement II is correct. If a company is wound up, creditors including bondholders must be paid before shareholders get anything, so Statement III is correct. Both the fixed, contractual claim and the priority in repayment explain why bondholders bear lower risk than stockholders (Statement I).

Q 8 | eco | Money, Banking & Inflation
Consider the following statements:
I. India accounts for a very large portion of all equity option contracts traded globally thus exhibiting a great boom.
II. India's stock market has grown rapidly in the recent past even overtaking Hong Kong's at some point of time.
III. There is no regulatory body either to warn the small investors about the risks of options trading or to act on unregistered financial advisors in this regard.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) I and II only
(2) II and III only
(3) I and III only
(4) I, II and III
= 1
~ Statement I is correct: driven by retail trading in index options on the NSE, India accounted for about 84% of all equity options traded worldwide in 2023, according to Futures Industry Association data, up from about 15% a decade earlier. Statement II is correct: in January 2024 the total market capitalisation of Indian stocks (about 4.33 trillion US dollars) overtook Hong Kong's (about 4.29 trillion), making India the fourth-largest equity market at the time. Statement III is wrong: SEBI regulates the market and has repeatedly warned small investors, for instance through studies showing that about nine in ten individual F&O traders lose money, and it acts against unregistered investment advisers and finfluencers under its Investment Advisers Regulations.

Q 9 | env | Climate Change & Pollution
Consider the following statements:
Statement-I: Circular economy reduces the emissions of greenhouse gases.
Statement-II: Circular economy reduces the use of raw materials as inputs.
Statement-III: Circular economy reduces wastage in the production process.
Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?
(1) Both Statement II and Statement III are correct and both of them explain Statement I
(2) Both Statement II and Statement III are correct but only one of them explains Statement I
(3) Only one of the Statements II and III is correct and that explains Statement I
(4) Neither Statement II nor Statement III is correct
= 1
~ A circular economy replaces the linear "take-make-dispose" model with one that keeps products and materials in use through reduce, reuse, repair, remanufacture and recycling. By recovering materials it cuts the demand for virgin raw materials (Statement II correct), and by designing out waste and using by-products it reduces wastage in production (Statement III correct). Both explain Statement I: extracting and processing raw materials is very energy-intensive and waste in landfills releases methane, so using fewer fresh inputs and generating less waste both lower greenhouse gas emissions.

Q 10 | eco | Public Finance & Taxation
Consider the following statements:
I. Capital receipts create a liability or cause a reduction in the assets of the Government.
II. Borrowings and disinvestment are capital receipts.
III. Interest received on loans creates a liability of the Government.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) I and II only
(2) II and III only
(3) I and III only
(4) I, II and III
= 1
~ In government budgeting, capital receipts are receipts that either create a liability (market borrowings, external loans, small savings) or reduce the government's assets (disinvestment of public sector shares, recovery of loans), so Statement I is correct. Borrowings create a liability and disinvestment reduces assets, so both are capital receipts (Statement II correct). Interest received by the government on the loans it has given is a non-tax revenue receipt: it neither creates a liability nor reduces an asset, so Statement III is wrong.

Q 11 | ih | Modern India & Freedom Struggle
Consider the following statements about Raja Ram Mohan Roy:
I. He possessed great love and respect for the traditional philosophical systems of the East.
II. He desired his countrymen to accept the rational and scientific approach and the principle of human dignity and social equality of all men and women.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) I only
(2) II only
(3) Both I and II
(4) Neither I nor II
= 3
~ Both statements reproduce the standard textbook assessment of Raja Ram Mohan Roy (1772–1833). He had great love and respect for the traditional philosophical systems of the East: he translated the Upanishads into Bengali and English and defended Vedantic monotheism against idolatry, while believing that Western science and culture would help regenerate Indian society. He wanted his countrymen to accept a rational and scientific approach and the principle of human dignity and social equality of all men and women, which is why he campaigned against sati (abolished in 1829), polygamy and caste rigidity, supported women's property rights and modern education, and founded the Brahmo Sabha (1828).

Q 12 | ih | Modern India & Freedom Struggle
Consider the following subjects with regard to Non-Cooperation Programme:
I. Boycott of law-courts and foreign cloth
II. Observance of strict non-violence
III. Retention of titles and honours without using them in public
IV. Establishment of Panchayats for settling disputes
How many of the above were parts of Non-Cooperation Programme?
(1) Only one
(2) Only two
(3) Only three
(4) All the four
= 3
~ The Non-Cooperation programme adopted at the Calcutta special session (September 1920) and endorsed at Nagpur (December 1920) included the boycott of government schools and colleges, law courts, legislative councils and foreign cloth (I correct), and the setting up of national schools and of panchayats or arbitration courts to settle disputes (IV correct), along with hand-spinning, khadi and Hindu-Muslim unity. Gandhi insisted on strict non-violence throughout (II correct), and called off the movement after the Chauri Chaura violence in February 1922. Titles and honorary offices were to be surrendered, not retained, as the very first step of the programme, so III is wrong. Hence three.

Q 13 | ih | Medieval India
The irrigation device called 'Araghatta' was
(1) a water bag made of leather pulled over a pulley
(2) a large wheel with earthen pots tied to the outer ends of its spokes
(3) a larger earthen pot driven by bullocks
(4) a large water bucket pulled up by rope directly by hand
= 2
~ The araghatta, mentioned in early medieval texts and inscriptions, was a water-lifting wheel: a large wheel with earthen pots tied to the outer ends of its spokes or to a rope around its rim, which filled as they dipped into a well or tank and emptied into a channel as they came up. Turned at first by hand or foot and later by bullocks through gearing, it developed into the Persian wheel (rahat or saqiya). Option (1) describes the charas or mot, a leather bag drawn over a pulley; option (4) is a simple rope-and-bucket lift; option (3) is not a recognised irrigation device.

Q 14 | ih | Ancient India
Who among the following rulers in ancient India had assumed the titles 'Mattavilasa', 'Vichitrachitta' and 'Gunabhara'?
(1) Mahendravarman I
(2) Simhavishnu
(3) Narasimhavarman I
(4) Simhavarman
= 1
~ The Pallava king Mahendravarman I (c. 600–630 CE) bore titles such as Mattavilasa, Vichitrachitta, Gunabhara, Chitrakarapuli and Shatrumalla, which appear in his cave-temple inscriptions; the Mandagapattu inscription calls him Vichitrachitta for building a shrine without brick, timber, metal or mortar. He wrote the Sanskrit farce Mattavilasa Prahasana and pioneered rock-cut temples in the Tamil country. Simhavishnu was his father, Narasimhavarman I (Mamalla), who defeated Pulakeshin II and developed Mamallapuram, was his son, and Simhavarman was an earlier Pallava ruler.

Q 15 | ih | Ancient India
Fa-hien (Faxian), the Chinese pilgrim, travelled to India during the reign of
(1) Samudragupta
(2) Chandragupta II
(3) Kumaragupta I
(4) Skandagupta
= 2
~ The Chinese Buddhist monk Fa-hien (Faxian) left China in 399 CE and spent about a decade in India in the early fifth century, collecting Vinaya texts and visiting Buddhist sites. This fell in the reign of the Gupta ruler Chandragupta II Vikramaditya (c. 375–415 CE), though Fa-hien does not name the king. He described the Madhyadesha as prosperous and peaceful, with mild punishments, light taxes, free hospitals at Pataliputra and Chandalas living outside the towns. Samudragupta ruled before him (c. 335–375 CE), and Kumaragupta I and Skandagupta after.

Q 16 | ih | Medieval India
Who among the following led a successful military campaign against the kingdom of Srivijaya, the powerful maritime State, which ruled the Malay Peninsula, Sumatra, Java and the neighbouring islands?
(1) Amoghavarsha (Rashtrakuta)
(2) Prataparudra (Kakatiya)
(3) Rajendra I (Chola)
(4) Vishnuvardhana (Hoysala)
= 3
~ Rajendra I (c. 1014–1044 CE) sent a naval expedition around 1025 CE against the Srivijaya kingdom and defeated its ruler Sangrama Vijayottungavarman; his inscriptions list Kadaram (Kedah), Srivijaya and other ports of the Malay Peninsula and Sumatra among the places taken, and he was called Kadaram Kondan. The campaign is usually linked to protecting Chola trade with South-east Asia and China. Amoghavarsha I was a 9th-century Rashtrakuta ruler known for patronage of literature, Prataparudra the last Kakatiya king, defeated by the Delhi Sultanate in 1323, and Vishnuvardhana the 12th-century Hoysala king who built the Chennakeshava temple at Belur.

Q 17 | ih | Ancient India
With reference to ancient India (600–322 BC), consider the following pairs:
Territorial region : River flowing in the region
I. Asmaka : Godavari
II. Kamboja : Vipas

(2) Only two

Q 18 | ih | Art & Culture
The first Gandharva Mahavidyalaya, a music training school, was set up in 1901 by Vishnu Digambar Paluskar in
(1) Delhi
(2) Gwalior
(3) Ujjain
(4) Lahore
= 4
~ Pandit Vishnu Digambar Paluskar (1872–1931), who wanted to take Hindustani classical music out of the closed world of court and gharana patronage and teach it to the public through organised classes and a written notation, founded the first Gandharva Mahavidyalaya at Lahore on 5 May 1901. He later shifted his base to Bombay, where another Gandharva Mahavidyalaya opened in 1908, and his pupils spread such schools across India. Q 19 | ih | Ancient India
Ashokan inscriptions suggest that the 'Pradeshika', 'Rajuka' and 'Yukta' were important officers at the
(1) village-level administration
(2) district-level administration
(3) provincial administration
(4) level of the central administration
= 2
~ Ashoka's Rock Edict III orders the yuktas, rajukas and pradeshikas to go on tour (anusamyana) every five years throughout his dominions to teach dhamma along with their ordinary work. Historians treat them as the key officers of district administration: the pradeshika as the head of a district, responsible for revenue and law and order; the rajuka, given powers of reward and punishment in Pillar Edict IV, in charge of the rural janapada and of land matters; and the yuktas as subordinate officials who kept accounts and records. Provinces were governed by princes (kumaras or aryaputras), and villages by the gramika, so district level is the best fit.

I. The Congress declared the attainment of 'Swaraj' by all legitimate and peaceful means to be its objective.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) I only
(2) II only
(3) Both I and II
(4) Neither I nor II

At the Nagpur session (December 1920) the Congress changed its creed from attaining self-government by constitutional means to the attainment of Swaraj by all legitimate and peaceful means, and endorsed the non-cooperation resolution passed at the Calcutta special session (September 1920). Through how many of them does the equator pass?
(1) Only one

Q 25 | geo | Physical Geography
Which of the following are the evidences of the phenomenon of continental drift?
I. The belt of ancient rocks from Brazil coast matches with those from Western Africa.
II. The gold deposits of Ghana are derived from the Brazil plateau when the two continents lay side by side.
III. The Gondwana system of sediments from India is known to have its counterparts in six different landmasses of the Southern Hemisphere.
Select the correct answer using the code given below.
(1) I and III only
(2) I and II only
(3) I, II and III
(4) II and III only
= 3
~ All three are standard evidences for continental drift. The belt of ancient rocks, about 2,000 million years old, along the Brazilian coast matches rocks of the same age in western Africa (I). The rich placer gold deposits of the Ghana coast have no source rock nearby; the gold-bearing veins lie in Brazil, so the deposits are taken to have been derived from the Brazil plateau when the two continents lay side by side (II). The Gondwana system of sediments, including tillite left by ancient glaciation, has counterparts in six landmasses of the Southern Hemisphere, such as Africa, Madagascar, Antarctica, Australia and South America (III). Other evidence includes the jig-saw fit of coastlines and fossils such as Mesosaurus.

Q 26 | geo | Physical Geography
Consider the following statements:
Statement-I: The amount of dust particles in the atmosphere is more in subtropical and temperate areas than in equatorial and polar regions.
Statement-II: Subtropical and temperate areas have less dry winds.
Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?
(1) Both Statement I and Statement II are correct and Statement II explains Statement I
(2) Both Statement I and Statement II are correct but Statement II does not explain Statement I
(3) Statement I is correct but Statement II is not correct
(4) Statement I is not correct but Statement II is correct
= 3
~ Statement I is correct: as NCERT (Fundamentals of Physical Geography, Class XI) puts it, a higher concentration of dust particles is found in subtropical and temperate regions than in equatorial and polar regions. The reason is the opposite of Statement II: these belts have more dry winds, which lift and carry dust from deserts and dry land, while the humid equatorial belt (frequent rain washes dust out) and the ice-covered polar regions supply little dust. So Statement II, which says these areas have less dry winds, is not correct.

Q 27 | geo | Physical Geography
Consider the following statements:
Statement-I: In January, in the Northern Hemisphere, the isotherms bend equatorward while crossing the landmasses, and poleward while crossing the oceans.
Statement-II: In January, the air over the oceans is warmer than that over the landmasses in the Northern Hemisphere.
Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?
(1) Both Statement I and Statement II are correct and Statement II explains Statement I
(2) Both Statement I and Statement II are correct but Statement II does not explain Statement I
(3) Statement I is correct but Statement II is not correct
(4) Statement I is not correct but Statement II is correct
= 1
~ In January (northern winter) land loses heat quickly and becomes much colder than the sea at the same latitude, while the oceans, with their high specific heat and warm currents such as the Gulf Stream and North Atlantic Drift, stay relatively warm (Statement II correct). A given temperature is therefore found farther south over the cold continents and farther north over the warm oceans, so the isotherms bend equatorward over land and poleward over the oceans, as NCERT shows for the North Atlantic and Europe (Statement I correct). Statement II is exactly the reason for Statement I.

Q 28 | geo | Physical Geography
Consider the following statements:
Statement-I: In the context of effect of water on rocks, chalk is known as a very permeable rock whereas clay is known as quite an impermeable or least permeable rock.
Statement-II: Chalk is porous and hence can absorb water.
Statement-III: Clay is not at all porous.
Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?
(1) Both Statement II and Statement III are correct and both of them explain Statement I
(2) Both Statement II and Statement III are correct but only one of them explains Statement I
(3) Only one of the Statements II and III is correct and that explains Statement I
(4) Neither Statement II nor Statement III is correct
= 3
~ Statement I is correct: chalk lets water soak through it readily, whereas clay hardly lets water pass and is treated as impermeable. Statement II is correct and explains this: chalk is a porous limestone whose pores and joints absorb water and let it move through the rock. Statement III is wrong: clay is actually very porous (its pore space can exceed 40%), but the pores are so tiny that, once wet, the clay swells and holds the water instead of transmitting it. Clay's impermeability therefore comes from fine pore size, not from a lack of pores. So only Statement II is correct, and it explains Statement I.

Q 29 | geo | Physical Geography
Consider the following statements:
I. Without the atmosphere, temperature would be well below freezing point everywhere on the Earth's surface.
II. Heat absorbed and trapped by the atmosphere maintains our planet's average temperature.
III. Atmosphere's gases, like carbon dioxide, are particularly good at absorbing and trapping radiation.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) I and III only
(2) I and II only
(3) I, II and III
(4) II and III only
= 3
~ All three statements describe the natural greenhouse effect. Statement II is not correct: under Article 74(1) the President acts on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers, and Maru Ram held that this applies to the pardoning power as well, so the President cannot exercise it independently of the Union Government.

Q 87 | pol | Union & State Government
Consider the following statements:
I. On the dissolution of the House of the People, the Speaker shall not vacate his/her office until immediately before the first meeting of the House of the People after the dissolution.

III. The Speaker of the House of the People may be removed from his/her office by a resolution of the House of the People passed by a majority of all the then Members of the House, provided that no resolution shall be moved unless at least fourteen days' notice has been given of the intention to move the resolution.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) I and II only
(2) II and III only
(3) I and III only
(4) I, II and III
= 3
~ Statement I is correct: the second proviso to Article 94 says that whenever the House of the People is dissolved, the Speaker shall not vacate office until immediately before the first meeting of the House after the dissolution. Statement II is not correct: the Constitution does not require the Speaker to resign from his or her party; that is a British convention, and Indian Speakers usually remain party members (paragraph 5 of the Tenth Schedule only allows a Speaker to give up party membership without being disqualified). Statement III is correct: Article 94(c) allows removal by a resolution passed by a majority of all the then Members of the House, and its first proviso requires at least fourteen days' notice of the intention to move such a resolution.

Q 89 | pol | Federalism & Local Government
Consider the following statements:

(2) Both Statement I and Statement II are correct but Statement II does not explain Statement I
(3) Statement I is correct but Statement II is not correct
(4) Statement I is not correct but Statement II is correct
= 4

Q 90 | env | Environmental Laws, Bodies & Conventions
Which organization has enacted the Nature Restoration Law (NRL) to tackle climate change and biodiversity loss?
(1) The European Union
(2) The World Bank
(3) The Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development
(4) The Food and Agriculture Organization
= 1
~ The Nature Restoration Law is a regulation of the European Union (Regulation (EU) 2024/1991), approved by the European Parliament and finally adopted by the EU Council on 17 June 2024; it came into force on 18 August 2024. It is the first continent-wide law of its kind and a key part of the EU Biodiversity Strategy and the European Green Deal. It obliges member States to put restoration measures in place on at least 20% of the EU's land and sea areas by 2030 and on all ecosystems in need of restoration by 2050, through national restoration plans. The World Bank, the OECD and the FAO cannot enact binding laws.
