Q 1 | ih | Art & Culture
Which one of the following Carnatic music ragas is similar to Raga Bilawal in Hindustani music?
(1) Nat Bhairavi
(2) Kamavardhini
(3) Hanumatodi
(4) Dheera Shankarabharanam
= 4
~ Bilawal thaat, the basic Hindustani scale since Bhatkhande, uses all seven natural (shuddha) notes, the equivalent of the Western major scale; its Carnatic counterpart is Dheera Shankarabharanam, the 29th melakarta, which also uses only natural notes. The other options correspond to different thaats: Natabhairavi (20th melakarta) to Asavari, Kamavardhini or Pantuvarali (51st) to Purvi, and Hanumatodi (8th) to the Hindustani Bhairavi thaat, so they are not similar to Bilawal.

Q 2 | ih | Modern India & Freedom Struggle
The artificially fixed rupee-sterling exchange rate prescribed by the Hilton-Young Commission (1926) was adopted by the British Government for which one of the following reasons?
(1) Aiding the flow of remittances from India and maintaining India's creditworthiness
(2) Providing support to Indian importers
(3) Encouraging export of cotton produce from India
(4) Preventing depreciation of the Rupee in terms of gold
= 1
~ The Hilton-Young Commission (Royal Commission on Indian Currency and Finance) recommended stabilising the rupee at 1s 6d instead of the pre-war 1s 4d, which the Currency Act of 1927 adopted. A higher-valued rupee reduced the rupee cost of India's large sterling payments to Britain (Home Charges, interest, pensions, profits) and kept India's credit sound in the London market, which is why the government favoured it. Nationalists such as Purshottamdas Thakurdas opposed the ratio because it hurt Indian exporters, including cotton growers, and helped British imports; supporting Indian importers or protecting the rupee's gold value was not the stated aim.

Q 3 | ih | Ancient India
Consider the following statements:
I. Pali texts contain the first definite references to coins, e.g., kahapana, nikkha, kamsa, and kakanika.
II. The literary evidence from Pali texts is corroborated by archaeological evidence of punch-marked coins from many sites, most of them made of silver.
The above statements have been associated with which of the following?
1. Emergence of urban life
2. Transition to money economy
Select the answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 3
~ Both statements describe the first coins of the Ganga valley in the 6th–5th centuries BCE. The early Pali (Buddhist) texts mention coins such as kahapana (karshapana), nikkha, kamsa and kakanika, and excavations at many sites have yielded silver punch-marked coins. Historians treat the appearance of coined metallic money as part of the "second urbanisation", when cities, trade and crafts grew, and as the marker of a shift from barter towards a money economy in which wages, prices, taxes and loans could be reckoned in coin. So the evidence is linked with both the emergence of urban life and the transition to money economy.

Q 4 | ih | Art & Culture
Which of the following temples has/have a Nagara-style shikhara?
1. Malegitti Shivalaya, Badami
2. Huchimalligudi Temple, Aihole
3. Dashavatara Temple, Deogarh
4. Virupaksha Temple, Pattadakal
Select the answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 and 2
(2) 2 and 3
(3) 3 only
(4) 3 and 4
= 2
~ The Huchimalligudi temple at Aihole (early Chalukya) carries a curvilinear rekha-prasada (Nagara-type) shikhara over its sanctum, and the Gupta-period Dashavatara temple at Deogarh (c. 6th century) is among the earliest temples with a Nagara shikhara, now partly ruined. The Malegitti Shivalaya at Badami (7th century) and the Virupaksha temple at Pattadakal (c. 740, built by queen Lokamahadevi) have stepped, pyramidal Dravida-style vimanas. Q 5 | ih | Ancient India
Among the four main forms of existence of life recognized in Jainism, which one of the following is not included?
(1) Deva (gods)
(2) Yaksha (demi-gods)
(3) Manushya (humans)
(4) Tiryancha (animals and plants)
= 2
~ Jainism classifies worldly souls into four gatis (states of existence) in which they are reborn according to karma: deva (heavenly beings), manushya (humans), tiryancha (animals, plants and other lower life forms, including one-sensed beings) and naraki (hell beings). Yakshas are not a separate gati; in Jain cosmology they are one class of vyantara devas, and they also appear as attendant figures (yaksha-yakshi) of the Tirthankaras. So Yaksha is the form not included among the four.

Q 6 | ih | Art & Culture
The Hallisalasya painting in the Bagh Caves represents:
(1) A joyous folk dance
(2) Buddha in a meditative pose
(3) The depiction of Shiva and Parvati on Kailasha
(4) Samudramanthan (Churning of the Ocean)
= 1
~ The famous Hallisaka-lasya scene in Cave 4 of the Bagh caves (Madhya Pradesh, c. 5th–6th century CE) shows a group of women musicians with drums, cymbals and sticks arranged in a circle around a central dancer. Hallisaka was a circular group dance, and the painting is admired for its lively, joyous movement, a secular theme. The Bagh caves are Buddhist viharas, but this panel is not a meditating Buddha, and Shiva-Parvati on Kailasha or the churning of the ocean are Brahmanical themes not depicted in this panel.

Q 7 | ih | Ancient India
Consider the following statements relating to the use of the place-value system in India:
1. The earliest epigraphic use of the place-value system in India is found in the Mankani plates from Gujarat (AD 595 – 596).
2. In the ninth century, place-values become general in inscriptions all over India.
3. The place-values have been found in Sanskrit inscriptions in South-east Asia as early as the seventh century.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 1 and 3 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ All three statements are taken as correct. The Mankani copper plates of Taralasvamin from Gujarat, dated Kalachuri year 346 (AD 595–596) in place-value numerals, are cited in standard histories as the earliest epigraphic use of the system in India, although V.V. Mirashi, who edited them in the Corpus Inscriptionum Indicarum, judged the grant spurious. Place-value notation became general in inscriptions across India by the ninth century, as in the Gwalior inscription of 876 with its zero. In South-east Asia it appears even earlier, in inscriptions dated Saka 605 (AD 683) from Cambodia and Sumatra, which also show a zero sign. (Provisional key; some sources differ.)

Q 8 | ih | Ancient India
Consider the following statements about the archaeological findings in Harappan towns:
I. There is wide occurrence of spindle-whorls in the houses but absence of spinning wheels.
II. Weights and measurement scales, complete with graduations have been discovered.
III. There are houses built in large part with baked bricks, around relatively spacious courtyards, with their own wells, bathing platforms, and large rooms.
Which of the following inferences can be drawn from the above statements?
1. Statement I suggests that spinning was a laborious activity done at home.
2. Statement II suggests the extent of the scientific knowledge that the Harappans possessed.
3. Statement III suggests the emergence of a common property system.
Select the answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 1
~ Inference 1 is valid: spindle-whorls in many houses without any spinning wheel mean yarn was spun by hand with a spindle, a slow, labour-intensive task done in the household. Inference 2 is valid: standardised cubical chert weights following a binary-then-decimal system and graduated scales (shell scale at Mohenjodaro, ivory scale at Lothal) show considerable knowledge of measurement and mathematics. Inference 3 is wrong: large baked-brick houses with their own wells, bathing platforms and courtyards point to private household property and social differentiation, not a common property system.

Q 9 | ih | Modern India & Freedom Struggle
Which one of the following statements about the Eka Movement and Bardoli Satyagraha is correct?
(1) The Eka Movement was throughout supported and organized by the Congress while Bardoli Satyagraha was initially independent of Congress influence and was only in the last stages supported by the Congress.
(2) The Eka Movement was provided leadership by the taluqdars of Awadh, whereas the Bardoli Satyagraha was a movement of the landless labourers.
(3) The Bardoli Satyagraha was a campaign against the enhancement of land revenue, while the Eka Movement was a protest against excessive extraction of rents.
(4) The Eka Movement was located in the Varanasi and Mirzapur districts of the present-day U.P., while the Bardoli Satyagraha took place in Saurashtra.
= 3
~ The Bardoli Satyagraha (1928, Surat district, Gujarat), led by Vallabhbhai Patel, resisted a roughly 22 per cent enhancement of land revenue, while the Eka (unity) movement of 1921–22 in Awadh protested against rents recorded far above the agreed level, forced labour and other exactions by landlords. Option 1 is wrong: Eka began with Congress and Khilafat support but soon slipped out of Congress control under low-caste leaders such as Madari Pasi. Option 2 is wrong: taluqdars were the targets, not leaders, and Bardoli was a movement of landholding peasants. Option 4 is wrong: Eka centred on Hardoi, Bahraich, Sitapur and Barabanki, and Bardoli is in Gujarat, not Saurashtra.

Q 10 | ih | Ancient India
Consider the following statements about the Rigvedic period:
I. Irrigation from wells allowed agriculture to expand away from flood plains and strips on river margins into the present Punjab and Haryana plains having underground water levels reasonably close to the surface.
II. Draught-animal power was employed to draw up water out of the wells.
Which of the following information support/supports the above statements?
1. There is evidence in the Rigveda of the use of ashma chakra (stone pulley wheel) and ahava (strapped wooden pails) to draw up water.
2. Mention has been made in the Rigveda of the use of implements like parashu/kulisha (axe) and datra/sreni (sickle).
3. There is a history of the use of ox, even before the Rigveda, for ploughing the land and pulling the carts.
Select the answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 1, 2 and 3
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 3 only
= 3
~ Information 1 supports the statements directly: the Rigveda (X.101) refers to wells (avata) from which water was lifted with a stone pulley wheel (ashma chakra) and strapped buckets (ahava), showing well irrigation. Q 11 | geo | Indian Geography
Consider the following assertion:
In the Pleistocene period either the Yamuna once flowed into the Indus, or the Sutlej flowed into the Yamuna and one major tributary of either had shifted from the Ganga to the Indus or vice versa.
Which of the following is/are the basis of the above assertion?
1. The Nadi-Sukta of the Rigveda
2. The explorations of the Sutlej and the Yamuna by Robert Bruce Foote
3. The presence of the same species of dolphins in both the Indus and the Ganga river systems
Select the answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) 1 and 2
(4) 3
= 4
~ The assertion rests on biological evidence. River dolphins of the genus Platanista live in both the Indus and the Ganga systems and were long treated as one species (today often split into two closely related species). Being strictly freshwater animals that cannot cross the sea, they could have reached both basins only if the two drainages were once joined, most plausibly through river capture between the Sutlej and the Yamuna across the low Indo-Gangetic divide. The Nadi-Sukta (Rigveda X.75) merely lists rivers of the Vedic age, long after the Pleistocene, and Robert Bruce Foote is known for discovering Palaeolithic tools at Pallavaram (1863) and in South India, not for exploring the Sutlej and Yamuna.

Q 12 | ih | Art & Culture
What does an empty seat represent in early Buddhist iconography?
(1) The meditation of the Buddha
(2) The Buddha's First Sermon
(3) The Buddha's Mahaparinibbana
(4) The Buddha's Mahabhinishkramana
= 1
~ In early Buddhist sculpture at Bharhut and Sanchi (c. 2nd–1st century BCE) the Buddha was not shown in human form but through symbols. An empty seat, often beneath the Bodhi tree, indicated the meditation of the Buddha leading to his enlightenment. Other symbols had other meanings: the wheel (dharmachakra) stood for the First Sermon at Sarnath, the stupa for the mahaparinibbana (his passing away), and a riderless horse for the Great Departure (mahabhinishkramana). This is the reading given in NCERT's Themes in Indian History, Part I.

Q 16 | ih | Modern India & Freedom Struggle
Which of the following factors contributed to the formation of the Forward Bloc by Subhas Chandra Bose in 1939?
1. Bose failed to win the confidence of Mahatma Gandhi.
2. The Congress Left was disunited and failed to support Bose.
3. The Communists did not support Bose in his endeavours.
4. The supporters of M.N. Roy and socialist leaders like Jayaprakash Narayan preferred Congress unity to supporting Bose.

(1) 1, 2 and 3
(2) 1, 2 and 4
(3) 1, 3 and 4
(4) 2 and 4 only
= 2
~ Bose was re-elected Congress president in 1939, defeating Gandhi's nominee Pattabhi Sitaramayya, but Gandhi took the result as his own defeat. Consider the following statements regarding the British policy in Awadh immediately after its annexation in 1856:

3. The British believed in taking revenue directly from the peasants by removing the taluqdars.

(1) 2 and 3 only
(2) 1 and 3 only
(3) 1, 2 and 3
(4) 2 only

Q 18 | ih | Modern India & Freedom Struggle
Consider the following assertion:
The genesis of political alliances based on community lay in the very nature of the Montague-Chelmsford Reforms, 1919.
Which of the following statements support/supports the above assertion?
1. Reforms retained and extended the principle of separate electorates.
2. Separate electorates were supposed to counter Indian nationalism, which was growing stronger.
3. Deprived classes rallied around the favours inherent in separate electorates.
Select the answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 2 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ All three statements support the assertion. The Government of India Act, 1919 kept the separate Muslim electorates of 1909 and extended communal representation to Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians and Europeans, with reserved seats for non-Brahmins in Madras (1). The British saw such representation as a counterweight to a nationalism that had grown stronger after the Home Rule agitation and the Lucknow Pact, dividing Indians along community lines (2). Thus alliances based on community grew out of the very design of the reforms.

Q 20 | ih | Ancient India
In which one among the following texts does the term kshetra-patni ('mistress of the field') originate?
(1) Rigveda
(2) Atharvaveda
(3) Ashtadhyayi
(4) Arthashastra
= 2
~ The Atharvaveda, rich in charms and hymns for agriculture, cattle and the household, invokes kshetrasya patni, the "mistress of the field", alongside heaven, earth and the atmosphere (Atharvaveda II.12.1). The Rigveda (IV.57) invokes the masculine Kshetrasya Pati, "lord of the field", and the furrow Sita, but not the feminine kshetra-patni. Panini's Ashtadhyayi is a work of grammar and Kautilya's Arthashastra deals with statecraft, using kshetra only in technical or administrative senses. Hence the term goes back to the Atharvaveda.

Q 21 | env | Climate Change & Pollution
Consider the following statements with reference to India's response to climate change:
I. India's Long-Term Low Emission Development Strategy (LT-LEDS) is a crucial tool for achieving net-zero emissions by 2070.
II. India's 4th Biennial Update Report (BUR-4) submitted in December, 2024 recorded around 8% decrease in Greenhouse gas emissions in 2020 over 2019.
III. Climate-resilient development necessarily depends on quick and short-term achievement of emission reduction targets.
Which of the following relationships among the above statements is/are correct?
1. Statement I is empirically supported by statement II.
2. Statement III contradicts the approach implicit in statement I.
3. Statement I and statement III together establish the premise of long-term sustainability.
Select the answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 2
(3) 2 and 3
(4) 3 only
= 2
~ India's LT-LEDS, submitted to the UNFCCC at COP27 (November 2022), maps a gradual, development-first pathway to net-zero emissions by 2070. Its BUR-4, submitted on 30 December 2024, reported that greenhouse gas emissions in 2020 were 7.93% lower than in 2019; though largely a COVID-19 effect, this is read as empirical evidence that India's emissions can be bent downward, supporting I (relationship 1 correct). Relationship 1 is the weakest link, but with 2 clearly right and 3 clearly wrong, '1 and 2' is the only code that fits.

Q 23 | env | Ecology & Ecosystems
Which of the following best explain(s) the rationale for protecting mangrove ecosystems in the context of climate resilience?
1. Mangroves reduce tidal energy and store freshwater, making them ideal sites for paddy cultivation in saline estuarine belts.
2. Their salt-sensitive roots filter seawater, making mangroves key to converting coastal land into freshwater aquaculture zones.
3. By withstanding tidal surges and offering biomass resources, mangroves function both as natural bio-shields and livelihood bases for rural communities.
Select the answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 2
(3) 2 and 3
(4) 3 only
= 4
~ Only statement 3 gives the climate-resilience rationale: dense mangrove roots and stems absorb wave and storm-surge energy, shielding coasts from cyclones and tsunamis (as seen in the 1999 Odisha super cyclone and the 2004 tsunami), while supplying fish nurseries, honey, fuelwood and other resources, besides storing blue carbon. Statement 1 is wrong: mangroves grow on saline intertidal mudflats and do not store freshwater, and clearing them for paddy destroys the ecosystem. Q 24 | eco | External Sector & Trade
In what way(s) does the Vizhinjam International Seaport represent a structural shift in India's maritime trade and logistics policy?
1. By functioning exclusively as a domestic cargo hub to reduce reliance on coastal shipping and eliminate the need for foreign collaborations.
2. By focusing primarily on passenger cruise tourism and heritage shipping to increase Kerala's profile as a maritime heritage destination.
3. By leveraging its natural deep draft and strategic location to reduce dependence on foreign trans-shipment ports, enhance revenue retention, and reposition India in regional maritime trade.
Select the answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 2
(3) 2 and 3
(4) 3 only
= 4
~ Vizhinjam, near Thiruvananthapuram, is India's first dedicated deep-water container transshipment port, built by the Kerala government with Adani Ports as a public-private partnership and dedicated to the nation on 2 May 2025. Q 25 | geo | Indian Geography
Identify the river of the Indian sub-continent on the basis of the following information:
1. It has an antecedent drainage system.
2. It flows through three countries.
3. It originates in the Tibetan Plateau and is an important river for irrigation.
4. It does not form distributaries.
Select the answer from the following:
(1) Brahmaputra
(2) Indus
(3) Sutlej
(4) Teesta
= 3
~ The Sutlej fits every clue. It rises near Rakas Tal close to Mansarovar in Tibet, cuts across the Himalaya through an antecedent gorge near Shipki La, and flows through China (Tibet), India and Pakistan. It feeds the Bhakra-Nangal and Indira Gandhi canal systems, making it vital for irrigation. The Indus and Brahmaputra are also antecedent, Tibetan and trans-boundary, but both end in deltas with many distributaries; the Teesta rises in Sikkim, not Tibet, and flows only through India and Bangladesh.

Q 26 | geo | Indian Geography
Which of the following with reference to Indian States is/are not correct?
1. Uttar Pradesh shares its boundary with the highest number of other Indian States.
2. Rajasthan shares the longest international border among all Indian States.
3. Sikkim is the only State that shares its boundary with just one other Indian State.
Select the answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 only
(2) 1 and 2
(3) 2 and 3
(4) 3 only
= 3
~ Statement 1 is correct: Uttar Pradesh touches eight States (Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, Haryana, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand and Bihar) plus the NCT of Delhi, more than any other State.
