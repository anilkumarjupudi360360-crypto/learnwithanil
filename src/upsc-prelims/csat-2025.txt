Q 1 | rc | Passages
Read the following passage and answer the question that follows. Your answer should be based on the passage only.
Maintaining an ecosystem just to conserve biodiversity will affect its commercial potential as well as the livelihoods dependent on the ecosystem. There is also a conflict between using an ecosystem only for livelihoods, for commercial exploitation, or strictly for conservation. Deforestation caused due to commercial exploitation will lead to indirect harm like floods, siltation problems and microclimatic instability, apart from adversely affecting livelihoods dependent on forests. These conflicts are particularly acute in developing countries where the dependence of people on the ecosystem is significant, and commercial exploitation has the potential to boost national income.
Which one of the following statements best reflects the critical message conveyed by the author of the passage?
(1) Conservation of biodiversity is not an issue to be worried about when some people depend on ecosystems for their livelihoods.
(2) Commercial exploitation of forests goes against the fundamental rights of the people dependent on forests for food and shelter.
(3) Sustenance of livelihood and degradation of ecosystem while being together exacerbate one another, leading to conflicts and imbalance.
(4) Commercial exploitation of ecosystems should be completely stopped.
= 3
~ The passage describes competing uses of an ecosystem: keeping it only for conservation hurts its commercial potential and dependent livelihoods, while commercial exploitation (deforestation) brings floods, siltation and microclimatic instability and also harms forest-dependent livelihoods; these conflicts are sharpest in developing countries. Of the four options, (c) comes closest to this message: livelihood needs and ecosystem degradation work against each other and produce conflict and imbalance. Option (a) contradicts the passage, which treats conservation as a real concern. Option (b) brings in "fundamental rights", which the passage never mentions. Option (d) is extreme: the passage notes that commercial exploitation can boost national income and calls for no ban.

Q 5 | qa | Number System
A natural number N is such that it can be expressed as N = p + q + r, where p, q and r are distinct factors of N. How many numbers below 50 have this property?
(1) 6
(2) 7
(3) 8
(4) 9
= 3
~ None of p, q, r can be N itself (the sum would exceed N), so write p = N/a, q = N/b, r = N/c with distinct whole numbers a, b, c ≥ 2. Then N = p + q + r gives 1/a + 1/b + 1/c = 1, whose only solution in distinct numbers is 2, 3, 6 (3, 3, 3 and 2, 4, 4 repeat). So N must be divisible by 6, and then N = N/2 + N/3 + N/6 always works, e.g. 6 = 3 + 2 + 1 and 12 = 6 + 4 + 2. The multiples of 6 below 50 are 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42 and 48, i.e. 8 numbers. A number such as 20 fails: no three of its factors 1, 2, 4, 5, 10 add up to 20.

Q 6 | qa | Number System
Three prime numbers p, q and r, each less than 20, are such that p − q = q − r. How many distinct possible values can we get for (p + q + r)?
(1) 4
(2) 5
(3) 6
(4) More than 6
= 4
~ From p − q = q − r we get p + r = 2q, so p + q + r = 3q, and the number of values equals the number of possible middle primes q. The question does not say the three primes are different, so p = q = r (common difference 0) is allowed and q can be any prime below 20: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19. That gives 8 values (6, 9, 15, 21, 33, 39, 51, 57), more than 6. If the primes had to be distinct, only q = 5 (3, 5, 7), q = 7 (3, 7, 11), q = 11 (5, 11, 17 or 3, 11, 19) and q = 13 (7, 13, 19) would work, giving 4 values, option (a); the official key follows the first reading. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 7 | qa | Number System
How many possible values of (p + q + r) are there satisfying 1/p + 1/q + 1/r = 1, where p, q and r are natural numbers (not necessarily distinct)?
(1) None
(2) One
(3) Three
(4) More than three
= 3
~ Take p ≤ q ≤ r. p = 1 is impossible (1/p alone would be 1), and since 1/p is the largest of the three terms, 1/p ≥ 1/3, so p = 2 or 3. If p = 3, then q = r = 3. If p = 2, then 1/q + 1/r = 1/2, i.e. (q − 2)(r − 2) = 4, giving (q, r) = (3, 6) or (4, 4). So the only solutions, in any order, are (3, 3, 3), (2, 4, 4) and (2, 3, 6), with sums 9, 10 and 11. Reordering p, q, r does not change the sum, so p + q + r has exactly three possible values.

Q 8 | lr | Series & Patterns
What comes at X and Y respectively in the following sequence?
January, January, December, October, X, March, October, Y, September
(1) July, May
(2) July, April
(3) June, May
(4) June, April
= 2
~ Each term is obtained by going back from the previous month by 0, 1, 2, 3, ... months in turn: January (back 0) January, (back 1) December, (back 2) October, (back 3) July, (back 4) March, (back 5) October, (back 6) April, (back 7) September. So X = July and Y = April. Check: four months before July is March and seven months before April is September, both matching the printed terms. June for X would need a step of 4, breaking the pattern.

Q 9 | qa | Arithmetic
Team X scored a total of N runs in 20 overs. Team Y tied the score in 10% less overs. Had team Y’s average run rate (runs per over) been 50% higher, the scores would have been tied in 12 overs. How many runs were scored by team X?
(1) 72
(2) 144
(3) 216
(4) Cannot be determined
= 4
~ Team Y took 10% fewer overs, i.e. 18 overs, so its run rate was N/18 runs per over. A rate 50% higher is 1.5 × N/18 = N/12 runs per over, which reaches N runs in exactly 12 overs whatever N is. So the second condition is automatically true and adds no new information. Every value of N fits: for example N = 72, 144 or 216 all satisfy both conditions (rates 4, 8, 12 per over, raised to 6, 12, 18). Hence the runs scored by team X cannot be determined.

Q 10 | qa | Arithmetic
The price (p) of a commodity is first increased by k%; then decreased by k%; again increased by k%; and again decreased by k%. If the new price is q, then what is the relation between p and q?
(1) p(10⁴ − k²)² = q × 10⁸
(2) p(10⁴ − k²)² = q × 10⁴
(3) p(10⁴ − k²) = q × 10⁴
(4) p(10⁴ − k²) = q × 10⁸
= 1
~ An increase of k% multiplies the price by (100 + k)/100 and a decrease by (100 − k)/100. After two increases and two decreases, q = p × (100 + k)²(100 − k)²/100⁴ = p(10⁴ − k²)²/10⁸, since (100 + k)(100 − k) = 10⁴ − k² and 100⁴ = 10⁸. Hence p(10⁴ − k²)² = q × 10⁸. Check with p = 100, k = 10: 100 → 110 → 99 → 108.9 → 98.01, and 100 × 9900²/10⁸ = 98.01. Option (c) would hold after only one increase and one decrease; (b) and (d) use the wrong power of 10.

Q 15 | qa | Geometry & Mensuration
A solid cube is painted yellow on all its faces. The cube is then cut into 60 smaller but equal pieces by making the minimum number of cuts. Which of the following statements is/are correct?
I. The minimum number of cuts is 9.
II. The number of smaller pieces which are not painted on any face is 6.
Select the correct answer using the code given below :
(1) I only
(2) II only
(3) Both I and II
(4) Neither I nor II
= 3
~ Cutting the cube into an a × b × c arrangement of equal pieces needs (a − 1) + (b − 1) + (c − 1) cuts, which is least when a, b and c are as close as possible. 60 = 3 × 4 × 5 needs 2 + 3 + 4 = 9 cuts; other splits need more (2 × 5 × 6 needs 10, 2 × 3 × 10 needs 12, 1 × 6 × 10 needs 14). So Statement I is correct. The pieces with no painted face are the inner ones: (3 − 2) × (4 − 2) × (5 − 2) = 1 × 2 × 3 = 6, so Statement II is also correct. Hence both I and II.

Q 16 | lr | Series & Patterns
If 7 * 24 = 25 and 12 * 16 = 20, then what is 16 * 63 equal to?
(1) 70
(2) 66
(3) 65
(4) 64
= 3
~ The rule is a * b = √(a² + b²), the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs a and b. Check: 7² + 24² = 49 + 576 = 625 = 25², and 12² + 16² = 144 + 256 = 400 = 20². So 16 * 63 = √(16² + 63²) = √(256 + 3969) = √4225 = 65. Indeed 16, 63, 65 is a Pythagorean triple. The other options (70, 66, 64) are not the square root of 4225.

Q 17 | qa | Arithmetic
The petrol price shot up by 10% as a result of the hike in crude oil prices. The price of petrol before the hike was ₹ 90 per litre. A person travels 2200 km every month and his car gives a mileage of 16 km per litre. By how many km should he reduce his travel if he wants to maintain his expenditure at the previous level?
(1) 180 km
(2) 200 km
(3) 220 km
(4) 240 km
= 2
~ Earlier monthly spending = (2200 ÷ 16) litres × ₹90 = 137.5 × 90 = ₹12,375. The new price is ₹99 per litre, so the same money buys 12,375 ÷ 99 = 125 litres, enough for 125 × 16 = 2000 km. He must therefore cut his travel by 2200 − 2000 = 200 km. Shortcut: with a 10% price rise, petrol use must fall to 100/110 = 10/11 of the original, i.e. by 1/11 of 2200 km = 200 km. Option (c) 220 km wrongly cuts travel by 10%.

Q 18 | qa | Number System
A 4-digit number N is such that when divided by 3, 5, 6, 9 leaves a remainder 1, 3, 4, 7 respectively. What is the smallest value of N?
(1) 1068
(2) 1072
(3) 1078
(4) 1082
= 3
~ In every case the remainder is 2 less than the divisor (3 − 1 = 5 − 3 = 6 − 4 = 9 − 7 = 2), so N + 2 is divisible by 3, 5, 6 and 9, hence by their LCM, 90. The multiples of 90 near 1000 are 990, giving N = 988 (only three digits), and 1080, giving N = 1078, the smallest 4-digit value. Check: 1078 = 3 × 359 + 1 = 5 × 215 + 3 = 6 × 179 + 4 = 9 × 119 + 7. The other options fail: for example 1068 leaves remainder 3 on division by 5 but 0 on division by 3.

Q 19 | lr | Logical Reasoning
Consider the following statements :
I. If A ≤ B > C < D > E > F ≥ G = H; then B is always greater than E.
II. If P > Q = R ≥ S = T ≤ U = V > W; then S is always less than V.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) I only
(2) II only
(3) Both I and II
(4) Neither I nor II
= 4
~ Statement I: B > C and C < D > E give no fixed link between B and E, because the chain changes direction at C. For example A = 0, B = 1, C = 0, D = 2, E = 1, F = G = H = 0 satisfies every sign but gives B = E (B < E is also possible), so B is not always greater than E. Statement II: S = T ≤ U = V gives only S ≤ V, which allows S = V; for example P = 2, Q = R = S = T = U = V = 1, W = 0 satisfies every sign. So S is not always less than V. Hence neither statement is correct.

Q 20 | qa | Number System
What is the unit digit in the multiplication of 1 × 3 × 5 × 7 × 9 × ... × 999?
(1) 1
(2) 3
(3) 5
(4) 9
= 3
~ The product contains the factor 5, and all the factors are odd, so the product is an odd multiple of 5. An odd multiple of 5 always ends in 5: it could end in 0 only if it contained a factor 2, which no odd number has. Hence the unit digit is 5. Check with the first few terms: 1 × 3 × 5 = 15, 15 × 7 = 105, 105 × 9 = 945, and every further odd factor keeps the last digit 5 (5 × odd ends in 5).

~ Value-I: the average of 11 consecutive integers is the middle (6th) number, so it is smallest when the set starts as low as allowed, at −5. The set −5, −4, …, 5 has average 0, so Value-I = 0. Value-II: the numbers are consecutive non-negative integers, so the product can never be negative; if the set contains 0 (0, 1, …, 10) the product is 0, so Value-II = 0. Both values are 0. Hence option (c).

Q 78 | qa | Arithmetic
The average of three numbers p, q and r is k. p is as much more than the average as q is less than the average. What is the value of r?
(1) k
(2) k − 1
(3) k + 1
(4) k/2
= 1
~ Let p = k + d and q = k − d, since p exceeds the average by the same amount d by which q falls short of it. The sum of the three numbers is 3k, so (k + d) + (k − d) + r = 3k, giving 2k + r = 3k and r = k. Check: with p = 7, q = 3 and r = 5, the average is 15 ÷ 3 = 5 = k, and 7 is 2 above while 3 is 2 below. The deviations of p and q cancel, so r must equal the average itself. Hence option (a).

Q 79 | qa | Algebra
Let x be a real number between 0 and 1. Which of the following statements is/are correct?
I. x² > x³.
II. x > √x.
Select the correct answer using the code given below :
(1) I only
(2) II only
(3) Both I and II
(4) Neither I nor II
= 1
~ For 0 < x < 1, multiplying by x (a positive number less than 1) makes a quantity smaller. Statement I: x² − x³ = x²(1 − x) > 0, so x² > x³ is correct (e.g., x = 0.5: 0.25 > 0.125). Statement II: x = (√x)² and √x also lies between 0 and 1, so x < √x; the statement x > √x is wrong (e.g., x = 0.25: √x = 0.5, which is larger than 0.25). Hence I only, option (a).

Q 80 | qa | Number System
The difference between any two natural numbers is 10. What can be said about the natural numbers which are divisible by 5 and lie between these two numbers?
(1) There is only one such number.
(2) There are only two such numbers.
(3) There can be more than one such number.
(4) No such number exists.
= 3
~ Let the numbers be a and a + 10. If a is itself a multiple of 5 (e.g., 5 and 15), exactly one multiple of 5 lies strictly between them (10). If a is not a multiple of 5 (e.g., 3 and 13), two multiples lie between them (5 and 10). So the count is 1 or 2 depending on the pair: it can be more than one, but neither “only one” nor “only two” always holds, and since any 9 consecutive numbers between them include at least one multiple of 5, option (d) is wrong. Hence option (c).
