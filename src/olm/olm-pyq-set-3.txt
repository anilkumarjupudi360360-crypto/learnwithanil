Real questions from OSSSC's Forester, Forest Guard & Excise Constable CBT (CRE-2025 II), 16 April - 3 May 2026 (11 shifts), read from candidates' official response sheets; the shift is in brackets before each question; answers: the option OSSSC's answer key marks correct on the response sheet

Q 1 | liv | SHGs & NRLM
[OSSSC Forester, Forest Guard & Excise Constable 2026 (20 Apr, Shift 3)]
Self-Help Groups (SHGs) play an important role in rural development. What is their main function in rural areas?
(1) Supplying fertilisers and seeds to farmers
(2) Providing subsidies to farmers
(3) Providing loans to members
(4) Purchasing crops at minimum support prices
= 3
~ An SHG's core function is mobilising its members' small savings and lending among themselves (internal lending), and it later gets bank linkage for bigger loans. Supplying seeds or fertiliser, giving subsidies and buying at MSP are jobs of cooperatives, government departments and agencies, not SHGs' main role. Under DAY-NRLM and OLM, SHGs usually have 10–20 members and get a Revolving Fund and Community Investment Fund before bank linkage.

Q 9 | rd | Poverty & Employment
[OSSSC Forester, Forest Guard & Excise Constable 2026 (2 May, Shift 1)]
Which of the following indicators is NOT included in the National Multidimensional Poverty Index of India?
(1) Electricity
(2) Nutrition
(3) Years of schooling
(4) Exchange rate
= 4
~ NITI Aayog's National Multidimensional Poverty Index uses 12 indicators under three equal-weight dimensions. Health covers nutrition, child and adolescent mortality, and maternal health. Education covers years of schooling and school attendance. Standard of living covers cooking fuel, sanitation, drinking water, electricity, housing, assets and bank account. Electricity, nutrition and years of schooling are all indicators, but the exchange rate is a macroeconomic variable and is not part of the index. The bank-account indicator links the MPI directly to financial inclusion work under NRLM.

Q 10 | rd | Poverty & Employment

Which of the following government schemes is included under SDG Goal 2?
(1) National Social Assistance Programme
(2) PM Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana
(3) National Food Security Mission
(4) Atal Pension Yojana
= 3

(1) Organisation for Economic Cooperation and Development

Q 12 | rd | Poverty & Employment
[OSSSC Forester, Forest Guard & Excise Constable 2026 (18 Apr, Shift 1)]
Which of the following best describes structural unemployment?
(1) Unemployment caused by seasonal changes in agricultural production
(2) Unemployment caused by mismatch between skills and job requirements
(3) Unemployment caused by temporary slowdown in industrial activities
(4) Unemployment caused by population growth and labour force increase
= 2
~ Structural unemployment happens when workers' skills or location don't match the jobs on offer, often after a change in technology or in how the economy is organised. Unemployment that depends on the farm season is seasonal unemployment. A temporary slowdown in industry causes cyclical unemployment. Labour-force growth alone is not the definition of any of these types. Skilling schemes such as DDU-GKY and RSETIs, which OLM aspirants should know, are meant to tackle exactly this skills mismatch among rural youth.

Q 13 | rd | Poverty & Employment
[OSSSC Forester, Forest Guard & Excise Constable 2026 (18 Apr, Shift 2)]
Which major Indian social welfare legislation was replaced by the Viksit Bharat–Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission Act, 2025, to address rural employment and livelihood?
(1) Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act
(2) Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MGNREGA)
(3) Pradhan Mantri Kaushal Vikas Yojana (PMKVY)
(4) National Rural Livelihood Mission (NRLM)
= 2
~ The Viksit Bharat–Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Gramin) Act, 2025 (VB-G RAM G) got Presidential assent in December 2025. It replaced the Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act, 2005 as the legal basis for rural wage employment. PMKVY is a skilling scheme and NRLM (DAY-NRLM) is the SHG-based livelihoods mission, and neither is an Act. Child labour law has nothing to do with rural employment guarantees. OLM aspirants should note that the SHG-based NRLM, under which OLM works, continues as before.

Q 16 | rd | Panchayati Raj
[OSSSC Forester, Forest Guard & Excise Constable 2026 (20 Apr, Shift 2)]
Which of the following is the purpose of involving Panchayati Raj Institutions in the implementation of development programmes in India?
(1) To supervise planning and management related to regional urban development.
(2) To centralise authority and strengthen administrative control at local levels.
(3) To enhance state power and regulate community affairs more effectively.
(4) To decentralise development planning and ensure effective local implementation.
= 4
~ PRIs are used to implement development programmes so that planning is decentralised and local bodies can implement schemes with people's participation. This is the idea behind the 73rd Amendment (1992) and the Eleventh Schedule's 29 subjects. Centralising authority or increasing state control is the opposite of that aim. Urban development falls under municipalities (74th Amendment), not PRIs. In Odisha, Gram Panchayats and OLM's SHG federations work together on village planning, for example in the Gram Panchayat Development Plan.

Q 17 | fin | Cooperatives & Agri Marketing
[OSSSC Forester, Forest Guard & Excise Constable 2026 (3 May, Shift 1)]
Which of the following is a government policy instrument used to protect farmers’ income?
(1) Minimum Support Price
(2) Foreign Exchange Control
(3) Import Duty Reduction
(4) Income Tax Reduction
= 1
~ The Minimum Support Price (MSP) is the price at which the government buys notified crops such as paddy. It protects farmers' income against a fall in market prices. Foreign-exchange control, lower import duties and income-tax cuts are general trade or fiscal tools, not farm-income support instruments, and cheaper imports can actually hurt farm prices. In Odisha, paddy is bought at MSP through cooperative societies (PACS) and SHG-run procurement centres.

(2) East Coast Expressway

(1) Child marriage ban

Q 23 | gk | Odisha GK
[OSSSC Forester, Forest Guard & Excise Constable 2026 (3 May, Shift 1)]
Which crop is considered the staple and most widely cultivated crop in Odisha?
(1) Wheat
(2) Rice
(3) Cotton
(4) Maize
= 2
~ Rice (paddy) is Odisha's staple food and its most widely grown crop. It covers most of the state's net sown area, especially in kharif. Wheat needs a cooler winter and is grown very little in Odisha. Cotton is grown mainly in parts of Kalahandi, Rayagada and Bolangir, and maize mostly in the tribal uplands, so neither is the main crop. Odisha's 'Samruddha Krushak Yojana' pays an assured paddy price that includes an input subsidy on top of MSP.

Q 24 | gk | Odisha GK
[OSSSC Forester, Forest Guard & Excise Constable 2026 (3 May, Shift 1)]
Which part of Odisha is most vulnerable to cyclones due to its geography?
(1) Northern Highlands
(2) Western Odisha
(3) Coastal Plains
(4) Southern Plateau region
= 3
~ Odisha's coastal plains (the Mahanadi–Brahmani–Baitarani delta and the Ganjam–Puri coast) face the Bay of Bengal. Because they are low-lying and flat, they bear the brunt of cyclone winds and storm surge. The 1999 Super Cyclone, Phailin (2013) and Fani (2019) all hit this belt. Western Odisha and the northern and southern uplands are inland and more prone to drought than to cyclones. Women's SHGs in coastal blocks help run cyclone shelters and community kitchens during evacuations.

Q 27 | gk | Odisha GK

Originating in Odisha's Kalahandi district, the Vansadhara river eventually flows into the Bay of Bengal from which of the below Indian states?
(1) West Bengal
(2) Kerala
(3) Tamil Nadu
(4) Andhra Pradesh
= 4
~ The Vansadhara (Vamsadhara) rises in the Eastern Ghats near Lanjigarh in Kalahandi district. Q 31 | gk | Odisha GK
[OSSSC Forester, Forest Guard & Excise Constable 2026 (16 Apr, Shift 2)]
Which of the following correctly reflects the climate vulnerability of Odisha?
(1) Rare occurrence of extreme events since 1965
(2) Only droughts have affected the state
(3) No major cyclones after 1999
(4) Frequent floods, cyclones and heatwaves
= 4
~ Odisha is one of India's most climate-vulnerable states. It faces frequent floods (Mahanadi delta), cyclones (1999 Super Cyclone, Phailin, Fani, Dana) and severe summer heatwaves, sometimes along with droughts in western districts. Q 32 | gk | Odisha GK
[OSSSC Forester, Forest Guard & Excise Constable 2026 (16 Apr, Shift 2)]
The climate of Odisha is primarily characterized as which type?
(1) Mediterranean
(2) Temperate
(3) Arid
(4) Tropical
= 4
~ Odisha has a tropical monsoon climate: hot summers, heavy south-west monsoon rain (around 1,450 mm a year) and mild winters. It lies wholly within the tropics, south of the Tropic of Cancer. 'Mediterranean' (winter rain), 'temperate' and 'arid' climates do not describe the state. Because the monsoon decides kharif paddy, rainfall variability directly affects rural livelihoods in Odisha.

Q 34 | gk | Odisha GK
