Q 1 | rd | Rural Development Programmes
[UPPSC RO/ARO Prelims 2025]
Which one of the following is a correct match ?
(1) Grow More Food Campaign – Jawaharlal Nehru
(2) Etawah Pilot Project – Albert Myre
(3) Co-operative Movement – Mahatma Gandhi
(4) Sevagram Project – Vinoba Bhave
= 2
~ The Etawah Pilot Project (1948, Mahewa, Etawah district, UP) was a rural development experiment led by the American town planner Albert Mayer; it became a model for the Community Development Programme of 1952. The Grow More Food Campaign began in 1943 under the British government, the co-operative movement began with the 1904 Act, and Sevagram is linked with Mahatma Gandhi.

Q 2 | rd | Rural Development Programmes
[UPPSC RO/ARO Prelims 2021]

(I) Launching of Community Development Programme (CDP)

(III) Initiation of Key Village Scheme (KVS)
(IV) Setting up of District Rural Development Agency (DRDA) at district level
Select correct answer from the code given below -
(1) (I), (II), (III), (IV)
(2) (II), (I), (III), (IV)
(3) (III), (I), (II), (IV)

Q 3 | rd | Rural Development Programmes
[APPSC Executive Officer 2026 Paper 1]
Integrated Rural Development Program envisages
(1) Self-employment programme for the rural poor
(2) To take over the functions of the erstwhile agricultural credit
(3) To help scheduled castes and tribes with adequate credit facility
(4) Assistance to poor rural families to come up above the poverty line
= 4
~ The Integrated Rural Development Programme (IRDP), started in 1978 and extended across the country in 1980, aimed to help identified rural poor families rise above the poverty line. (2) Control of pollution in cities
(3) Rural development

Q 18 | rd | Poverty & Employment
[OPSC ASO 2022 Paper 1]
Which of the following is accountable for the assessment of poverty ?
(1) HRD Ministry
(2) Ministry of Defence
(3) NITI Aayog
(4) Home Ministry
= 3
~ NITI Aayog is now the body responsible for assessing poverty in India. Earlier this was done by the Planning Commission, using expert groups such as the Lakdawala (1993), Tendulkar (2009) and Rangarajan (2014) committees, and household consumption data from the National Sample Survey. When NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission on 1 January 2015, this role passed to it. NITI Aayog set up a Task Force on Elimination of Poverty in 2015 and, with the help of the Oxford Poverty and Human Development Initiative and UNDP, released the National Multidimensional Poverty Index, which measures health, education and standard of living through 12 indicators. Its 2023 report estimated that about 13.5 crore people moved out of multidimensional poverty between 2015-16 and 2019-21. The HRD (now Education), Defence and Home Ministries have no role in poverty estimation. The Ministry of Statistics (MoSPI) provides survey data, but the assessment is done by NITI Aayog. Memory tip: Planning Commission before 2015, NITI Aayog after. Trap: MPI measures deprivations, not just income.

Q 19 | rd | Poverty & Employment
[OPSC ASO 2025 Paper 1]
Which data is used to measure Multidimensional Poverty Index in India ?
(1) Human Development Index
(2) National Family Health Survey
(3) Gini Coefficient
(4) Poverty Gap Index
= 2
~ In India the Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) is measured with data from the National Family Health Survey (NFHS), a large household survey of the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, run through the International Institute for Population Sciences, Mumbai. NITI Aayog's National MPI uses the Alkire–Foster method of the Oxford Poverty and Human Development Initiative (OPHI), the same method used in the global MPI of UNDP and OPHI, and covers three equal dimensions, health, education and standard of living, through 12 indicators such as nutrition, years of schooling, cooking fuel, sanitation, housing and bank accounts. Its 2023 progress review, comparing NFHS-4 (2015–16) with NFHS-5 (2019–21), estimated that about 13.5 crore people moved out of multidimensional poverty. The other options are not data sources: the Human Development Index (A) is UNDP's composite index of health, education and income; the Gini coefficient (C) measures inequality; and the Poverty Gap Index (D) measures how far the poor fall below the poverty line. Trap: MPI counts deprivations, not income, so it uses NFHS rather than consumption surveys.

Q 20 | rd | Poverty & Employment
[UPSC CSE Prelims 2026]
Consider the following statements about Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI):
1. MPI is calculated using Alkire-Foster methodology.
2. MPI calculated by NITI Aayog has a total of twelve indicators.
3. Maternal Health and Bank Account are common indicators in the MPI of NITI Aayog and MPI of United Nations Development Programme (UNDP).
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 1, 2 and 3
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 2 only
= 1
~ Both the global MPI and NITI Aayog's National MPI use the Alkire-Foster method developed at OPHI, Oxford, which counts overlapping deprivations of a household, so statement 1 is correct. NITI Aayog's National MPI covers three equally weighted dimensions with 12 indicators: nutrition, child and adolescent mortality, maternal health (health); years of schooling, school attendance (education); and cooking fuel, sanitation, drinking water, electricity, housing, assets and bank account (standard of living), so statement 2 is correct. The UNDP-OPHI global MPI has only 10 indicators; maternal health and bank account are India's additions, so they are not common indicators and statement 3 is wrong. Hence 1 and 2 only.

Q 25 | rd | Panchayati Raj
[UPSC CSE Prelims 2025]
Consider the following statements:
I. Panchayats at the intermediate level exist in all States.
II. To be eligible to be a Member of a Panchayat at the intermediate level, a person should attain the age of thirty years.
III. The Chief Minister of a State constitutes a commission to review the financial position of Panchayats at the intermediate levels and to make recommendations regarding the distribution of net proceeds of taxes and duties, leviable by the State, between the State and Panchayats at the intermediate level.
Which of the statements given above are not correct?
(1) I and II only
(2) II and III only
(3) I and III only
(4) I, II and III
= 4
~ None of the three statements is correct. Statement I: under Article 243B(2), a State with a population not exceeding twenty lakh need not constitute Panchayats at the intermediate level, so small States such as Goa and Sikkim have only two tiers. Statement II: under Article 243F(1)(a) a person who has attained 21 years can be a member of a Panchayat, although the general age for a State Legislature is 25; there is no thirty-year rule. Statement III: under Article 243-I it is the Governor, not the Chief Minister, who constitutes a Finance Commission every five years to review the finances of Panchayats and recommend how State taxes, duties, tolls and fees are shared between the State and the Panchayats at all levels.

Q 26 | rd | Panchayati Raj

(2) This is the symbol of direct democracy
(3) It is expected to review the functioning of the Grama Panchayat

Q 32 | rd | Panchayati Raj
[OPSC ASO 2025 Paper 1]
The National Institute of Rural Development and Panchayati Raj (NIRDPR) is located in which of the following cities ?
(1) Cuttack
(2) New Delhi
(3) Pune
(4) Hyderabad
= 4
~ The National Institute of Rural Development and Panchayati Raj (NIRDPR) is at Rajendranagar in Hyderabad, Telangana. It is an autonomous body under the Union Ministry of Rural Development and India's apex institute for training, research and consultancy in rural development and Panchayati Raj. Its roots go back to a community-development institute set up at Mussoorie in 1958, which became the National Institute of Community Development in 1962 and moved to Hyderabad in 1964–65. It also has a North-Eastern Regional Centre at Guwahati, while each State runs its own State Institute of Rural Development (SIRD) to train local officials and panchayat members. The other cities host different national bodies: Cuttack (A) has ICAR's National Rice Research Institute, Pune (C) has VAMNICOM for cooperative management, and New Delhi (B) is the seat of the Union Ministry of Rural Development, not of NIRDPR. Trap: Hyderabad is also home to the Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel National Police Academy and the National Institute of Nutrition, so link each institute to its city carefully.

Q 40 | fin | Financial Inclusion Schemes

= 3

Q 46 | fin | Cooperatives & Agri Marketing
[UPSC EPFO EO/AO 2023]

1. There is negligence in the performance of duties.
2. There is any act prejudicial to the interest of the co-operative society or its members.
3. The body has failed to conduct elections in accordance with the provisions of the State Act.
4. There is no Government shareholdings or loan or financial assistance or any guarantee by the Government.
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 1 and 3 only
(3) 3 and 4 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ Option 4 is correct: 1, 2 and 3. Article 243ZL(1), added by the 97th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2011, says no co-operative board shall be superseded or kept under suspension for more than six months. It permits supersession or suspension only for persistent default, negligence in the performance of duties (statement 1), an act prejudicial to the interests of the society or its members (statement 2), a stalemate in the constitution or functions of the board, or the failure of the State's election authority to conduct elections as per the State Act (statement 3).
