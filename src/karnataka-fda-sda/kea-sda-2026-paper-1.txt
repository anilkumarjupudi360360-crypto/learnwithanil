# KEA SDA-level (RPC) Paper-1 General Knowledge, held 25 January 2026, version B1; answers: KEA provisional key of 27 January 2026 with KEA's revised key of 30 January 2026

Q 1 | pol | Judiciary & Constitutional Bodies
Which among the following Tribunal/Commission settles disputes related to recruitment and all service matters of Central Government employees ?
(1) Central Administrative Tribunal
(2) Union Public Service Commission
(3) State Administrative Tribunal
(4) Securities Appellate Tribunal
= 1
~ The Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT), set up in 1985 under the Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985 (made under Article 323A), decides disputes on recruitment and service conditions of Central Government employees. UPSC only conducts recruitment and advises; State Administrative Tribunals deal with State employees; the Securities Appellate Tribunal hears appeals against SEBI orders.

Q 2 | pol | Constitution & Rights
Who has the power to prescribe qualifications to acquire Indian citizenship ?
(1) The President
(2) The Governor
(3) The State Legislature
(4) The Parliament
= 4
~ Article 11 empowers Parliament to make any provision on the acquisition and termination of citizenship; citizenship is also in the Union List. Using this power Parliament enacted the Citizenship Act, 1955, which lays down how citizenship is acquired (birth, descent, registration, naturalisation, incorporation of territory).

Q 3 | pol | Constitution & Rights
Consider the following statements regarding secularism in the Constitution of India :
Statement I : The term ‘Secular’ was added to the Preamble by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act of 1976.
Statement II : Articles 25 – 28, guaranteeing freedom of religion, mandate India to be a secular state.
Which one of the following is correct with respect to the above statements ?
(1) Both Statement I and Statement II are correct and Statement II explains Statement I.
(2) Both Statement I and Statement II are correct, but Statement II does not explain Statement I.
(3) Statement I is correct, but Statement II is incorrect.
(4) Statement I is incorrect, but Statement II is correct.
= 1
~ The 42nd Amendment (1976) added 'Socialist' and 'Secular' to the Preamble, so Statement I is right. Articles 25–28 (freedom of conscience and religion, freedom to manage religious affairs, no tax for promoting a religion, no religious instruction in State-funded institutions) already made the Indian State secular; the 1976 amendment only made this explicit, so per KEA's key Statement II explains Statement I.

Q 4 | pol | Panchayat Raj & Local Bodies
The minimum age limit to contest elections in the Local Self-Government is :
(1) 21 years
(2) 25 years
(3) 30 years
(4) 35 years
= 1
~ Article 243F (panchayats) and Article 243V (municipalities) say a person shall not be disqualified on the ground of age if he or she has attained 21 years. So the minimum age to contest panchayat and municipal elections is 21 years (compared with 25 for MLA/MP in Lok Sabha and 30 for Rajya Sabha/MLC).

Q 5 | pol | Constitution & Rights
Identify the Article and Part, with reference to Amendment of the Constitution :
(1) Article 366 and Part XI
(2) Article 367 and Part XI
(3) Article 368 and Part XX
(4) Article 368 and Part XXI
= 3
~ Part XX of the Constitution contains a single article, Article 368, which gives Parliament the power to amend the Constitution and lays down the procedure. Articles 366 (definitions) and 367 (interpretation) are in Part XIX; Part XI deals with Centre–State relations and Part XXI with temporary, transitional and special provisions.

Q 6 | pol | Judiciary & Constitutional Bodies
Read the following statements regarding the National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (STs) :
a. The Commission is a constitutional body.
b. Article 338A provides for the establishment of the Commission.
c. The separate National Commission for STs came into existence in 2004.
d. The President of India is the Chairperson of the Commission.
Identify the correct statements :
(1) a, c and d
(2) a, b and d
(3) a, b and c
(4) b, c and d
= 3
~ The 89th Amendment Act, 2003 inserted Article 338A and bifurcated the old joint commission, so a separate National Commission for STs, a constitutional body, came into existence in 2004. Q 8 | pol | Union & State Government
A new All-India Service can be created by :
(1) A resolution of the Rajya Sabha
(2) An act of Parliament
(3) An order of the President
(4) A resolution of the UPSC
= 1
~ Under Article 312, a new All-India Service can be created only after the Rajya Sabha passes a resolution, supported by two-thirds of the members present and voting, declaring it necessary in the national interest; Parliament then sets it up by law. The Rajya Sabha resolution is the decisive special step, so KEA keys option 1. Neither the President nor UPSC can create a service.

Q 9 | pol | Constitution & Rights
Consider the following statements regarding 101st Constitutional Amendment of the Constitution of India :
a. It was passed by the Parliament in 2015.
b. It paved the way for GST.
c. It provided for the establishment of the GST Council.
Which of the above statements is/are incorrect ?
(1) Only a
(2) Only a and b
(3) a, b and c
(4) Only b and c
= 1
~ The 122nd Amendment Bill (GST) was passed by the Lok Sabha in 2015, but Parliament completed its passage only in August 2016 (Rajya Sabha 3 Aug, Lok Sabha 8 Aug); it received assent on 8 September 2016 as the 101st Amendment. So statement a is incorrect. It paved the way for GST and inserted Article 279A creating the GST Council, so b and c are correct.

Q 10 | pol | Panchayat Raj & Local Bodies
Who appoints the Municipal Commissioner of the Municipal Corporation ?
(1) The President of India
(2) The Mayor
(3) The Governor
(4) The State Government
= 4
~ The Municipal Commissioner is the chief executive officer of a Municipal Corporation and is appointed by the State Government, usually from the IAS. The Mayor is the elected (largely ceremonial) head of the corporation; the President and Governor have no role in this appointment.

Q 11 | pol | Constitution & Rights
Read the statements with regard to the Directive Principles of State Policy :
a. The Directive Principles of State Policy are incorporated in Part IV of the Constitution.
b. The Directive Principles of State Policy are legally enforceable.
c. The Directive Principles of State Policy are legally justiciable.
Identify the correct statement/s :
(1) Only a
(2) b and c
(3) Only b
(4) a and b
= 1
~ The DPSPs are in Part IV (Articles 36–51). Article 37 says they shall not be enforceable by any court, though they are fundamental in governance and the State must apply them in making laws. So they are neither legally enforceable nor justiciable; only a is correct. (The Kannada version of b reads 'can be implemented through law', but the intended sense, as in English, is enforceability in court.)

Q 12 | pol | Constitution & Rights
Consider the following statements with regard to the Government of India Act, 1935 :
a. The Act divided the powers between the Centre and the Provinces.
b. The Act provided for the adoption of diarchy at the Centre.
c. The Act introduced Universal Adult Franchise.
d. The Act provided for the establishment of the Reserve Bank of India.
Identify the correct statements :
(1) a, b and c
(2) a, b and d
(3) a, c and d
(4) b, c and d
= 2
~ The 1935 Act divided powers through Federal, Provincial and Concurrent Lists (a), abolished provincial diarchy but provided for diarchy at the Centre (b, never implemented), and provided for the establishment of the Reserve Bank of India (d). Q 14 | pol | Union & State Government
Consider the following statements with regard to India’s Central Legislature :

c. In the Union Legislature, the Upper House is more powerful than the Lower House.

(1) Only a and b
(2) Only a and c
(3) Only a, b and d
(4) Only a, b and c

Q 16 | his | Modern India & Freedom Struggle
Consider the following statements regarding the 1857 Revolt of Delhi :
Statement I : The last Mughal emperor, Bahadur Shah II was declared as the ‘Emperor of India’ and proclaimed as the leader of the Delhi revolt.
Statement II : The British military officer Sir John Nicholson captured Delhi.

(2) Both Statement I and Statement II are correct, but Statement II does not explain Statement I.

Q 17 | his | Modern India & Freedom Struggle
Consider the following statements regarding Bengal :

Statement II : The British were given the Diwani rights to collect revenue and the Nizam was given the responsibility of administration, law and order.
Which one of the following is correct with respect to the above statements ?
(1) Both Statement I and Statement II are correct and Statement II explains Statement I.
(2) Both Statement I and Statement II are correct, but Statement II does not explain Statement I.

= 1

Q 18 | his | Medieval India
In 1672, Shivaji’s army defeated the Mughals in which battle ?
(1) Battle of Pratapgad
(2) Battle of Salher
(3) Battle of Torna
(4) Battle of Purandar
= 2
~ At Salher (Nashik district) in 1672 the Maratha army under Prataprao Gujar and Moropant Pingle defeated a large Mughal force, one of the first major open-field Maratha victories over the Mughals. Pratapgad (1659) was against Bijapur's Afzal Khan, Torna was captured from Bijapur in 1646, and at Purandar (1665) Shivaji had to sign a treaty with the Mughal general Jai Singh.

Q 19 | his | Art & Culture
In which of the following places Stupa was not built ?
(1) Bharhut
(2) Sanchi
(3) Sarnath
(4) Karle
= 4
~ Bharhut and Sanchi (Madhya Pradesh) and Sarnath (Dhamek Stupa, Uttar Pradesh) are famous for structural (built) stupas. Karle (Karla, Maharashtra) is famous for its rock-cut Buddhist chaitya hall; the stupa there is carved out of the rock inside the hall, not built, so Karle is the answer.

Q 37 | sci | Chemistry
