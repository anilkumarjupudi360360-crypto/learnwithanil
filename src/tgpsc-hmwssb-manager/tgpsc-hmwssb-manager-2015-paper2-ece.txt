Q 1 | nwk | AC Circuits & Resonance
A capacitor C is connected across a coil with resistance R and inductance L. The effective [impedance] of the circuit at resonance is
(1) 1/(RLC)
(2) RC/L
(3) L/(RC)
(4) L/√(RC)
= 3
~ For a coil (R in series with L) in parallel with C, the admittance at resonance is purely real: Y = R/(R² + ω₀²L²), and the resonance condition gives R² + ω₀²L² = L/C. So the effective (dynamic) impedance is Z = 1/Y = (L/C)/R = L/(RC), a pure resistance that is large when R is small. (The booklet leaves out the word "impedance"; it is added in square brackets.)

Q 3 | nwk | AC Circuits & Resonance
The current i(t) through a 10Ω resistor in series with an inductance is given by i(t) = 3 + 4 sin(100t + 45°) + 4sin(300t + 60°) Amperes. The rms value of the current and the power dissipated in the circuit are

(2) 10A, 350W
(3) 5A, 250W
(4) 11A, 250W
= 3

Q 4 | nwk | Network Laws & Theorems
An ideal voltage source and current sources are connected in parallel. This circuit has
(1) neither Thevenin nor Norton's equivalent
(2) both Thevinin and Norton's equivalent
(3) a Thevenin equivalent but not Norton's equivalent
(4) a Norton's equivalent but not Thevenin equivalent
= 3
~ An ideal voltage source fixes the terminal voltage whatever current sources are in parallel with it, so the combination is just that voltage source: a Thevenin equivalent with V_th = V and R_th = 0. A Norton equivalent would need I_N = V_th/R_th = V/0, which is infinite, so it does not exist.

Q 6 | nwk | Two-Port Networks & Network Synthesis
F(s) = (s+1)(s+3)/(s(s+2)) represents an
(1) RC impedance and an RL admittance
(2) RL admittance
(3) RC impedance
(4) RC admittance
= 1
~ The poles (0, −2) and zeros (−1, −3) are simple, lie on the negative real axis and alternate, with a pole nearest the origin, and F(0) = ∞ > F(∞) = 1: these are the properties of an RC driving-point impedance, which are exactly those of an RL driving-point admittance. Expanding, F(s) = 1 + 1.5/s + 0.5/(s + 2): as an impedance this is 1 Ω in series with a 2/3 F capacitor and a parallel 2 F, 0.25 Ω section; as an admittance it is 1 S in parallel with a 2/3 H inductor and a branch of 2 H in series with 4 Ω. So it represents both.

Q 8 | edv | Semiconductor Physics
Doping materials are called impurities because they
(1) change the temperature of the material
(2) alter the crystal structure of the pure semiconductor
(3) change the chemical properties of semiconductors
(4) decrease the number of charge carriers
= 3
~ Dopants are foreign (group III or group V) atoms added to an otherwise pure (intrinsic) semiconductor, so the material is no longer chemically pure: its chemical make-up and hence its properties change, which is why they are called impurities. They sit substitutionally in the lattice in parts per million, so the crystal structure is not altered, and they increase (not decrease) the number of charge carriers.

Q 9 | edv | IC Fabrication
In IC technology, dry oxidation as compared to wet oxidation produces
(1) superior quality oxide with a lower growth rate
(2) superior quality oxide with a higher growth rate
(3) inferior quality oxide with a lower growth rate
(4) inferior quality oxide with a higher growth rate
= 1
~ Dry oxidation (Si + O₂ → SiO₂) grows a dense, low-defect oxide of high quality, used for MOS gate oxides, but it grows slowly. Wet oxidation (Si + 2H₂O → SiO₂ + 2H₂) is much faster because H₂O diffuses through the oxide more easily, but the oxide is less dense and is used for thick field oxides.

Q 12 | nwk | Network Laws & Theorems
KCL is a consequences of law of conservation of
(1) flux
(2) energy
(3) potential
(4) charge
= 4
~ Kirchhoff's current law says the currents entering a node sum to zero: charge can neither be created nor pile up at a node, so KCL follows from conservation of charge. KVL follows from conservation of energy.

Q 13 | nwk | Network Laws & Theorems
When a source is delivering maximum power to a load, the efficiency of the circuit
(1) is always 50%
(2) is always 75%
(3) depends on the circuit parameters
(4) is always 100%
= 1
~ Maximum power transfer occurs when R_L = R_th; the same current then flows through R_th and R_L, so the power lost inside the source equals the power delivered to the load. Hence the efficiency is P_L/(P_L + P_loss) = 1/2 = 50%.

Q 14 | nwk | Network Laws & Theorems
In the Thevenin equivalent circuit, V_th equals
(1) short-circuit terminal voltage
(2) open-circuit terminal voltage
(3) net voltage available in the circuit
(4) voltage of the source
= 2
~ The Thevenin voltage is the voltage that appears across the terminals of interest when the load is removed, i.e. the open-circuit terminal voltage. The short-circuit current gives the Norton current, and V_th/I_sc gives R_th.

Q 15 | nwk | AC Circuits & Resonance
For a series RLC circuit, the power factor at the lowest half power frequency is
(1) 0.707 lagging
(2) 0.5 leading
(3) 1.0
(4) 0.707 leading
= 4
~ At a half-power frequency the net reactance equals R in magnitude, so the impedance angle is 45° and the power factor is cos 45° = 0.707. Below resonance X_C > X_L, so the circuit is capacitive and the current leads: at the lower half-power frequency the power factor is 0.707 leading (it is 0.707 lagging at the upper one).

Q 16 | nwk | AC Circuits & Resonance
A series RLC circuit has a resonance frequency of 1 kHz and a quality factor Q = 100. If each of R, L and C is doubled from its original value, the new Q of the circuit
(1) 200
(2) 100
(3) 25
(4) 50
= 4
~ For a series RLC circuit Q = (1/R)√(L/C). Doubling R, L and C gives Q' = (1/(2R))√(2L/(2C)) = Q/2 = 100/2 = 50. (The resonance frequency also halves, to 1/(2π√(4LC)) = 500 Hz.)

Q 17 | nwk | Transients & Laplace Analysis
An inductance and a capacitance are connected in series. If a unit step voltage is applied across the combination, then the initial and final currents in the circuit will be
(1) 0, ∞ respectively
(2) 0, 0 respectively
(3) ∞, ∞ respectively
(4) ∞, 0 respectively
= 2
~ At t = 0⁺ the inductor does not allow its current to change suddenly, so it acts as an open circuit and i(0⁺) = 0. In the steady state the capacitor charges to the applied voltage and blocks dc, so the final current is also 0 (with ideal, lossless L and C the current would keep oscillating, but any resistance in a real circuit damps it to zero).

Q 19 | nwk | Two-Port Networks & Network Synthesis
The network function F(s) = (s+2)/((s+1)(s+3)) Represents an

(2) RC impedance
(3) RL impedance
(4) RC impedance and an RL admittance
= 4

Q 20 | nwk | Two-Port Networks & Network Synthesis
If a two-port network is reciprocal as well as symmetrical, which one of the following conditions true

Q 21 | edv | Semiconductor Physics
An intrinsic semiconductor at absolute zero temperature
(1) has large number of holes
(2) has a large number of electrons
(3) behaves like an insulator
(4) behaves like a metallic conductor
= 3
~ At 0 K no electron has the thermal energy to cross the band gap, so the valence band is completely full and the conduction band completely empty. With no free electrons or holes there is no conduction, and the intrinsic semiconductor behaves as an insulator.

Q 22 | edv | Diodes & Transistors
A diode clamper also referred to as a
(1) series rectifier
(2) series clamper
(3) shunt rectifier
(4) shunt clamper
= 3
~ A clamper is a capacitor in series with the signal followed by a diode connected in shunt across the output; the shunt diode conducts on one peak and charges the capacitor to the peak value, so it works as a shunt-diode (peak) rectifier, and this circuit is known as a shunt rectifier or dc restorer. "Series" and "shunt" are the usual names for the two kinds of clipper, not of clampers.

The Ebers-Moll model is applicable to
(1) Junction FET
(2) UJT
(3) NMOS
(4) BJT
= 4
~ The Ebers-Moll model represents a bipolar junction transistor as two coupled back-to-back diodes (emitter-base and collector-base junctions) with dependent current sources α_F I_F and α_R I_R. It describes the BJT in all regions (active, saturation, cut-off, reverse active).

Q 24 | dge | Logic Families, Memories & Converters
When the frequency of the input signal to a CMOS gate is increased, the average power dissipation
(1) does not change
(2) increases
(3) decreases
(4) decreases exponentially
= 2
~ A static CMOS gate draws almost no current except while switching, so its power is mainly dynamic: P = C_L V_DD² f, plus a short-circuit term that also grows with f. Hence the average power dissipation increases (linearly) as the switching frequency increases.

Q 25 | edv | IC Fabrication
Photo masking
(1) controls the depth of diffusion
(2) is used to prevent ambient light shining on the silicon slice
(3) is used in the process to remove selected regions of silicon oxide
(4) reduce the size of the circuit elements
= 3
~ In photolithography the oxidised wafer is coated with photoresist and exposed to UV light through a mask; after developing, the oxide is etched away only in the unprotected regions. These windows in the SiO₂ define where diffusion or implantation takes place; the depth of diffusion is set by time and temperature, not by the mask.

Q 26 | anc | Transistor Amplifiers & Biasing
The self bias is used in amplifiers to
(1) reduce the cost of the circuit
(2) reduce the dc base current
(3) make the operating point almost independent of β
(4) limit the input ac signal going to the base terminal
= 3
~ In self bias (voltage-divider bias with an emitter resistor R_E), any rise in I_C raises the drop across R_E and reduces V_BE, which pulls I_C back. When (1 + β)R_E ≫ R_th, I_C ≈ (V_th − V_BE)/R_E, so the Q-point becomes almost independent of β and of temperature.

Q 27 | anc | Transistor Amplifiers & Biasing
The h- parameter equivalent circuit of a BJT is valid for
(1) low frequency, large signal operation
(2) low frequency, small signal operation
(3) high frequency, small signal operation
(4) high frequency, large signal operation
= 2
~ The h-parameter model linearises the transistor about its Q-point, so it holds only for small signals, and it ignores the junction capacitances, so it holds only at low (mid-band) frequencies. At high frequencies the hybrid-π model with C_π and C_μ is used instead.

Q 30 | anc | Transistor Amplifiers & Biasing
The gain of a transistor amplifier falls at high frequency due to the
(1) coupling capacitor at the output
(2) skin effect
(3) internal capacitances of device
(4) coupling capacitor at the input
= 3
~ At high frequencies the transistor's junction capacitances (C_π, C_μ, magnified by the Miller effect) and stray capacitances have low reactance and shunt the signal, so the gain falls. The coupling and bypass capacitors act as short circuits at high frequency; they are what cause the fall in gain at low frequencies.

Q 31 | anc | Transistor Amplifiers & Biasing
Darlington pair consists of the following two stages
(1) both CE
(2) CE and CB
(3) CE and CC
(4) both CC
= 4
~ In a Darlington pair the emitter of the first transistor drives the base of the second, and the output is taken from the second emitter, so it is two emitter followers (common-collector stages) in cascade. The combination has current gain ≈ β₁β₂, very high input impedance and voltage gain slightly less than 1.

Q 33 | anc | Transistor Amplifiers & Biasing
The main function of the transformer used in the output of a power amplifier is
(1) to step up the voltage
(2) to step down the voltage
(3) to match the load impedance with dynamic output resistance of the transistor
(4) to increase voltage gain
= 3
~ The output transformer reflects the load to its primary as R_L' = (N₁/N₂)² R_L, so a low-impedance load such as a loudspeaker appears as the optimum load for the transistor. This impedance matching gives maximum power transfer; it also keeps the dc collector current out of the load.

Q 34 | anc | Transistor Amplifiers & Biasing
The bandwidth of a double tuned transformer coupled amplifier can be adjusted by varying the
(1) coupling coefficient
(2) value of the inductance
(3) value of the emitter biasing resistance
(4) value of resistance
= 1
~ In a double tuned amplifier the shape of the response depends on the coupling coefficient k between the two tuned circuits: below critical coupling (k < 1/Q) the response has a single narrow peak, at critical coupling it is flat-topped, and above critical coupling it has two humps and a wider bandwidth. So the bandwidth is adjusted by changing k, without retuning the circuits.

Q 35 | anc | Transistor Amplifiers & Biasing
Crossover distortion results in
(1) class B output stage
(2) common emitter output stage
(3) class AB output stage
(4) class A output stage
= 1
~ In a class B push-pull stage each transistor is biased at cut-off and needs about 0.7 V of V_BE before it conducts. Near the zero crossings of the input neither transistor conducts, which produces crossover distortion; class AB bias (a small forward bias on both transistors) is used to reduce it.

Q 36 | anc | Feedback, Oscillators & Power Supplies
In transistor series voltage regulator the transistor behaves like a variable
(1) resistor
(2) capacitor
(3) inductor
(4) resistor and capacitor
= 1
~ The series pass transistor carries the full load current, and the feedback (error) circuit changes its base drive so that its V_CE absorbs any change in input voltage or load current. It therefore acts as a variable resistance in series with the load, which keeps the output voltage constant.

Q 37 | nwk | Network Laws & Theorems
If b is the number of branches and n the number of nodes in a connected graph, the number of links corresponding to any tree of the graph

(2) b − n + 1
(3) n − b − 1

~ A tree of a connected graph with n nodes has n − 1 branches (twigs). The remaining branches are links, so the number of links = b − (n − 1) = b − n + 1, which is also the number of independent loops (meshes).

Q 39 | nwk | Network Laws & Theorems
Superposition theorem is not applicable to networks containing
(1) nonlinear elements
(2) dependent voltage sources
(3) dependent current sources
(4) transformers
= 1
~ Superposition needs the network to be linear (additive and homogeneous), so it fails when there are nonlinear elements such as diodes. Linear dependent sources and transformers are allowed; dependent sources are simply kept active while each independent source acts alone.

Q 40 | nwk | AC Circuits & Resonance
In an RLC parallel circuit the impedance at resonance is
(1) ∞
(2) minimum
(3) maximum
(4) zero
= 3
~ At resonance the susceptances of L and C cancel, so the admittance of the parallel RLC circuit is only G = 1/R, its minimum value. Hence the impedance is maximum and equal to R; it would be ∞ only for an ideal lossless LC tank (R → ∞).

Q 41 | nwk | Two-Port Networks & Network Synthesis
Any two-port network having a 6dB loss will give
(1) an output power which is 0.707 of the input power
(2) an output power which is one quarter of the input power
(3) an output power which is one half of the input power

Q 43 | edv | Diodes & Transistors
Which of the following is not associated with a PN junction?
(1) depletion capacitance
(2) junction capacitance
(3) charge storage capacitance
(4) channel length modulation
= 4
~ A PN junction has a depletion (transition or junction) capacitance under reverse bias and a diffusion (charge storage) capacitance under forward bias. Channel length modulation is a MOSFET effect: in saturation the effective channel length shrinks as V_DS increases, so I_D rises slightly.

Q 44 | edv | Diodes & Transistors
Zener diode break down voltage———with temperature
(1) decreases
(2) constant
(3) increases
(4) may increase or decrease
= 1
~ In true Zener breakdown (heavily doped diodes, V_Z below about 5 V) the electrons cross the junction by tunnelling; a rise in temperature gives the valence electrons more energy and narrows the band gap, so breakdown occurs at a lower voltage: the Zener breakdown voltage decreases with temperature (negative temperature coefficient). Note, however, that diodes sold as "Zeners" above about 6 V break down by avalanche, whose voltage increases with temperature, so many textbooks would accept "may increase or decrease". (Official final key; some sources differ.)

Q 45 | edv | Diodes & Transistors
The output V-I characteristics of an enhancement type MOSFET has
(1) an ohmic region at low voltage value followed by a saturation region at higher voltages
(2) an ohmic region at large voltage values preceded by a saturation region at lower voltages
(3) only an ohmic region
(4) only a saturation region
= 1
~ For V_GS > V_T, at small V_DS (V_DS < V_GS − V_T) the channel behaves as a voltage-controlled resistor and I_D rises almost linearly: the ohmic region. Once V_DS ≥ V_GS − V_T the channel pinches off at the drain end and I_D becomes nearly constant: the saturation region at higher voltages.

Q 48 | anc | Op-Amps & Linear ICs
The ideal Op-Amp has following characteristics
(1) R_i = ∞, A = ∞, R_0 = 0

Q 49 | anc | Feedback, Oscillators & Power Supplies
In a half-wave rectifier, if an a.c supply is 60Hz, then the a.c ripple at output will be
(1) 6Hz
(2) 30Hz
(3) 60Hz
(4) 120Hz
= 3
~ A half-wave rectifier passes only one half-cycle in every cycle of the supply, so the output has one pulse per cycle and its fundamental ripple frequency equals the supply frequency, 60 Hz. A full-wave rectifier gives two pulses per cycle, i.e. 120 Hz.

Q 50 | anc | Transistor Amplifiers & Biasing
Maximum theoretical conversion efficiency of class B amplifier is
(1) 7.85%
(2) 25%
(3) 50%
(4) 78.5%
= 4
~ For a class B push-pull stage, P_dc = 2V_CC V_p/(πR_L) and P_ac = V_p²/(2R_L), so η = (π/4)(V_p/V_CC). The maximum, with V_p = V_CC, is π/4 = 78.5%. A series-fed class A stage gives 25% and a transformer-coupled class A stage 50%.

Q 51 | dge | Number Systems & Logic Gates
In a logic equation A(A + B̄C̄ + C) + B̄(C̄ + Ā + BC) + (A + B̄C + AC̄) = 1, if C = Ā then
(1) A + B = 1
(2) Ā + B = 1
(3) A + B̄ = 1
(4) A = 1
= 3
~ A(A + B̄C̄ + C) = A by absorption, and B̄(C̄ + Ā + BC) = B̄C̄ + ĀB̄ because B̄B = 0. The whole left side is then A + B̄C̄ + ĀB̄ + B̄C + AC̄; AC̄ is absorbed by A, B̄C̄ + B̄C = B̄, and ĀB̄ is absorbed by B̄, leaving A + B̄. ~ For differential PSK detected by comparing each bit with the previous one, the bit error probability is P_e = (1/2) exp(−E_b/η), where η (= N₀) is the noise power spectral density parameter. It is about 1 dB poorer than coherent PSK, (1/2)erfc(√(E_b/η)). Option (4) is impossible, since it would grow with E_b and exceed 1.

Q 144 | acm | Frequency & Phase Modulation
The Foster-Seeley discriminator responds to the input FM signal's
(1) amplitude variations only
(2) amplitude as well as frequency variations
(3) frequency variations only
(4) variations neither in amplitude nor in frequency
= 2
~ The Foster–Seeley (phase) discriminator's output is the difference of two diode-detected voltages, each proportional to the input amplitude as well as depending on the frequency deviation. So it responds to amplitude variations as well as frequency variations and must be preceded by a limiter; the ratio detector was developed to be largely insensitive to amplitude changes.

Q 145 | acm | Amplitude Modulation & Receivers
A superheterodyne AM broadcast receiver has an IF of 455 kHz. If it is tuned to a frequency of 700 kHz, the image frequency is
(1) 1610 kHz
(2) 1155 kHz
(3) 245 kHz
(4) 210 kHz
= 1
~ With the local oscillator above the signal, f_LO = 700 + 455 = 1155 kHz. The image lies IF above the LO: f_image = f_s + 2 × IF = 700 + 910 = 1610 kHz. 1155 kHz is the LO frequency, not the image.

Q 146 | emw | Vector Calculus & Electrostatics
Which is not an example of convection current?
(1) A moving charged belt
(2) Electronic movement in a vacuum tube
(3) An electron beam in a television tube
(4) Electric current flowing in a copper wire
= 4
~ Convection current (J = ρ_v u) is due to charges moving through vacuum or an insulating medium, such as a charged belt, the electron stream in a vacuum tube or the beam in a picture tube; it does not obey Ohm's law. Current in a copper wire is conduction current (J = σE), carried by electrons drifting through a conductor.

Q 147 | emw | Vector Calculus & Electrostatics
The value of electric field at a distance of 1 m from an infinite line charge density 1 C/m is
(1) 2πε₀
(2) ½πε₀
(3) ε₀/2π
(4) 2π/ε₀
= 2
~ By Gauss's law the field of an infinite line charge is E = ρ_L/(2πε₀ r). With ρ_L = 1 C/m and r = 1 m, E = 1/(2πε₀) V/m (≈ 1.8 × 10¹⁰ V/m). Option (2), printed "½πε₀", stands for 1/(2πε₀); taken literally it would be πε₀/2, which is not the field.

Q 148 | emw | Transmission Lines
A line terminated in its characteristic impedance has a SWR of..............................
(1) infinity
(2) unity
(3) zero
(4) two
= 2
~ With Z_L = Z₀ the reflection coefficient Γ = (Z_L − Z₀)/(Z_L + Z₀) = 0, so there is no reflected wave and SWR = (1 + |Γ|)/(1 − |Γ|) = 1. SWR can never be less than 1, so zero is impossible; infinity occurs for a short, open or purely reactive load.

Q 149 | mwa | Waveguides & Cavity Resonators
Which one of the following modes has the highest cut-off wavelength in a rectangular waveguide?
(1) TE_10
(2) TE_01
(3) TM_01
(4) TM_11
= 1
~ The cut-off wavelength is λ_c = 2/√((m/a)² + (n/b)²). With the usual a > b, TE_10 gives λ_c = 2a, the largest of all modes, which is why it is the dominant mode. TE_01 gives 2b and TM_11 gives 2ab/√(a² + b²); TM_01 does not exist because TM modes need both m and n non-zero.

Q 150 | mwa | Antennas & Arrays
In end fire array the principal direction of radiation
(1) Is perpendicular to the array axis
(2) Is perpendicular to the array axis and also to the plane containing the array elements
(3) Coincides with the direction of the array axis
(4) Is at 45 degrees to the direction of array axis.
= 3
~ In an end-fire array the elements are fed with a progressive phase shift equal to βd (opposite in sign to the path difference), so their fields add in phase along the line of the array: maximum radiation is along the array axis. Radiation perpendicular to the axis, option (1), describes a broadside array.
