# UPSC EPFO Recruitment Test for Assistant Provident Fund Commissioner (APFC), Advt. 51/2023 (Special), held 2 July 2023. Booklet Series A (BNYR-B-GNS), General Studies, 120 questions, 300 marks.
# Source: the official UPSC question paper; answers from UPSC's official answer key, Series A (upsc.gov.in, 12 August 2024). No question dropped. Explanations by Learn with Anil.

Q 1 | eng | Sentence Arrangement
Rearrange the parts P, Q, R and S to form a meaningful sentence.
P: before the judge
Q: given that he was clearly innocent
R: was incomprehensible
S: his decision to plead guilty
(1) SQRP
(2) QPSR
(3) QSPR
(4) SRQP
= 3
~ The sentence is "Given that he was clearly innocent, his decision to plead guilty before the judge was incomprehensible." Q is an introductory clause ("given that" = considering that) that sets the context, S supplies the subject "his decision to plead guilty", P must follow it directly because one pleads guilty before a judge, and R "was incomprehensible" is the predicate. QPSR wrongly attaches "before the judge" to "innocent" instead of to "plead guilty", while SQRP and SRQP leave "before the judge" stranded at the end, where it attaches to "incomprehensible" or "innocent" and distorts the meaning.

Q 2 | eng | Sentence Arrangement
Rearrange the parts P, Q, R and S to form a meaningful sentence.
P: when your stated principles
Q: it is difficult
R: are at odds with your ground realities
S: to practise what you preach
(1) PRSQ
(2) SPRQ
(3) QPRS
(4) QSPR
= 4
~ The sentence is "It is difficult to practise what you preach when your stated principles are at odds with your ground realities." Q is an anticipatory "it" clause and needs the infinitive S ("to practise what you preach") as its real subject immediately after it; the "when" clause then follows, with P giving its subject and R its verb. PRSQ and SPRQ end with a stranded "it is difficult", and QPRS separates "it is difficult" from the infinitive it introduces. To practise what you preach means to act as you advise others to act.

Q 3 | eng | Sentence Arrangement
Rearrange the parts P, Q, R and S to form a meaningful sentence.
P: people in dire need
Q: it is a truth universally acknowledged that
R: for assistance
S: reach out to their friends and family
(1) QPSR
(2) PSRQ
(3) QRPS
(4) RQPS
= 1
~ The sentence is "It is a truth universally acknowledged that people in dire need reach out to their friends and family for assistance." Q is the main clause and ends in "that", so it must open the sentence and be followed by a full noun clause: P is its subject, S its verb ("reach out to their friends and family") and R completes it, since one reaches out to someone for assistance. PSRQ leaves "that" hanging at the end, while QRPS and RQPS detach "for assistance" from "reach out". The opening echoes the first line of Jane Austen's Pride and Prejudice.

Q 4 | eng | Sentence Arrangement
Rearrange the parts P, Q, R and S to form a meaningful sentence.
P: his vengeful
Q: by the expression on his face
R: and diabolical nature
S: were clearly revealed
(1) QSPR
(2) QPRS
(3) PSQR
(4) PRSQ
= 4
~ The sentence is "His vengeful and diabolical nature were clearly revealed by the expression on his face." P and R together form the subject (two adjectives joined by "and" before the noun "nature"), S is the passive verb, and Q is the agent phrase with "by", which naturally comes after a passive verb. PSQR splits the adjectives from "nature", and the options beginning with Q put the "by" phrase before the verb it depends on, which is unnatural. Vengeful means seeking revenge; diabolical means wicked or devilish. (Strictly, the singular "nature" takes "was", but the parts are fixed as printed.)

Q 5 | eng | Sentence Arrangement
Rearrange the parts P, Q, R and S to form a meaningful sentence.
P: bitter fights
Q: between the two rival teams
R: on more than one occasion
S: I have witnessed
(1) SRQP
(2) RSPQ
(3) QPRS
(4) SQRP
= 2
~ The sentence is "On more than one occasion I have witnessed bitter fights between the two rival teams." R is a fronted adverbial of frequency, S gives the subject and verb, P is the object, and Q must follow P directly because it tells us which fights ("fights between the two rival teams"). SRQP and SQRP both put "between the two rival teams" before "bitter fights", which it should follow, and QPRS opens with a phrase that has nothing to attach to. SPQR would also be correct, but it is not among the options.

Q 6 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Choose the word or phrase closest in meaning to the underlined word "platitudes".
The essay was badly researched and contained several "platitudes" and generalizations.
(1) Incorrect statements
(2) Imprecise comments
(3) Often used and unoriginal ideas
(4) Vague, subjective statements
= 3
~ A platitude is a remark, often with a moral tone, that has been used so often that it is stale and unoriginal, for example "honesty is the best policy" in an essay that offers nothing new. That fits an essay that was badly researched. Platitudes are usually true, so "incorrect statements" is wrong. "Imprecise comments" and "vague, subjective statements" describe generalizations, which the sentence mentions separately. The key idea is triteness, not inaccuracy or vagueness. Related words: cliche, banality, truism.

Q 7 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Choose the word or phrase closest in meaning to the underlined word "disinterested".
The judgment of beauty is based on the human capacity for "disinterested" pleasure in the natural world.
(1) Indifference
(2) Free of all self-interest
(3) Not caring for something
(4) Extremely focused
= 2
~ Disinterested means impartial: not influenced by personal advantage or any wish to own or use the thing. The sentence echoes Kant's idea in aesthetics that pleasure in beauty is "disinterested", enjoyed for its own sake and not because we want or gain anything from the object. The trap is to confuse "disinterested" with "uninterested", which means indifferent or not caring (options 1 and 3). A disinterested judge is fair, not bored. "Extremely focused" has no connection with the word.

Q 8 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Choose the word or phrase closest in meaning to the underlined word "derelict".
The hundred-year old college building was now in a "derelict" state.
(1) Preserved for historical value
(2) Unsafe for occupation
(3) Neglected and broken down
(4) Retaining its original beauty
= 3
~ Derelict (of a building or property) means in very poor condition because it has been abandoned and neglected, that is, dilapidated or run-down, as in "a derelict factory". "Neglected and broken down" gives exactly both parts of this sense. A derelict building may well be unsafe to live in, but that is a consequence, not the meaning of the word. "Preserved for historical value" and "retaining its original beauty" are nearly the opposite. As a noun, a derelict is a homeless, destitute person.

Q 9 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Choose the word or phrase closest in meaning to the underlined word "serendipitous".
My "serendipitous" encounter with the man on the bus was to have a profound impact on my life.
(1) Fortunate
(2) Of historical significance
(3) Sudden and abrupt
(4) A chance event
= 4
~ Serendipitous means happening or found by chance, usually in a happy or beneficial way. The essential idea is chance: the meeting on the bus was unplanned. "Fortunate" captures only the happy side and misses the element of accident, while the good effect is already stated separately ("a profound impact on my life"). "Sudden and abrupt" refers to speed, not chance, and "of historical significance" is unrelated. The word was coined by Horace Walpole in 1754 from the tale The Three Princes of Serendip (Serendip being Sri Lanka).

Q 10 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Choose the word or phrase closest in meaning to the underlined word "complacency".
Notwithstanding our victory today we must be careful to avoid a feeling of "complacency".
(1) Smug and self-satisfied
(2) A defensive attitude
(3) Pride
(4) Feeling of superiority
= 1
~ Complacency is a smug, uncritical satisfaction with oneself or one's achievements, which makes a person relax and ignore dangers or the need to improve. After a victory the risk is exactly that: feeling self-satisfied and letting one's guard down, which is why the speaker says "notwithstanding our victory". "Pride" and "feeling of superiority" are related but lack the sense of contented carelessness, and "a defensive attitude" is unrelated. Do not confuse it with complaisance, which means willingness to please others.

Q 11 | eng | Reading Comprehension
Read the passage given below and answer the question that follows.
The millet project of the MS Swaminathan Research Foundation had three objectives—to preserve crop diversity in local millet varieties, to increase production and the consumption of millets, and to enhance farm incomes. The Kolli Hills block of Namakkal district, the project area, is a distinct geographic and agroecological region of the Eastern Ghats, populated by income-poor Scheduled Tribe households.
There had been a rapid decline in minor millet cultivation, and a shift in land use towards more profitable crops such as cassava (tapioca), pineapple, coffee, and pepper. As the Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) has pointed out, the area under nutri-cereals has declined steeply in India since the mid-1980s—from 41 million hectares in the 1980s to 24 million hectares in 2017–18.
The reasons for a shift in land use include low yields. Further, processing of millets is a time-consuming and laborious task, undertaken by women. Additionally, very little was marketed, and a tiny share of grain was processed into value-added products.
The project intervened in three areas. First, yield enhancement was attempted, using a combination of participatory varietal trials for improved seeds, new agronomic practices, and new technology. Community seed banks were designed and constructed to conserve, restore, revive, strengthen and improve local seed systems.
Second, customized post-harvest machineries (pulverizers and dehullers) were introduced. The introduction of small-scale localized mechanical milling, operated by self-help groups, was a game-changer.
The third major initiative was training. The Kolli Hills Agrobiodiversity Conservers' Federation (KHABCOFED) was formed to oversee all activities relating to training and value-addition. Ready-to-cook products were branded under the Kolli Hills Natural Foods label and market links established. Net returns from value-added products were five to ten times higher than those from grains.
Yields have risen as a result of improved seeds, agronomic practices and intercropping. There have been significant improvements in incomes from millet farming. The shift from hand-pounding to milling has reduced the drudgery for women and encouraged millet consumption. The number of private mills with customized dehullers and pulverizers has risen (and the technology has been marketed to Krishi Vigyan Kendras across Tamil Nadu).
What does "agroecological region of the Eastern Ghats" mean in the passage?
(1) Area marked by excessive agricultural yield
(2) Area suitable for developing tourism
(3) Area with pollution-free characteristics
(4) Area meant for sustainable farming that works in tandem with nature
= 4
~ An agroecological region is an area marked out by its own climate, soil, terrain and vegetation, where farming is adapted to these ecological conditions. The passage calls the Kolli Hills a distinct agroecological region and describes a project to preserve the diversity of local millet varieties, revive local seed systems and use intercropping, all of which is farming in harmony with nature. Option 1 is contradicted by the passage, which cites low yields, and nothing is said about tourism or pollution.

Q 16 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Choose the option that best describes the meaning of the phrase in italics, "too great and beautiful to be expressed in words".
Her poetry not only succeeds in brilliantly capturing the beauty of nature but also carries the power to suggest that which is "too great and beautiful to be expressed in words".
(1) the unknown
(2) the proscribed
(3) the intangible
(4) the ineffable
= 4
~ Ineffable means too great, intense or beautiful to be described in words (from Latin ineffabilis, "unutterable"), as in "the ineffable beauty of the mountains". This matches the italicised phrase exactly. "The intangible" is what cannot be touched or clearly grasped, "the unknown" is simply what is not known, and "the proscribed" is what is forbidden or banned. None of these carries the specific sense of being beyond the power of words. A near-synonym is "inexpressible"; a related word is "indescribable".

Q 17 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Choose the option that best describes the meaning of the phrase in italics, "the poverty-stricken".
The Government's policies were aimed at relieving the distress of the homeless, the unemployed and "the poverty-stricken" sections of society.
(1) needy
(2) indigent
(3) disadvantaged
(4) challenged
= 2
~ Indigent means extremely poor, lacking money and the basic necessities of life; "the indigent" are the poor. It is the precise one-word equivalent of "poverty-stricken". "Needy" also suggests poverty but is broader, since a person can be needy of help, attention or affection. "Disadvantaged" refers to unfavourable social or economic circumstances in general, not poverty alone, and "challenged" is usually a euphemism for a disability. Related words: impoverished, destitute, penurious.

Q 18 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Choose the option that best describes the meaning of the phrase in italics, "speeches that tended to provoke and ignite passions".
The organizers refused to allow him to speak at the function since he was known to make "speeches that tended to provoke and ignite passions".
(1) irreverent speeches
(2) incendiary speeches
(3) unsubstantiated speeches
(4) irrelevant speeches
= 2
~ Incendiary literally means designed to cause fires (an incendiary bomb). Applied to speech or writing it means likely to stir up anger, conflict or violence, which matches "provoke and ignite passions", where "ignite" keeps the fire image. This explains why the organizers kept him off the stage. Irreverent speeches are disrespectful or lacking in seriousness, unsubstantiated speeches are not backed by evidence, and irrelevant speeches are off the point. None of these need provoke anyone. Related words: inflammatory, seditious, rabble-rousing.

Q 19 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Choose the option that best describes the meaning of the phrase in italics, "restricted to small local issues".
She was criticized for her views that were often "restricted to small local issues" and ignored matters of national importance.
(1) unilateral
(2) illiberal
(3) parochial
(4) limited
= 3
~ Parochial (from "parish") means having a narrow outlook that is concerned only with local matters, which is exactly "restricted to small local issues" and fits the contrast with "matters of national importance". "Limited" is too general, because views can be limited in many ways and not specifically by locality. "Illiberal" means intolerant of others' opinions or freedoms, and "unilateral" means done by one side without the agreement of others. The opposite of parochial is cosmopolitan or broad-minded.

Q 20 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Choose the option that best describes the meaning of the phrase in italics, "dropping steeply all of a sudden".
When profits began "dropping steeply all of a sudden" he realized that he ought to wind up his business soon.
(1) precipitously
(2) instantaneously
(3) cataclysmically
(4) dramatically
= 1
~ Precipitously (related to precipice, a steep cliff face) means very steeply and suddenly, like a fall from a cliff, so it covers both "steeply" and "all of a sudden". A precipitous fall in profits is a sharp, abrupt drop. "Instantaneously" means in an instant and says nothing about steepness. "Cataclysmically" suggests a violent, disastrous upheaval and is too extreme. "Dramatically" means strikingly or to a great extent but not necessarily suddenly. The word can also mean hastily, as in acting precipitously.

Q 21 | ih | Ancient India
Which one among the following observations pertaining to the works of Nineteenth-Twentieth Century archaeologists is not correct?
(1) Alexander Cunningham, the first Director-General of the Archaeological Survey of India, was of the view that the history of India began with the origins of the Indus Valley Civilization.
(2) John Marshall generally adopted the method of excavation along regular horizontal units and ignored the stratigraphy of the site.
(3) R. E. M. Wheeler first recognized the necessity to follow the stratigraphy of the mound rather than dig along horizontal lines.
(4) Amalananda Ghosh was the first to identify similarities between pre-Harappan and mature-Harappan cultures.
= 1
~ Statement (1) is the incorrect one. Alexander Cunningham, who became the first Director-General of the ASI in 1871, was mainly interested in the Early Historic period and followed the accounts of Chinese Buddhist pilgrims; he failed to see the significance of a Harappan seal shown to him, so for him Indian history effectively began with the first cities of the Ganga valley, not the Indus Valley Civilisation (recognised only in the 1920s). The rest match NCERT: Marshall dug in uniform horizontal units ignoring stratigraphy, Wheeler (DG from 1944) insisted on following the stratigraphy of the mound, and Amalananda Ghosh first identified similarities between pre-Harappan and mature Harappan cultures.

Q 22 | ih | Ancient India
Which one of the following was an important contributing factor in the rise of Magadha as the most powerful Mahajanapada between the Sixth and the Fourth Centuries BCE?
(1) Easy access to rich gold mines
(2) Superior cavalry
(3) An agriculturally productive region
(4) Republican system of governance
= 3
~ Historians explain Magadha's rise in the sixth to fourth centuries BCE largely by its geography: the middle Ganga valley was an especially productive agricultural region, iron mines (in present-day Jharkhand) were within reach for tools and weapons, forests supplied elephants for the army, and the Ganga and its tributaries gave cheap, easy communication. So (3) is the answer. Its key mineral resource was iron, not gold (1); its military edge came from elephants rather than a superior cavalry (2); and Magadha was a monarchy under ambitious kings such as Bimbisara, Ajatashatru and Mahapadma Nanda, not a republic (4).

Q 23 | ih | Art & Culture
Which one among the following statements about the Kailashanatha temple at Ellora is not correct?
(1) The temple was carved out of rocks in the Eighth Century CE.
(2) The temple is dedicated to Lord Shiva.
(3) The superstructure of the temple corresponds to the Nagara style.
(4) The temple has both Shaiva and Vaishnava sculptures.
= 3
~ The Kailashanatha (Kailasa) temple, Cave 16 at Ellora, was cut out of the living rock under the Rashtrakuta king Krishna I in the eighth century CE and is dedicated to Shiva, so (1) and (2) are correct. Q 41 | acc | Accounting Basics

(3) capital expenditure

= 1
~ The claim compensates the business for the loss of a fixed asset (machinery), so it is a non-recurring receipt of a capital nature: it is credited to the Machinery (or asset disposal) account against the asset's book value, and any surplus or shortfall is a capital profit or loss, not operating income. Q 44 | acc | Financial Statements

Q 52 | sci | Physics

Q 54 | sci | Physics

Q 55 | sci | Chemistry

= 1

Q 102 | qa | Arithmetic
Container X contains a mixture of oil and water in the ratio 1:3, whereas container Y contains a mixture of oil and water in the ratio 2:3. If 2 litres of liquid from container X and 5 litres of liquid from container Y are mixed together in container Z that initially contains 1 litre of pure oil, then what is the percentage of oil in container Z?
(1) 41.25
(2) 42.75
(3) 43.75
(4) 44.25
= 3
~ Oil from X = 1/4 of 2 L = 0.5 L, since oil is 1 part in 1 + 3 = 4. Oil from Y = 2/5 of 5 L = 2 L. Container Z already holds 1 L of pure oil. Total oil = 0.5 + 2 + 1 = 3.5 L, and total liquid = 2 + 5 + 1 = 8 L. Percentage of oil = 3.5/8 × 100 = 43.75%. Q 103 | qa | Geometry & Mensuration
A solid circular metallic disc of radius 12 inches and thickness 4 inches is melted and shaped into a solid disc of radius 16 inches. What is the thickness of the new disc in inches?
(1) 2
(2) 2.25
(3) 2.50
(4) 2.75
= 2
~ Melting and recasting does not change the volume. A disc is a short cylinder with volume πr²h, so π × 12² × 4 = π × 16² × h. Then 144 × 4 = 256 × h, and h = 576/256 = 2.25 inches. As a shortcut, thickness varies inversely with the square of the radius: h = 4 × (12/16)² = 4 × 9/16 = 2.25. Q 107 | acc | Accounting Basics
The value of long-term investment in shares is subject to wide fluctuations. A provision created against fluctuation in value of investments is based on the convention of
(1) conservatism
(2) full disclosure
(3) materiality
(4) consistency
= 1
~ The convention of conservatism (prudence) says "anticipate no profit, but provide for all possible losses". Because the market value of shares may fall, a provision for fluctuation in the value of investments is made in advance, so that assets and profits are not overstated, while unrealised gains are not recorded. Consistency means using the same accounting methods year after year, materiality means treating only significant items separately, and full disclosure means revealing all material information. None of these three is the basis for such a provision.

Q 108 | acc | Auditing & Company Accounts

Q 110 | acc | Accounting Basics
Goodwill Account is a/an
(1) Personal Account
(2) Real Account
(3) Nominal Account
(4) Expense Account
= 2
~ Goodwill is an intangible asset. Accounts of assets, whether tangible (land, machinery, cash) or intangible (goodwill, patents, trademarks), are Real Accounts, and they follow the rule "debit what comes in, credit what goes out". Personal Accounts relate to persons, firms and companies (debtors, creditors, capital). Nominal Accounts relate to expenses, losses, incomes and gains, and an expense account is one kind of nominal account. Goodwill appears on the assets side of the balance sheet; it is not closed to the profit and loss account.

Q 111 | eco | Money, Banking & Inflation
The principle of subrogation in insurance allows
(1) investment of policy amount
(2) refund for insured and insured's insurance company
(3) auto-renewal of policy
(4) indemnification of the insured
= 2
~ Subrogation follows from the principle of indemnity. After the insurer pays the insured's claim, it steps into the insured's shoes and may recover the amount from the third party who caused the loss. The recovery refunds the insurer what it paid, and any amount recovered beyond that, such as an uninsured deductible, goes back to the insured. Subrogation also stops the insured from being compensated twice. Indemnification of the insured is the principle of indemnity itself, which subrogation follows. Investment of the policy amount and auto-renewal have nothing to do with subrogation.
