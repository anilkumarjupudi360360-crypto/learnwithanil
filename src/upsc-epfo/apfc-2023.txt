# UPSC EPFO Recruitment Test for Assistant Provident Fund Commissioner (APFC), Advt. 51/2023 (Special), held 2 July 2023. Booklet Series A (BNYR-B-GNS), General Studies, 120 questions, 300 marks.
# Source: the official UPSC question paper; answers from UPSC's official answer key, Series A (upsc.gov.in, 12 August 2024). No question dropped. Explanations by Learn with Anil.

Q 1 | eng | Sentence Arrangement
Rearrange the parts P, Q, R and S to form a meaningful sentence.
P: before the judge
Q: given that he was clearly innocent
R: was incomprehensible
S: his decision to plead guilty
(1) SQRP
(2) QPSR
(3) QSPR
(4) SRQP
= 3
~ Option 3, QSPR, gives "Given that he was clearly innocent, his decision to plead guilty before the judge was incomprehensible." Q opens the sentence: "given that" means "considering that" and introduces the background fact. S then supplies the subject, "his decision to plead guilty", and P must follow it at once, because a person pleads guilty before a judge. R, "was incomprehensible", is the predicate. QPSR (option 2) wrongly ties "before the judge" to "innocent" and separates it from "plead guilty". SQRP (option 1) pushes "before the judge" to the very end, where it seems to say the decision was incomprehensible before the judge, and SRQP (option 4) makes him "innocent before the judge", which distorts the meaning. Tip: in sentence-rearrangement questions, first find the opener (an introductory clause such as "given that" or the main subject), then keep every modifying phrase next to the word it describes; a misplaced modifier is the usual trap.

Q 2 | eng | Sentence Arrangement
Rearrange the parts P, Q, R and S to form a meaningful sentence.
P: when your stated principles
Q: it is difficult
R: are at odds with your ground realities
S: to practise what you preach
(1) PRSQ
(2) SPRQ
(3) QPRS
(4) QSPR
= 4
~ Option 4, QSPR, gives "It is difficult to practise what you preach when your stated principles are at odds with your ground realities." Q uses the anticipatory (dummy) "it": the real subject is the infinitive phrase S, "to practise what you preach", so S must come straight after Q. The "when" clause follows, with P supplying its subject and R its verb and complement. PRSQ (option 1) and SPRQ (option 2) end with a dangling "it is difficult" that has nothing to refer to, and QPRS (option 3) separates "it is difficult" from the infinitive it introduces, leaving "to practise what you preach" stranded at the end. Meanings: to practise what you preach is to act as you advise others to act; "at odds with" means in conflict with. Spelling tip: the question uses British spelling, in which "practise" is the verb and "practice" the noun, just as "advise" is the verb and "advice" the noun.

Q 3 | eng | Sentence Arrangement
Rearrange the parts P, Q, R and S to form a meaningful sentence.
P: people in dire need
Q: it is a truth universally acknowledged that
R: for assistance
S: reach out to their friends and family
(1) QPSR
(2) PSRQ
(3) QRPS
(4) RQPS
= 1
~ Option 1, QPSR, gives "It is a truth universally acknowledged that people in dire need reach out to their friends and family for assistance." Q is the main clause and ends in "that", so it must open the sentence and be followed by a complete noun clause. P supplies that clause's subject ("people in dire need"), S its verb ("reach out to their friends and family"), and R completes the idea, since one reaches out to someone for assistance. PSRQ (option 2) leaves "that" hanging at the end with nothing to introduce. QRPS (option 3) and RQPS (option 4) detach "for assistance" from "reach out" and place it where it modifies nothing. Meanings: "in dire need" means in urgent or desperate need, and "reach out to" means to contact someone, often to seek or offer help. The opening echoes the famous first line of Jane Austen's Pride and Prejudice (1813). Tip: a part ending in "that", "which" or "who" can never come last.

Q 4 | eng | Sentence Arrangement
Rearrange the parts P, Q, R and S to form a meaningful sentence.
P: his vengeful
Q: by the expression on his face
R: and diabolical nature
S: were clearly revealed
(1) QSPR
(2) QPRS
(3) PSQR
(4) PRSQ
= 4
~ Option 4, PRSQ, gives "His vengeful and diabolical nature were clearly revealed by the expression on his face." P and R together make the subject: two adjectives joined by "and" ("vengeful and diabolical") before the noun "nature". S is the passive verb, and Q is the agent phrase with "by", which normally follows a passive verb (subject + be + past participle + by + agent). PSQR (option 3) splits the adjectives from their noun and makes no sense. QSPR (option 1) and QPRS (option 2) put the "by" phrase first, ahead of the subject and verb it depends on; QSPR is a clumsy inversion, and neither follows the natural passive order that the parts point to. Meanings: vengeful means seeking revenge; diabolical means wicked or devilish (from the Greek word for devil). Strictly, the singular "nature" takes "was", not "were", but the parts are fixed as printed, so judge the order, not the agreement.

Q 5 | eng | Sentence Arrangement
Rearrange the parts P, Q, R and S to form a meaningful sentence.
P: bitter fights
Q: between the two rival teams
R: on more than one occasion
S: I have witnessed
(1) SRQP
(2) RSPQ
(3) QPRS
(4) SQRP
= 2
~ Option 2, RSPQ, gives "On more than one occasion I have witnessed bitter fights between the two rival teams." R is an adverbial of frequency placed at the front for emphasis; S gives the subject and verb ("I have witnessed"); P is the object ("bitter fights"); and Q must follow P directly because it tells us which fights, namely fights between the two rival teams. SRQP (option 1) and SQRP (option 4) place "between the two rival teams" before "bitter fights", separating the phrase from the noun it describes, and QPRS (option 3) opens with a prepositional phrase that has nothing to attach to and ends awkwardly with the verb. SPQR ("I have witnessed bitter fights between the two rival teams on more than one occasion") would also be correct, but it is not offered, which is why the version with the fronted adverbial is the answer. Tip: adverbials of time and frequency can move to the start or the end of a sentence, but a phrase that qualifies a noun must stay beside that noun.

Q 6 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Choose the word or phrase closest in meaning to the underlined word "platitudes".
The essay was badly researched and contained several "platitudes" and generalizations.
(1) Incorrect statements
(2) Imprecise comments
(3) Often used and unoriginal ideas
(4) Vague, subjective statements
= 3
~ Option 3. A platitude is a remark, often with a moral tone, that has been used so often that it is stale and unoriginal, for example "honesty is the best policy" or "education is the key to progress" offered as if it were a fresh insight. The word comes from French plat, "flat", so a platitude is a flat, dull remark. That fits an essay that was badly researched: instead of evidence and fresh analysis, it relies on worn-out ideas. Option 1 is wrong because platitudes are usually true, merely trite. Options 2 and 4 ("imprecise comments", "vague, subjective statements") describe generalizations, which the sentence lists separately, so they cannot be the meaning of platitudes. The key idea is triteness, not inaccuracy or vagueness. Synonyms: cliche, truism, banality, commonplace, bromide. Answer-writing tip: examiners mark down platitudes, so support every general claim with a fact, an example or data.

Q 7 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Choose the word or phrase closest in meaning to the underlined word "disinterested".
The judgment of beauty is based on the human capacity for "disinterested" pleasure in the natural world.
(1) Indifference
(2) Free of all self-interest
(3) Not caring for something
(4) Extremely focused
= 2
~ Option 2. Disinterested means impartial: not influenced by personal advantage, or by any wish to own, use or profit from the thing. The sentence echoes Immanuel Kant's aesthetics, in which the pleasure we take in beauty is "disinterested": we enjoy a sunset or a flower for its own sake, not because we want to possess it or gain anything from it. The common trap is to confuse "disinterested" with "uninterested", which means indifferent or not caring; option 1 ("indifference") and option 3 ("not caring for something") give that wrong sense. A disinterested judge is fair and unbiased, not bored. Option 4, "extremely focused", has no connection with the word. Linked idea from the ethics side of the GS syllabus: impartiality and non-partisanship are core civil-service values, and a disinterested officer decides a claim on its merits, with no personal stake in the outcome. Remember: dis-interested means no self-interest; un-interested means no interest at all.

Q 8 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Choose the word or phrase closest in meaning to the underlined word "derelict".
The hundred-year old college building was now in a "derelict" state.
(1) Preserved for historical value
(2) Unsafe for occupation
(3) Neglected and broken down
(4) Retaining its original beauty
= 3
~ Option 3. Derelict, used of a building, ship or piece of land, means in very poor condition because it has been abandoned and neglected, that is, dilapidated or run-down, as in "a derelict factory" or "derelict land". It comes from the Latin derelinquere, "to abandon completely". "Neglected and broken down" gives both parts of this sense, the neglect and the resulting decay, which suits a hundred-year-old building that has been left to fall apart. Option 2, "unsafe for occupation", is wrong: a derelict building may well be unsafe, but that is a consequence, not the meaning of the word. Options 1 ("preserved for historical value") and 4 ("retaining its original beauty") are nearly the opposite. As a noun, a derelict is a homeless, destitute person or an abandoned ship. Linked phrase often asked: "dereliction of duty", a shameful failure to do what one is required to do, which is a ground for disciplinary action against a public servant.

Q 9 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Choose the word or phrase closest in meaning to the underlined word "serendipitous".
My "serendipitous" encounter with the man on the bus was to have a profound impact on my life.
(1) Fortunate
(2) Of historical significance
(3) Sudden and abrupt
(4) A chance event
= 4
~ Option 4. Serendipitous means happening or discovered by chance, usually in a happy or beneficial way; the noun is serendipity. The essential idea is chance: the meeting with the man on the bus was unplanned and accidental. Option 1, "fortunate", captures only the happy side and misses the element of accident, and the good result is already stated separately in the sentence ("a profound impact on my life"). Option 3, "sudden and abrupt", refers to speed, not chance, and option 2, "of historical significance", is unrelated. The word was coined by Horace Walpole in 1754 from the fairy tale The Three Princes of Serendip, whose heroes kept making lucky discoveries by accident; Serendip is an old name for Sri Lanka. A classic example is Alexander Fleming's chance discovery of penicillin in 1928. Opposites: planned, deliberate. Tip: in synonym questions pick the option that carries the core idea of the word, not a side effect or a result.

Q 10 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Choose the word or phrase closest in meaning to the underlined word "complacency".
Notwithstanding our victory today we must be careful to avoid a feeling of "complacency".
(1) Smug and self-satisfied
(2) A defensive attitude
(3) Pride
(4) Feeling of superiority
= 1
~ Option 1. Complacency is a smug, uncritical satisfaction with oneself or one's achievements, which makes a person relax and ignore dangers or the need to keep improving. After a victory the risk is exactly that: feeling self-satisfied and letting one's guard down, which is why the speaker warns against it "notwithstanding" (in spite of) the win. "Smug and self-satisfied" captures both the contentment and the carelessness. Option 3, "pride", and option 4, "feeling of superiority", are related but lack the sense of contented carelessness; one can be proud and still alert. Option 2, "a defensive attitude", is unrelated. The adjective is complacent, as in "we cannot afford to be complacent about safety". Trap: do not confuse it with complaisance (complaisant), which means a willingness to please others or to go along with them. Synonyms: self-satisfaction, smugness, self-congratulation. Opposites: vigilance, alertness. Remember: complacent people stop trying; complaisant people try too hard to please.

Q 11 | eng | Reading Comprehension
Read the passage given below and answer the question that follows.
The millet project of the MS Swaminathan Research Foundation had three objectives—to preserve crop diversity in local millet varieties, to increase production and the consumption of millets, and to enhance farm incomes. The Kolli Hills block of Namakkal district, the project area, is a distinct geographic and agroecological region of the Eastern Ghats, populated by income-poor Scheduled Tribe households.
There had been a rapid decline in minor millet cultivation, and a shift in land use towards more profitable crops such as cassava (tapioca), pineapple, coffee, and pepper. As the Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) has pointed out, the area under nutri-cereals has declined steeply in India since the mid-1980s—from 41 million hectares in the 1980s to 24 million hectares in 2017–18.
The reasons for a shift in land use include low yields. Further, processing of millets is a time-consuming and laborious task, undertaken by women. Additionally, very little was marketed, and a tiny share of grain was processed into value-added products.
The project intervened in three areas. First, yield enhancement was attempted, using a combination of participatory varietal trials for improved seeds, new agronomic practices, and new technology. Community seed banks were designed and constructed to conserve, restore, revive, strengthen and improve local seed systems.
Second, customized post-harvest machineries (pulverizers and dehullers) were introduced. The introduction of small-scale localized mechanical milling, operated by self-help groups, was a game-changer.
The third major initiative was training. The Kolli Hills Agrobiodiversity Conservers' Federation (KHABCOFED) was formed to oversee all activities relating to training and value-addition. Ready-to-cook products were branded under the Kolli Hills Natural Foods label and market links established. Net returns from value-added products were five to ten times higher than those from grains.
Yields have risen as a result of improved seeds, agronomic practices and intercropping. There have been significant improvements in incomes from millet farming. The shift from hand-pounding to milling has reduced the drudgery for women and encouraged millet consumption. The number of private mills with customized dehullers and pulverizers has risen (and the technology has been marketed to Krishi Vigyan Kendras across Tamil Nadu).
What does "agroecological region of the Eastern Ghats" mean in the passage?
(1) Area marked by excessive agricultural yield
(2) Area suitable for developing tourism
(3) Area with pollution-free characteristics
(4) Area meant for sustainable farming that works in tandem with nature
= 4
~ Option 4. An agroecological region is an area marked out by its own climate, soil, terrain, water and vegetation, where farming is suited to those natural conditions. The passage calls the Kolli Hills of Namakkal district (Tamil Nadu) a distinct agroecological region of the Eastern Ghats and then describes a project that preserves local millet varieties, revives local seed systems through community seed banks and uses intercropping, all of which is farming that works with nature, as option 4 says. Option 1 is contradicted by the passage, which lists low yields as a reason farmers abandoned millets. Options 2 and 3 bring in tourism and pollution, which the passage never mentions. Linked fact: ICAR's National Bureau of Soil Survey and Land Use Planning has divided India into 20 agro-ecological regions, while the Planning Commission divided the country into 15 agro-climatic regions for planning. Tip: in comprehension questions, choose the meaning the passage itself supports, not an outside idea.

Q 16 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Choose the option that best describes the meaning of the phrase in italics, "too great and beautiful to be expressed in words".
Her poetry not only succeeds in brilliantly capturing the beauty of nature but also carries the power to suggest that which is "too great and beautiful to be expressed in words".
(1) the unknown
(2) the proscribed
(3) the intangible
(4) the ineffable
= 4
~ Option 4. Ineffable means too great, intense or beautiful to be described in words. It comes from the Latin ineffabilis, "unutterable" (in-, "not", + effari, "to speak out"), and is used in phrases such as "the ineffable beauty of the Himalayas" or "ineffable joy", so it matches the phrase in italics exactly. Option 3, "the intangible", means what cannot be touched or clearly grasped, like goodwill or morale; it is not about words. Option 1, "the unknown", is simply what is not known. Option 2, "the proscribed", means what is forbidden or banned (the trap is to confuse it with "prescribed", which means laid down or recommended). None of these carries the specific sense of being beyond the power of language. Near-synonyms: inexpressible, indescribable. (2) John Marshall generally adopted the method of excavation along regular horizontal units and ignored the stratigraphy of the site.
(3) R. E. M. Wheeler first recognized the necessity to follow the stratigraphy of the mound rather than dig along horizontal lines.
(4) Amalananda Ghosh was the first to identify similarities between pre-Harappan and mature-Harappan cultures.
= 1
~ Statement (1), option 1, is the incorrect one. Q 24 | ih | Ancient India

(4) Equitable Right
= 3

PQ and RS are chords on a circle centred at O. Suppose A is the point of intersection of PQ and RS. Which of the following statements is/are correct?

2. If A and O do not coincide, then two of the four parts of the circle produced by the chords shall always have equal areas.

(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 4

Q 37 | qa | Arithmetic
P, Q, R, S and T are five friends. The mean of weights of P, Q, R and S is 50 kg, whereas the mean of weights of Q, R, S and T is also 50 kg. Which of the following statements is/are correct?
1. The weight of T is 50 kg.
2. The weights of P and T are equal.
Select the correct answer using the code given below.
(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 2
~ Option 2, 2 only, is correct. Step 1: the mean of P, Q, R and S is 50 kg, so P + Q + R + S = 4 × 50 = 200 kg. Step 2: the mean of Q, R, S and T is also 50 kg, so Q + R + S + T = 200 kg. Step 3: subtracting, P − T = 0, so P = T and statement 2 is correct. Step 4: the data fix only the sum Q + R + S = 200 − T, not T itself. So T need not be 50 kg; statement 1 is not necessarily true, which rules out options (1) and (3), and option (4) fails because statement 2 is true. Q 41 | acc | Accounting Basics
The insurance claim received on account of machinery damaged completely by fire is
(1) capital receipt
(2) revenue receipt
(3) capital expenditure
(4) revenue expenditure
= 1
~ Option 1, capital receipt, is correct. Machinery is a fixed asset, so an insurance claim received because it was completely destroyed by fire compensates the business for the loss of a capital asset. It is a non-recurring receipt of a capital nature: it is credited to the Machinery (or asset disposal) account against the asset's book value, and any surplus or shortfall is a capital profit or loss, not operating income. It is not a revenue receipt, option (2), because revenue receipts arise from normal operations, such as sales, commission, rent or interest; by contrast, an insurance claim for stock destroyed by fire would be a revenue receipt, since stock is a current asset. Being money received, it cannot be an expenditure, which rules out options (3) and (4). Remember the rule: a receipt takes the character of the item it replaces, so compensation for a fixed asset is capital, while compensation for stock or for lost income is revenue. Capital receipts go to the Balance Sheet; revenue receipts go to the Profit and Loss Account.

= 4
~ The official key is option (4). Q 44 | acc | Financial Statements

(2) Dividend equalization reserve is an example of general reserve.
(3) General reserve can be used only for some specific purposes.
(4) ‘Provision’ is a charge against profit.
= 4
~ Option 4 is correct: a provision is a charge against profit. It is an amount debited to the Profit and Loss Account to meet a known liability or loss whose amount cannot be determined exactly, such as a provision for doubtful debts or for taxation, so it must be made whether or not there is a profit. Reserves, by contrast, are appropriations of profit. = 4
~ Option 4 is correct: failing to record a cash sale in the cash book is not an error of commission. Q 52 | sci | Physics
For an inelastic collision between two objects, which one among the following statements is correct?
(1) The kinetic energy remains conserved, but not the momentum.
(2) The momentum remains conserved, but not the kinetic energy.
(3) Both the kinetic energy and the momentum remain conserved.
(4) Neither the kinetic energy nor the momentum remains conserved.
= 2
~ Option 2 is correct. In any collision in which no external force acts on the system, the total linear momentum is conserved; this follows from Newton's third law, since the forces the bodies exert on each other are equal and opposite. In an inelastic collision, however, part of the kinetic energy is converted into heat, sound or the energy of permanent deformation, so kinetic energy is not conserved, although total energy still is. Only in an elastic collision (for example, collisions of ideal gas molecules or of hard billiard balls, approximately) are both momentum and kinetic energy conserved, so option 3 is wrong; momentum is never lost in an isolated collision, so options 1 and 4 are wrong. In a perfectly inelastic collision the bodies stick together and move with a common velocity, and the loss of kinetic energy is the largest, as when a bullet embeds itself in a wooden block. Remember: the coefficient of restitution e is 1 for an elastic collision, between 0 and 1 for an inelastic one and 0 for a perfectly inelastic one.

Q 54 | sci | Physics

(2) A virtual and inverted image would be formed at the focal point.
(3) A real and erect image would be formed at infinity.
(4) A virtual and erect image would be formed at the focal point.
= 1
~ Option 1 is correct. Rays from an object at infinity reach the mirror parallel to the principal axis, and a concave (converging) mirror reflects such rays so that they actually meet at the principal focus F. Q 55 | sci | Chemistry
The main cause of ocean acidification is
(1) carbon dioxide
(2) carbon monoxide
(3) hydrogen sulfide
(4) chlorofluorocarbon
= 1
~ Option 1, carbon dioxide, is correct. The oceans absorb roughly a quarter to a third of the CO2 released by human activities such as burning fossil fuels and clearing forests. Dissolved CO2 reacts with water to form carbonic acid (CO2 + H2O → H2CO3), which releases hydrogen ions and lowers the pH of seawater; the extra hydrogen ions also combine with carbonate ions, leaving less carbonate for corals, molluscs, oysters and plankton to build their calcium carbonate shells and skeletons. Average surface-ocean pH has fallen from about 8.2 to about 8.1 since the Industrial Revolution, roughly a 30% rise in acidity, because the pH scale is logarithmic. The other options are not the main drivers: carbon monoxide is a toxic air pollutant that binds haemoglobin, hydrogen sulfide is associated with oxygen-starved 'dead zones', and chlorofluorocarbons are known for depleting the stratospheric ozone layer. Remember: ocean acidification is often called the 'evil twin' of global warming, because both come from rising CO2.

Q 74 | qa | Algebra & Numbers
Suppose that x and y are distinct variables that take values from {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}. What is the probability that the value of the expression xy + x + y is even?
(1) 1/2
(2) 1/3
(3) 1/4
(4) 1/5
= 4
~ Option 4, 1/5, is correct. Step 1: add 1 to the expression to factorise it: xy + x + y + 1 = (x + 1)(y + 1), so xy + x + y = (x + 1)(y + 1) − 1. Step 2: this is even exactly when (x + 1)(y + 1) is odd, which needs both x + 1 and y + 1 to be odd, i.e. both x and y even. Q 75 | qa | Algebra & Numbers
Suppose that a, b > 0. If the equation x² + ax + b = 0 has real roots, namely α and β, then which one of the following is correct?
(1) α, β > 0
(2) If α > 0, then β < 0
(3) α, β < 0
(4) −1 < α/β < 1
= 3
~ Option 3, α, β < 0, is correct. Step 1: for x² + ax + b = 0, the sum of the roots is α + β = −a and the product is αβ = b. Step 2: since b > 0, αβ > 0, so both roots have the same sign (and neither is zero). Step 3: since a > 0, α + β = −a < 0, so that common sign must be negative; hence α, β < 0. Shortcut: if all the coefficients of a quadratic are positive, it cannot have a positive or zero root, because every term would then be positive.

(4) 45

Q 77 | acc | Accounting Basics

(2) matching concept
(3) going concern assumption
(4) consistency principle
= 3
~ Option 3, the going concern assumption, is correct. Under this assumption the business is expected to continue operating for the foreseeable future, with no intention or need to wind up or sharply cut down its operations. Q 83 | sci | Computer Applications
In mesh topology, if we have six computers, how many links must be there?
(1) 6
(2) 12
(3) 13
(4) 15
= 4
~ Option 4, 15, is correct. In a full mesh topology every computer has a dedicated point-to-point link to every other computer. Step 1: each of the n computers connects to the n − 1 others, giving n(n − 1) link-ends. Step 2: each link joins two computers and so is counted twice; number of links = n(n − 1)/2. Step 3: for six computers, 6 × 5/2 = 15 links, and each computer needs n − 1 = 5 input/output ports. Trap: 6 × 5 = 30 without halving counts every link twice; six links would only join the computers in a ring, and 12 and 13 do not follow from the formula. Linked facts: mesh is the most reliable and secure topology because the failure of one link cuts off no device, but it needs the most cabling and ports; a star topology needs only n links to a central hub, and a bus uses a single backbone cable. Shortcut: links in a mesh = nC2, the number of ways of choosing two computers.

Q 84 | sci | Computer Applications
The binary equivalent of the octal number 473 is
(1) 100111011
(2) 100111111
(3) 110111011
(4) 110001011
= 1
~ Option 1, 100111011, is correct. Since 8 = 2³, each octal digit converts directly into a group of three binary digits. Step 1: 4 = 100, 7 = 111 and 3 = 011. Step 2: write the groups side by side: (473)₈ = 100 111 011 = (100111011)₂. Step 3, check through decimal: 4 × 64 + 7 × 8 + 3 = 256 + 56 + 3 = 315, and 100111011 = 256 + 32 + 16 + 8 + 2 + 1 = 315. Linked fact: hexadecimal works the same way with four-bit groups because 16 = 2⁴, so binary 1 0011 1011 = (13B)₁₆ = 315. Reverse conversion: split a binary number into groups of three from the right.

Q 86 | sci | Computer Applications
Which one of the following registers contains the address of the next location in the memory to be accessed?
(1) IR
(2) MAR
(3) MBR
(4) DR
= 2
~ Option 2, MAR, is correct. The Memory Address Register holds the address of the memory location that is to be accessed next, whether for reading or for writing. The CPU places the address in the MAR, and the data then passes between memory and the processor through the Memory Buffer Register (MBR), also called the Memory Data Register (MDR). Option 1, the Instruction Register (IR), holds the instruction currently being decoded and executed; option 3, the MBR, and option 4, a data register (DR), hold data, not addresses. Q 87 | sci | Chemistry

(2) Plastic cup manufacturing industries
(3) Soap and detergent industries
(4) Lead battery manufacturing industries
= 4

Q 88 | sci | Chemistry
Which one of the following is not a soap?
(1) Sodium stearate
(2) Sodium palmate
(3) Sodium benzoate
(4) Sodium oleate
= 3
~ Option 3, sodium benzoate, is not a soap. Soaps are sodium or potassium salts of long-chain fatty acids, made by saponification, the alkaline hydrolysis of fats or oils: fat + NaOH → soap + glycerol. Sodium stearate (option 1, C17H35COONa, from stearic acid), sodium palmate (option 2, the sodium salts of palm-oil fatty acids such as palmitic and oleic acids) and sodium oleate (option 4, from oleic acid) are therefore soaps. Potassium soaps are softer and are used in shaving creams.

Q 89 | sci | Biology
The human eyeball is composed of three layers. The correct sequence of the layers from exterior to interior is
(1) Sclera → Choroid → Retina
(2) Cornea → Choroid → Retina
(3) Cornea → Sclera → Choroid
(4) Sclera → Cornea → Choroid
= 1
~ Option 1, sclera → choroid → retina, is correct. The wall of the eyeball has three coats. The outermost is the tough, fibrous sclera, the white of the eye, whose transparent, bulging front part is the cornea; the middle coat is the choroid, rich in blood vessels and pigment, which nourishes the retina and absorbs stray light and in front forms the ciliary body and the iris; the innermost is the light-sensitive retina, which contains the photoreceptor rods and cones. Q 90 | sci | Chemistry
Consider the following reaction :
2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(l)
Which one of the following statements about the reaction given above is correct?
(1) The oxidation state of hydrogen increases while that of oxygen decreases.
(2) The oxidation state of hydrogen decreases while that of oxygen increases.
(3) There is no change in the oxidation state of hydrogen as well as that of oxygen.
(4) During the reaction, hydrogen is reduced while oxygen is oxidized.
= 1
~ Option 1 is correct: the oxidation state of hydrogen increases while that of oxygen decreases. Step 1: in H₂ and O₂ both elements are in the free, uncombined state, so their oxidation number is 0. Step 2: in H₂O hydrogen is +1 and oxygen is −2 (check: 2(+1) + (−2) = 0). Q 102 | qa | Arithmetic
Container X contains a mixture of oil and water in the ratio 1:3, whereas container Y contains a mixture of oil and water in the ratio 2:3. If 2 litres of liquid from container X and 5 litres of liquid from container Y are mixed together in container Z that initially contains 1 litre of pure oil, then what is the percentage of oil in container Z?
(1) 41.25
(2) 42.75
(3) 43.75
(4) 44.25
= 3
~ Option 3, 43.75%, is correct. Step 1: In X, oil is 1 part out of 1 + 3 = 4, so 2 L of X carries 2 × 1/4 = 0.5 L of oil. Q 103 | qa | Geometry & Mensuration
A solid circular metallic disc of radius 12 inches and thickness 4 inches is melted and shaped into a solid disc of radius 16 inches. What is the thickness of the new disc in inches?
(1) 2
(2) 2.25
(3) 2.50
(4) 2.75
= 2
~ Option 2, 2.25 inches, is correct. Step 1: Melting and recasting does not change the volume of metal. Step 2: A disc is a short cylinder, so its volume is πr²h. Step 3: π × 12² × 4 = π × 16² × h, so 144 × 4 = 256 × h, that is, 576 = 256h. Step 4: h = 576/256 = 2.25 inches. X and Z, with jumps of about 25% and 17%, are well behind. The same idea appears in growth-rate questions on GDP or prices: a small base produces large percentage changes.

Q 107 | acc | Accounting Basics
The value of long-term investment in shares is subject to wide fluctuations. A provision created against fluctuation in value of investments is based on the convention of
(1) conservatism
(2) full disclosure
(3) materiality
(4) consistency
= 1
~ Option 1, conservatism, is correct. The convention of conservatism, also called prudence, says 'anticipate no profit, but provide for all possible losses'. Because the market value of shares can fall, a provision for fluctuation in the value of investments is created in advance, so that assets and profits are not overstated, while unrealised gains are not recorded. Other common examples are the provision for doubtful debts and valuing closing stock at cost or net realisable value, whichever is lower. In the same spirit, AS 13 carries current investments at the lower of cost and fair value and requires a provision for a decline, other than temporary, in long-term investments. Consistency means following the same accounting methods year after year so that results are comparable; materiality means only significant items need separate treatment; full disclosure means revealing all material information in the statements or notes. None of these is the basis for such a provision. Remember: a provision against a possible loss points to conservatism.

Q 108 | acc | Auditing & Company Accounts
Which one of the following aspects is not covered in audit?
(1) Examination of the system of accounting and internal control
(2) Preparation of books of accounts
(3) Reporting to the appropriate person/body
(4) Verification of the authenticity and validity of transactions
= 2
~ Option 2, preparation of books of accounts, is the aspect not covered by an audit. An audit is an independent examination of the books, vouchers and financial statements that the entity has already prepared, leading to an opinion and a report. It covers examining the system of accounting and internal control (option 1), vouching to verify that transactions are authentic and valid (option 4), and reporting to the appropriate person or body (option 3); for a company, the auditor reports to its members under Section 143 of the Companies Act, 2013. Preparing the books and financial statements is accounting work and the responsibility of management. Section 144 of the same Act even bars a company's auditor from providing accounting and book-keeping services, because he would then be checking his own work, which defeats the auditor's independence. Remember the classic line: 'auditing begins where accounting ends'. The auditor examines the accounts and gives an opinion on them; he does not write them.

Q 109 | acc | Auditing & Company Accounts
Which of the following sets the scope, timing and direction of the audit, and guides the development of the more detailed audit plan?
(1) Audit program
(2) Overall audit strategy
(3) Completion memorandum
(4) Audit plan
= 2
~ Option 2, overall audit strategy, is correct. SA 300 (Planning an Audit of Financial Statements) requires the auditor to establish an overall audit strategy that sets the scope, timing and direction of the audit and guides the development of the audit plan; the question repeats this wording. The audit plan (option 4) is narrower and more detailed and is built from the strategy: it sets out the nature, timing and extent of the risk assessment procedures and further audit procedures. An audit programme (option 1) lists the specific audit steps for the audit staff to carry out, and a completion memorandum (option 3) is prepared at the end of the audit to summarise the significant matters found. So the strategy is the broad framework at the top, and the plan and programme work it out in detail. SA 300 also treats planning as a continuous process rather than a single phase: the strategy and plan are updated as the audit proceeds and new information comes to light.

Q 110 | acc | Accounting Basics
Goodwill Account is a/an
(1) Personal Account
(2) Real Account
(3) Nominal Account
(4) Expense Account
= 2
~ Option 2, Real Account, is correct. Under the traditional (British) classification, accounts are personal, real or nominal. Real accounts are accounts of assets and property, both tangible (land, building, machinery, cash, stock) and intangible (goodwill, patents, trademarks, copyrights). Goodwill is an intangible asset, so it is an intangible real account and follows the rule 'debit what comes in, credit what goes out'. Personal accounts (option 1) relate to persons, firms and companies, such as debtors, creditors and capital; their rule is 'debit the receiver, credit the giver'. Nominal accounts (option 3) relate to expenses, losses, incomes and gains, with the rule 'debit all expenses and losses, credit all incomes and gains'; an expense account (option 4) is only one kind of nominal account. Goodwill appears on the assets side of the balance sheet and is not closed to the profit and loss account. Under AS 26, only purchased goodwill is recorded; internally generated goodwill is not recognised as an asset.

Q 111 | eco | Money, Banking & Inflation
The principle of subrogation in insurance allows
(1) investment of policy amount
(2) refund for insured and insured's insurance company
(3) auto-renewal of policy
(4) indemnification of the insured
= 2
~ Option 2 is correct. Subrogation means substituting the insurer in the place of the insured. After the insurer pays the insured's claim, it steps into the insured's shoes and can recover the amount from the third party who caused the loss. The recovery refunds the insurer for what it paid, and any amount recovered beyond that, such as an uninsured deductible, goes back to the insured, so the principle brings a refund to both the insured and the insurance company. Subrogation follows from the principle of indemnity and stops the insured from being paid twice, once by the insurer and again by the wrongdoer. Indemnification of the insured (option 4) is the principle of indemnity itself, not subrogation; investment of the policy amount (option 1) and auto-renewal (option 3) are unrelated. Subrogation applies to contracts of indemnity, such as fire, marine and motor insurance, not to life insurance. The other basic principles of insurance are utmost good faith, insurable interest, contribution and proximate cause.

Q 113 | eco | Growth & National Income

(1) 1 only
(2) 2 only
(3) Both 1 and 2
(4) Neither 1 nor 2
= 1
~ Option 1 is correct: only statement 1 is right. The NCERT textbook Fundamentals of Human Geography (Class XII) states that population change in an area is an important indicator of economic development, social upliftment and the historical and cultural background of the region. Changes in birth rates, death rates and migration reflect improvements in health care, education, the status of women and living conditions, so population change does indicate social upliftment. Q 114 | eco | Poverty, Employment & Welfare
