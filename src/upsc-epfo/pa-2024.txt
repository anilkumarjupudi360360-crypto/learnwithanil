# UPSC EPFO Recruitment Test for Personal Assistant (PA), Advt. 51/2024 (Special), held 7 July 2024. Booklet Series A (RTJL-A-GAT), General Ability Test, 120 questions, 300 marks.
# Source: the official UPSC question paper; answers from UPSC's official answer key, Series A (upsc.gov.in, published 29 September 2026). No question dropped. Explanations by Learn with Anil.

Q 1 | eng | Reading Comprehension
Read the passage given below and answer the question that follows.
The responsibility that devolves on the press is very great. The freedom of the press enjoys a sacred privilege, but it must not be exercised to serve communal and sectional interests. Passions and prejudices must not be allowed to dominate this privilege. It should not be abused to foster a partisan spirit and encourage fissiparous tendencies. It should mirror public opinion, but it also should guard public welfare. It must remember to exercise restraint and wisdom while throwing light on contemporary events.
Which one of the following statements best reflects the critical message conveyed by the author of the passage?
(1) The press must be responsible as it shapes public opinion.
(2) The press is the guardian of freedom of expression.
(3) The press is an independent institution and is free to influence people's way of thinking.
(4) The only task of press is to report events happening around.
= 1
~ The passage opens with "The responsibility that devolves on the press is very great" and then lists what that responsibility demands: no communal or partisan use of its freedom, guarding public welfare, and restraint and wisdom in reporting. Because the press mirrors and influences public opinion, it must act responsibly, so (1) captures the central message. (2) is not discussed; (3) ignores the passage's stress on restraint and limits; (4) contradicts the passage, which expects the press to guard public welfare, not merely report events.

Q 2 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Consider the following statements:
I. He regretted for his mistake.
II. We ought to make exercise daily.
III. This has been going on for a long time.
Which of the statements given above is/are grammatically correct?
(1) I and II
(2) II only
(3) I and III
(4) III only
= 4
~ Only III is correct: "has been going on for a long time" is the present perfect continuous with "for" plus a period of time. Sentence I is wrong because "regret" is a transitive verb and takes a direct object without "for": "He regretted his mistake" (one "repents of" a mistake, but simply "regrets" it). Sentence II is wrong because the accepted collocation is "take exercise" or "do exercise", not "make exercise": "We ought to take exercise daily."

Q 3 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Consider the following statements:
I. He is now ashamed from his conduct.
II. He is now ashamed of his conduct.
III. I congratulate you for your success.
IV. I congratulate you on your success.
Which two of the statements given above are correct?
(1) I and III
(2) II and IV
(3) I and IV
(4) II and III
= 2
~ The question tests fixed prepositions. "Ashamed" takes "of": one is ashamed of one's conduct, so II is correct and I ("ashamed from") is wrong. "Congratulate" takes "on" before the achievement or occasion: "I congratulate you on your success", so IV is correct. "Congratulate for" (III) is treated as incorrect in standard grammar texts, though it is heard in casual use. Hence II and IV.

Q 4 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Consider the following statements:
I. No less than seventy people were hurt in the accident.
II. One must finish his duty on time.
III. He took off his sweater.
Which of the statements given above is/are grammatically correct?
(1) I and II
(2) II only
(3) I and III
(4) III only
= 4
~ Only III is correct: "take off" (remove clothing) is used correctly. Sentence I breaks the traditional rule that "fewer" is used with countable nouns (number) and "less" with uncountable ones (quantity); since people are counted, it should be "No fewer than seventy people were hurt". Sentence II breaks the rule that the indefinite pronoun "one" is followed by "one's", not "his": "One must finish one's duty on time." Both rules are standard in Indian grammar texts such as Wren and Martin, though informal usage is looser.

Q 5 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Consider the following:
The teacher has become so stern that his popularity is on the wane.
It means:
(1) The teacher's increasing popularity has made him more strict.
(2) The teacher's increasing strictness has made him more popular.
(3) The teacher's decreasing popularity has made him more strict.
(4) The teacher's increasing strictness has made him less popular.
= 4
~ "On the wane" means decreasing or declining, like the waning moon. In a "so ... that" sentence the first part is the cause and the second the result: the teacher has become so stern (cause) that his popularity is declining (result). So his increasing strictness has made him less popular. (1) and (3) reverse cause and effect, making popularity the cause of strictness, and (2) wrongly says his popularity has increased.

Q 6 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Consider the following:
He bears his troubles bravely, never begs for sympathy, never yields to sorrow and never shows pain.
What is the most appropriate word that describes him?
(1) Vivacious
(2) Stoical
(3) Magnanimous
(4) Effusive
= 2
~ A stoical person endures pain and hardship patiently, without complaining or showing feelings, exactly as described: he bears his troubles bravely, never begs for sympathy and never shows pain. The word comes from the Stoic philosophers of ancient Greece, who taught calm acceptance of misfortune. "Vivacious" means lively and animated; "magnanimous" means generous and forgiving, especially towards a rival; "effusive" means expressing feelings in an unrestrained, gushing way, which is almost the opposite of stoical.

Q 7 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Consider the following:
"This boy takes after his father."
What does it mean?
(1) This boy follows his father's advice.
(2) This boy steals from his father's wallet.
(3) This boy manages his father's affairs.
(4) This boy resembles his father.
= 4
~ The phrasal verb "take after" means to resemble an older member of one's family, usually a parent, in appearance, character or behaviour: "She takes after her mother; both are musical." So "This boy takes after his father" means he resembles his father. It does not mean following advice (that would be "listen to" or "follow"), taking something without permission, or looking after someone's affairs ("look after" or "manage").

Q 8 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Consider the following statements:
I. We happened to meet at the house of common friend.
II. The book you mention is out of print.
III. The Himalayas lie to north of India.
In which of the above statements is an article missing?
(1) I and II
(2) II only
(3) I and III
(4) III only
= 3
~ In I, "friend" is a singular countable noun and needs an article: "at the house of a common friend". In III, a direction used after "to" or "in" takes "the": "The Himalayas lie to the north of India." Sentence II is complete: "The book you mention is out of print" uses "the" correctly, and "out of print" (no longer published) is a fixed phrase with no article. Hence an article is missing in I and III.

Q 9 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Consider the following pairs of statements:
Affirmative form : Negative form
I. He is sometimes foolish. : He is not always wise.
II. Every man makes mistakes sometimes. : Every man does not make mistakes.
III. He is greater than I. : I am not as great as he.
Which of the pairs given above is/are correctly matched?
(1) I, II and III
(2) II only
(3) I and III only
(4) III only
= 3
~ An affirmative sentence can be changed into a negative one only if the meaning stays the same. Pair I is correct: being sometimes foolish means one is not always wise. Pair III is correct: "He is greater than I" means "I am not as great as he". Pair II is wrong: "Every man does not make mistakes" means that not every man makes mistakes, which contradicts the original. A correct negative form would be "There is no man who does not make mistakes sometimes" or "No man is free from mistakes".

Q 10 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Consider the following pairs of statements:
Statement : Meaning
I. My heart is too full for words. : My heart is so full of words that I am compelled to talk.
II. If you eat too much you will be ill. : Do not eat too much lest you will be ill.
III. But for you I should have caught the train. : I should have caught the train to be with you.
Which of the pairs given above is/are correctly matched?
(1) I and II
(2) II only
(3) I and III
(4) III only
= 2
~ Only pair II keeps the meaning: both versions warn that eating too much will make you ill ("lest" means "for fear that"; strictly it takes "should", as in "lest you should be ill"). Pair I is wrong: "too full for words" means the heart is so full of emotion that one cannot speak, the opposite of being compelled to talk. Pair III is wrong: "But for you I should have caught the train" means "If it had not been for you, I would have caught the train", that is, because of you I missed it.

Q 11 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
When somebody says "the boss was livid when yet another deadline was missed", which one of the following words appropriately describes the boss's state of mind?
(1) Contrite
(2) Furious
(3) Placid
(4) Amenable
= 2
~ "Livid" in everyday use means extremely angry; it originally described the bluish-grey colour of a bruise or of a face pale with rage. A boss who is livid when yet another deadline is missed is therefore furious. "Contrite" means feeling sorry for a wrong one has done, which would describe the person who missed the deadline, not the boss. "Placid" means calm and not easily upset, the opposite of livid. "Amenable" means willing to agree or be persuaded.

Q 12 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
If your friend reveals his personal life to you because he trusts you to be discreet, which one of the following appropriately describes you?
(1) Conciliatory
(2) Dauntless
(3) Gregarious
(4) Prudent
= 4
~ To be discreet is to be careful in what one says or does, especially to keep confidences and avoid causing embarrassment. A friend who is trusted with private matters is expected to be prudent, that is, careful, sensible and showing good judgement; "prudent" is the closest of the options to "discreet". "Conciliatory" means intended to make peace or pacify; "dauntless" means fearless and determined; "gregarious" means fond of company and sociable, which says nothing about keeping secrets.

Q 13 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
The phrases 'look down upon' and 'intense dislike' denote which one of the following?
(1) Conceit
(2) Disdain
(3) Enigmatic
(4) Indignant
= 2
~ "Disdain" is the feeling that someone or something is unworthy of respect: a mixture of contempt and strong dislike. It combines both phrases, looking down upon a person and disliking him intensely, as in "She regarded his lies with disdain." "Conceit" is an excessively high opinion of oneself, not of others. "Enigmatic" means mysterious or puzzling, and "indignant" means angry at something unfair; both are adjectives and neither involves looking down on someone.

Q 14 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Consider the following pairs:
Phrase : Meaning
I. Likely to happen without delay : Imminent
II. Inclined to silence : Parsimonious
III. Unwilling to spend : Taciturn
Which of the above is/are correctly matched?
(1) I only
(2) II and III
(3) I and III
(4) II only
= 1
~ Only pair I is correct: "imminent" means about to happen, likely to occur very soon, as in "an imminent storm". Pairs II and III have their words swapped. "Inclined to silence", reserved and saying little, is "taciturn", while "unwilling to spend money", extremely frugal or stingy, is "parsimonious". So II and III are mismatched, and the answer is I only.

Q 15 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Consider the following words:
I. Advance
II. Escalate
III. Lighten
IV. Retain
Which two of the above words constitute one synonym and one antonym for the word "Alleviate"?
(1) I and II
(2) II and III
(3) I and IV
(4) III and IV
= 2
~ "Alleviate" means to make pain, suffering or a problem less severe, as in "medicine to alleviate the pain". "Lighten" (make a burden or load less heavy) is a synonym, and "escalate" (increase rapidly in intensity or seriousness) is an antonym. "Advance" (move forward) and "retain" (keep) are neither synonyms nor antonyms of "alleviate". Hence II and III.

Q 16 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Consider the following words:
I. Calm
II. Excite
III. Polite
IV. Relieve
Which of the above words are synonyms for the word 'Assuage'?
(1) I and II
(2) II and III
(3) I and IV
(4) III and IV
= 3
~ "Assuage" means to make an unpleasant feeling less intense, or to soothe and satisfy, as in "to assuage grief" or "to assuage one's hunger". Its synonyms are "calm" (soothe, quieten) and "relieve" (lessen pain or distress). "Excite" is closer to an antonym, since it means to stir up feelings, and "polite" is an adjective meaning courteous, which has nothing to do with lessening pain or anger. Hence I and IV.

Q 17 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Which one of the following words correctly means "above board"?
(1) Across
(2) Honest
(3) Invincible
(4) Imperious
= 2
~ "Above board" means honest, open and legitimate, with nothing concealed, as in "The deal was completely above board." The phrase is said to come from card games, where a player who keeps his hands above the table (board) cannot cheat. "Across" is a preposition of place, "invincible" means too strong to be defeated, and "imperious" means arrogant and domineering, expecting to be obeyed. None of these means honest.

Q 18 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
What does 'be in the same boat' mean?
(1) Be in the same difficult circumstances as others
(2) Be in competition with others to win a race or prize
(3) To work tirelessly for the welfare of one's fellow beings
(4) To be faithful and loyal to norms and traditions of one's community
= 1
~ To "be in the same boat" is to be in the same unpleasant or difficult situation as others: "Don't complain about the delay; we are all in the same boat." The image is of passengers in a small boat who all face the same danger at sea. It does not refer to competition, to working for the welfare of others or to loyalty to the traditions of a community, so the other options are wrong.

Q 19 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
What does the phrase 'the coast is clear' mean?
(1) Life is pleasant and comfortable
(2) To be free of responsibilities and liabilities
(3) There is no danger of being caught
(4) Free of threat by sea
= 3
~ "The coast is clear" means that there is no one around to see or stop you, so there is no danger of being seen or caught: "When the coast was clear, the boys climbed over the wall." It is believed to come from smugglers, who landed goods only when no coastguard was watching the shore. Option (4) takes the phrase literally, which is the trap. It has nothing to do with a comfortable life or freedom from responsibilities.

Q 20 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Consider the following statement:
"The way he appears or behaves is not true or real."
Which one of the following words most appropriately describes the person's character?
(1) Caveat
(2) Felony
(3) Masquerade
(4) Narcissist
= 3
~ A "masquerade" is a false show or pretence, an outward appearance that hides the truth; as a verb, to masquerade is to pretend to be someone or something one is not. It fits a person whose appearance and behaviour are not true or real. A "caveat" is a warning or proviso, a "felony" is a serious crime, and a "narcissist" is a person with an excessive interest in and admiration for himself, which is not the same as being false.

Q 21 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Consider the following statement:
"He does not reveal his thoughts or feelings readily."
Which one of the following words most appropriately describes the person's character?
(1) Reticent
(2) Intangible
(3) Upfront
(4) Transient
= 1
~ A "reticent" person is reserved and does not readily reveal his thoughts or feelings; the statement is practically the dictionary definition of the word. "Upfront" means frank and open, which is the opposite. "Intangible" means something that cannot be touched or clearly grasped, such as goodwill, and "transient" means lasting only a short time. Neither of these describes a person who keeps his feelings to himself.

Q 22 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Fill in the blank by choosing the correct option:
"I think she is busy at the moment because ______ (she, wash) her clothes."
(1) she was washing
(2) she has just washed
(3) she is washing
(4) she had been washing
= 3
~ The present continuous tense (is/am/are + -ing) is used for an action going on at the time of speaking. "At the moment" and "she is busy" show that the washing is happening now, so the answer is "she is washing her clothes". "She was washing" and "she had been washing" put the action in the past, and "she has just washed" means the washing is already finished, which would not explain why she is busy now.

Q 23 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
What is the meaning of the underlined part "paddle my own canoe" of the following sentence?
In future I will "paddle my own canoe" without seeking support.
(1) To lean on one's siblings for support
(2) To utilize one's contacts
(3) To be independent and self-reliant
(4) To use one's own pitch
= 3
~ To "paddle one's own canoe" means to be independent and self-reliant, to manage one's own affairs without help from others; the image is of a person moving his canoe with his own paddle. The words "without seeking support" in the sentence point the same way. Leaning on siblings or using one's contacts is the opposite of self-reliance, and "to use one's own pitch" is a distractor with no connection to the idiom.

Q 24 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
What is the meaning of the underlined part "roller-coaster" of the following sentence?
She went through an emotional "roller-coaster" while attending the tumultuous family event.
(1) Enjoying life to the fullest
(2) Taking the emotions very seriously
(3) Talking to people who are emotional
(4) Undergoing great mood swings
= 4
~ A roller-coaster is a fairground ride that rises and plunges steeply, so an "emotional roller-coaster" is a situation in which one's feelings change rapidly between extremes such as joy and sadness, that is, great mood swings. The word "tumultuous" (confused, noisy and full of upheaval) supports this. The phrase does not mean enjoying life, taking emotions seriously or talking to emotional people.

Q 25 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Read the following two statements, each given in the form of Active Voice and Passive Voice:
Statement | Active Voice | Passive Voice
I. | I know you. | I am known to you.
II. | His presence irritates me sometimes. | I am sometimes irritated by his presence.
In which of the above is the Active Voice correctly expressed in the Passive Voice?
(1) I only
(2) II only
(3) Both I and II
(4) Neither I nor II
= 2
~ In the passive voice the object of the active sentence becomes the subject. In I the object is "you", so the passive is "You are known to me" (or "by me"); "I am known to you" reverses the meaning, saying that you know me. In II the object "me" correctly becomes the subject "I", the verb becomes "am irritated", and the doer "his presence" follows "by", with the tense (simple present) unchanged. Hence only II is correct.

Q 26 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Read the following two statements, each given in the form of Active Voice and Passive Voice:
Statement | Active Voice | Passive Voice
I. | We were investigating the matter. | The matter is being investigated by us.
II. | The Government gave him an award. | He got an award from the Government.
In which of the above is the Active Voice correctly expressed in the Passive Voice?
(1) I only
(2) II only
(3) Both I and II
(4) Neither I nor II
= 4
~ Neither conversion is correct. In a change of voice the tense must stay the same. "We were investigating the matter" is in the past continuous, so its passive is "The matter was being investigated by us"; the given "is being investigated" wrongly shifts it to the present continuous. In II, "He got an award from the Government" only rephrases the idea: "He" is still the subject doing the action "got", so the sentence is still in the active voice. The correct passive is "He was given an award by the Government" or "An award was given to him by the Government."

Q 27 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Read the following two statements, each given in the form of Direct Speech and Indirect Speech:
Statement | Direct Speech | Indirect Speech
I. | He said to me, "Please wait here until I return." | He requested me to wait here until he returned.
II. | The taxi driver said to us, "Where are you going?" | The taxi driver enquired of us where were we going.
In which of the above is the Direct Speech correctly expressed in the Indirect Speech?
(1) I only
(2) II only
(3) Both I and II
(4) Neither I nor II
= 4
~ In indirect speech, besides the change of tense, words showing nearness change to words showing distance: now becomes then, this becomes that and here becomes there. So I should read "He requested me to wait there until he returned"; "Please" correctly becomes "requested" and "return" correctly becomes "returned", but keeping "here" makes it wrong by this standard rule. In II, a reported question takes the word order of a statement, not of a question: "The taxi driver enquired of us (asked us) where we were going." The given "where were we going" keeps the question order. So neither is correct.

Q 28 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Read the following two statements, each given in the form of Direct Speech and Indirect Speech:
Statement | Direct Speech | Indirect Speech
I. | I said to him, "Go away." | I ordered him to go away.
II. | I shouted, "Let me go." | I ordered them to let me go.
In which of the above is the Direct Speech correctly expressed in the Indirect Speech?
(1) I only
(2) II only
(3) Both I and II
(4) Neither I nor II
= 1
~ A command is reported with a verb such as ordered, told or asked followed by the to-infinitive, so "I said to him, 'Go away'" correctly becomes "I ordered him to go away." In II the direct speech has no listener, and "Let me go" is an urgent plea to be released, not an order given to particular people. The given version invents "them" and turns the plea into an order, which changes the meaning. It is better reported as "I shouted that I should be allowed to go." Hence only I is correct.

Q 29 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Read the following pairs:
Pair | Idiom | Meaning
I. | A rolling stone gathers no moss | A person who does not settle down will not attain success
II. | To have a bee in your bonnet | To talk and think excessively about something
Which of the above pairs is/are correctly matched?
(1) I only
(2) II only
(3) Both I and II
(4) Neither I nor II
= 3
~ Both pairs are correctly matched. "A rolling stone gathers no moss" is a proverb saying that a person who keeps moving from place to place or from job to job, never settling down, gains no wealth, standing or lasting success; the moss stands for what builds up over time. "To have a bee in your bonnet" means to be obsessed with an idea and keep thinking and talking about it, like a bee trapped in a hat that buzzes all the time: "She has a bee in her bonnet about healthy eating."

Q 30 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Fill in the blanks with appropriate prepositions given below:
We cut ______ the fence and snuck ______ the secret private basement.
(1) into, through
(2) in, into
(3) into, by
(4) through, into
= 4
~ "Through" means from one side of something to the other, so we cut through a fence to make a way across it. "Into" shows movement from outside to the inside of an enclosed space, so we snuck (sneaked) into the basement: "We cut through the fence and snuck into the secret private basement." "Cut into" means to make a cut in a surface or to eat into something (cut into profits), not to pass across it; "snuck by the basement" would mean passing beside it, not entering it; and "in" shows position, not movement into a place.

Q 31 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Consider the following sentence with a blank to be filled in:
"Sound education is ______ to pupils in this institution."
(1) conceded
(2) dispensed
(3) furnished
(4) imparted
= 4
~ The standard collocation is "impart education (or knowledge, training) to someone", meaning to pass on knowledge or skill by teaching: "Sound education is imparted to pupils in this institution." "Conceded" means admitted or yielded, often unwillingly (concede defeat, concede a point). "Dispensed" means handed out or distributed (medicines, justice, food), and "furnished" means supplied or provided, usually with furniture or information; neither is the natural verb for passing on education through teaching.

Q 32 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Fill in the blank with the correct idiom:
He has ______ by establishing the first private university in India.
(1) created a genre
(2) initiated a yardstick
(3) made history
(4) designed a milestone
= 3
~ "To make history" means to do something important that has never been done before and will be remembered, so being the first to establish a private university fits exactly: "He has made history by establishing the first private university in India." The other options are not idioms and pair the nouns with the wrong verbs: a genre is created in art or literature, a yardstick is set or used as a standard of comparison, not initiated, and a milestone is reached or achieved, not designed.

Q 33 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
A person who is born into a rich, famous or important family is called:
(1) Factotum
(2) Scion
(3) Warmonger
(4) Recusant
= 2
~ A "scion" is a descendant or heir of a rich, famous or important family, as in "a scion of a business dynasty"; the word first meant a young shoot of a plant cut for grafting. A "factotum" is a servant or employee who does all kinds of jobs. A "warmonger" is a person who encourages or tries to start a war. A "recusant" is one who refuses to obey an authority or rule; historically, a Roman Catholic in England who refused to attend Church of England services.

Q 34 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
If your boss continually praises you for meticulous attention to detail and polite conduct, which of the following most appropriately describes you?
(1) Peremptory
(2) Perfunctory
(3) Punctilious
(4) Proficient
= 3
~ "Punctilious" means showing great attention to details and to correct, proper behaviour, so it covers both the qualities praised here: meticulous attention to detail and polite conduct. "Peremptory" means insisting on immediate obedience in a brusque, commanding way, the opposite of polite. "Perfunctory" means done carelessly as a mere routine, with the least effort, the opposite of meticulous. "Proficient" means skilled or competent, which says nothing about care for detail or good manners.

Q 35 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
What is the synonym of the underlined word "languid" in the following sentence?
The "languid" student was always late to the class.
(1) Lamenting
(2) Indifferent
(3) Difficult
(4) Engaged
= 2
~ "Languid" means lacking energy, vigour or interest: slow, listless and unenthusiastic. Dictionaries give "lacking in spirit or interest; listless; indifferent" as one of its senses, so "indifferent" (showing no interest or concern) is the closest synonym among the options, and it suits a student who is always late for class. "Lamenting" means expressing grief or sorrow. "Difficult" means hard to do or to deal with. "Engaged" means busy, involved or interested, which is nearly the opposite of languid.

Q 36 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
What is the antonym of the underlined word "paucity" in the following sentence?
There is a "paucity" of information on the nutritive value of this item.
(1) Abundance
(2) Scarcity
(3) Deficiency
(4) Inadequacy
= 1
~ "Paucity" means the presence of something in only small or insufficient quantity, a scarcity or dearth, as in "a paucity of information". Its opposite is "abundance", a very large or plentiful quantity, as in "an abundance of information". "Scarcity", "deficiency" and "inadequacy" all mean a shortage or lack of something, so they are synonyms of paucity, not antonyms; they are the traps in this question.

Q 37 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Fill in the blank in the following sentence using one of the words that follow:
The policeman was running very fast ______ the thief.
(1) unto
(2) behind
(3) for
(4) into
= 2
~ The policeman is chasing the thief, who is ahead of him, so the preposition must show position at the back of someone moving ahead: "The policeman was running very fast behind the thief." ("Running after the thief" is the commoner idiom for a chase, but "after" is not among the options.) "Into" would mean colliding with the thief, "for" shows purpose or destination (run for the bus, run for help), and "unto" is an archaic form of "to" that does not fit the sentence.

Q 38 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Consider the following sentences:
I. You have to choose within bread and milk.

Q 43 | eng | Grammar & Usage
What is the correct format for the interrogative sentences in present perfect continuous tense?
(1) Has/have + subject + been + present participle + ..... ?
(2) Has/have + have + subject + present participle + ..... ?
(3) Been + has/have + subject + present participle + ..... ?
(4) Subject + been + has/have + present participle + ..... ?
= 1
~ The present perfect continuous is formed with has/have + been + present participle (the -ing form): "She has been working." To make a question, only the first auxiliary, has/have, moves before the subject, while "been" stays after it: "Has she been working since morning?", "Have they been waiting long?" So the pattern is Has/have + subject + been + present participle. The other options add a second "have" or put "been" before the auxiliary or the subject, which no English question does.

Q 44 | eng | Grammar & Usage

(1) Was/were + not + subject + present participle + ..... ?
(2) Was/were + subject + not + present participle + ..... ?
(3) Was/were + present participle + subject + not + ..... ?
(4) Present participle + was/were + not + subject + ..... ?
= 2
~ The past continuous is was/were + present participle: "He was playing." In a question the auxiliary was/were comes before the subject, and in the full (uncontracted) negative question "not" follows the subject: "Was he not playing?", "Were they not listening?" So the pattern is Was/were + subject + not + present participle. "Not" stands before the subject only in the contracted form ("Wasn't he playing?"); "Was not he playing?" is not standard. If p implies '+'; q implies '−'; r implies '×'; s implies '÷', then insert the proper letter between the figures in order to satisfy the equality 12 __ 5 __ 50 __ 10 __ 13 = 0. Which one is the correct sequence of letters?
(1) r, q, s, p
(2) p, r, s, q
(3) p, s, r, q
(4) q, r, s, p
= 4
~ Option (4), q, r, s, p, gives 12 − 5 × 50 ÷ 10 + 13. By BODMAS, 5 × 50 ÷ 10 = 25, so the expression is 12 − 25 + 13 = 0, as required. Option (1) gives 12 × 5 − 50 ÷ 10 + 13 = 68 and option (2) gives 12 + 5 × 50 ÷ 10 − 13 = 24. However, option (3), p, s, r, q, gives 12 + 5 ÷ 50 × 10 − 13 = 12 + 1 − 13 = 0 as well (5 ÷ 50 = 0.1, then × 10 = 1, by the same rules), so strictly two options satisfy the equality; (3) fails only if × is done before ÷. UPSC keyed (4). (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 110 | qa | Reasoning
In a certain code, MANGO is coded as 50 and DATE is coded as 30. What should be the code for ORANGE?
(1) 75
(2) 70
(3) 65
(4) 60
= 4
~ The code is the sum of the alphabetical positions of the letters (A = 1, B = 2, … Z = 26). MANGO: M 13 + A 1 + N 14 + G 7 + O 15 = 50. DATE: D 4 + A 1 + T 20 + E 5 = 30. Both match the given codes. Q 111 | qa | Algebra & Numbers

Q 115 | qa | Arithmetic

Q 119 | qa | Geometry & Mensuration

Q 120 | qa | Algebra & Numbers
A Question is given followed by two Statements I and II. Consider the Question and the Statements and mark the correct option.
Question: What is the remainder when (x + 1)ⁿ is divided by x (n is a natural number)?
Statement-I: n is divisible by 4.
Statement-II: n is divisible by 3.
Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above Question and the Statements?
(1) The Question can be answered by using one of the Statements alone, but cannot be answered using the other Statement alone.
(2) The Question can be answered by using either Statement alone.
(3) The Question can be answered by using both the Statements together, but cannot be answered using either Statement alone.
(4) The Question can be answered without using these Statements.
= 4
~ By the binomial theorem, (x + 1)ⁿ = xⁿ + nC1·xⁿ⁻¹ + … + nC(n−1)·x + 1, and every term except the final 1 is a multiple of x. So the remainder on dividing by x is always 1, whatever the natural number n (as polynomial division, or for any whole number x greater than 1). For example, 4³ = 64 leaves remainder 1 on division by 3. Knowing that n is divisible by 4 or by 3 adds nothing, so the question can be answered without the statements.
