Q 1 | eng | Sentence Arrangement
The first and sixth sentences of a passage are given as S1 and S6. The four sentences in between have been jumbled up and labelled P, Q, R and S. Choose the proper sequence of the four sentences.
S1: Geological mapping, when properly done, demands skill and judgment.
S6: Nothing is more naive than to believe that a field geologist should gather only 'facts', the interpretation of which is to be made at a later date.
P: While the field work progresses he should conceive as many interpretations as are consistent with the known facts.
Q: As the field work progresses and the larger geological picture begins to unfold, experience and judgment are essential if the geologist is to evaluate properly the vast number of facts gathered from thousands of outcrops.
R: Such mapping requires keen observation and a knowledge of what data are significant.
S: Above all, the field geologist must use the method of "working multiple hypotheses" to deduce the geological structure.
(1) SRQP
(2) RQSP
(3) PSQR
(4) SRPQ
= 2
~ Option 2, RQSP, is correct. S1 says that geological mapping "demands skill and judgment", and R must follow at once because "Such mapping" points back to it and names the first skill needed: keen observation and knowing which data are significant. Q then moves the passage forward in time: "as the field work progresses", experience and judgment, the second word of S1, are needed to evaluate facts gathered from thousands of outcrops. S opens with "Above all", which signals the most important requirement of all, the method of "working multiple hypotheses". P explains that method, and its pronoun "he" needs "the field geologist" of S just before it. P's idea of forming many interpretations while the work goes on leads straight into S6, which mocks gathering only facts now and interpreting them later. Options 1 and 4 open with S, but "Above all" cannot begin a list of requirements, and option 3 opens with P, whose "he" has no clear antecedent. Tip: first look for pronouns and demonstratives ("such", "these", "he") and for linkers such as "Above all", which tie a sentence to the one before it.

Q 2 | eng | Sentence Arrangement
The first and sixth sentences of a passage are given as S1 and S6. The four sentences in between have been jumbled up and labelled P, Q, R and S. Choose the proper sequence of the four sentences.
S1: Under the colonial regime, basic infrastructure such as railways, ports, water transport, posts and telegraphs did develop.
S6: Naturally, therefore, these areas remained inaccessible to the people.
P: There always remained an acute shortage of all-weather roads to reach out to the rural areas during the rainy season.
Q: Roads constructed in India prior to the advent of British rule were not fit for modern transport.
R: However, the real motive behind this development was not necessarily to provide basic amenities to the people but to subserve various colonial interests.
S: The roads that were built primarily served the purposes of mobilizing the army within India and drawing out raw materials from the countryside to the nearest railway station.
(1) SPQR
(2) PQSR
(3) QPRS
(4) RQSP
= 4
~ Option 4, RQSP, is correct. R must follow S1: "However" contrasts with S1's admission that infrastructure "did develop", and "this development" refers back to it before revealing the real motive, to serve colonial interests rather than the people. Q and S then narrow the discussion to roads: roads built before British rule were unfit for modern transport, and the roads the British built mainly moved the army and carried raw materials from the countryside to the nearest railway station. P gives the result, an acute shortage of all-weather roads to the rural areas in the rainy season, and S6's "these areas" can only mean those rural areas, so P must come last. Option 1 opens with S, whose "The roads that were built" has no context, option 2 opens with P and leaves R without a link to S1, and option 3 puts R in the middle and ends with S, so S6's "these areas" loses its referent. The passage echoes the NCERT account of the Indian economy on the eve of independence: railways (first train Bombay to Thane, 1853), ports and telegraphs were built mainly to serve British trade and administration.

Q 3 | eng | Sentence Arrangement
The first and sixth sentences of a passage are given as S1 and S6. The four sentences in between have been jumbled up and labelled P, Q, R and S. Choose the proper sequence of the four sentences.
S1: Contemporary history is necessary, but it is at best a tentative record.
S6: Some portentous events can be seen to be so from the moment of their occurrence, like the discovery of the New World; others of comparable momentousness are hidden in obscurity.
P: The relevance of the issue of proportion is significant.
Q: It possesses, indeed, a massive supply of public record, but it necessarily lacks the inner knowledge of politicians' minds, of planners' proposals, of scientists' discoveries, of technicians' break-throughs.
R: It also lacks perspective since it is bereft of a sense of proportion.
S: It is impossible, over just a few years of observation, to estimate accurately the significance of various events.
(1) QPSR
(2) SRPQ
(3) QSRP
(4) PRSQ
= 3
~ Option 3, QSRP, is correct. Q follows S1 directly: "It" is contemporary history, and its "massive supply of public record", introduced with the concession "indeed", picks up S1's "tentative record" before the turn "but it necessarily lacks" the inner knowledge of politicians, planners and scientists. S gives a second limitation: over just a few years of observation, the true significance of events cannot be estimated accurately. R adds a third with "also lacks": contemporary history has no perspective because it has no sense of proportion. P can only come after R, because "the issue of proportion" needs R to introduce the word, and P's remark that proportion matters leads into S6, which illustrates it: some momentous events, like the discovery of the New World, are obvious at once, while others of equal weight stay hidden. Option 1 (QPSR) and option 4 (PRSQ) mention proportion before R introduces it, and option 2 (SRPQ) pushes Q to the end, although its "massive supply of public record" must answer S1 at once. Tip: words such as "also" and "the issue of" show that a sentence depends on an earlier one.

Q 4 | eng | Sentence Arrangement
The first and sixth sentences of a passage are given as S1 and S6. The four sentences in between have been jumbled up and labelled P, Q, R and S. Choose the proper sequence of the four sentences.
S1: Income inequality in less-developed countries is worse than most estimates.
S6: The tax regime in these countries needs to be progressive and futuristic.
P: There is a staggering level of difference between the income metrics and the actual income of the prosperous in such countries.
Q: The effective tax rates are not really progressive with regard to income.
R: They need to be reengineered to bring into the tax net the enormous sums of 'missing' income.
S: They are even less so with regard to wealth.
(1) PQSR
(2) QSPR
(3) QSRP
(4) SRPQ
= 2
~ Option 2, QSPR, is the keyed order. Q and S form a fixed pair: S's "They are even less so with regard to wealth" needs Q's "not really progressive with regard to income" immediately before it, so tax rates are judged first on income and then, even more harshly, on wealth. P then points to the staggering gap between measured income and the actual income of the prosperous, and R gives the remedy: the tax rates must be reengineered to bring this "missing" income into the tax net, which S6 sums up as the need for a progressive and futuristic tax regime. Option 4 (SRPQ) fails because S cannot open the passage, and option 3 (QSRP) speaks of the "missing" income before P explains it. However, option 1 (PQSR), with P directly explaining S1's claim that inequality is "worse than most estimates" and R's "They" following S's "They", reads at least as naturally, which is why the key is debated. Concept: a tax is progressive when its rate rises as income or wealth rises, as with India's income tax slabs. (Official key; some sources differ.)

Q 5 | eng | Sentence Arrangement
The first and sixth sentences of a passage are given as S1 and S6. The four sentences in between have been jumbled up and labelled P, Q, R and S. Choose the proper sequence of the four sentences.
S1: The history of English literature is usually divided into different periods.
S6: This is the real danger of periodization, which tends to give the impression of distinct categories where there are none.
P: However there are important discontinuities to be kept in mind.
Q: Each period has unique features that produce certain family resemblances between the writers of a particular period.
R: Scholars often specialize in periods such as Medieval, Renaissance, Romantic, Victorian, and Modern literature.
S: Failure to detect these discontinuities often produce a false sense of uniformity and consensus within a period.
(1) PQRS
(2) RQPS
(3) PSQR
(4) RSPQ
= 2
~ Option 2, RQPS, is correct. S1 says that the history of English literature is usually divided into periods, and R follows by naming the periods scholars specialise in: Medieval, Renaissance, Romantic, Victorian and Modern. Q explains what holds a period together: unique features that produce family resemblances among its writers. P, opening with "However", turns against this neat picture by warning of important discontinuities, and S must follow P because "these discontinuities" refers back to them. S then states the effect, a false sense of uniformity and consensus within a period, and S6's "This is the real danger of periodization" sums up exactly that, so S comes last. Options 1 and 3 open with P, but "However" needs a view to contrast with, which R and Q supply, and option 3 also separates S from S6. Option 4 (RSPQ) uses "these discontinuities" before P has mentioned them. Tip: a sentence beginning with "However" needs the idea it contradicts before it. Note that in S, the printed "produce" should strictly be "produces", agreeing with the singular subject "Failure".

Q 6 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Find the part of the sentence that contains an error. If there is no error, choose "No error".
Ramesh liked his friend's music player to his own.
(1) Ramesh liked
(2) his friend's music player
(3) to his own.
(4) No error
= 1
~ Option 1, "Ramesh liked", contains the error. The verb "like" cannot take a "to" phrase of comparison; the verb that compares one thing with another using "to" is "prefer". The correct sentence is "Ramesh preferred his friend's music player to his own." Parts 2 and 3 are correct once the verb is changed: "his friend's music player" is the object, and "to his own" (to his own music player) completes the comparison. "Liked his friend's music player more than his own" would also be correct, but with "to" the sentence needs "preferred". Rule: "prefer" takes "to" before a noun or gerund (I prefer tea to coffee; she prefers walking to driving), and "rather than" before an infinitive (I prefer to walk rather than drive). Never write "prefer ... than". The same "to" pattern follows the Latin comparatives superior, inferior, senior, junior and prior, and also the adjective preferable: "He is senior to me", not "senior than me". Trap: students often look for the error in "to his own", but the preposition is right; it is the verb that cannot take it.

Q 7 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Find the part of the sentence that contains an error. If there is no error, choose "No error".
Given the complex of the problem the committee is not expected to arrive at a decision any time soon.
(1) Given the complex of the problem
(2) the committee is not expected
(3) to arrive at a decision any time soon.
(4) No error
= 1
~ Option 1, "Given the complex of the problem", contains the error. As a noun, "complex" means a group of connected buildings or parts (a sports complex, a temple complex) or a psychological fixation (an inferiority complex); it does not mean the quality of being complicated. The abstract noun needed after "the ... of the problem" is "complexity": "Given the complexity of the problem, the committee is not expected to arrive at a decision any time soon." "Given" here works as a preposition meaning "considering", and a comma after the introductory phrase is good style. The adjective would also work in a different structure: "Given how complex the problem is". Part 2 is a correct passive ("is not expected"), and part 3 uses the right collocation "arrive at a decision" and the idiom "any time soon", meaning in the near future. Remember similar adjective-noun pairs: simple and simplicity, intense and intensity, scarce and scarcity, complex and complexity. Trap: an adjective or a concrete noun placed where an abstract noun is required is a favourite UPSC error.

Q 8 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Find the part of the sentence that contains an error. If there is no error, choose "No error".
The group of students on a visit to Srinagar circumnavigated around the Dal Lake.
(1) The group of students
(2) on a visit to Srinagar
(3) circumnavigated around the Dal Lake.
(4) No error
= 3
~ Option 3, "circumnavigated around the Dal Lake", contains the error. To circumnavigate means to sail, fly or travel all the way around something, from the Latin circum ("around") and navigare ("to sail"), so "around" is already inside the verb and "circumnavigated around" is a redundancy (pleonasm). The correct sentence is "The group of students on a visit to Srinagar circumnavigated the Dal Lake", or more simply "went around the Dal Lake". Parts 1 and 2 are correct: "The group of students" is a singular collective subject, and "on a visit to Srinagar" describes it. Other redundancies that UPSC tests are "return back", "revert back", "repeat again", "descend down", "cooperate together" and "the reason is because"; in each, the extra word repeats a meaning the main word already carries. Usage note: circumnavigation is most often used of voyages around the world, as in Magellan's expedition, the first to circumnavigate the globe. Tip: when a verb has a Latin prefix such as circum-, re- or de-, check whether the next word merely repeats it.

Q 9 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Find the part of the sentence that contains an error. If there is no error, choose "No error".
Jitender was the smartest of the two children in the room.
(1) Jitender was the smartest
(2) of the two
(3) children in the room.
(4) No error
= 1
~ Option 1, "Jitender was the smartest", contains the error. When two persons or things are compared, English uses the comparative degree, not the superlative, so the sentence should read "Jitender was the smarter of the two children in the room." The superlative "smartest" is used only for three or more, as in "the smartest of all the children in the class". Note that "the" stays before the comparative in the fixed pattern "the + comparative + of the two": the taller of the two brothers, the better of the two offers, the lesser of two evils. Parts 2 and 3 are correct as they stand; "of the two" is in fact the clue that a comparative is needed. Related rules often tested: do not use double comparatives ("more smarter") or double superlatives ("most smartest"), and use "than" after the comparative only when there is no "of the two" structure ("Jitender is smarter than his brother"). Trap: in casual speech many people say "the best of the two", so read every "of the two" in an error-spotting question as a signal to check the degree of the adjective.

Q 10 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Find the part of the sentence that contains an error. If there is no error, choose "No error".
He asked me if I would be interested in signing up as a member of the group.
(1) He asked me if I would be
(2) interested in signing up
(3) as a member of the group.
(4) No error
= 4
~ Option 4, "No error", is correct, because every part of the sentence is grammatical. It reports a yes/no question whose direct form is "Would you be interested in signing up as a member of the group?" Such questions are reported with "if" or "whether", followed by normal statement word order ("if I would be", not "if would I be"), and without a question mark. The pronoun changes from "you" to "I", and "would" is correct after the past reporting verb "asked". In part 2, "interested in" takes the right preposition and is followed by the gerund "signing up", since a verb after a preposition takes the -ing form. In part 3, "sign up as a member of" is an idiomatic way of saying to enrol in or join a group, just as one signs up for a course. Rules to remember for reported questions: no inversion, no auxiliary "do" ("He asked where I lived", not "where did I live"), and a backshift of tense after a past reporting verb. Trap: do not mark a part as wrong just because "if" looks informal; "if" and "whether" are both acceptable here.

Q 11 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Select the most appropriate option for the blank space.
I ______ walking past this particular shop for the past three years.
(1) was
(2) have
(3) have been
(4) had been
= 3
~ Option 3, "have been", is correct. The phrase "for the past three years" describes an action that began in the past and is still continuing at the moment of speaking, which calls for the present perfect continuous (has/have been + -ing): "I have been walking past this particular shop for the past three years." Option 1, "was", gives the past continuous, which describes an action in progress at a past moment and cannot be joined to a period that runs up to the present. Option 2 makes "have walking", which is not an English verb form, since "have" as an auxiliary needs a past participle ("have walked"). Option 4, "had been walking", is the past perfect continuous; it needs an earlier past point of reference, for example "I had been walking past this shop for three years when it closed down." Rule: "for" with a period (for three years, for a long time) and "since" with a point of time (since 2020, since Monday) usually signal a perfect or perfect continuous tense. Trap: "for the past three years" is a present-linked phrase, so a past or past perfect tense will not fit.

Q 12 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Select the most appropriate option for the blank space.
All effects can be connected with immediate causes, but it ______ whether they provide the whole explanation.
(1) is doubted
(2) is a doubt
(3) may be a doubt
(4) may be doubted
= 4
~ Option 4, "may be doubted", is correct. The impersonal passive "it may be doubted whether ..." means "one may doubt whether" or "it is open to question whether", a formal construction common in academic writing: "All effects can be connected with immediate causes, but it may be doubted whether they provide the whole explanation." The modal "may" matches the tentative, balanced tone set by "can" in the first clause: the writer grants one point and then raises a measured doubt. Option 1, "is doubted", is grammatical but states that people actually doubt it, which is a flatter claim and less apt. Options 2 and 3, "is a doubt" and "may be a doubt", are unidiomatic, because with a noun English says "there is a doubt whether" or uses the adjective, "it is doubtful whether". Similar impersonal patterns are "it may be argued that", "it can be said that" and "it is believed that". Tip: "doubt whether" or "doubt if" expresses uncertainty, while "doubt that" often leans towards disbelief. When two options are grammatical, choose the one that matches the tone of the sentence.

Q 13 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Select the most appropriate option for the blank space.
A bird ______ is better than two in the bush.
(1) in hand
(2) to hand
(3) handy
(4) in one's hands
= 1
~ Option 1, "in hand", is correct. The proverb is "A bird in hand is better than two in the bush", more often worded "A bird in the hand is worth two in the bush". It means that a small advantage you already have is better than a bigger one that is only possible, so you should not give up something certain for something uncertain. "In hand" means held, in one's possession, as in "cash in hand" or "the matter in hand". Option 2, "to hand", means within reach or readily available ("Keep a pen to hand"), option 3, "handy", is an adjective meaning convenient or useful, and option 4, "in one's hands", breaks the fixed wording of the proverb, which speaks of one bird held in one hand. Related expressions: "at hand" means near or about to happen, "on hand" means available, and "out of hand" means out of control. A proverb with a similar lesson is "Don't count your chickens before they are hatched". Tip: proverbs and idioms have fixed wording, so the option that sounds closest in meaning is not enough; choose the exact traditional form.

Q 14 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Select the most appropriate option for the blank space.
Human history is ample proof that human ambition ______ no limits.
(1) knows
(2) keeps
(3) crosses
(4) marks
= 1
~ Option 1, "knows", is correct. The idiom "to know no limits", also "to know no bounds", means to be boundless, to have no limit at all: "Human history is ample proof that human ambition knows no limits." Here "know" is used figuratively, as in "her generosity knew no bounds" or "his anger knew no limits". The sentence means that history shows human ambition has always reached for more. Option 2, "keeps no limits", and option 4, "marks no limits", are not idiomatic, although one can "keep within limits" or "mark the limits" of something. Option 3, "crosses no limits", would say the opposite of what is meant, since ambition that crosses no limits stays inside them. Note the singular verb "knows", which agrees with the singular subject "human ambition". Related idioms: "know no fear" (be fearless), "know one's limits" (be aware of what one cannot do) and "the sky is the limit" (there is no upper limit). Tip: in fill-in-the-blank questions on collocations, test each verb with the noun; only one verb forms a recognised fixed phrase with "no limits".

Q 15 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Select the most appropriate option for the blank space.
Good history presents several perspectives on a period but these divergent views must ______ into an integrated whole.
(1) be made
(2) be fused
(3) fit
(4) be balanced
= 2
~ Option 2, "be fused", is correct. The sentence contrasts "several perspectives" and "divergent views" with "an integrated whole", so the verb must mean blending separate things into one: "these divergent views must be fused into an integrated whole." To fuse is to join or blend two or more things so that they become a single unit, as metals fuse when melted together; the noun is "fusion", and a near synonym is "synthesise". The passive "must be fused" is right because the views are acted upon by the historian. Option 1, "be made into", is grammatical but vague and adds nothing about combining. Option 3, "fit into", suggests slotting the views into a whole that already exists rather than forming a new one, and it lacks the passive sense. Option 4, "be balanced", does not collocate with "into"; one balances views against each other, not into a whole. So "be fused into" is the most precise collocation. The idea itself is a good principle for UPSC answer writing too: present several perspectives, then integrate them into a balanced conclusion. Tip: match the verb to its preposition before choosing.

Q 16 | eng | Reading Comprehension
Read the passage given below and answer the question that follows.
The world's life-supporting resources are being depleted at a faster rate than ever before! We have to change our practices and those of the world as a whole towards a more sustainable future. To give us an idea about the dimensions of change, the General Assembly of the United Nations has announced its 'Agenda 2030'. The 17 Sustainable Development Goals describe the framework of our common global efforts. Within each goal, education holds a key position.
Of course education and learning do not function in isolation. In a traditional sense they help to understand the world one lives in and prepare all for a job market with both intrinsic and instrumental values. What are the many roles of education in sustainable development? Its purpose is transformative in nature, in the sense that it should widen one's worldview, make one question 'unsustainability', and help one build competencies to address global challenges and be accommodated into a global system. Beyond this, education for sustainable development means integrating the 'future' as a specific dimension of our learning activities and teaching. How to build a sustainable future must play a central role in educational processes. Globalization, which is sometimes merely seen as global capitalism, has an inevitability about it. Whether we like it or not, we live in a highly interconnected and interdependent world. It is now well recognized that 21st century challenges are global in nature and require cooperation beyond national boundaries. In a sustainable development sense we have to see ourselves as 'citizens' of the world and respond accordingly to the global challenges in the present century. In fact, global challenges have expanded the very notion of citizenship itself. There is an increasing recognition of the importance of global citizenship.
Which one of the following areas holds a common key position in United Nations 'Agenda 2030'?
(1) Environment
(2) Gender
(3) Globalization
(4) Education
= 4
~ Option 4, education, is correct. The first paragraph says that the 17 Sustainable Development Goals of Agenda 2030 describe the framework of our common global efforts and that "within each goal, education holds a key position". So education is the one area that is common to, and central in, all the goals. Option 1, environment, and option 2, gender, are subjects of particular goals, but the passage does not say that either holds a key position in every goal. Option 3, globalization, comes up only in the second paragraph as a feature of our interconnected world, not as an area of the Agenda. For general studies, remember that the UN General Assembly adopted Agenda 2030 in September 2015, with 17 goals and 169 targets to be met by 2030. Education is SDG 4 (Quality Education), gender equality is SDG 5, decent work and economic growth, which covers employment and labour rights, is SDG 8, and climate action is SDG 13. Tip: in reading comprehension, look for the exact words of the question in the passage; "key position" appears only once, attached to education.

(3) Education will increase the global capital

= 1

Q 18 | eng | Reading Comprehension
Read the passage given below and answer the question that follows.
The world's life-supporting resources are being depleted at a faster rate than ever before! We have to change our practices and those of the world as a whole towards a more sustainable future. To give us an idea about the dimensions of change, the General Assembly of the United Nations has announced its 'Agenda 2030'. The 17 Sustainable Development Goals describe the framework of our common global efforts. Within each goal, education holds a key position.
Of course education and learning do not function in isolation. In a traditional sense they help to understand the world one lives in and prepare all for a job market with both intrinsic and instrumental values. What are the many roles of education in sustainable development? Its purpose is transformative in nature, in the sense that it should widen one's worldview, make one question 'unsustainability', and help one build competencies to address global challenges and be accommodated into a global system. Beyond this, education for sustainable development means integrating the 'future' as a specific dimension of our learning activities and teaching. How to build a sustainable future must play a central role in educational processes. Globalization, which is sometimes merely seen as global capitalism, has an inevitability about it. Whether we like it or not, we live in a highly interconnected and interdependent world. It is now well recognized that 21st century challenges are global in nature and require cooperation beyond national boundaries. In a sustainable development sense we have to see ourselves as 'citizens' of the world and respond accordingly to the global challenges in the present century. In fact, global challenges have expanded the very notion of citizenship itself. There is an increasing recognition of the importance of global citizenship.
'We live in a highly interconnected and interdependent world'. What does this phrase mean in the passage?
(1) We are now more connected and dependent upon each other for our survival
(2) The means of transport have become faster
(3) We have become dependent on other countries
(4) We are facing challenges from globalization
= 1
~ Option 1 is correct: we are now more connected and dependent upon each other for our survival. The sentence follows the remark that globalization "has an inevitability about it", and the next sentence says that 21st-century challenges are global in nature and require cooperation beyond national boundaries. So "interconnected and interdependent" means that people everywhere are now linked and rely on one another, and must work together to survive the challenges they share. Option 2, faster transport, may be one cause of interconnection, but the passage does not mention it and it ignores the idea of dependence. Q 36 | qa | Algebra & Numbers
In a desert, X and Y are two spots that are separated by 100 km. At point X, are placed two pillars, one black and one white. Along the line joining X and Y, a white pillar is placed for every 180 m and a black pillar is placed for every 350 m. (2) 15
(3) 16
(4) 17
= 3
~ Option 3, 16, is correct. Step 1: a white and a black pillar stand together wherever the distance from X is a common multiple of 180 m and 350 m. Q 37 | qa | Reasoning
P, Q, R, S, T and U are six friends. Which of the following is/are sufficient to compare heights of S and T?
1. R is shorter than P but taller than other friends.
2. U is taller than Q but shorter than S although Q is not the shortest among the friends.
Select the correct answer using the code given below:
(1) 1 alone is sufficient
(2) 2 alone is sufficient
(3) Both 1 and 2 are not sufficient
(4) 1 and 2 together are sufficient
= 4
~ Option 4, 1 and 2 together are sufficient, is correct. Statement 1 alone: P > R > the other four (Q, S, T, U), but it says nothing about S versus T, so it is not sufficient. Statement 2 alone: S > U > Q, and Q is not the shortest; so S, U and Q are all ruled out as the shortest, and the shortest must be P, R or T. If P or R is the shortest, T could be taller or shorter than S, so statement 2 alone is not sufficient either. Together: statement 1 makes P and R the two tallest, so neither can be the shortest; the shortest must therefore be T, giving P > R > S > U > Q > T. Hence S is taller than T, and the question can be answered only by combining both statements; options 1 and 2 fail as shown, and option 3 ignores the combined deduction. Data-sufficiency tip: test each statement alone first, then combine them, and look for the hidden clue (here, "Q is not the shortest") that closes the gap.

Q 38 | qa | Reasoning
Eight chairs are placed at a uniform distance from each other around a round table. C and D are equal distance away from both A and B; E sits between A and C; G and F sit opposite each other. Where does H sit?
(1) Adjacent to D
(2) Adjacent to A
(3) Adjacent to F
(4) Adjacent to G
= 1
~ Option 1, adjacent to D, is correct. Step 1: number the eight chairs 0 to 7 clockwise and seat A at 0. Step 2: read "E sits between A and C" as E occupying the single chair separating them, so C is two seats from A; take C at 2 and E at 1. Step 3: C must be equally distant from A and B; besides A's own seat, only chair 4, directly opposite A, is two seats from C, so B is at 4. Step 4: D is also equidistant from A and B, so D takes the other midway chair, 6, opposite C. Step 5: chairs 3, 5 and 7 are left; G and F must face each other, and only 3 and 7 are opposite, so H gets chair 5, between B (4) and D (6). Hence H is adjacent to D (and to B), not to A, F or G. The mirror-image seating (C at 6, E at 7) gives the same result. Note: the wording is loose; if "between" allowed more than one chair between A and C, other seatings could fit, so use the intended reading above.

Q 39 | qa | Arithmetic
A, B and C can individually finish a job in 10, 15 and 6 days, respectively. If all of them work together, in how many days will they finish the job?
(1) 2 days
(2) 3 days
(3) 4 days
(4) 5 days
= 2
~ Option 2, 3 days, is correct. Step 1: one day's work of each: A = 1/10, B = 1/15 and C = 1/6 of the job. Step 2: together they do 1/10 + 1/15 + 1/6 = 3/30 + 2/30 + 5/30 = 10/30 = 1/3 of the job per day. Step 3: time = 1 ÷ (1/3) = 3 days. Shortcut (unit method): take the total work as LCM(10, 15, 6) = 30 units; A does 3 units a day, B 2 and C 5, together 10 units, so 30 ÷ 10 = 3 days. Why not the others: in 2 days they finish only 20 of the 30 units, while 4 or 5 days is more time than they need. Sense check: a group must finish sooner than its fastest member alone (C, 6 days). Trap: never add or average the days (10 + 15 + 6 = 31); add the rates, then invert. For two workers, the formula ab ÷ (a + b) gives the combined time directly.

Q 40 | qa | Algebra & Numbers
A dice is thrown two times. The number of ways that the number appearing on the first throw is not less than that on the second throw is:
(1) 15
(2) 20
(3) 21
(4) 36
= 3
~ Option 3, 21, is correct. Step 1: two throws of a die give 6 × 6 = 36 ordered outcomes (first throw, second throw). Step 2: "the first number is not less than the second" means first ≥ second. Step 3: count by the first throw: if it shows 1, only (1, 1) works; if 2, two outcomes; and so on up to 6, which allows six. Total = 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6 = 21. Shortcut: the 6 doubles always qualify, and of the 30 unequal outcomes exactly half (15) have the larger number first, so 6 + 15 = 21. Linked fact: the probability of this event is 21/36 = 7/12. Trap: "not less than" includes equality, so read it as ≥; "greater than" alone would give 15.

Q 41 | acc | Accounting Basics
Overhauling expenses of ₹25,000 for the engine of a motor car to get better fuel efficiency is:
(1) Deferred revenue expenditure
(2) Revenue receipt
(3) Capital expenditure
(4) Revenue expenditure
= 3
~ Option 3, capital expenditure, is correct. Capital expenditure is an outlay that acquires a fixed asset or improves an existing one, increasing its capacity, efficiency or life, so that the benefit lasts beyond the current accounting year. Overhauling the engine of a motor car to get better fuel efficiency is not routine upkeep: it improves the car's performance and cuts its future running costs, so the ₹25,000 is added to the cost of the car and depreciated with it. Revenue expenditure (4) would cover ordinary repairs that merely keep the car in working order, or petrol and insurance, whose benefit is used up within the year. Deferred revenue expenditure (1) is a heavy revenue outlay, such as a large advertising campaign, written off over a few years. A revenue receipt (2) is income such as sales or interest, and an expense is never a receipt. Linked point: costs of putting a newly bought second-hand asset into working condition, such as its first overhaul, freight and installation, are also capitalised. Test: does the spending raise earning capacity or cut operating cost for years ahead? If yes, capitalise it.

Q 42 | acc | Depreciation & Valuation
Which one of the following statements is not correct?
(1) Depreciation is a non-cash expense.
(2) Depreciation is the process of valuation of assets.
(3) The main cause of depreciation is wear and tear caused by usage.
(4) Depreciation must be charged so as to ascertain true profit or loss of a business.
= 2
~ Option 2 is the statement that is not correct. Depreciation is a process of allocation, not of valuation: it spreads the cost of a fixed asset, less its estimated residual value, over the asset's useful life, and AS 10 and Ind AS 16 define it as the systematic allocation of the depreciable amount of an asset over its useful life. The written-down value left in the books is unallocated cost, not the asset's market value, which may be higher or lower. The other statements are standard textbook points. Statement 1: depreciation is a non-cash expense, because no cash leaves the business when it is charged; the cash went out when the asset was bought. Statement 3: wear and tear from use is the main cause, along with passage of time, obsolescence and depletion (in mines and oil wells). Statement 4: depreciation must be charged to match the asset's cost with the revenue it helps earn, so that the true profit or loss is shown and the asset is not overstated. Linked point: the straight-line method charges an equal amount every year, while the written-down value method applies a fixed rate to the reducing balance.

Q 43 | acc | Accounting Basics
A credit purchase of machinery recorded in Purchase Book instead of Journal Proper is an example of:
(1) Compensating errors
(2) Errors of commission
(3) Errors of principle
(4) Errors of omission
= 3
~ Option 3, an error of principle, is correct. An error of principle arises when a transaction is recorded in violation of an accounting principle, typically by treating a capital item as revenue or a revenue item as capital. The Purchase Book is meant only for credit purchases of the goods in which the firm trades; a credit purchase of machinery, a fixed asset, belongs in the Journal Proper (Machinery A/c Dr. To Supplier's A/c). Entering it in the Purchase Book posts it to the Purchases Account, so a capital asset is treated as a revenue item. It is not an error of omission (4), because the transaction has been recorded; not an error of commission (2), which is a wrong amount, wrong posting or wrong totalling within the correct class of account; and not a compensating error (1), where one error cancels another. Linked points: an error of principle does not disturb the agreement of the trial balance, so it is found only by scrutiny; here gross profit and fixed assets are both understated until the rectification entry Machinery A/c Dr. To Purchases A/c is passed.

Q 44 | acc | Accounting Basics
The practice of appending notes regarding contingent liability in accounting statements is pursuant to:
(1) Convention of consistency
(2) Money measurement concept
(3) Convention of conservatism
(4) Convention of full disclosure
= 4
~ Option 4, the convention of full disclosure, is correct. This convention requires financial statements to disclose all information material to their users, either in the statements themselves or in notes attached to them. A contingent liability, such as a claim against the firm not acknowledged as a debt, a guarantee given for another's loan or a bill discounted but not yet matured, is not entered in the accounts because it may never become payable, yet users must know of it; so it is shown as a note to the accounts, often as a footnote to the balance sheet. Linked points: AS 29 (Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets) requires contingent liabilities to be disclosed, not recognised, unless the chance of an outflow is remote, and Schedule III to the Companies Act, 2013 requires them in the notes. The four conventions usually listed are consistency, full disclosure, conservatism and materiality.

Q 45 | acc | Accounting Basics
'Outstanding rent' may be classified as:
(1) Natural personal account
(2) Representative personal account
(3) Real account
(4) Nominal account
= 2
~ Option 2, representative personal account, is correct. Accounts are classified as personal, real and nominal, and personal accounts are of three kinds: natural (individuals, such as Ram's account), artificial (bodies such as a company, bank or club) and representative. A representative personal account stands for a person or group of persons who cannot be named directly. Q 48 | sci | Computer Applications
In the context of a buffer in memory area or disk, spool refers to:
(1) Simple peripheral operation on-line
(2) Simple peripheral operation off-line
(3) Simultaneous peripheral operation on-line
(4) Simultaneous peripheral operation off-line
= 3
~ Option 3, Simultaneous Peripheral Operation On-line, is correct. SPOOL is the acronym for Simultaneous Peripheral Operations On-Line. In spooling, output meant for a slow device such as a printer is first written to a buffer area on disk (or in memory), and the device takes the jobs from this queue at its own speed. Meanwhile the CPU and other programs carry on, so the input/output of one job overlaps with the computation of others, and several jobs can wait in the queue; the print spooler is the commonest example. Linked points: buffering holds data temporarily in memory between a fast and a slow device, usually for one job, whereas spooling uses the disk as a large buffer that can queue many jobs; in early computers, off-line operation meant copying data to tape on a separate machine, a method that spooling replaced.

Q 49 | sci | Computer Applications
Which one of the following is not a commutative law?
(1) A * b = b * A
(2) A + b = b + A
(3) A − b = b − A
(4) A * B = B * A
= 3
~ Option 3, A − b = b − A, is correct, because it is not a commutative law. A binary operation is commutative when changing the order of the operands does not change the result. Addition and multiplication are commutative: A + b = b + A (option 2) and A * b = b * A (options 1 and 4, which differ only in letter case; * is the multiplication sign used in computing). Subtraction is not commutative, because A − b is the negative of b − A: for example, 5 − 3 = 2 but 3 − 5 = −2, so the equation holds only when A = b. Division is not commutative either (6 ÷ 3 = 2 but 3 ÷ 6 = 0.5). Linked points from Boolean algebra, often asked in computer papers: the commutative laws are A + B = B + A (OR) and A · B = B · A (AND); the associative laws are (A + B) + C = A + (B + C) and (A · B) · C = A · (B · C); and the distributive law is A · (B + C) = A · B + A · C. Remember: commutative means the order can be swapped; associative means the grouping can be changed.

Q 50 | sci | Computer Applications
Which OSI layer is responsible for managing the communication between computers in the network?
(1) Network layer
(2) Transport layer
(3) Session layer
(4) Data link layer
= 3
~ Option 3, the session layer, is correct. The session layer is layer 5 of the seven-layer OSI model. It establishes, manages and terminates sessions, that is, the dialogues between applications running on different computers: it controls which side may transmit and when (dialogue control, in half-duplex or full-duplex mode) and inserts checkpoints so that a long transfer can resume from the last checkpoint after a failure (synchronisation). The other layers do different jobs: the network layer (layer 3) handles logical (IP) addressing and the routing of packets; the transport layer (layer 4) gives reliable end-to-end delivery with segmentation, flow control and error recovery; and the data link layer (layer 2) handles framing, physical (MAC) addressing and error detection on a single link. Q 54 | sci | Chemistry
Which one of the following elements has the highest boiling point?
(1) Lithium
(2) Sodium
(3) Potassium
(4) Rubidium
= 1
~ Option 1, lithium, is correct. In Group 1, the alkali metals, the melting and boiling points fall as we go down the group: lithium boils at about 1342 °C, sodium at 883 °C, potassium at 759 °C and rubidium at 688 °C. Down the group the atoms become larger, the single valence electron lies farther from the nucleus, and the metallic bonding between the atoms becomes weaker, so less energy is needed to separate them. Lithium, the smallest atom of the four, has the strongest metallic bonding and therefore the highest boiling point, so sodium, potassium and rubidium are wrong. The same order holds for melting points: lithium melts at about 181 °C, while caesium melts at about 28 °C and can turn liquid on a hot day. Linked facts: lithium is the lightest metal and shows a diagonal relationship with magnesium, and reactivity rises down the group, the reverse of the boiling-point trend. Remember: in the alkali metals, as size goes up, bonding strength and boiling point come down.

Q 73 | qa | Algebra & Numbers
What is the digit in the unit place of 2^(2^100)?
(1) 2
(2) 4
(3) 6
(4) 8
= 3
~ Option 3, 6, is correct. Step 1: the unit digits of powers of 2 repeat in a cycle of four: 2¹ = 2, 2² = 4, 2³ = 8, 2⁴ = 16 (unit digit 6), and then 2⁵ = 32 starts the cycle again with 2. Step 2: so the unit digit of 2ⁿ depends only on the remainder when n is divided by 4: remainder 1 gives 2, remainder 2 gives 4, remainder 3 gives 8 and remainder 0 gives 6. Step 3: here n = 2^100 = 4 × 2^98, which is divisible by 4, so the remainder is 0. Hence 2^(2^100) ends in 6, just as 2⁴ = 16, 2⁸ = 256 and 2¹⁶ = 65536 do. Q 75 | qa | Algebra & Numbers

(2) 9
(3) 10
(4) 11
= 3
~ Option 3, 10 ways, is correct. Q 81 | sci | Computer Applications

(3) IPsec
(4) MIME
= 4
~ Option 4, MIME, is correct. TLS (Transport Layer Security), its predecessor SSL (Secure Sockets Layer) and IPsec (Internet Protocol Security) are security protocols: they authenticate the communicating parties, protect data integrity and encrypt the data. (3) 0.8124
(4) 0.8125
= 4
~ Option 4, 0.8125, is correct. Step 1: the digits after the binary point have place values 2⁻¹, 2⁻², 2⁻³ and 2⁻⁴, that is 0.5, 0.25, 0.125 and 0.0625. Step 2: (.1101)₂ = 1 × 0.5 + 1 × 0.25 + 0 × 0.125 + 1 × 0.0625 = 0.8125. Shortcut: four binary places mean sixteenths, and 1101 in binary is 13, so the value is 13/16 = 0.8125. Check in reverse by repeated multiplication by 2: 0.8125 × 2 = 1.625 (1), 0.625 × 2 = 1.25 (1), 0.25 × 2 = 0.5 (0), 0.5 × 2 = 1.0 (1), which gives .1101 again. Trap: a four-place binary fraction is always an exact multiple of 1/16 = 0.0625, so 0.8122, 0.8123 and 0.8124 are impossible values. Remember: convert the whole-number part by repeated division by 2 and the fractional part by repeated multiplication by 2.

Q 85 | sci | Computer Applications
Which one among the following is used to combine several object modules and libraries to a single executable program?
(1) Interpreter
(2) Compiler
(3) Linker
(4) Loader
= 3
~ Option 3, linker, is correct. A linker (link editor) takes the object modules produced by the compiler or assembler, together with the library routines they call, resolves the references between them and combines them into a single executable program. A compiler (option 2) translates the whole high-level source program into object code at once. An interpreter (option 1) translates and executes the program statement by statement and produces no executable file. A loader (option 4), part of the operating system, places the executable program in main memory, adjusts its addresses if needed (relocation) and starts its execution. Remember the order: source code → compiler or assembler → object code → linker → executable file → loader → running program. Linked fact: in static linking the library code is copied into the executable file, while in dynamic linking (DLLs or shared libraries) it is joined only at load time or run time, which keeps executables smaller.

Q 86 | sci | Physics
An incandescent bulb rated as 100 W at 110 V is connected to a 220 V power supply. The power that dissipates in the bulb would be:
(1) 50 W
(2) 100 W
(3) 200 W
(4) 400 W
= 4
~ Option 4, 400 W, is correct. Step 1: find the bulb's resistance from its rating: R = V²/P = 110²/100 = 12,100/100 = 121 Ω. Step 2: taking the resistance as constant, the power at 220 V is P = V²/R = 220²/121 = 48,400/121 = 400 W. Shortcut: for a fixed resistance P ∝ V², so doubling the voltage multiplies the power by 2² = 4, giving 100 × 4 = 400 W. The current also doubles, from 100/110 ≈ 0.91 A to about 1.82 A. Trap: 200 W (option 3) wrongly assumes that power is directly proportional to voltage, 100 W (option 2) assumes the rated power stays the same, and 50 W (option 1) inverts the ratio. Q 87 | sci | Physics
If the length of a current carrying wire is halved, for a given potential difference, the current in the wire would:
(1) be doubled
(2) be halved
(3) remain unchanged
(4) become zero
= 1
~ Option 1 is correct: the current is doubled. Step 1: the resistance of a wire is R = ρL/A, where ρ is the resistivity of the material, L the length and A the area of cross-section. For the same material and thickness, R is directly proportional to L, so halving the length halves the resistance. Step 2: by Ohm's law I = V/R; with the potential difference V held constant, the new current = V/(R/2) = 2V/R, that is, double the original. The current would be halved (option 2) only if the length were doubled, would remain unchanged (option 3) only if the resistance did not change, and would become zero (option 4) only if the circuit were broken. Linked facts: resistance is inversely proportional to the area of cross-section, so a thicker wire has less resistance; resistivity depends on the material and temperature, not on length or thickness. Trap: if a wire is stretched to twice its length, its area halves and its resistance becomes four times.

Q 101 | qa | Arithmetic
A train of length 400 m takes 15 seconds to cross a train of length 300 m travelling at 60 km per hour from the opposite direction along a parallel track. What is the speed of the longer train, in km per hour?
(1) 108
(2) 102
(3) 98
(4) 96
= 1
~ Option 1, 108 km/h. Step 1: the trains move in opposite directions, so their relative speed is the sum of their speeds. Step 2: to cross each other completely they must cover the sum of their lengths, 400 + 300 = 700 m, in 15 s. Step 3: relative speed = 700/15 m/s = 700/15 × 18/5 = 168 km/h. Q 105 | qa | Reasoning
A man walks in a certain direction for 5 km and then walks in the south direction for 4 km. If he ends up in the east direction with respect to the starting position, how far is he from the starting position?
(1) 2 km
(2) 3 km
(3) 4 km
(4) 5 km
= 2
~ Option 2, 3 km. Step 1: take the start as the origin and let the first 5 km walk have an east component x and a north component y, so x² + y² = 5² = 25. Step 2: after walking 4 km south he is due east of the start, so his net north-south displacement is zero: y − 4 = 0, giving y = 4. Step 3: x = √(25 − 16) = √9 = 3. So he ends 3 km east of the starting point. The first walk was towards the north-east, and that 5 km walk is the hypotenuse of a right triangle whose other sides are the 4 km southward leg and the 3 km eastward distance, the familiar 3-4-5 Pythagorean triple. Traps: options 3 and 4 merely repeat the given lengths, and 2 km fits no right triangle with a 5 km hypotenuse and a 4 km side (that would need 3 km). Tip: in direction-sense questions, draw a rough diagram; 'ends up due east' means the north and south movements cancel out exactly.

~ Option 3: statement I is correct but statement II is incorrect. Section 45ZA of the RBI Act, 1934, inserted by the Finance Act, 2016, says the Central Government shall, in consultation with the Reserve Bank, determine the inflation target in terms of the Consumer Price Index once in every five years. Q 113 | eco | External Sector

A very strong US Dollar squeezes global credit.
Statement II:
Many countries and companies outside America borrow in Dollars.
Which of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

(2) Both statement I and statement II are correct and statement II is not the correct explanation for statement I
(3) Statement I is correct but statement II is incorrect
(4) Statement I is incorrect but statement II is correct
= 1
~ Option 1: both statements are correct and statement II explains statement I. Many governments and companies outside the United States borrow in dollars, through loans and bonds, because the dollar is the main currency of world trade, finance and reserves, so statement II is correct.
