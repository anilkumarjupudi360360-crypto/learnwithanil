Q 1 | eng | Reading Comprehension
Read the passage given below and answer the question that follows.
Africa is on the move. At least 20 million (i) ______ live outside the continent, a three-fold increase since 1990. Europe accounts for about half of African (ii) ______ outside Africa, but its share has steadily declined since 1990. America, China, Saudi Arabia and Turkey have all seen surges in their African-born populations. It may seem otherworldly to predict that this will continue, given the fierce backlash against (iii) ______ across the West. Notwithstanding pushback, African (iv) ______ is an unstoppable force that will long outlast today's populists and help define the 21st century. Demography is one cause. It is well-known that in the developed world the number of people of working age will decline, exacerbating labour shortages. What is less widely appreciated is that emerging countries that are associated with exporting people, such as Mexico and the Philippines, are also getting older and richer — meaning smaller shares of their populations will, in future, wish to emigrate in search of work. In Africa, by contrast, the working-age population is forecast to increase by around 700 million people by 2050. Another cause is economics. Many African countries are entering a phase in their development in which people are still poor enough to wish to leave but, for the first time, rich enough to be able to travel long distances. African countries create only around a fifth of the number of formal jobs required to absorb their expanding workforce.
In the above passage there are four blanks numbered (i), (ii), (iii) and (iv). Select the option from those given below in which the most appropriate term has been used for each of the blanks respectively:
(1) (i) immigrants; (ii) migration; (iii) emigrants; (iv) immigration
(2) (i) emigrants; (ii) migrants; (iii) immigration; (iv) migration
(3) (i) immigrants; (ii) emigrants; (iii) immigration; (iv) migrants
(4) (i) emigrants; (ii) migrants; (iii) migration; (iv) emigration
= 2
~ Option 2 fits all four blanks. Seen from Africa, people who have left the continent are emigrants, so (i) is "emigrants". (ii) "African migrants outside Africa" uses the neutral word for people living abroad. (iii) The West objects to people coming in, so its backlash is against "immigration". (iv) The overall movement that will "help define the 21st century" is African "migration", and the singular verb "is" needs this singular noun. Options 1 and 3 wrongly call people who left Africa "immigrants"; option 1 also has the West reacting against "emigrants", and option 3 puts the plural "migrants" before "is". Option 4's backlash against "migration" misses that the West objects to arrivals, not departures. Remember: an Emigrant Exits the home country, an Immigrant comes In to the host country, and "migrant" is neutral. The same frame appears in Indian law: the Emigration Act, 1983 regulates Indian workers going abroad for jobs.

Q 2 | eng | Reading Comprehension
Read the passage given below and answer the question that follows.
Africa is on the move. At least 20 million (i) ______ live outside the continent, a three-fold increase since 1990. Europe accounts for about half of African (ii) ______ outside Africa, but its share has steadily declined since 1990. America, China, Saudi Arabia and Turkey have all seen surges in their African-born populations. It may seem otherworldly to predict that this will continue, given the fierce backlash against (iii) ______ across the West. Notwithstanding pushback, African (iv) ______ is an unstoppable force that will long outlast today's populists and help define the 21st century. Demography is one cause. It is well-known that in the developed world the number of people of working age will decline, exacerbating labour shortages. What is less widely appreciated is that emerging countries that are associated with exporting people, such as Mexico and the Philippines, are also getting older and richer — meaning smaller shares of their populations will, in future, wish to emigrate in search of work. In Africa, by contrast, the working-age population is forecast to increase by around 700 million people by 2050. Another cause is economics. Many African countries are entering a phase in their development in which people are still poor enough to wish to leave but, for the first time, rich enough to be able to travel long distances. African countries create only around a fifth of the number of formal jobs required to absorb their expanding workforce.
Which of the following sentences with respect to the passage is/are correct?
1. All rich western nations have been seeing an increase in emigration from Africa over the past few decades.
2. Over the past thirty-five odd years, the number of African origin people living outside Africa has gone up by a factor of three.
3. The fierce resistance to labour incursion into the West will temper emigration from Africa in the 21st century.
Select the answer using the codes given below:
(1) 1 and 2
(2) 1 and 3
(3) 2 and 3
(4) 2 only
= 4
~ Option 4: only statement 2 is supported. The passage says at least 20 million Africans live outside the continent, "a three-fold increase since 1990"; 1990 to 2025 is about 35 years, so their number has gone up by a factor of three. Statement 1 overgeneralises: the passage names America, China, Saudi Arabia and Turkey, not all of them Western, and says Europe's share of African migrants has steadily declined; it never claims that all rich Western nations saw increases. Statement 3 contradicts the passage, which calls African migration "an unstoppable force" that will long outlast today's populists, notwithstanding the pushback. Exam tip: in UPSC comprehension, sweeping words such as "all", "always" and "only" usually make a statement wrong unless the passage says exactly that, and a statement that reverses the author's conclusion ("will temper" against "unstoppable") is a classic trap. Read the figures carefully too: "three-fold" means multiplied by three, not increased by three million.

It may seem otherworldly to predict that this will continue, given the fierce backlash against (iii) ______ across the West. Notwithstanding pushback, African (iv) ______ is an unstoppable force that will long outlast today's populists and help define the 21st century. Demography is one cause. It is well-known that in the developed world the number of people of working age will decline, exacerbating labour shortages. What is less widely appreciated is that emerging countries that are associated with exporting people, such as Mexico and the Philippines, are also getting older and richer — meaning smaller shares of their populations will, in future, wish to emigrate in search of work. In Africa, by contrast, the working-age population is forecast to increase by around 700 million people by 2050. Another cause is economics. With reference to the passage, which of the following are factors that can be seen to be determining the movement of Africans to other countries to seek work?
1. Younger populations in developing countries like the Philippines and Mexico
2. Only about 20 per cent of the required jobs being available within African nations
3. Fierce backlash from Western countries given the increasing immigration load
Select the answer using the codes given below:
(1) 1 and 2
(2) 2 and 3
(3) 1 and 3
(4) 2 only
= 4
~ Option 4: only statement 2 is a factor named in the passage. The passage gives two causes, demography and economics. Statement 2 restates the economic cause: African countries create "only around a fifth" (about 20 per cent) of the formal jobs needed for their expanding workforce, so people look abroad for work. Statement 1 is wrong because Mexico and the Philippines are said to be getting older and richer, so fewer of their people will wish to emigrate; the fast-growing young population, about 700 million more working-age people by 2050, is Africa's. Statement 3 is wrong because the Western backlash is presented as an obstacle that migration will overcome ("Notwithstanding pushback"), not as something that drives Africans abroad. Linked idea for EPFO aspirants: a formal job is one with a regular contract and social-security cover, the kind of employment that brings a worker into schemes such as the EPF in India; a shortage of such jobs pushes workers into the informal sector or into migration.

Read the passage given below and answer the question that follows.

America, China, Saudi Arabia and Turkey have all seen surges in their African-born populations. It may seem otherworldly to predict that this will continue, given the fierce backlash against (iii) ______ across the West. Notwithstanding pushback, African (iv) ______ is an unstoppable force that will long outlast today's populists and help define the 21st century. Demography is one cause. It is well-known that in the developed world the number of people of working age will decline, exacerbating labour shortages. What is less widely appreciated is that emerging countries that are associated with exporting people, such as Mexico and the Philippines, are also getting older and richer — meaning smaller shares of their populations will, in future, wish to emigrate in search of work. In Africa, by contrast, the working-age population is forecast to increase by around 700 million people by 2050. Another cause is economics. Many African countries are entering a phase in their development in which people are still poor enough to wish to leave but, for the first time, rich enough to be able to travel long distances. Which one of the following options most appropriately describes the phrase "exacerbating labour shortages"?
(1) Increasing labour shortages
(2) Worsening the problem of labour shortages
(3) Mitigating the issue of labour shortages
(4) Intractably unburdening labour shortages
= 2
~ Option 2. To exacerbate is to make a problem, a bad situation or a feeling worse (Latin exacerbare, to irritate). A shrinking working-age population in the developed world worsens an existing problem of labour shortages, so "worsening the problem of labour shortages" is the most appropriate paraphrase. Option 1, "increasing labour shortages", comes close but loses the idea of aggravating a problem that already exists. Option 3, "mitigating", means the opposite, making something less severe, and option 4, "intractably unburdening", makes no sense here ("intractable" means hard to control or solve). Remember the word family: synonyms of exacerbate are aggravate, worsen and compound; antonyms are mitigate, alleviate, ameliorate and ease. Usage: "Scratching will only exacerbate the rash"; "The new policy exacerbated tensions between the two groups." Trap: "exasperate" (to irritate a person) looks similar but is used for people, not problems.

Q 5 | eng | Reading Comprehension
Read the passage given below and answer the question that follows.
Africa is on the move. At least 20 million (i) ______ live outside the continent, a three-fold increase since 1990. Europe accounts for about half of African (ii) ______ outside Africa, but its share has steadily declined since 1990. America, China, Saudi Arabia and Turkey have all seen surges in their African-born populations. It may seem otherworldly to predict that this will continue, given the fierce backlash against (iii) ______ across the West. Notwithstanding pushback, African (iv) ______ is an unstoppable force that will long outlast today's populists and help define the 21st century. Demography is one cause. It is well-known that in the developed world the number of people of working age will decline, exacerbating labour shortages. What is less widely appreciated is that emerging countries that are associated with exporting people, such as Mexico and the Philippines, are also getting older and richer — meaning smaller shares of their populations will, in future, wish to emigrate in search of work. In Africa, by contrast, the working-age population is forecast to increase by around 700 million people by 2050. Another cause is economics. Many African countries are entering a phase in their development in which people are still poor enough to wish to leave but, for the first time, rich enough to be able to travel long distances. African countries create only around a fifth of the number of formal jobs required to absorb their expanding workforce.
Which one of the following is the figure of speech in the expression "Africa is on the move"?
(1) Personification
(2) Irony
(3) Anthropomorphism
(4) Antithesis
= 1
~ Option 1, personification, which gives human actions or qualities to something that is not human. Here a whole continent is said to be "on the move", as if it were a person setting out on a journey, when what is meant is that Africans are migrating. Anthropomorphism is the broader idea of attributing human form or behaviour to animals, gods or objects, usually literally, as with talking animals in fables and cartoons; it is not the usual name of this figure of speech. There is no irony (saying the opposite of what is meant, or an outcome opposite to what is expected) and no antithesis (contrasting ideas in balanced phrases, as in "Man proposes, God disposes"). Strictly, using "Africa" for "Africans" is also metonymy (naming a thing by something closely associated with it, as "the Crown" for the monarch), but that is not offered, so personification is the best choice. Other examples of personification: "Time flies", "The wind howled".

Q 6 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Select the most appropriate option for the blank space.
The evening looked ______ against the stretched out sky.
(1) red, beautiful and soul-quenching
(2) beautiful, red and soul-quenching
(3) soul-quenching, beautiful and red
(4) beautiful, soul-quenching and red
= 4
~ Option 4. Adjectives follow a broad order: opinion, size, age, shape, colour, origin, material, purpose. Opinion adjectives ("beautiful", "soul-quenching") therefore come before a fact adjective such as colour ("red"), which goes last; this rules out options 1 and 2. Between two opinion adjectives, the general one that can describe almost anything ("beautiful") comes before the more specific one ("soul-quenching", which describes the effect on the viewer), as in "a lovely, comfortable chair". Option 3 reverses the two opinion adjectives. Hence: "The evening looked beautiful, soul-quenching and red against the stretched out sky." Remember the mnemonic OSASCOMP (Opinion, Size, Age, Shape, Colour, Origin, Material, Purpose), as in "a lovely small old round red Italian wooden dining table". Note also that when a list of adjectives follows a linking verb such as "looked", "seemed" or "was", the last two are joined by "and", as here.

Q 7 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Select the most appropriate option for the blank space.
Summers ______ be really hot at times, so make sure you stay hydrated.
(1) can
(2) could
(3) might
(4) may
= 1
~ Option 1, "can". "Can" expresses general or theoretical possibility, that is, what is sometimes true of something: "Summers can be really hot at times, so make sure you stay hydrated." The phrase "at times" signals this typical, recurring possibility, and the advice that follows rests on it. "May" and "might" express possibility about a particular situation ("It may rain tomorrow") and sound odd in a general statement about summers. "Could" suggests past ability or a more remote, conditional possibility ("It could get hotter if the monsoon is late"). Compare "Snakes can be dangerous" (a general truth) with "That snake may be dangerous" (a guess about one case). Trap: all four modals express possibility, so look for the clue that tells you whether the sentence states a general truth or guesses about one occasion; general truths take "can".

Q 8 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Select the most appropriate option for the blank space.
I was wondering which festival ______ we celebrate together this year?
(1) will
(2) might
(3) shall
(4) do
= 3
~ Option 3, "shall". "Shall" is used with "I" and "we" in questions that ask for a suggestion, an offer or a decision: "Which festival shall we celebrate together this year?", like "Shall we go?" or "What shall I wear?" The speaker is wondering what the two of them should choose, not predicting a fact, so "shall" fits best. "Will we celebrate" asks about a future fact, "might" weakens it into a vague possibility, and "do we celebrate" is the simple present used for habits and routines, which does not fit a choice to be made "this year". Remember: in modern English "will" has largely replaced "shall" for plain future time, but "Shall I/we ...?" survives for offers and suggestions. In legal drafting, "shall" expresses an obligation ("The employer shall pay ..."), which is how statutes such as the Employees' Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952 impose duties.

Q 9 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Select the most appropriate option for the blank space.
I was ______ exhausted by the end of the week spent in the library doing research work.
(1) exceedingly
(2) inordinately
(3) very
(4) completely
= 4
~ Option 4, "completely". "Exhausted" is an extreme (non-gradable) adjective: it already means "extremely tired". Such adjectives are intensified with absolute adverbs such as "completely", "totally", "utterly" or "absolutely", not with grading adverbs such as "very" or "exceedingly", which go with gradable adjectives like "tired". Hence: "I was completely exhausted by the end of the week spent in the library doing research work." "Inordinately" means excessively, beyond reasonable limits ("inordinately long delays"), which does not suit tiredness either. Other pairs to remember: very cold but absolutely freezing, very big but absolutely enormous, very good but absolutely wonderful, very surprised but utterly astonished. Trap: "very exhausted" is common in casual speech, but standard written English, which these papers test, follows the gradable and non-gradable rule.

Q 10 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Select the most appropriate option for the blank space.
They ______ be very far away. I saw them a little while ago.
(1) shall not
(2) cannot
(3) should not
(4) ought not
= 2
~ Option 2, "cannot". "Cannot" (can't) expresses a logical deduction that something is impossible: since the speaker saw them a little while ago, they cannot be very far away. The positive deduction would use "must" ("They must be nearby"). "Should not" and "ought not" express advice, obligation or a weaker expectation, and "ought not" would also need "to" ("ought not to be"). "Shall not" expresses a future intention, a promise or a prohibition. Only "cannot" conveys the certainty drawn from the evidence in the second sentence. Remember the deduction scale: must (almost certainly true), may, might or could (possibly true), cannot (almost certainly not true). "Mustn't" is not the negative of deductive "must"; it means prohibition ("You mustn't smoke here"). For past deductions use "must have" and "can't have": "He can't have left; his car is still here."

Q 11 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Choose the word or phrase closest in meaning to the underlined word "cordial".
The fact that the deliberations were carried out in a "cordial" manner pleased the old guard very much.
(1) joust
(2) convivial
(3) jocular
(4) zestful
= 2
~ Option 2, "convivial". "Cordial" (from Latin cor, heart) means warm, friendly and sincere, as in cordial relations between two countries. "Convivial" means friendly, pleasant and sociable, describing a person, a gathering or an atmosphere, so it is the closest in meaning: the deliberations were held in a friendly, good-natured manner, which pleased the old guard. "Jocular" means joking or humorous, "zestful" means full of energy and enthusiasm, and "joust" is a combat between knights on horseback or, figuratively, any contest or argument, almost the opposite of a cordial discussion; it is also a noun or verb, not an adjective. Related words: amicable, genial, affable and congenial are synonyms of cordial; hostile, frosty and acrimonious are antonyms. "The old guard" means the long-standing, usually conservative, members of a group or organisation.

Q 12 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Choose the word or phrase closest in meaning to the underlined word "impugned".
His views on the matter have been "impugned" by those evaluating his role in the community as its erstwhile ruler.
(1) challenged
(2) belittled
(3) enhanced
(4) endured
= 1
~ Option 1, "challenged". To impugn is to dispute the truth, validity or honesty of something, to call it into question (Latin impugnare, to attack). So his views were challenged by those assessing his role as the community's former ("erstwhile") ruler. "Belittled" means made to seem unimportant; it is disparaging but is not the same as questioning validity. "Enhanced" (improved) is the opposite in spirit, and "endured" (put up with, or lasted) is unrelated. The word is common in Indian legal and administrative writing: courts speak of the "impugned order" or "impugned provision", meaning the order or law whose validity is being challenged in the case, and one may impugn a witness's credibility or someone's motives. Do not confuse it with "impute" (to attribute a fault or quality to someone) or "impunity" (freedom from punishment).

Q 13 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Choose the word or phrase closest in meaning to the underlined word "egregious".
His ad hominem comments on his colleague were considered "egregious" by those present.
(1) extensively elaborate
(2) flagrant and shocking
(3) out of context
(4) impromptu and unintended
= 2
~ Option 2, "flagrant and shocking". "Egregious" means outstandingly bad, flagrant or shocking (Latin egregius, standing out from the flock; in older English it meant remarkably good). Ad hominem comments attack a person rather than his arguments, and those present found such personal attacks on a colleague flagrantly offensive. "Extensively elaborate", "out of context" and "impromptu and unintended" describe the length, setting or spontaneity of the comments, not their shocking badness. Typical collocations: an egregious error, an egregious violation of the rules, egregious misconduct. Synonyms: flagrant, glaring, gross, blatant, outrageous. "Ad hominem" (Latin, "to the person") names the fallacy of attacking the speaker instead of the argument, the opposite of debating on the merits. Trap: "egregious" has nothing to do with "gregarious" (sociable), though both come from the Latin grex, flock.

Q 14 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Choose the word or phrase closest in meaning to the underlined phrase "an apostate".
Once he began to savour the taste of true wealth, he abandoned all his erstwhile ideas regarding social welfare and was subsequently labelled "an apostate".
(1) an arrogant and haughty person
(2) a self-important person
(3) a renouncer of held beliefs
(4) an affluent person
= 3
~ Option 3, "a renouncer of held beliefs". An apostate is a person who renounces or abandons a religious, political or other belief or principle that he once held (from Greek, literally "standing away", that is, defection). The man gave up his earlier ideas on social welfare once he tasted true wealth, so he was labelled an apostate. Options 1 and 2 describe arrogance (haughty, self-important), and option 4 (affluent) describes his new wealth; becoming rich is the cause of his change, not the meaning of the word. The noun for the act is apostasy and the verb is apostatise. Related words: renegade, defector, turncoat, recreant; the opposite is a loyalist, adherent or devotee. Trap: do not confuse "apostate" with "apostle", a committed preacher or champion of a cause, who is almost the opposite.

Q 15 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Choose the word or phrase closest in meaning to the underlined word "corporeity".
The debate around the "corporeity" of the soul presents a compelling paradox.
(1) the quality of physical existence
(2) collective consciousness
(3) embodied in divinity
(4) corpuscular existence
= 1
~ Option 1, "the quality of physical existence". "Corporeity" (Latin corpus, body) is the quality or state of having a material body, that is, bodily or physical existence, materiality. The paradox is whether the soul, usually thought of as immaterial, can have a physical existence. "Corpuscular" refers to corpuscles or minute particles (as in Newton's corpuscular theory of light), so "corpuscular existence" is a distractor built on a similar-looking root. "Collective consciousness" (the shared beliefs and values of a society) and "embodied in divinity" have nothing to do with bodily existence. Related words: corporeal (physical, bodily) and its opposite incorporeal (without a body, spiritual); corporeality is a commoner synonym of corporeity. The same root gives corpse, corps and corporal punishment, that is, punishment inflicted on the body.

Q 16 | eng | Grammar & Usage
In the following sentences prepositions or phrasal verbs have been used, which have been underlined (shown here in double quotes). Select the sentence(s) wherein the use of the preposition or phrasal verb is/are correct.
1. The delivery reached its destination just "about" time.
2. The evidence presented by Shyam states something different "than" what is being presented by Ram.
3. That is the difference "of" somebody doing his duty and not doing his duty.
Select the answer using the codes given below:
(1) 1 and 2
(2) 1 and 3
(3) 1 only
(4) None of the above
= 4
~ Option 4: none of the three sentences uses its preposition correctly. In sentence 1 the idiom for arriving with barely any time to spare is "just in time"; "about time" means "high time", as in "It's about time you came", and "on time" means punctually. In sentence 2, in the British usage followed in Indian examinations, "different" takes "from": "something different from what is being presented by Ram" ("different than" is mainly American, and "different to" is informal British). In sentence 3 a difference between two things takes "between ... and": "the difference between somebody doing his duty and not doing his duty"; "difference of" is used for a quality, as in "a difference of opinion". Since sentence 1 is wrong, options 1, 2 and 3, each of which marks it correct, fall away. Remember: in time (early enough), on time (punctual), about time (overdue).

Q 17 | eng | Grammar & Usage
In the following sentences prepositions or phrasal verbs have been used, which have been underlined (shown here in double quotes). Select the sentence(s) wherein the use of the preposition or phrasal verb is/are correct.
1. "Amidst" the scuffle and the resultant confusion, the thief managed to slip away.
2. There was not a single person "amongst" those assembled who was willing to become the leader.
3. "Among" the members of the community the local doctor was the one who was most respected.
Select the answer using the codes given below:
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ Option 4: all three sentences are correct. "Amid" or "amidst" means in the middle of or during, and is used with a situation, an event or a mass of things: "Amidst the scuffle and the resultant confusion, the thief managed to slip away." "Among" or "amongst" means in the company of, or included in, a group of three or more and takes a plural noun or pronoun: "amongst those assembled", "Among the members of the community". "Amidst" and "amongst" are standard, slightly more formal or British forms of "amid" and "among", not errors, so none of the sentences is faulty. Related rule: "between" is used for two people or things (or for clearly separate individual relationships), "among" for three or more seen as a group: "divide the sweets between the two children" but "among the whole class". Trap: examiners often show "amongst" or "amidst" to tempt candidates into marking a correct sentence as wrong.

Q 18 | eng | Grammar & Usage
In the following sentences prepositions or phrasal verbs have been used, which have been underlined (shown here in double quotes). Select the sentence(s) wherein the use of the preposition or phrasal verb is/are correct.
1. Raj's apartment was three floors "underneath" the Subramanian residence.
2. There was little space "below" the tin roof and the scaffolding for the squirrel to hide in.
3. "Inside" the box was the secret he had been searching for all his life.
Select the answer using the codes given below:
(1) 3 only
(2) 1 and 2
(3) 2 and 3
(4) None of the above
= 1
~ Option 1: only sentence 3 is correct. "Inside" means within an enclosed space, so "Inside the box was the secret he had been searching for all his life" is right (note the inversion, with the verb placed before the long subject). Sentences 1 and 2 swap two prepositions. "Below" means at a lower level than something, which suits the floors of a building: "Raj's apartment was three floors below the Subramanian residence." "Under" or "underneath" means directly beneath something and covered or hidden by it, which suits a hiding place: "little space underneath the tin roof and the scaffolding for the squirrel to hide in." The same contrast appears in "below sea level" and "below average" (levels) against "under the bed" and "underneath the carpet" (covered). The swap is what the question appears to test, although "space below the roof" is heard in everyday English and some keys accept sentence 2 and give option 3 (2 and 3). (No official key; some sources differ.)

Q 19 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
In the following sentences prepositions or phrasal verbs have been used, which have been underlined (shown here in double quotes). Select the sentence(s) wherein the use of the preposition or phrasal verb is/are correct.
1. The ship "put in" at the first available port to repair its damaged hull.
2. The advertisement was "put out" for wide circulation.
3. The matter had become so urgent that it could not be "put off" for another day.
Select the answer using the codes given below:
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 1 and 3 only
(3) 2 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ Option 4: all three phrasal verbs are used correctly. "Put in" (of a ship) means to enter a port or harbour, usually for a short stop, so the ship put in at the first available port to repair its damaged hull. "Put out" means to publish, issue or broadcast something, so the advertisement was put out, that is released, for wide circulation. Q 96 | acc | Depreciation & Valuation
The principle of inventory valuation – "cost or net realisable value, whichever is lower" is based on:
(1) Accrual Concept
(2) Matching Concept
(3) Money Measurement Concept
(4) Convention of Conservatism
= 4
~ Option 4, the convention of conservatism, is correct. Conservatism (prudence) means 'anticipate no profit, but provide for all possible losses': expected losses are recognised as soon as they are foreseen, while gains are recognised only when they are realised. Valuing inventory at the lower of cost and net realisable value (NRV) applies this rule, and AS 2 and Ind AS 2 require it. A fall in value below cost is provided for at once, but a rise above cost is ignored until the goods are sold. NRV is the estimated selling price less the estimated costs of completion and of making the sale. The accrual concept records income and expenses when they arise, not when cash moves (option 1); the matching concept sets expenses against the revenue of the same period (option 2); and money measurement records only items that can be expressed in money (option 3). None of them produces a 'lower of' rule. Other examples of conservatism are the provision for doubtful debts and not recording contingent gains. Trap: overdone conservatism creates secret reserves.

Q 97 | acc | Accounting Basics
Which one of the following is not a 'fundamental accounting assumption' in the preparation of financial statements?

(2) Going concern
(3) Accrual
(4) Consistency
= 1
~ Option 1, the matching concept, is correct. Accounting Standard 1 (Disclosure of Accounting Policies) names three fundamental accounting assumptions: going concern, consistency and accrual. If financial statements follow these assumptions, no disclosure is needed; if they depart from any of them, that fact must be disclosed. (2) Error of omission
(3) Error of commission

Q 100 | acc | Financial Statements
In accounting context, which one of the following statements is correct?

(2) Capital reserves are normally created out of distributable profits.
(3) General reserve can be used only for some specific purposes.
(4) 'Provision' is a charge against profit.
= 4
~ Option 4 is correct: a provision is a charge against profit. A provision is an amount set aside for a known liability, or for a fall in the value of an asset, whose amount cannot be determined accurately, such as provision for doubtful debts, depreciation or taxation. It is debited to the Profit and Loss Account before net profit is found, so it must be made whether or not there is a profit. Statement 1 is wrong: a reserve is an appropriation of profit, made after profit is found, not a charge against it. Statement 2 is wrong: capital reserves are created out of capital profits, such as profit on the reissue of forfeited shares or profit prior to incorporation, and are normally not available for dividend. Q 103 | acc | Financial Statements
Which one of the following is correct?
(1) Operating profit = Net profit − Non-operating expenses − Non-operating incomes
(2) Operating profit = Net profit + Non-operating expenses + Non-operating incomes
(3) Operating profit = Net profit + Non-operating expenses − Non-operating incomes
(4) Operating profit = Net profit − Non-operating expenses + Non-operating incomes
= 3
~ Option 3 is correct. Operating profit is the profit earned from the main business operations, before non-operating items. Q 106 | eco | Money, Banking & Inflation

(2) Principle of Utmost Good Faith
(3) Principle of Co-operation
(4) Principle of Mitigation
= 4
~ Option 4, the principle of mitigation (minimisation) of loss, is correct. Q 107 | eco | Money, Banking & Inflation
Which one of the following is not a Contract of Indemnity?
(1) Contract of Fire Insurance
(2) Contract of Life Insurance
(3) Contract of Marine Insurance
(4) Contract of Motor Insurance
= 2
~ Option 2, the contract of life insurance, is not a contract of indemnity. A contract of indemnity compensates the insured only for the actual loss suffered, putting the insured back in the same financial position as before the loss, without any profit. Fire, marine and motor insurance (options 1, 3 and 4) are contracts of indemnity, because the loss to property, or the liability to third parties, can be measured in money. ~ Option 3 is correct. Vouching is the examination of documentary evidence, such as invoices, receipts, vouchers, contracts, agreements and minutes, that supports the entries in the books of account. Its primary objective is to establish the authenticity of the recorded transactions: that each one actually took place, relates to the business, was properly authorised, belongs to the accounting period and is correctly recorded. Because the truth of the final accounts depends on genuine entries, vouching is called the essence or backbone of auditing. Option 1 is wrong because detecting errors and frauds is a by-product of vouching, not its main aim. Option 2 is wrong because internal controls are evaluated through the study and testing of the internal control system, which helps the auditor decide how much test checking is needed. Option 4 is wrong because assessing management performance is the work of a management or performance audit, not of a statutory financial audit. Remember: vouching tests the transactions recorded during the year; verification tests the assets and liabilities on the balance sheet date.

Q 110 | acc | Auditing & Company Accounts
Which one of the following is not the objective of verification in auditing?
(1) Checking the historical accuracy of accounts
(2) Verification of the existence of the assets
(3) Verification of the valuation of the assets
(4) Verification of the authority of the acquisition of the assets
= 1
~ Option 1 is not an objective of verification, so it is the answer. Verification is the auditor's examination, as at the balance sheet date, of the assets and liabilities shown in the balance sheet. Standard auditing texts list its objectives as confirming the existence and possession of the assets (option 2), their ownership and title, their proper valuation (option 3), their freedom from any charge or lien, that they were acquired under proper authority for the purposes of the business (option 4), and that they are properly recorded and disclosed. Checking the historical accuracy of the accounts, that is, whether the transactions recorded during the year are genuine and correctly entered, is the job of vouching and routine checking, not of verification. Example: for land and buildings, the auditor inspects the title deeds, checks for mortgages and examines the basis of valuation; for investments, the auditor inspects the securities or obtains a confirmation from the custodian. Remember: vouching looks at the year's transactions, while verification looks at the balance sheet position on a particular date.

Q 120 | eco | External Sector
The Union Cabinet had approved a new Framework on Currency Swap Arrangement with which regional bloc for the period 2024 – 2027?
(1) South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation (SAARC)
(2) African Union (AU)
(3) Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN)
(4) European Union (EU)
= 1
~ Option 1, SAARC, is correct. The SAARC currency swap facility, operational since November 2012, gives short-term foreign-exchange support to the central banks of SAARC countries facing balance of payments or liquidity stress, until longer-term arrangements are made. The revised Framework on Currency Swap Arrangement for SAARC countries for 2024–2027 was put in place by the Reserve Bank of India, with the concurrence of the Government of India, on 27 June 2024. It has a separate INR swap window with a corpus of ₹250 billion, offering concessions for swaps drawn in Indian rupees, and a US dollar/euro swap window with a corpus of US$2 billion. The RBI signs bilateral swap agreements with the SAARC central banks that wish to use it; for example, the RBI signed one with the Maldives Monetary Authority in October 2024 for up to US$400 million and ₹30 billion. India has no such framework with the African Union, ASEAN or the European Union. Linked facts: the rupee window also promotes the international use of the Indian rupee, and SAARC has eight members, with its secretariat in Kathmandu.
