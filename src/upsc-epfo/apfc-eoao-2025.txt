Q 1 | eng | Reading Comprehension
Read the passage given below and answer the question that follows.
Africa is on the move. At least 20 million (i) ______ live outside the continent, a three-fold increase since 1990. Europe accounts for about half of African (ii) ______ outside Africa, but its share has steadily declined since 1990. America, China, Saudi Arabia and Turkey have all seen surges in their African-born populations. It may seem otherworldly to predict that this will continue, given the fierce backlash against (iii) ______ across the West. Notwithstanding pushback, African (iv) ______ is an unstoppable force that will long outlast today's populists and help define the 21st century. Demography is one cause. It is well-known that in the developed world the number of people of working age will decline, exacerbating labour shortages. What is less widely appreciated is that emerging countries that are associated with exporting people, such as Mexico and the Philippines, are also getting older and richer — meaning smaller shares of their populations will, in future, wish to emigrate in search of work. In Africa, by contrast, the working-age population is forecast to increase by around 700 million people by 2050. Another cause is economics. Many African countries are entering a phase in their development in which people are still poor enough to wish to leave but, for the first time, rich enough to be able to travel long distances. African countries create only around a fifth of the number of formal jobs required to absorb their expanding workforce.
In the above passage there are four blanks numbered (i), (ii), (iii) and (iv). Select the option from those given below in which the most appropriate term has been used for each of the blanks respectively:
(1) (i) immigrants; (ii) migration; (iii) emigrants; (iv) immigration
(2) (i) emigrants; (ii) migrants; (iii) immigration; (iv) migration
(3) (i) immigrants; (ii) emigrants; (iii) immigration; (iv) migrants
(4) (i) emigrants; (ii) migrants; (iii) migration; (iv) emigration
= 2
~ Seen from Africa, people who have left the continent are emigrants, so (i) is "emigrants". (ii) "African migrants outside Africa" is the neutral word for those people living abroad. (iii) The West objects to people coming in, so its backlash is against "immigration". (iv) The overall movement that will "help define the 21st century" is African "migration". Options 1 and 3 wrongly call those who left Africa "immigrants", option 3 also puts the plural "migrants" before the singular verb "is", and option 4's backlash against "migration" misses that the West objects to arrivals.

(2) Worsening the problem of labour shortages

(4) Intractably unburdening labour shortages

Q 5 | eng | Reading Comprehension
Read the passage given below and answer the question that follows.
Africa is on the move. At least 20 million (i) ______ live outside the continent, a three-fold increase since 1990. Europe accounts for about half of African (ii) ______ outside Africa, but its share has steadily declined since 1990. America, China, Saudi Arabia and Turkey have all seen surges in their African-born populations. It may seem otherworldly to predict that this will continue, given the fierce backlash against (iii) ______ across the West. Notwithstanding pushback, African (iv) ______ is an unstoppable force that will long outlast today's populists and help define the 21st century. Demography is one cause. It is well-known that in the developed world the number of people of working age will decline, exacerbating labour shortages. What is less widely appreciated is that emerging countries that are associated with exporting people, such as Mexico and the Philippines, are also getting older and richer — meaning smaller shares of their populations will, in future, wish to emigrate in search of work. In Africa, by contrast, the working-age population is forecast to increase by around 700 million people by 2050. Another cause is economics. Many African countries are entering a phase in their development in which people are still poor enough to wish to leave but, for the first time, rich enough to be able to travel long distances. African countries create only around a fifth of the number of formal jobs required to absorb their expanding workforce.
Which one of the following is the figure of speech in the expression "Africa is on the move"?
(1) Personification
(2) Irony
(3) Anthropomorphism
(4) Antithesis
= 1
~ Personification gives human actions or qualities to something that is not human. Here a continent is said to be "on the move", as if it were a person setting out on a journey, when what is meant is that Africans are migrating. Anthropomorphism is the broader idea of attributing human form or behaviour to animals, gods or objects, usually literally in stories (talking animals), and is not the usual name of this figure of speech. There is no irony (saying the opposite of what is meant) and no antithesis (contrasting ideas in balanced phrases). Strictly, "Africa" for "Africans" is also metonymy, which is not offered.

Q 6 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Select the most appropriate option for the blank space.
The evening looked ______ against the stretched out sky.
(1) red, beautiful and soul-quenching
(2) beautiful, red and soul-quenching
(3) soul-quenching, beautiful and red
(4) beautiful, soul-quenching and red
= 4
~ Adjectives of opinion come before adjectives of fact such as colour (the usual order is opinion, size, age, shape, colour, origin, material, purpose), so "red" goes last; this rules out options 1 and 2. Between the two opinion adjectives, the general one ("beautiful", which can describe almost anything) comes before the more specific one ("soul-quenching"), as in "a lovely, comfortable chair". Hence: "The evening looked beautiful, soul-quenching and red against the stretched out sky." Option 3 reverses the two opinion adjectives.

Q 7 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Select the most appropriate option for the blank space.
Summers ______ be really hot at times, so make sure you stay hydrated.
(1) can
(2) could
(3) might
(4) may
= 1
~ "Can" expresses general or theoretical possibility, that is, what is sometimes true of something: "Summers can be really hot at times, so make sure you stay hydrated." The phrase "at times" signals this typical, recurring possibility. "May" and "might" express a possibility about a particular situation (it may be hot tomorrow) and sound odd in a general statement about summers, while "could" suggests a past or more remote, conditional possibility.

Q 8 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Select the most appropriate option for the blank space.
I was wondering which festival ______ we celebrate together this year?
(1) will
(2) might
(3) shall
(4) do
= 3
~ "Shall" is used with "I" and "we" in questions that ask for a suggestion or a decision: "Which festival shall we celebrate together this year?" The speaker is wondering what the two of them should choose, not predicting a fact, so "shall" is the most appropriate. "Will we celebrate" asks about a future fact, "might" weakens it into a vague possibility, and "do we celebrate" is the simple present used for habits, which does not fit "this year".

Q 9 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Select the most appropriate option for the blank space.
I was ______ exhausted by the end of the week spent in the library doing research work.
(1) exceedingly
(2) inordinately
(3) very
(4) completely
= 4
~ "Exhausted" is an extreme (non-gradable) adjective: it already means "extremely tired". Such adjectives are intensified with "completely", "totally", "utterly" or "absolutely", not with grading adverbs such as "very" or "exceedingly", which go with gradable adjectives like "tired". Hence: "I was completely exhausted by the end of the week spent in the library doing research work." "Inordinately" means excessively, beyond reasonable limits, which does not suit tiredness either.

Q 10 | eng | Grammar & Usage
Select the most appropriate option for the blank space.
They ______ be very far away. I saw them a little while ago.
(1) shall not
(2) cannot
(3) should not
(4) ought not
= 2
~ "Cannot" (can't) expresses a logical deduction that something is impossible: since the speaker saw them a little while ago, they cannot be very far away. The positive deduction would use "must" (They must be nearby). "Should not" and "ought not" express advice, obligation or at most a weaker expectation, and "ought not" would also need "to" (ought not to be). "Shall not" expresses a future intention or prohibition. Only "cannot" conveys the certainty drawn from the evidence in the second sentence.

Q 11 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Choose the word or phrase closest in meaning to the underlined word "cordial".
The fact that the deliberations were carried out in a "cordial" manner pleased the old guard very much.
(1) joust
(2) convivial
(3) jocular
(4) zestful
= 2
~ "Cordial" means warm, friendly and sincere, as in cordial relations between two countries. Q 12 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Choose the word or phrase closest in meaning to the underlined word "impugned".
His views on the matter have been "impugned" by those evaluating his role in the community as its erstwhile ruler.
(1) challenged
(2) belittled
(3) enhanced
(4) endured
= 1
~ To impugn is to dispute the truth, validity or honesty of something, to call it into question (Latin impugnare, to attack). So his views were challenged by those assessing his role as the community's former ("erstwhile") ruler. "Belittled" means made to seem unimportant, which is disparaging but is not the same as questioning validity. Q 13 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Choose the word or phrase closest in meaning to the underlined word "egregious".
His ad hominem comments on his colleague were considered "egregious" by those present.
(1) extensively elaborate
(2) flagrant and shocking
(3) out of context
(4) impromptu and unintended
= 2
~ "Egregious" means outstandingly bad, flagrant or shocking (Latin egregius, standing out from the flock; it once meant remarkably good). Ad hominem comments attack a person rather than his arguments, and those present found such personal attacks on a colleague flagrantly offensive. Q 15 | eng | Vocabulary & Idioms
Choose the word or phrase closest in meaning to the underlined word "corporeity".
The debate around the "corporeity" of the soul presents a compelling paradox.
(1) the quality of physical existence
(2) collective consciousness
(3) embodied in divinity
(4) corpuscular existence
= 1
~ "Corporeity" (Latin corpus, body) is the quality or state of having a material body, that is, bodily or physical existence, materiality. The paradox is whether the soul, usually thought of as immaterial, can have a physical existence. "Corpuscular" refers to corpuscles or minute particles, so "corpuscular existence" is a distractor built on the similar root. "Collective consciousness" and "embodied in divinity" have nothing to do with bodily existence.

(1) 1 and 2
(2) 1 and 3

Q 17 | eng | Grammar & Usage
In the following sentences prepositions or phrasal verbs have been used, which have been underlined (shown here in double quotes). Select the sentence(s) wherein the use of the preposition or phrasal verb is/are correct.
1. "Amidst" the scuffle and the resultant confusion, the thief managed to slip away.
2. There was not a single person "amongst" those assembled who was willing to become the leader.
3. "Among" the members of the community the local doctor was the one who was most respected.
Select the answer using the codes given below:
(1) 1 and 2 only
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 and 3 only
(4) 1, 2 and 3
= 4
~ All three are correct. "Amid" or "amidst" means in the middle of or during, and is used with a situation or a mass of things: "Amidst the scuffle and the resultant confusion". "Among" or "amongst" means in the company of, or included in, a group of three or more and takes a plural noun or pronoun: "amongst those assembled", "Among the members of the community". "Amidst" and "amongst" are standard, slightly more formal or British forms of "amid" and "among", not errors.

Q 21 | ih | Modern India & Freedom Struggle
The rebellions that broke out at regular intervals against the British authorities in the hill region of Visakhapatnam, popularly known as the Gudem area, were due to which of the following reason(s)?
1. The feudal affinities of the muttadars with the Bhupathi family of Golconda zamindari
2. Placing the ex-zamindari family on the seat of Golconda
3. British authorities' refusal to grant annual maintenance for the Bhupathi family
Select the answer using the codes given below:
(1) 1 and 2
(2) 2 and 3
(3) 1 and 3
(4) 3 only
= 1
~ The Golconda (Golugonda) zamindari in the Visakhapatnam hills belonged to the Bhupathi family, and the muttadars (hill chiefs) of the Gudem area were bound to that family by old feudal ties (1). After the British dispossessed the family, these muttadars rose again and again between the late 1830s and the 1850s, aiming to place the ex-zamindari family back on the seat of Golconda (2). Q 22 | ih | Modern India & Freedom Struggle
Consider the following:
1. Deccan Revolt
2. Santhal Rebellion
3. Kherwar Movement
4. Rampa Rebellion
Which of the following is the correct chronological order of the above (starting with the earliest)?
(1) 1-2-3-4
(2) 4-3-2-1
(3) 2-3-1-4
(4) 1-3-2-4
= 3
~ The Santhal Rebellion (Hul) under Sidhu and Kanhu broke out in 1855–56 in the Rajmahal hills. The Kherwar (Sapha Har) movement among the Santhals, led by Bhagirath Manjhi, followed in the early-to-mid 1870s. The Deccan Revolt of peasants against Marwari and Gujarati moneylenders began at Supa in Poona district in May 1875. The Rampa Rebellion in the Godavari hill tracts came in 1879–80; a later Rampa rising under Alluri Sitarama Raju was in 1922–24. So the order is 2-3-1-4.

Q 23 | ih | Modern India & Freedom Struggle
Which of the following statements about Home Rule Movement is/are correct?
1. The advocates of the Home Rule Movement believed in constitutional methods and were opposed to violence and revolutionary action.
2. In 1917, the two Home Rule Leagues of Lokmanya Tilak and Annie Besant worked in cooperation with each other.
3. Annie Besant confined her activities to the Bombay Presidency and the Central Provinces and the rest of India was left to Lokmanya Tilak.
Select the answer using the codes given below:
(1) 1 and 2
(2) 2 and 3
(3) 1 only
(4) 3 only
= 1
~ Tilak's Home Rule League (April 1916) and Annie Besant's League (September 1916) both sought self-government within the Empire through constitutional agitation such as meetings, pamphlets and petitions, and kept away from violence and revolutionary action (1). The two Leagues stayed separate but co-ordinated their work, which became close in 1917, especially after Besant's internment in June 1917 (2). Statement 3 reverses the division: Tilak's League worked in Maharashtra (except Bombay city), Karnataka, the Central Provinces and Berar, and Besant's League covered the rest of India. Hence 1 and 2.

Q 24 | ih | Modern India & Freedom Struggle
Which of the following statements regarding the Freedom Struggle from 1920 to 1935 is/are correct?
1. In Bihar and Bengal, the Non-Cooperation Movement owed its effectiveness to the participation of peasants.
2. In Bihar they were organised against the planters under the banner of Kisan Sabha.
3. In Midnapore in Bengal, Mahishya peasants rallied against the taxes of the Union Board under the leadership of Birendranath Sasmal.

(1) 1, 2 and 3
(2) 2 and 3 only
(3) 1 only
(4) 3 only
= 1
~ Peasant participation gave the Non-Cooperation Movement its force in Bihar and Bengal (1). In north Bihar, peasants of districts such as Champaran, Darbhanga and Muzaffarpur were organised through Kisan Sabhas against the planters and big landlords (2). Q 25 | ih | Art & Culture
Which of the following statements about Bharata's Natyashastra is/are correct?
1. It is dated by scholars between the second century BCE and the second century CE.
2. It analyses theatrical spectacle as an amalgam of speech, vocal and instrumental music, gesture, mime, etc.
3. The ground it covers is less extensive than Aristotle's Poetics, its nearest ancient Greek counterpart.
Select the answer using the codes given below:
(1) 2 only
(2) 1 and 2 only
(3) 1, 2 and 3
(4) 1 and 3 only
= 2
~ The Natyashastra attributed to Bharata is dated by most scholars between the 2nd century BCE and the 2nd century CE (1). It treats drama as a composite spectacle combining speech, vocal and instrumental music, gesture (abhinaya), mime, dance, costume and stagecraft (2). Statement 3 is wrong: its scope is much wider than that of Aristotle's Poetics, its nearest Greek counterpart, because besides dramatic theory and rasa it also covers music, dance, acting and theatre architecture. Hence 1 and 2 only.

(1) Archibald Carlleyle
(2) Alexander Cunningham
(3) V.S. Wakankar

= 3

Q 52 | sci | Science & Technology
As a countermeasure to drone swarm attack, an effective way is to use electromagnetic (EM) drone jamming to disrupt the GPS and communication system of the drones. Which one among the following is the correct frequency range used for EM drone jamming?
(1) Far-infrared frequency
(2) Microwave frequency
(3) Visible frequency
(4) Infrared frequency
= 2

Q 53 | sci | Science & Technology
Which one among the following is the correct method employed by GPS to find the location of a target?
(1) Trilateration method, which measures lengths of sides of triangle with target location as one of the heads of the triangle
(2) Dilation method, which measures distance of target location from two already known landmarks
(3) Measurement of the distance of target from the designated geostationary satellite
(4) Measurement of the distance of target from the designated polar satellite
= 1
~ GPS uses trilateration. Each satellite broadcasts its position and the exact time; the receiver turns the travel time of each signal into its distance from that satellite. Distances from several satellites, at least three for position and a fourth to correct the receiver's clock, meet at only one point, the receiver's location; this is the triangle-side method of option (1) extended to three dimensions. GPS satellites are in medium Earth orbit about 20,200 km high, not in geostationary or polar orbit, and one satellite alone cannot fix a position. "Dilation method" is not a positioning technique.

Q 54 | sci | Biology

(1) Liquid
(2) Dry air
(3) Humid air
(4) Fatty muscle
= 1
~ The pleural cavity is the thin space between the visceral pleura covering the lungs and the parietal pleura lining the chest wall. Q 56 | sci | Chemistry
Which one among the following are the primary reactants of photochemical smog?
(1) Sulphur Hexafluoride and Soot
(2) Carbon Monoxide and Ozone
(3) Nitric Oxide and Unburnt Hydrocarbons
(4) Peroxy Radicals and Sub-oxides
= 3
~ Photochemical smog forms in warm, sunny weather when sunlight acts on nitrogen oxides and unburnt hydrocarbons, mainly from vehicle exhaust. Nitric oxide (NO) is oxidised to NO₂, which sunlight splits to give atomic oxygen and then ozone; ozone and NO react with the hydrocarbons to form secondary pollutants such as formaldehyde, acrolein and peroxyacetyl nitrate (PAN). Q 57 | sci | Chemistry
Which one among the following is known as Carbolic acid?
(1) Phenol
(2) Alcohol
(3) Ether
(4) Acetone
= 1
~ Phenol (C₆H₅OH, hydroxybenzene) is called carbolic acid. It was first obtained from coal tar and is weakly acidic, because the phenoxide ion left after losing H⁺ is stabilised by resonance; so phenol reacts with sodium hydroxide to form sodium phenoxide, which ordinary alcohols do not. Joseph Lister used carbolic acid as the first antiseptic in surgery. Alcohols such as ethanol, ethers and acetone (a ketone) are neutral compounds and are not called carbolic acid.

~ Under Section 4(1) of the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972, gratuity is payable on superannuation, retirement, resignation, or death or disablement due to accident or disease, after at least five years of continuous service. Resignation after six years (1) and retirement after five years (3) both qualify. The proviso waives the five-year condition on death or disablement, so death after two years (2) also qualifies. Q 96 | acc | Depreciation & Valuation
The principle of inventory valuation – "cost or net realisable value, whichever is lower" is based on:
(1) Accrual Concept
(2) Matching Concept
(3) Money Measurement Concept
(4) Convention of Conservatism
= 4
~ The convention of conservatism (prudence) means that expected losses are recognised as soon as they are foreseen, while gains are recognised only when realised. Valuing inventory at the lower of cost and net realisable value (as AS 2 and Ind AS 2 require) applies this rule. A fall in value below cost is provided for at once, but a rise above cost is ignored until the goods are sold. The accrual, matching and money-measurement concepts do not produce this lower-of rule.

Q 97 | acc | Accounting Basics
Which one of the following is not a 'fundamental accounting assumption' in the preparation of financial statements?
(1) Matching concept
(2) Going concern
(3) Accrual
(4) Consistency
= 1
~ Accounting Standard 1 (Disclosure of Accounting Policies) names three fundamental accounting assumptions: going concern, consistency and accrual. If financial statements follow them, no disclosure is needed. If they depart from them, the fact must be disclosed. The matching concept matches expenses against the revenue of the same period. It is an important accounting principle but is not one of the three fundamental accounting assumptions, so (1) is the answer.

Q 98 | acc | Accounting Basics
Which one of the following is a revenue expenditure?
(1) Overhaul expenses of a second hand machinery purchased
(2) Legal fees to acquire a property
(3) Amount spent for replacement of worn-out portion of a machine
(4) Expenses in connection with obtaining a license for running the cinema hall
= 3
~ Replacing a worn-out part only keeps an existing machine in working order and does not add to its capacity or life. It is a recurring maintenance cost, so it is revenue expenditure charged to the Profit and Loss Account. Overhauling second-hand machinery before first use is part of the cost of making the asset usable, so it is capital. Legal fees to acquire property and the cost of a licence to run a cinema hall give long-term benefit, so they are also capital.

Q 99 | acc | Accounting Basics
When purchase of an asset is treated as an expense, in the accounting context, it is called:
(1) Error of principle
(2) Error of omission
(3) Error of commission
(4) Compensating error
= 1
~ An error of principle breaks a basic accounting principle, usually by confusing capital and revenue items. Debiting the purchase of an asset (such as furniture) to an expense account (such as Purchases or Repairs) is the standard example. The trial balance still agrees, but profit and assets are both understated. An error of omission leaves out an entry. An error of commission is a wrong posting, such as the wrong amount or the wrong person's account. A compensating error is cancelled out by another error.

Q 100 | acc | Financial Statements
In accounting context, which one of the following statements is correct?
(1) Reserve created is a charge against profits.
(2) Capital reserves are normally created out of distributable profits.
(3) General reserve can be used only for some specific purposes.
(4) 'Provision' is a charge against profit.
= 4
~ A provision is set aside for a known liability or loss of value whose amount cannot be fixed exactly, such as doubtful debts or depreciation. It is a charge against profit, debited to the Profit and Loss Account before net profit is found. A reserve is an appropriation of profit, not a charge, so (1) is wrong. Capital reserves come from capital profits, such as a premium on issue of shares, not from distributable profits, so (2) is wrong. A general reserve may be used for any purpose, while a specific reserve is kept for a set purpose, so (3) is wrong.

Q 101 | acc | Accounting Basics
Which one of the following statements is correct?
(1) Agreement of trial balance is a conclusive proof of the accuracy.
(2) Suspense account opened in a trial balance is a permanent account.
(3) At the end of the accounting year, all the nominal accounts of the ledger book are balanced.
(4) A ledger is known as the principal book of accounts.
= 4
~ The ledger is called the principal book because every transaction recorded in the journal or a subsidiary book is finally classified there account-wise, and the trial balance and final accounts are prepared from it. An agreed trial balance proves only arithmetical accuracy: complete omissions, errors of principle, compensating errors and some errors of commission do not disturb it. A suspense account is a temporary account opened to make the trial balance agree and is closed once the errors are found. Nominal accounts are not balanced at the year end; they are closed by transfer to the trading and profit and loss account, while real and personal accounts are balanced.

Q 102 | acc | Accounting Basics
Which one of the following statements is not correct?
(1) Capital account has a debit balance.
(2) Discount column of cash book records cash discount.
(3) Under traditional approach, rent outstanding is a personal account.
(4) Cash sales are not recorded in the sales day book.
= 1
~ The capital account normally has a credit balance, because capital is the owner's claim on the business, a liability of the business to the proprietor; it turns debit only when losses and drawings exceed the capital. The other statements are correct. The discount column of a three-column cash book records only cash discount (allowed on the debit side, received on the credit side); trade discount is never entered in the books. Under the traditional classification, outstanding rent is a representative personal account. The sales day book records only credit sales of goods, so cash sales go to the cash book.

Q 103 | acc | Financial Statements
Which one of the following is correct?
(1) Operating profit = Net profit − Non-operating expenses − Non-operating incomes
(2) Operating profit = Net profit + Non-operating expenses + Non-operating incomes
(3) Operating profit = Net profit + Non-operating expenses − Non-operating incomes
(4) Operating profit = Net profit − Non-operating expenses + Non-operating incomes
= 3
~ Operating profit is the profit earned from the main business operations. Net profit is struck after non-operating items, so to get back to operating profit we add the non-operating expenses that reduced it (such as loss on sale of fixed assets or interest on loans) and deduct the non-operating incomes that raised it (such as dividend or interest received, or profit on sale of assets). Hence Operating profit = Net profit + Non-operating expenses − Non-operating incomes. Example: net profit ₹1,00,000, non-operating expenses ₹20,000 and non-operating income ₹30,000 give 1,00,000 + 20,000 − 30,000 = ₹90,000. The other options reverse one or both signs.

Q 104 | acc | Depreciation & Valuation
Which one of the following costs is generally not included in computing the cost of inventory?
(1) Administration overheads
(2) All cost of purchase
(3) Normal wastage of materials
(4) Storage cost assuming a special storage is required as part of production process
= 1
~ Under AS 2 (Ind AS 2), Valuation of Inventories, the cost of inventories comprises all costs of purchase, costs of conversion and other costs incurred in bringing the inventories to their present location and condition. The standard excludes abnormal wastage, storage costs unless they are necessary in the production process before a further production stage, administrative overheads that do not contribute to bringing inventories to their present location and condition, and selling and distribution costs. So administration overheads are generally excluded and charged as expenses. Normal wastage is absorbed in cost, and storage that is a necessary part of production (for example, maturing) is included.

Q 105 | acc | Financial Statements
Which one of the following will be the adjustment journal entry if the rent received in advance is ₹2,000?
(1) Debit profit and loss account and Credit rent account
(2) Debit rent received account and Credit rent received in advance account
(3) Debit rent received in advance account and Credit rent received account
(4) Debit rent account and Credit profit and loss account
= 2
~ Rent received in advance is income received during the year that belongs to the next accounting period, so on the balance sheet date it is a liability. The adjusting entry takes it out of this year's income: Rent Received A/c Dr ₹2,000, To Rent Received in Advance A/c ₹2,000. In the final accounts it is deducted from rent income and shown as a current liability. Q 106 | eco | Money, Banking & Inflation
In case of contract of insurance, the principle which states that it is the duty of the insured to take reasonable steps to minimize the loss or damage to the insured property is called:
(1) Principle of Subrogation
(2) Principle of Utmost Good Faith
(3) Principle of Co-operation
(4) Principle of Mitigation
= 4
~ The principle of mitigation (minimisation) of loss requires the insured, when the insured event happens, to take all reasonable steps to save the property and keep the loss as small as possible, acting as a prudent uninsured owner would, for example by trying to put out a fire or moving undamaged goods to safety. The insured cannot neglect the property just because it is insured, and loss caused by such neglect may not be paid. Subrogation means that the insurer, after paying the claim, steps into the insured's shoes to recover from third parties. Utmost good faith requires both parties to disclose all material facts.

Q 107 | eco | Money, Banking & Inflation
Which one of the following is not a Contract of Indemnity?
(1) Contract of Fire Insurance
(2) Contract of Life Insurance
(3) Contract of Marine Insurance
(4) Contract of Motor Insurance
= 2
~ A contract of indemnity compensates the insured only for the actual loss suffered, putting the insured back in the same financial position without any profit. Fire, marine and motor insurance are contracts of indemnity, because the loss to property (or liability) can be measured in money. Life insurance is not: a human life cannot be valued in money, so the insurer pays the fixed sum assured on death or maturity, whatever the actual loss. Life insurance is therefore a contract of assurance, and the related principles of indemnity, subrogation and contribution do not apply to it.

Q 108 | acc | Auditing & Company Accounts

Q 109 | acc | Auditing & Company Accounts
In the context of auditing, the primary objective of vouching is:
(1) To detect errors in financial statements
(2) To evaluate internal controls
(3) To verify the authenticity of transactions
(4) To assess management performance
= 3
~ Vouching is the examination of documentary evidence, such as invoices, receipts, vouchers, contracts and minutes, that supports the entries in the books of account. Its primary objective is to establish the authenticity of the recorded transactions: that each one actually took place, relates to the business, was properly authorised and is correctly recorded. That is why it is called the essence or backbone of auditing. Detecting errors and frauds is a result of vouching, not its main aim. Evaluating internal controls is done through the review and testing of controls, and assessing management performance is the work of a management or performance audit.

Q 110 | acc | Auditing & Company Accounts
Which one of the following is not the objective of verification in auditing?
(1) Checking the historical accuracy of accounts
(2) Verification of the existence of the assets
(3) Verification of the valuation of the assets
(4) Verification of the authority of the acquisition of the assets
= 1
~ Verification is the auditor's examination, as at the balance sheet date, of the assets and liabilities shown in the balance sheet. Standard auditing texts list its objectives as confirming the existence and possession of the assets, their ownership and title, proper valuation, freedom from any charge, and that they were acquired under proper authority for the business, so options (2), (3) and (4) are objectives of verification.
