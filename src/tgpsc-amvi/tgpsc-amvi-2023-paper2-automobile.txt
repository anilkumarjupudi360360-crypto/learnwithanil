Q 1 | thm | Thermodynamics
Which of the following laws provides the basis for the temperature measurement?
(1) Zeroth law of thermodynamics
(2) First law of thermodynamics
(3) Second law of thermodynamics
(4) Boyle’s law
= 1
~ The zeroth law states that if two bodies are each in thermal equilibrium with a third body, they are in thermal equilibrium with each other. This lets a thermometer (the third body) compare temperatures, so it is the basis of temperature measurement. The first law deals with conservation of energy, the second with the direction of heat flow and entropy, and Boyle's law with the P–V relation of a gas at constant temperature.

Q 2 | thm | Thermodynamics
For an isothermal process, which of the following is correct based on first law of thermodynamics?
(1) Heat added or rejected by the system equals to the work done by the system or work done on the system
(2) Work done by the system or work done on the system equals to the change in internal energy
(3) Heat added or rejected equals to the change in internal energy
(4) Heat added or rejected is equals to the sum of change in internal energy (not equal to zero) and work done by a system or work done on a system
= 1
~ By the first law, Q = ΔU + W. For an ideal gas the internal energy depends only on temperature, so in an isothermal process ΔU = 0 and Q = W: the heat added equals the work done by the system, and the work done on it is rejected as heat. Option 2 describes an adiabatic process (Q = 0), option 3 a constant-volume process (W = 0), and option 4 wrongly assumes ΔU ≠ 0.

Q 3 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
In context of I.C. engines, what is the ratio of shaft energy to the energy available at the piston is called?
(1) Brake thermal efficiency
(2) Indicated thermal efficiency
(3) Mechanical efficiency
(4) Volumetric efficiency
= 3
~ Mechanical efficiency ηm = brake power / indicated power, i.e. the useful output at the shaft divided by the power developed by the gases on the piston; the difference is lost in friction and pumping. Brake thermal efficiency compares brake power with the fuel energy supplied, indicated thermal efficiency compares indicated power with the fuel energy, and volumetric efficiency compares the charge actually inducted with the swept volume.

Q 4 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
For the I.C. engine, the ratio of total volume of the cylinder to clearance volume is called ______.
(1) clearance ratio
(2) efficiency
(3) expansion ratio
(4) compression ratio
= 4
~ Compression ratio r = total cylinder volume / clearance volume = (Vs + Vc)/Vc, where Vs is the swept (stroke) volume and Vc the clearance volume left above the piston at TDC. Typical values are about 6–10 for petrol (SI) engines and 14–22 for diesel (CI) engines. The clearance ratio is Vc/Vs, and a higher compression ratio raises the air-standard efficiency.

Q 5 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
Which of the following is an advantage of reciprocating internal combustion engines over external combustion engines?
(1) Starting torque is high
(2) Weight to power ratio is generally low
(3) Require more space
(4) Cheaper fuel can be used
= 2
~ Because the fuel burns inside the cylinder, an IC engine needs no boiler, condenser or separate furnace, so it is compact, with a low weight-to-power ratio and higher overall efficiency, which suits vehicles. External combustion engines such as steam engines give high starting torque and can burn cheaper fuels like coal or wood, but they need more space; these are not advantages of IC engines.

Q 6 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
Which of the following is a function of Gudgeon pin in internal combustion engines?
(1) It acts as a face to receive gas pressure and transmits the thrust to connecting rod
(2) Provide a good sealing fit between the piston and cylinder
(3) It supports and allows the swivel of the connecting rod
(4) It reduces friction and takes care of the fluctuations of speed during a cycle
= 3
~ The gudgeon (piston or wrist) pin joins the piston to the small end of the connecting rod and acts as a pivot, supporting the rod and allowing it to swivel as the crank turns. The piston crown receives the gas pressure, the piston rings give the seal between piston and cylinder, and the flywheel takes care of speed fluctuations during a cycle.

Q 7 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
During which of the following stroke, the flywheel stores energy so that it can supply the energy during the idle strokes?
(1) Suction stroke
(2) Expansion stroke
(3) Compression stroke
(4) Exhaust stroke
= 2
~ In a four-stroke engine only the expansion (power) stroke produces work. During this stroke the flywheel absorbs the surplus energy as kinetic energy, its speed rising slightly, and gives it back during the idle suction, compression and exhaust strokes, keeping the crankshaft speed nearly uniform. A multi-cylinder engine overlaps power strokes and so needs a lighter flywheel.

Q 8 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
To allow the fresh charge to enter the cylinder, the inlet valve is opened in advance of the T.D.C position. The inlet valve is opened in advance by ______.
(1) 10° to 30°
(2) 50° to 60°
(3) 30° to 70°
(4) 0° to 10°
= 1
~ In the actual valve timing of a four-stroke engine the inlet valve opens about 10°–30° of crank rotation before TDC, so that it is fully open when the suction stroke begins and the fresh charge can enter without restriction. It closes some degrees after BDC, using the inertia of the incoming charge to improve volumetric efficiency. The period when both valves are open near TDC is the valve overlap.

Q 9 | ice | Fuel Supply & Combustion
Which one of the following factors affect the ignition timing of an internal combustion engine?
(1) Type of fuel
(2) Flywheel
(3) Piston material
(4) Cylinder material
= 1
~ The spark must occur early enough for the flame to burn the charge near TDC, and the time needed depends on the fuel: its octane rating (knock resistance) and burning rate decide how much spark advance can be used. Engine speed, load and mixture strength also affect the timing, which is why distributors have centrifugal and vacuum advance. The flywheel and the piston or cylinder materials do not decide ignition timing.

Q 10 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
In a two-stroke engine, the process of clearing the cylinder after the expansion stroke is called ______.
(1) expansion
(2) scavenging
(3) compressing
(4) knocking
= 2
~ Scavenging is the sweeping out of the burnt gases from the cylinder of a two-stroke engine by the fresh charge or air entering through the transfer ports at the end of the expansion stroke. Common methods are cross-flow, loop (reverse-flow) and uniflow scavenging. Knocking is abnormal combustion, while expansion and compression are the normal working processes.

Q 11 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
What is the difference between indicated power and brake power of internal combustion engine?
(1) Total power
(2) Total available energy
(3) Total engine friction loss
(4) Total clearance
= 3
~ Indicated power is the power developed by the gases on the piston, and brake power is the useful power available at the crankshaft. Their difference, friction power = IP − BP, is the power lost in friction of pistons, rings and bearings, in pumping the charge and in driving accessories. The ratio BP/IP is the mechanical efficiency; friction power can be measured by the Morse or motoring test.

Q 12 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions
The lowest temperature at which lubricating oil changes from a liquid state to plastic or solid state is called ______.
(1) cloud point
(2) emulsification point
(3) flash point
(4) pour point
= 1
~ As an oil is cooled it reaches its cloud point when wax and other dissolved solids begin to separate and make it hazy, the start of its change from a liquid towards a plastic or solid state; the key takes this as the answer. On further cooling it reaches the pour point, the lowest temperature at which it will still flow, and the word "lowest" in the question fits the pour point better. The flash point concerns ignition of the oil's vapour. (Preliminary key; some sources differ.)

Q 13 | ice | Cooling & Lubrication
Which of the following is correct about cooling system of engines?
I. The cooling system is provided on an engine because high temperatures reduce strength of piston and cylinder liner.
II. The cooling system is provided on an engine because the overheated cylinder may lead to ignition lag in spark ignition engines.
(1) Only I
(2) Only II
(3) Both I and II
(4) Neither I nor II
= 1
~ Statement I is correct: at high temperatures the piston, cylinder liner and valves lose strength and the oil film breaks down, so the cooling system removes roughly a third of the fuel's heat. Statement II is wrong: an overheated cylinder causes pre-ignition and detonation (knocking) in spark ignition engines, not ignition lag, which is the short delay between the spark and a noticeable pressure rise.

Q 14 | ice | Fuel Supply & Combustion
Which of the following is correct regarding the diesel engines?
I. Diesel engines uses an injector.
II. There is no pre-ignition in diesel engines.
(1) Only I
(2) Only II
(3) Both I and II
(4) Neither I nor II
= 3
~ Both statements are correct. A diesel (CI) engine uses a fuel injection pump and an injector that sprays fuel into the hot compressed air, instead of a carburettor and spark plug. Since only air is compressed and fuel is admitted only at the end of the compression stroke, there is no charge in the cylinder that could ignite early, so pre-ignition does not occur.

Q 15 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions
Which of the following is correct about grease?
I. Grease is only used where there is a low bearing and shock loads.
II. Lubricating grease is a solid to semi solid dispersion of a thickening agent in liquid lubricant.
(1) Only I
(2) Only II
(3) Both I and II
(4) Neither I nor II
= 2
~ Statement II is the standard definition: a lubricating grease is a solid to semi-solid dispersion of a thickening agent, usually a metallic soap, in a liquid lubricant. Statement I is wrong: grease is used where bearing pressures are high, where shock loads occur, at low speeds and where oil cannot be retained or would leak away, as in wheel bearings and chassis joints.

Q 16 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions
Which of the following is correct about lubricating oils?
I. Fatty oils exhibit less oiliness than mineral oils of the same viscosity.
II. Multigrade oil are not required to change as per ambient temperature.
III. Multigrade oil has short warming up period.
(1) Only I and II
(2) Only II
(3) Only II and III
(4) Only I and III
= 3
~ Statement I is wrong: fatty (animal and vegetable) oils show greater oiliness, that is, stronger adherence to metal surfaces, than mineral oils of the same viscosity. Statements II and III are correct: a multigrade oil such as SAE 20W-40 meets both a low-temperature and a high-temperature grade, so it need not be changed with the season, and it flows freely when cold, giving a short warming-up period.

Q 17 | ice | Fuel Supply & Combustion
Which of the following is correct about the fuel injection system?
I. It controls the rate of fuel injection.
II. It filters the fuel.
III. It controls the quantity of fuel to be injected.
(1) Only I and II
(2) Only II and III
(3) All I, II and III
(4) Only I and III
= 3
~ A diesel fuel injection system must filter the fuel, meter the correct quantity of fuel for each cycle according to the load, time the injection, control the rate of injection, atomise the fuel finely and distribute it properly in the combustion chamber. Q 18 | ice | Fuel Supply & Combustion
Which of the following statement is correct regarding the effects of the air fuel mixtures in SI engine?
I. If the air fuel mixture is slightly richer, mean effective pressure reaches its maximum value.
II. The rate of fuel consumption is lowest for rich air fuel mixtures.
III. By decreasing the richness of air fuel mixture, the proportion of CO₂ will decrease while those of CO with certain traces of unburnt H₂ will increase.
(1) Only I
(2) Only II and III
(3) Only I and III
(4) Only II
= 1
~ Only I is correct: maximum power, and so maximum mean effective pressure, is obtained with a slightly rich mixture of about 12.5:1. II is wrong because the lowest specific fuel consumption is obtained with a slightly lean mixture. III is reversed: as the mixture is made less rich, more oxygen is available, so the proportion of CO₂ increases while CO and unburnt H₂ decrease.

Q 19 | ice | Fuel Supply & Combustion
Match the following statements in context of Spark Ignition engine.
Effect | Cause
I. Results in lowering of efficiency | 1. Running on the weakest air fuel mixture
II. Results in high efficiency | 2. Running on the richest air fuel mixture
III. Results in complete burning of the fuel | 3. During the cruising the air fuel mixture is approximately 16.5: 1 so that
(1) I-1, II-2, III-3
(2) I-2, II-1, III-3
(3) I-3, II-2, III-1
(4) I-2, II-3, III-1
= 2
~ With the richest mixture part of the fuel cannot find oxygen and leaves as CO and unburnt hydrocarbons, so efficiency falls: I-2. The weakest mixture that still burns gives the best economy and the highest efficiency: II-1. In the cruising range the carburettor supplies a slightly lean mixture of about 16.5:1 so that the excess air ensures complete burning of the fuel: III-3. Hence I-2, II-1, III-3.

Q 20 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions
Match the following fuel standard to its specialty.
Standard | Specialty
I. Iso-octane | 1. Very prone to knock
II. Normal heptane | 2. Measure of ignition quality of diesel
III. Cetane number | 3. Very high resistance to knock
| 4. Anti knock performance under severe operating conditions

(2) I-3, II-1, III-2
(3) I-1, II-3, III-4
(4) I-2, II-4, III-1

~ Octane numbers are fixed with two reference fuels: iso-octane (2,2,4-trimethylpentane), which has very high resistance to knock and is rated 100, and normal heptane, which is very prone to knock and is rated 0. Q 21 | thm | Thermodynamics
Which of the following is correctly matched pair regarding Clausius Inequality?
(1) δQ/T > 0 : Reversible cycle
(2) δQ/T = 0 : Irreversible cycle
(3) δQ/T < 0 : Irreversible cycle
(4) ΔS < 0 : Isolated system
= 3
~ The Clausius inequality states that for any cycle ∮δQ/T ≤ 0 (the cyclic integral sign is not printed in the options). The equality holds for a reversible cycle, ∮δQ/T < 0 for an irreversible cycle, and ∮δQ/T > 0 is impossible. For an isolated system the entropy change is ΔS ≥ 0 (principle of increase of entropy), so ΔS < 0 cannot occur. Only "δQ/T < 0 : Irreversible cycle" is correctly matched.

Q 22 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
For same compression ratio and same heat input, which of the following order of efficiency is correct?
(1) η_duel > η_otto > η_diesel
(2) η_diesel > η_duel > η_otto
(3) η_duel > η_diesel > η_otto
(4) η_otto > η_duel > η_diesel
= 4
~ For the same compression ratio and heat input, the Otto cycle adds all its heat at constant volume at TDC, so it rejects the least heat and is the most efficient; the Diesel cycle adds heat at constant pressure during part of the expansion and is the least efficient; the dual cycle, with part of each, lies in between. So η_Otto > η_Dual > η_Diesel ("duel" is printed for dual). For the same maximum pressure and temperature the order is reversed.

Q 23 | thm | Thermodynamics
Arrange the following in an increasing order of value of n in PVⁿ = C.
I. Constant pressure process
II. Reversible adiabatic process
III. Constant volume process
IV. Isothermal process
(1) I, II, III, IV
(2) II, I, III, IV
(3) II, III, I, IV
(4) I, IV, II, III
= 4
~ In the polytropic relation PVⁿ = C, n = 0 for a constant pressure process (P = C), n = 1 for an isothermal process of an ideal gas (PV = C), n = γ (about 1.4 for air) for a reversible adiabatic process, and n = ∞ for a constant volume process. The increasing order is therefore constant pressure, isothermal, reversible adiabatic, constant volume: I, IV, II, III.

Q 24 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
For the four stroke otto engines, arrange the following in the correct order (first to last).
I. The piston moves T.D.C. to B.D.C. and work is obtained after fuel is ignited.
II. The piston moves B.D.C. to T.D.C. and compresses the air fuel mixture.
III. The piston moves T.D.C. to B.D.C. and the inlet valve opens and air fuel mixture is sucked in.
IV. The piston moves B.D.C. to T.D.C. and gases are made to escape through exhaust value.
(1) I, III, IV, II
(2) III, II, I, IV
(3) IV, II, I, III
(4) III, I, IV, II
= 2
~ A four-stroke Otto (petrol) engine completes its cycle in two crankshaft revolutions: suction, with the piston moving from TDC to BDC and the charge drawn in (III); compression from BDC to TDC (II); power or expansion after the spark ignites the charge, TDC to BDC (I); and exhaust, BDC to TDC, pushing the burnt gases out through the exhaust valve (IV; "value" is printed for valve). Order: III, II, I, IV.

Q 25 | ice | Fuel Supply & Combustion
For a simple or elementary carburettor, arrange the following in a correct order (first to last).
I. The velocity of air increases as it passes through the constriction at the venturi.
II. Pressure decreases due to conversion of a portion of pressure head into kinetic energy.
III. The piston moves down and the suction is produced in the cylinder and the induction manifold.
IV. Due to pressure difference the jet issues fuel oil into air stream which vapourise forming air fuel mixture.
V. The air flows through the carburettor.
(1) III, V, I, II, IV
(2) II, V, III, I, IV
(3) III, II, I, IV, V
(4) II, IV, III, V, I
= 1
~ In a simple carburettor the piston's suction stroke creates a depression in the cylinder and induction manifold (III), so air flows through the carburettor (V). Its velocity rises at the venturi throat (I), where the pressure falls as part of the pressure head becomes kinetic energy (II). The pressure difference between the float chamber and the throat makes the jet discharge fuel, which vaporises in the air stream to form the mixture (IV). Order: III, V, I, II, IV.

Q 26 | chs | Chassis & Body
Which one of the following is an advantage of chassis with engine at front and drive is given to the wheels from rear of a vehicle?
(1) Increased efficiency of cooling system
(2) Inadequate space behind the rear seat for the luggage
(3) Elimination of propeller shaft
(4) The clutch, gear box and differential are usually made as one unit results in cost reduction
= 1
~ In the conventional layout, with the engine at the front driving the rear wheels, the radiator faces the oncoming air, so cooling is more efficient; weight is also well distributed and the front wheels only steer. Elimination of the propeller shaft and a combined clutch, gearbox and differential unit are advantages of front-wheel-drive or rear-engine layouts, and inadequate luggage space is a drawback, not an advantage.

Q 27 | chs | Chassis & Body
Which one of the following is a defect that may be found in chassis body of an automobile?
(1) Build up edges
(2) Broken welds
(3) Heat affected zone
(4) Toe out
= 2
~ The chassis frame and body are inspected for cracks, bent or twisted members, loose rivets and broken welds, which develop from fatigue, overloading, accidents and road shocks. A built-up edge forms on a cutting tool during machining, the heat-affected zone is the metallurgically altered region beside any weld, and toe-out is a wheel-alignment setting of the front wheels, not a chassis defect.

Q 28 | chs | Chassis & Body
For an automotive body, the magnitude of the drag force depends on ______.
(1) shear strength of automotive body
(2) magnitude of gravity
(3) velocity of automotive body
(4) strength of automotive body
= 3
~ The aerodynamic drag on a vehicle is F = ½ ρ C_d A V², where ρ is the air density, C_d the drag coefficient set by the body shape, A the frontal area and V the speed relative to the air. Drag therefore grows with the square of the velocity, and the power needed to overcome it with the cube. It does not depend on the strength of the body or on gravity.

Q 29 | chs | Chassis & Body
Which one of the following load acts on a chassis when a vehicle crosses a broken patch of road?
(1) Inertia load
(2) Short duration load
(3) Overload
(4) Impact load
= 2
~ Automobile engineering textbooks classify the loads on a chassis frame as static loads (weight of body, passengers and cargo), short-duration loads (while crossing a broken patch of road), momentary loads (while taking a curve), impact loads (from collisions), inertia loads (on sudden acceleration or braking) and overloads (beyond the design capacity). Crossing a broken patch is thus a short-duration load.

Q 30 | chs | Steering & Suspension
In which of the following the carriage unit of a car rolls about a transverse axis?
(1) Rolling
(2) Yawing
(3) Pitching
(4) Bending
= 3
~ The sprung body (carriage unit) of a car can rotate about three axes. Rolling is rotation about the longitudinal axis, as on cornering; pitching is rotation about the transverse axis, the nose diving under braking or rising under acceleration and over bumps; and yawing is rotation about the vertical axis. So rotation about a transverse axis is pitching; bending is a frame deformation, not a body motion.

Q 31 | chs | Steering & Suspension
Which one of the following is a function of the shackle in the context of a leaf spring?
(1) It helps in accommodating the change in the length of the spring.
(2) It is used to keep leaf spring in position on the axle.
(3) It is used to provide progressive stiffness against increasing load.
(4) It provides lubrication between the leaf spring.
= 1
~ A semi-elliptic leaf spring straightens and becomes longer as it deflects under load. Its front eye is pinned to a fixed bracket on the frame, while the rear eye is connected through a swinging shackle that allows this change in length. Q 32 | chs | Clutch & Gearbox
Which one of the following is a formula of the effective mean radius of a clutch for uniform pressure condition? (Where r₁ is inner radius and r₂ is outer radius).
(1) (1/3)[(r₂³ − r₁³)/(r₂² − r₁²)]
(2) (2/3)[(r₂³ − r₁³)/(r₂² − r₁²)]
(3) (r₂ + r₁)/2
(4) (2/3)[(r₁³ − r₂³)/(r₂² − r₁²)]
= 2
~ With uniform pressure p on the annular friction face, the axial load is W = πp(r₂² − r₁²) and the friction torque is T = (2/3)πμp(r₂³ − r₁³), so T = μWR with mean radius R = (2/3)(r₂³ − r₁³)/(r₂² − r₁²). With the uniform-wear assumption, used for worn clutches, the mean radius is (r₁ + r₂)/2 (option 3), giving a slightly smaller and safer torque estimate. Option 4 gives a negative value.

Q 33 | chs | Clutch & Gearbox
In gear box, the device that is used to lock collar into the gear is called ______.
(1) reverse gear
(2) dog clutch
(3) electromagnetic clutch
(4) diaphragm clutch
= 2
~ In constant-mesh and synchromesh gearboxes the gears turn freely on the main shaft, and a toothed sliding collar splined to the shaft, moved by the selector fork, engages dog teeth on the side of the chosen gear to lock it to the shaft. This arrangement is the dog clutch. Diaphragm and electromagnetic clutches are main clutches between engine and gearbox, and reverse gear is only a gear ratio.

Q 34 | chs | Drive Line, Axles & Differential
The terms used for a combination of transmission and rear axles as a unit of a vehicle is ______.
(1) live axle
(2) tandem axle
(3) split axle
(4) trans axle
= 4
~ A transaxle combines the gearbox (transmission) with the final drive and differential of the driving axle in a single housing. It is used in rear-engine rear-wheel-drive and front-engine front-wheel-drive vehicles and does away with a long propeller shaft. A live axle is one that transmits drive to the wheels, and a tandem axle is a pair of closely spaced axles used on heavy vehicles.

Q 35 | chs | Wheels & Tyres
If vehicle is taking a turn, what is the ratio of side force sustained to slip angle called?
(1) Camber force
(2) Cornering power
(3) Frictional power
(4) Camber angle
= 2
~ When a vehicle turns, the tyre deforms and runs at a slip angle to the plane of the wheel, developing a side or cornering force. The ratio of this side force to the slip angle, in newtons per degree, is the cornering power (cornering stiffness) of the tyre; it depends on inflation pressure, load, tyre construction and rim width. Camber force arises from the tilt of the wheel.

Q 36 | chs | Drive Line, Axles & Differential
Which of the following statement is correct about front axles of a vehicle?
I. The front wheels are mounted on stub axles which are connected to the front axle by king pin.
II. When brakes are provided at the front wheels the front axle withstand bending stresses only.
(1) Only I
(2) Only II
(3) Both I and II
(4) Neither I nor II
= 1
~ Statement I is correct: the front wheels run on stub axles pivoted to the ends of the axle beam by king pins, so that they can be steered. Q 37 | chs | Steering & Suspension
Which of the following is correct regarding steering mechanism of a vehicle?
I. In order to avoid skidding, the two front wheels instantaneous centre should not coincide with the two rear wheels instantaneous centre.
II. The fundamental equation for correct steering is cot φ − cot θ = c/b.
Where, c = distance between the pivots of front axle.
b = wheel base
θ = inner wheel turning angle
φ = outer wheel turning angle
(1) Only I
(2) Only II
(3) Both I and II
(4) Neither I nor II

Q 38 | chs | Drive Line, Axles & Differential
Which of the following statement is correct regarding transmission system of automobiles?
I. The main advantage of an overdrive is prolonged engine life since the engine turns slower.
II. A slip joint is attached to the driven yoke in order to increase or decrease the propeller shaft.
(1) Only I
(2) Only II
(3) Both I and II
(4) Neither I nor II
= 3
~ Both statements are correct. An overdrive makes the propeller shaft turn faster than the engine, so at cruising speed the engine runs slower, reducing wear and fuel consumption and prolonging engine life. A slip (sliding) joint, with splines between the shaft and a universal-joint yoke, lets the effective length of the propeller shaft increase or decrease as the rear axle moves up and down (statement II omits the word "length").

Q 39 | chs | Drive Line, Axles & Differential
Which of the following statement is correct regarding rear axles of a vehicle?
I. The Banjo axle arrangement is a central housing that contains the final drive and it is fitted with a tube on each side to carry the half axles and bearings.
II. The main advantage of three-quarter floating rear axle over the half floating axle is that the major part of the load is taken by the axle and not by the axle casing.
III. A full floating rear axle is not supported by bearing at either end, is very strong and used for heavy duty vehicles.
(1) Only II
(2) I and II
(3) Only III
(4) II and III
= 3
~ Only III is correct. Statement I describes the Salisbury (carrier) type; a banjo casing is a one-piece housing with a large central opening, shaped like a banjo, into which a separate final-drive carrier is bolted. Statement II is reversed: in a three-quarter floating axle the hub bearing sits on the axle casing, so most of the load is taken by the casing, not the shaft. In a full-floating axle the hub runs on two bearings on the casing, so the shaft itself needs no bearing and only transmits torque; it suits heavy vehicles.

Q 40 | chs | Drive Line, Axles & Differential
Which of the following is correct regarding causes of axle failure of a vehicle?
I. Lubricant is not maintained at the required level.
II. The vehicle is over loaded.
III. Using of lubricant of correct grade.
(1) Only I
(2) II and III
(3) I and II
(4) Only III
= 3
~ Axle failures commonly result from a low lubricant level, which leads to wear, overheating and seizure of bearings and gears, and from overloading the vehicle, which overstresses the shafts and casing. Q 131 | flu | Fluid Mechanics
A fluid in which shear stress is more than the yield value and shear stress is proportional to the rate of shear strain is called ______.
(1) Newtonian fluid
(2) Ideal fluid
(3) Ideal plastic fluid
(4) Real fluid
= 3
~ An ideal plastic (Bingham plastic) fluid does not flow until the shear stress exceeds a yield value; beyond it the shear stress increases linearly with the rate of shear strain, τ = τy + μ(du/dy). A Newtonian fluid has no yield stress (τ = μ du/dy), an ideal fluid has no viscosity, and a real fluid is any fluid that has viscosity.

Q 132 | flu | Fluid Mechanics

(1) Mass
(2) Kinematic viscosity
(3) Specific gravity
(4) Specific volume
= 2
~ Kinematic viscosity ν is the ratio of dynamic viscosity μ to density ρ, ν = μ/ρ. Its SI unit is m²/s and its CGS unit is the stoke (1 stoke = 1 cm²/s = 10⁻⁴ m²/s). Q 133 | flu | Hydraulic Machines & Pneumatics

(1) Volumetric efficiency
(2) Mechanical advantage
(3) Rankine efficiency
(4) Effort
= 2
~ In a hydraulic press a small effort F on the plunger of area a creates a pressure that acts on the large ram of area A, lifting a weight W = F × A/a. Q 134 | flu | Hydraulic Machines & Pneumatics
The ratio of power given by water to the runner of a turbine to the power supplied by the water at the inlet of the turbine is called ______.
(1) Overall efficiency
(2) Mechanical efficiency
(3) Hydraulic efficiency
(4) Volumetric efficiency
= 3
~ Hydraulic efficiency of a turbine is the ratio of the power given by the water to the runner (runner power) to the power supplied by the water at the inlet (water power, ρgQH). Mechanical efficiency is shaft power divided by runner power, overall efficiency is shaft power divided by water power (the product of the two), and volumetric efficiency compares the water acting on the runner with that supplied.

Q 135 | flu | Hydraulic Machines & Pneumatics

= 4

Q 136 | flu | Fluid Mechanics
