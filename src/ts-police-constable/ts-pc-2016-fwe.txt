Q 1 | eng | Vocabulary
What is the synonym of 'amenable' in 'Both parties were amenable to a peaceful settlement of the land dispute'.?
(1) Doubtful
(2) Unwilling
(3) Responsive
(4) Disagreeable
= 3
~ "Amenable" means open and responsive to a suggestion, that is, willing to accept it or be persuaded. Here both parties were willing to accept a peaceful settlement, so "responsive" is the synonym. "Unwilling", "doubtful" and "disagreeable" all express reluctance or opposition, which is nearly the opposite of "amenable".

Q 2 | eng | Vocabulary
What is the synonym of 'antidote' in 'Jogging is considered an antidote to mental stress'.?
(1) Poisonous
(2) Remedy
(3) Equivalent
(4) Dangerous
= 2
~ An "antidote" is literally a medicine that counteracts a poison; figuratively, it is anything that relieves or counteracts something harmful. Jogging relieves mental stress, so "remedy" is the synonym. "Poisonous" and "dangerous" describe the harmful thing itself, and "equivalent" means equal in value or meaning.

Q 3 | eng | Vocabulary
What is the antonym of 'transperant' in 'The window-glass is transperant'?
(1) Opaque
(2) Translucent
(3) Susceptible
(4) Fragile
= 1
~ "Transparent" (misspelt "transperant" in the booklet) describes something that lets light pass through completely, so that objects behind it are seen clearly. Its opposite is "opaque", which lets no light through. "Translucent" lets light pass but blurs the view, so it lies between the two, while "susceptible" (easily affected) and "fragile" (easily broken) are unrelated.

Q 4 | eng | Vocabulary
What is the antonym of 'unanimous' in 'It was a unanimous decision'?
(1) United
(2) Partial
(3) Compatible
(4) Harmonious
= 2
~ "Unanimous" means agreed by everyone, with no one dissenting. Its strict antonyms are words such as "divided", "split" or "dissenting", none of which is offered. "Partial" (agreed only in part, or by only some) is the only option that stands against complete agreement, so it is the best choice. "United", "compatible" and "harmonious" all suggest agreement, so they are close to "unanimous" in meaning rather than opposite.

Q 5 | eng | Grammar
His mother was ______ American though his father was ______ European.
(1) a, a
(2) a, an
(3) an, a
(4) an, an
= 3
~ The choice between "a" and "an" depends on the first sound of the next word, not its first letter. "American" begins with a vowel sound, so it takes "an". "European" is spelt with a vowel but begins with the consonant sound /j/ ("yoo-ro-pean"), so it takes "a", as in "a university" or "a one-day match". Hence "an American" and "a European".

Q 6 | eng | Grammar
The coordinator was asked to attend ______ the issue and resolve it immediately.
(1) by
(2) in
(3) to
(4) No preposition required
= 3
~ The phrasal verb "attend to" means to deal with or take care of a matter, so the coordinator was asked to attend to the issue and resolve it. "Attend" without a preposition means to be present at something (attend a meeting or a class), which does not fit "the issue", and "attend by" or "attend in" are not used in this sense.

Q 7 | eng | Grammar
Either of the roads ______ to Vasantha Nagar.
(1) lead
(2) leads
(3) have led
(4) are led
= 2
~ "Either of" refers to one of two things taken separately, so it is singular and takes a singular verb: "Either of the roads leads to Vasantha Nagar." The plural noun "roads" after "of" does not decide the verb. "Lead", "have led" and "are led" are plural (and the last one passive) forms that do not agree with the singular subject.

Q 8 | eng | Grammar
Please don't disturb me, I ______ the coins now.
(1) have been counting
(2) count
(3) was counting
(4) am counting
= 4
~ "Please don't disturb me" and "now" show an action going on at the moment of speaking, which needs the present continuous tense: "I am counting the coins now." "Was counting" is past, the simple present "count" is used for habits and general facts, and "have been counting" stresses how long an action has lasted, which the sentence does not express.

Q 9 | eng | Grammar
Rishi ______ his engineering course by the end of 2017.
(1) will completed
(2) complete
(3) will have completed
(4) was completed
= 3
~ "By the end of 2017" fixes a point in the future by which the action will be finished, which calls for the future perfect tense (will have + past participle): "Rishi will have completed his engineering course by the end of 2017." "Will completed" is ungrammatical, "complete" does not agree with the singular subject, and "was completed" is a past passive.

Q 10 | eng | Vocabulary
Choose the correctly spelt word from the options given below.
(1) occasional
(2) occassional
(3) ocasional
(4) occational
= 1
~ The correct spelling is "occasional", formed from "occasion": double "c", single "s" and the ending "-sion". The other options wrongly double the "s" (occassional), drop one "c" (ocasional) or use "-tion" instead of "-sion" (occational). A simple memory aid is "two c's, one s".

Q 11 | eng | Vocabulary
What is the meaning of underlined idiom in "We would be glad to put you up for a few days if you would like to stay"?
(1) tolerate someone
(2) build something
(3) provide accommodation
(4) contribute something
= 3
~ The underlined idiom is "put you up". To "put someone up" means to give someone a place to stay, usually in one's own home, for a short time. The speaker offers to let the listener stay for a few days, so it means "provide accommodation". "Put up with" someone means to tolerate them, and to "put up" a building means to build it; these are the traps.

Q 12 | eng | Vocabulary
What is the meaning of the underlined phrase in 'How did you wolf down that pizza so quickly'?
(1) To eat gently
(2) To eat hungrily
(3) To eat slowly
(4) To swallow slowly
= 2
~ The underlined phrasal verb is "wolf down", which means to eat something very quickly and greedily, like a hungry wolf. The word "quickly" in the sentence supports this, so "to eat hungrily" is the meaning. Eating gently or slowly and swallowing slowly are the opposite of wolfing food down.

Q 13 | eng | Comprehension
Choose the correct order of the following jumbled sentences into a meaningful paragraph.
(a) Wages will seldom rise in proportion to the rising prices
(b) The problem of food is intimately connect with population, the market is governed by demand and supply
(c) Without enough food, such people lack health, strength of efficiency and they fall an easy prey to all sorts of diseases
(d) If too many people demand goods to go round, prices will rise and poor classes will starve
(1) 1-(c), 2-(d), 3-(a), 4-(b)
(2) 1-(d), 2-(c), 3-(b), 4-(a)
(3) 1-(a), 2-(c), 3-(d), 4-(b)
(4) 1-(b), 2-(d), 3-(a), 4-(c)
= 4
~ Sentence (b) introduces the topic: food is linked with population, and the market runs on demand and supply. (d) applies this: when too many people demand too few goods, prices rise and the poor starve. (a) explains why the poor suffer, since wages seldom rise as fast as prices. (c) gives the result: "such people", the starving poor, lose health and fall prey to disease. Hence b, d, a, c.

Q 14 | eng | Comprehension
Choose the correct order of the following jumbled sentences to make it a meaningful paragraph.
(a) A dove, perched on a nearby tree, saw the ant's danger and dropped a leaf into the water
(b) Once, an ant who had come to drink at a stream fell into the water and was carried away by the swift current
(c) He was about to fire when the ant, who was nearby, crawled forward and bit him sharply on the ankle
(d) Sometime after this, a hunter, creeping through the bushes, saw the dove asleep, and took a careful aim with his gun
(1) 1-(d), 2-(c), 3-(b), 4-(a)
(2) 1-(c), 2-(d), 3-(a), 4-(b)
(3) 1-(b), 2-(a), 3-(d), 4-(c)
(4) 1-(a), 2-(c), 3-(d), 4-(b)
= 3
~ Sentence (b) opens the story with "Once": an ant falls into a stream. (a) follows, as a dove sees "the ant's danger" and drops a leaf to save it. (d) begins "Sometime after this", when a hunter takes aim at the sleeping dove, and (c) ends the story with the ant biting the hunter just as he is about to fire, returning the favour. Hence b, a, d, c.

Q 15 | eng | Grammar
Fill in the blank:
Manoj played cricket for two days, ______ he?
(1) did
(2) didn't
(3) wasn't
(4) wouldn't
= 2
~ A question tag uses the auxiliary of the main clause with the opposite polarity. "Manoj played" is a positive statement in the simple past with no auxiliary, so the tag takes "did" in the negative form: "didn't he?" "Did" would keep the same (positive) polarity, while "wasn't" and "wouldn't" do not match the verb or its tense.

Q 16 | eng | Grammar
Fill in the blank:
Johnson has not prepared for tomorrow's exam, ______ he?
(1) isn't
(2) hasn't
(3) has
(4) does
= 3
~ The statement "Johnson has not prepared ..." is negative and uses the auxiliary "has", so the question tag repeats "has" in the positive form: "has he?" A negative statement always takes a positive tag. "Hasn't" would repeat the negative, and "isn't" and "does" use an auxiliary that is not in the main clause.

Q 21 | soc | Ethics & Values
When you watch India Vs Pakistan Cricket Match Finals, your younger brother asks you to clear his doubts in Mathematics. How do you respond?
(1) You clear his doubts patiently
(2) You shout at him for not asking before
(3) You switch off TV and go away
(4) You feel irritated
= 1
~ The question tests responsibility, empathy and the ability to put duty before personal pleasure. A child's studies matter more than a cricket match, and a younger brother who comes for help should be encouraged, not scolded. Clearing his doubts patiently shows care and self-control. Shouting at him, feeling irritated, or switching off the TV and walking away all discourage him and leave his doubts unsolved.

Q 22 | soc | Ethics & Values
When you see a child facing a problem in understanding an important concept for a long time , you advise him to ______
(1) stop reading the subject
(2) leave that subject forever
(3) take a break and read it once again
(4) by heart the concept
= 3
~ A child who has struggled with an important concept for a long time is usually tired and frustrated. Advising a short break and then a fresh attempt lets the mind relax, helps the concept become clearer and keeps the child motivated. Stopping or giving up the subject only runs away from the problem, and learning by heart without understanding (rote learning) does not remove the difficulty.

Q 23 | soc | Ethics & Values
It somebody tries to hurt you intentionally, you should
(1) Retort
(2) Cry
(3) Complain to police
(4) Ignore
= 3
~ If someone deliberately tries to hurt you, the right course is to use the law rather than take revenge, so you should complain to the police. Retorting can escalate the conflict, crying does not help, and ignoring it may encourage the offender. Voluntarily causing hurt was an offence under Sections 321 and 323 of the IPC when this exam was held (23 October 2016); since 1 July 2024 it falls under Section 115 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita.

Q 24 | soc | Ethics & Values
If your friend tells you about his personal problems thinking that you are trustworthy person, you should ______
(1) Share it with everyone
(2) Feel happy about it
(3) Maintain confidentiality
(4) Look at him with contempt
= 3
~ A friend who shares personal problems trusts you to keep them private. Maintaining confidentiality honours that trust and is a mark of integrity. Telling everyone is a breach of trust, looking at the friend with contempt is unkind, and merely feeling happy about being trusted does nothing to help him. Respecting confidentiality is also a core value for a police officer, who handles sensitive information every day.

Q 25 | soc | Ethics & Values
Do you produce a wrong evidence to your parents regarding your performance in Exams?
(1) I do
(2) No
(3) Sometimes
(4) Depending upon the situation
= 2
~ Showing false evidence of one's exam performance to parents is dishonest. Honesty means telling the truth even when the result is disappointing, so the right answer is "No". Answers such as "Sometimes" or "Depending upon the situation" accept lying in some cases, which shows a lack of integrity, a quality expected above all of a police constable who must give truthful evidence.

Q 26 | soc | Gender & Social Sensitivity
According to you, ‘Gender Equality” means ________
(1) Having women reservations
(2) Women are superior to men
(3) Men are superior to women
(4) Having equal opportunities to both men and women
= 4
~ Gender equality means that women and men enjoy the same rights, responsibilities and opportunities, so that neither sex is treated as superior to the other; the Constitution reflects this in Articles 14, 15 and 16. Reservation for women is only one affirmative tool (allowed by Article 15(3)) to reach equality, not its meaning, and options (2) and (3) describe inequality.

Q 27 | soc | Social Issues & Welfare
What does it mean by “Weaker Sections”?
(1) Physically weak
(2) Financially weak
(3) Handicapped people
(4) People who can’t fight for their rights
= 4
~ "Weaker sections" are groups that, because of social, economic, educational or physical disadvantage, cannot protect or assert their own rights and interests, which is why Article 46 directs the State to promote their educational and economic interests and shield them from social injustice and exploitation. Physical weakness, poverty or disability alone describe only one kind of weakness, so the broadest answer is (4).

Q 29 | soc | Ethics & Values
Do you return the money to the owner, when it fell down from his/her pocket?
(1) Yes, always
(2) No, I can’t
(3) Sometimes
(4) Most of the times
= 1
~ Money that falls from someone's pocket belongs to that person, and keeping it would be dishonest; integrity means returning it every time, whether or not anyone is watching. "Sometimes" or "most of the times" makes honesty conditional, which is not acceptable in a person who aspires to a uniformed service.

Q 30 | soc | Ethics & Values
When two persons are quarrelling with each other, you will _________
(1) Run away from there
(2) Join one side
(3) Pacify two persons
(4) Call police
= 3
~ A responsible bystander tries to calm both persons down and settle the dispute peacefully, without taking sides or walking away. Running away shows indifference, joining one side escalates the quarrel, and an ordinary argument does not need the police unless it turns violent, so pacifying both is the best response.

Q 31 | ari | Numbers & Simplification
(1 − 1/2)(1 − 1/3)(1 − 1/4)....(1 − 1/n) =
(1) 1/[n(n + 1)]
(2) (n + 1)/n
(3) 2/n
(4) 1/n
= 4
~ Each bracket simplifies to (k − 1)/k, so the product is (1/2)(2/3)(3/4)....((n − 1)/n). Every numerator cancels the denominator of the previous factor (telescoping), leaving only the first numerator 1 over the last denominator n, so the value is 1/n. For example, with n = 4 the product is 1/2 × 2/3 × 3/4 = 1/4.

Q 32 | ari | Numbers & Simplification
The ratio of two numbers is 3:5 and their H.C.F is 5. Then, their L.C.M is
(1) 45
(2) 35
(3) 150
(4) 75
= 4
~ Numbers in the ratio 3:5 with H.C.F. 5 are 3 × 5 = 15 and 5 × 5 = 25 (3 and 5 are co-prime). Their L.C.M. is 5 × 3 × 5 = 75; check: H.C.F. × L.C.M. = 5 × 75 = 375 = 15 × 25, the product of the two numbers.

Q 33 | ari | Numbers & Simplification
The smallest number which when subtracted by 11 is divisible by 16, 18, 21, 24 and 28 is
(1) 1109
(2) 1119
(3) 1019
(4) 1214
= 3
~ After 11 is subtracted, the number must be a common multiple of 16, 18, 21, 24 and 28; the least such is their L.C.M. = 2⁴ × 3² × 7 = 1008. So the smallest required number is 1008 + 11 = 1019, and 1019 − 11 = 1008 is divisible by all five divisors.

Q 34 | ari | Ratio, Average & Ages
The ages of A and B are in the ratio 3:5. Ten years later, the ratio of their ages will be 31:45. Their present ages are (in years)
(1) 21, 35
(2) 24, 40
(3) 18, 30
(4) 27, 45
= 1
~ Let the present ages be 3x and 5x. Then (3x + 10)/(5x + 10) = 31/45, so 135x + 450 = 155x + 310, giving 20x = 140 and x = 7. The ages are 21 and 35 years; check: ten years later they are 31 and 45.

Q 35 | ari | Ratio, Average & Ages
Silver and copper are mixed in the ratios 2:5 and 4:7 to prepare two alloys A and B. Equal quantities of these alloys A and B are mixed to prepare a third alloy C. The ratio of silver and copper in C is
(1) 3:5
(2) 25:52
(3) 25:49
(4) 15:27
= 2
~ In one unit of alloy A, silver is 2/7 and copper 5/7; in one unit of B, silver is 4/11 and copper 7/11. Taking one unit of each, silver = 2/7 + 4/11 = 50/77 and copper = 5/7 + 7/11 = 104/77, so silver : copper = 50 : 104 = 25 : 52.

Q 36 | ari | Percentage, Profit & Loss
A shop keeper reduced the price of sugar by 10%. A person can buy 2 kg more sugar for Rs.900. The difference between the original price and the reduced price, per kg, is (in rupees)
(1) 5
(2) 6
(3) 4
(4) 3
= 1
~ A 10% price cut saves 10% of Rs. 900 = Rs. 90, which buys the extra 2 kg, so the reduced price is 90/2 = Rs. 45 per kg. The original price is 45/0.9 = Rs. 50 per kg, and the difference is Rs. 5; check: Rs. 900 buys 18 kg at Rs. 50 and 20 kg at Rs. 45.

Q 37 | ari | Percentage, Profit & Loss
In an election between two candidates 80% of the enrolled voters voted. Of these votes, 5% votes were declared invalid. The winning candidate got 32490 votes which is 60% of the valid votes. The total number of voters enrolled in the election is
(1) 75850
(2) 64750
(3) 82500
(4) 71250
= 4
~ Valid votes = 32490/0.60 = 54150. These are 95% of the votes polled, so votes polled = 54150/0.95 = 57000, and these are 80% of the enrolled voters, so enrolled voters = 57000/0.80 = 71250.

Q 38 | ari | Ratio, Average & Ages
A person A started a business with investment of Rs.3,00,000. A second person B joined A afterwards with contribution of Rs.4,00,000. If the profit at the end of the year is shared equally by A and B, after how many months did B join A?
(1) 1
(2) 2
(3) 3
(4) 4
= 3
~ Profit is shared in the ratio of capital × time. Equal shares need 3,00,000 × 12 = 4,00,000 × t, so B's money was in the business for t = 9 months. B therefore joined 12 − 9 = 3 months after A started.

Q 39 | ari | Ratio, Average & Ages
Three individuals A, B, C start a company and contribute a total of Rs.P towards its running. A contributes Rs.50,000 more than B and B contributes Rs.50,000 more than C. If the contributions of A, B and C are in the ratio 8:7:6, then P (in rupees) is
(1) 10,50,000
(2) 12,00,000
(3) 13,50,000
(4) 14,00,000
= 1
~ In the ratio 8:7:6, A exceeds B by one part and B exceeds C by one part, so one part = Rs. 50,000. The total has 8 + 7 + 6 = 21 parts, so P = 21 × 50,000 = Rs. 10,50,000 (A = 4,00,000, B = 3,50,000, C = 3,00,000).

Q 40 | ari | Ratio, Average & Ages
The average of four consecutive numbers A, B, C, D is 55.5. Then, the product BC =
(1) 2580
(2) 3060
(3) 3080

Q 170 | eco | Poverty, Employment & Welfare
Which one of the following statements is correct with regard to population explosion?
(1) Both birth and death rates are high.
(2) Both birth and death rates are low.
(3) Birth rate will be high and death rate decreases very fast.
(4) Birth rate will be low and death rate increases moderately.
= 3
~ A population explosion occurs in the second stage of the demographic transition: better health care, sanitation and food supply make the death rate fall very fast while the birth rate stays high. The widening gap between births and deaths makes the population grow rapidly, as India saw after 1951. When both rates are high (first stage) or both are low (last stage), population grows slowly.

Q 172 | eco | Poverty, Employment & Welfare

C) A person whose income or expenditure is so meagre that he lives below the subsistence level is called as ‘relative poverty’.

(2) B and C only
(3) C and A only

Q 173 | eco | Growth & Planning

Q 174 | geo | Environment & Ecology
Which of the following are the spheres of environment?
A) Atmosphere
B) Biosphere
C) Hydrosphere
D) Lithosphere
(1) A and B only
(2) A, B and C only
(3) A, B and D only
(4) A, B, C and D
= 4
~ The environment is studied as four interacting spheres: the atmosphere (the envelope of air), the hydrosphere (water in oceans, rivers, lakes, ice and groundwater), the lithosphere (the solid crust of rocks and soil) and the biosphere (the zone where the other three meet and support life).
