Q 1 | eng | Vocabulary
Choose the incorrectly spelt word from the following set of words :
(1) acquaintance
(2) maneouvre
(3) erroneously
(4) demurrage
= 2
~ The correct spelling is "manoeuvre" (British) or "maneuver" (American); "maneouvre" puts the o and e in the wrong order. "Acquaintance", "erroneously" and "demurrage" (a charge for keeping a ship, wagon or container beyond the agreed time) are all spelt correctly.

Q 2 | eng | Vocabulary
In the following question, four words are given, of which two words are nearly the same or opposite in meaning. Find the two words that are similar or opposite in meaning and indicate the number of the correct letter combination provided in the options.
A. Possessive
B. Dreadful
C. Secretive
D. Repugnant
(1) B-C
(2) B-D
(3) A-B
(4) A-C
= 2
~ "Dreadful" (very bad, causing fear or disgust) and "repugnant" (extremely distasteful, offensive) are nearly the same in meaning, so the pair is B-D. "Possessive" (wanting to own or control) and "secretive" (inclined to hide things) are neither synonyms nor antonyms of each other or of the other two words.

Q 3 | eng | Comprehension
Choose the correct rearrangement of the following jumbled sentences to make it a meaningful paragraph.
A. Music is perhaps the most popular of all the arts and we find that classical Indian music continues its traditions.
B. Both have deep traditions and changes only happen gradually.
C. For example, concerts are usually by a vocalist or by a solo musician playing an instrument, with a minimum of percussionists.
D. Indian culture is rich in the arts – literature, music, dance, theatre, fine arts and, in modern India, cinema and television as well.
E. The two main types of music in India are the North Indian Hindustani and the South Indian Karnatic music.
(1) EDBAC
(2) DEACB
(3) DAEBC
(4) ABECD
= 3
~ D is the general opening (Indian culture is rich in the arts); A narrows it to music and its traditions; E names the two main types, Hindustani and Karnatic; B ("Both have deep traditions") must follow E because "both" refers to these two types; and C gives an example of how slowly traditions change. Hence DAEBC.

(ii) Punctilious | b. Inpeccable

Q 5 | eng | Vocabulary
Pick out the most effective pair of words from the given options to make the sentence meaningfully complete.
The Prison guards degraded themselves with their ______ and ______ treatment of the prisoners.
(1) differential/regal
(2) cavern/fantabulous
(3) surreptitious/overt
(4) inhumane/ignominious
= 4
~ Guards "degrade themselves" by treatment that is cruel and shameful: "inhumane" (lacking compassion, cruel) and "ignominious" (disgraceful, humiliating) fit both the sense and the tone. "Differential/regal" makes no sense, "cavern" is a noun and "fantabulous" is slang, and "surreptitious" (secret) and "overt" (open) contradict each other.

Q 9 | eng | Comprehension
In the following question, the first and the last sentences of the passage are in order and numbered S₁ and S₆. The rest of the passage is split into four parts and named P, Q, R and S.
These four parts are not given in their proper order, as they should appear in the paragraph.
Read the passage and find out which of the four combinations correctly forms the sequence of sentences in the passage :
S₁: One key prevention is making personnel throughout the organisation aware of the fraud risk management program, including the types of fraud and misconduct they may occur.
P: With this in mind, prevention and deterrence are interrelated concepts.
Q: This awareness should enforce the notion that all of the techniques established in the programme are real and will be enforced.
R: The ongoing communication efforts could provide information on the potential disciplinary, criminal and civil actions that the organization could take against the individual.
S: If effective preventive controls are in place, working and well-known to potential fraud perpetrators, they serve as strong deterrents to those who might otherwise be tempted to commit fraud.
S₆: Fear of getting caught is always a strong deterrent. Effective preventive controls are, therefore, strong deterrence controls.
(1) PRQS
(2) QRPS
(3) QRSP
(4) RSPQ
= 2
~ Q ("This awareness ...") picks up the awareness in S₁; R continues with the ongoing communication about possible disciplinary, criminal and civil action. P ("With this in mind ...") then links prevention with deterrence, and S explains how well-known preventive controls deter fraud, which leads to S₆'s conclusion that fear of getting caught makes preventive controls strong deterrents. Hence QRPS.

Q 10 | eng | Comprehension
Answer the question after reading the following passage :
The traditional approach to parenthood is completely unsatisfactory. Women have to spend many hours in child-rearing. Those with professional skills may sacrifice their career in all respects for the benefit of only one child. Because women spend time caring for their children, the services of many expensively trained teachers, nurses, doctors and other professionals are altogether lost to society. Even if child-rearing is shared by the father, it simply means that two people waste time on an unproductive task for which they may be entirely ill-equipped. Society would be much better served if parenthood was made the responsibility of well-trained professional parents who would look after groups of children as a paid occupation. This would end amateur childrearing and allow the biological parents to fully develop their careers for the benefit of society. Critics may argue that children reared in this would feel rejected, at least to some extent, by their natural parents. This is quite untrue. Evidence from societies where collective childrearing is practiced shows that children merely experience minor upsets and are hardly affected by the separation.
What is the function of expressions like 'completely, in all respects, altogether, simply, entirely, much, at least to some extent, quite, merely and hardly' in the passage ?
(1) To maximize the points against their opinion
(2) To persuade the reader by well-structured argument
(3) To mislead the reader so that she/he keeps on worrying about the gist
(4) To minimize the points in favour of their opinion
= 2
~ These words are persuasive devices. Intensifiers such as "completely", "in all respects", "altogether", "simply", "entirely" and "much" strengthen the writer's own case, while "at least to some extent", "quite", "merely" and "hardly" play down the critics' objection. So they help to persuade the reader. Options (1) and (4) reverse their effect, since the writer magnifies points in favour and minimises points against, and nothing suggests an intention to mislead.

Q 11 | eng | Vocabulary
For the question below, determine the relationship between the pair in the question and then select, from the options the pair having a similar relationship to the first pair.
Mendicant : Impecunious ::
(1) Complainer : Petulant
(2) Hat : Askew
(3) Philanthropist : Prodigal
(4) Critic : Vagabond
= 1
~ A mendicant (beggar) is by nature impecunious (having little or no money), so the second word is a defining trait of the first. In the same way a complainer is typically petulant (peevish, sulky). A hat is not by nature askew (crooked), a philanthropist is generous rather than prodigal (wasteful), and "vagabond" (wanderer) has nothing to do with a critic.

Q 12 | eng | Vocabulary
In the following question, four pairs of words are given. Find the word pair which have dissimilar meaning from the options given below :
(1) retrospective/prospective
(2) entice/lure
(3) abhor/detest
(4) introspection/brooding
= 1
~ "Retrospective" means looking back at the past, while "prospective" means relating to the future, so this pair has opposite (dissimilar) meanings. "Entice" and "lure" both mean to tempt or attract; "abhor" and "detest" both mean to hate; and "introspection" and "brooding" both refer to dwelling on one's own thoughts and feelings.

Q 13 | eng | Vocabulary
Choose the right alternative to make it a meaningful sentence.
Though she is ______ by nature but she does not like that any stranger should ______ on her privacy.
(1) lugubrious, lonely
(2) garrulous, meddle
(3) salubrious, interfere
(4) gregarious, intrude
= 4
~ "Though" signals a contrast: she is "gregarious" (sociable, fond of company) yet dislikes strangers who "intrude on" her privacy, and "intrude on" is the correct collocation. "Lugubrious" (mournful) gives no contrast and "lonely" is not a verb; one "meddles in", not "meddles on"; and "salubrious" (healthy) describes places or climates, not people.

Q 14 | eng | Vocabulary
Fill in the blanks with appropriate words by choosing the suitable set of answers from the options given below the paragraph.
The disqualification as a (i) ______ of defection does not apply. If the original party of a member (ii) ______ with another party and he either changes his political party as a consequence of the merger. A merger is (iii) ______ to take place only if not less than two-third of the (iv) ______ of the Legislative party (v) ______ to such merger.
(1) (i) consequence (ii) merges (iii) deemed (iv) members (v) agree
(2) (i) consequence (ii) agree (iii) deemed (iv) members (v) merges
(3) (i) consequence (ii) deemed (iii) members (iv) merges (v) agree
(4) (i) consequence (ii) agree (iii) merged (iv) deemed (v) members
= 1
~ The paragraph paraphrases the merger exception in paragraph 4 of the Tenth Schedule (anti-defection law): disqualification as a consequence of defection does not apply if a member's original party "merges" with another party, and a merger is "deemed" to have taken place only if at least two-thirds of the "members" of the legislature party "agree" to it. Option (2) swaps "merges" and "agree", while (3) and (4) put words in impossible slots, such as "deemed with another party".

Q 15 | eng | Grammar
Which of the following is an ungrammatical sentence ?
(1) Have they not been practising yoga since their childhood ?
(2) The team has been working hard meticulously for six months before they submitted the project to the company.
(3) Hasn't he repaired the vehicle yet ?
(4) If I were you, I wouldn't write this examination.
= 2
~ An action that continued up to another past action ("before they submitted") needs the past perfect continuous: "The team had been working ... before they submitted ...". The present perfect "has been working" cannot be tied to a completed past event, and "hard meticulously" piles up two adverbs awkwardly. Sentences (1), (3) and (4) are correct, (4) using the subjunctive "were".

Q 16 | eng | Vocabulary
Determine which of these words fits well in all, or the maximum number of sentences.
(i) The bill ______ reduction in electoral expenses.
(ii) The plan ______ ten percent growth in production.
(iii) The budget for the current year ______ more or less double turn-over than that of last year.
(iv) The company ______ a good production due to better prospects of sugarcane production.
(1) seeks
(2) rated
(3) increased
(4) envisages
= 4
~ To "envisage" is to foresee or plan for something in the future. A bill envisages a reduction in expenses, a plan envisages 10% growth, a budget envisages double turnover and a company envisages good production, so it fits all four sentences. "Seeks" fits (i) and (ii) but not (iv), since a company does not "seek" production because prospects are good, and "rated" and "increased" do not make sense with these objects.

Q 21 | ari | Ratio, Average & Ages
Three persons A, B and C have started a business with a total investment of ₹ 5 lakhs. In this A invested ₹ 40,000 more than B, while B's investment is ₹ 50,000 more than that of C. If the share of B in the year-end profit is ₹ 25,500, then the share (in rupees) of A in the profit is
(1) 21,000
(2) 31,500
(3) 35,500
(4) 18,000
= 2
~ Let C invest x. Then B = x + 50,000 and A = x + 90,000, so 3x + 1,40,000 = 5,00,000, giving x = 1,20,000. Investments: A = 2,10,000, B = 1,70,000, C = 1,20,000, in the ratio 21 : 17 : 12. B's share of 17 parts is ₹ 25,500, so one part is ₹ 1,500 and A's share is 21 × 1,500 = ₹ 31,500.

Q 22 | ari | Percentage, Profit & Loss
A manufacturer undertakes to supply 2000 pieces of a particular component at the rate of ₹ 25 per piece. According to his estimate, even if 5% of the supply fail to pass the quality test, then he will make a profit of 25%. However, as it turned out, 50% of the supplied components were rejected. The loss to the manufacturer is
(1) ₹ 14,000
(2) ₹ 13,000
(3) ₹ 12,000
(4) ₹ 15,000
= 2
~ He is paid only for components that pass. If 5% fail, 1,900 pass and he receives 1,900 × 25 = ₹ 47,500, which is cost + 25%; so his cost of making the 2,000 pieces is 47,500 ÷ 1.25 = ₹ 38,000. Actually 50% were rejected, so only 1,000 were paid for: 1,000 × 25 = ₹ 25,000. Loss = 38,000 − 25,000 = ₹ 13,000.

Q 23 | ari | Interest
A certain sum P amounts to ₹ 29,400 in two years and to ₹ 34,300 in three years on compound interest. Then the sum P is (in rupees)
(1) 21,600
(2) 25,600
(3) 22,600
(4) 20,000
= 1
~ The amount for the third year grows from ₹ 29,400 to ₹ 34,300, so (1 + r) = 34,300/29,400 = 7/6, i.e. r = 16⅔% a year. Then P = 29,400 × (6/7)² = 29,400 × 36/49 = ₹ 21,600. Check: 21,600 × 7/6 = 25,200 after one year and 25,200 × 7/6 = 29,400 after two years.

Q 24 | ari | Time, Speed & Distance
A and B are at points P and Q respectively. The destinations of A and B are Q and P respectively. Both of them started at the same time in the opposite directions on the same route. After some time they met each other at the point R. A and B take respectively 4 hours and 9 hours to reach their destination from R. If the speed of A is 48 kmph then the speed of B (in kmph) is
(1) 39
(2) 32
(3) 46
(4) 72
= 2
~ For two people who start together towards each other and, after meeting, take t₁ and t₂ hours to finish, speed of A : speed of B = √t₂ : √t₁. Here A takes 4 hours and B 9 hours after meeting, so A : B = √9 : √4 = 3 : 2. Hence B's speed = 48 × 2/3 = 32 km/h. (They met after √(4 × 9) = 6 hours.)

Q 25 | ari | Ratio, Average & Ages
Shekhar started a business investing ₹ 25,000 in the year 1999. After one year in 2000, he invested an additional amount of ₹ 10,000 and Rajeev joined him with an amount of ₹ 35,000. After another year in 2001, Shekhar again invested another additional amount of ₹ 10,000 and Jatin joined them with an amount of ₹ 35,000. Rajeev's share in the profit of ₹ 1,50,000 earned at the end of 3 years from the start of the business in 1999 is
(1) ₹ 35,000
(2) ₹ 40,000
(3) ₹ 50,000
(4) ₹ 25,000
= 3
~ Profit is shared in the ratio of capital × time. Shekhar: 25,000 + 35,000 + 45,000 = 1,05,000 (one year each at three levels); Rajeev: 35,000 × 2 = 70,000; Jatin: 35,000 × 1 = 35,000. Ratio 105 : 70 : 35 = 3 : 2 : 1. Rajeev's share = 2/6 × 1,50,000 = ₹ 50,000.

Q 26 | ari | Ratio, Average & Ages
The average salary of all the workers in a factory is ₹ 15,000. The average salary of 12 technicians is ₹ 18,000 and the average salary of the rest is ₹ 12,000. Then, the total number of workers in the factory is
(1) 22
(2) 24
(3) 28
(4) 20
= 2
~ Let the total be n workers. Total salary = 15,000n = 12 × 18,000 + (n − 12) × 12,000. The 12 technicians earn Rs. 3,000 each above the average (Rs. 36,000 in all), so the other workers, each Rs. 3,000 below it, must also number 12. Q 27 | ari | Ratio, Average & Ages
The ratio of number of employees in the technical section and the management section of a company is 7 : 2. The average salaries of employees in the technical section and the management section are ₹ 25,000 and ₹ 47,500 respectively. The average salary of employees of these two sections together is (in rupees)
(1) 37,500
(2) 35,000
(3) 30,000
(4) 40,000
= 3
~ Take 7 technical and 2 management employees. The combined average is the weighted average (7 × 25,000 + 2 × 47,500) ÷ 9 = (1,75,000 + 95,000) ÷ 9 = 2,70,000 ÷ 9 = Rs. 30,000. Because there are far more technical staff, the result lies much closer to Rs. 25,000 than to Rs. 47,500.

Q 28 | ari | Time, Speed & Distance
Three persons A, B and C respectively take 30 seconds, 45 seconds and 60 seconds to make one round around a track. All of them start at the same point P on the track. Then match number of rounds made by the persons in the List-I as given in List-II when they meet again at P.
List – I | List – II
(i) A | (a) 6
(ii) B | (b) 3
(iii) C | (c) 4
 | (d) 2
The correct match is :
(1) i-a, ii-d, iii-b
(2) i-a, ii-c, iii-b
(3) i-c, ii-b, iii-d
(4) i-a, ii-c, iii-d
= 2
~ They are together at P again after the LCM of 30, 45 and 60 seconds, which is 180 seconds. In that time A makes 180/30 = 6 rounds, B makes 180/45 = 4 rounds and C makes 180/60 = 3 rounds, so the match is A-6 (a), B-4 (c), C-3 (b), i.e. i-a, ii-c, iii-b.

Q 29 | ari | Ratio, Average & Ages
A sum of ₹ 2,600 is divided amongst P, Q, R, S such that
(P's share)/(Q's share) = (Q's share)/(R's share) = (R's share)/(S's share) = 2/3.
Then P's share is
(1) ₹ 300
(2) ₹ 320
(3) ₹ 340
(4) ₹ 280
= 2

Q 30 | ari | Ratio, Average & Ages
Ramu earns in July thrice as much as in each of the other months. The ratio of his earnings in July to the annual earnings is :
(1) 3 : 13
(2) 3 : 14
(3) 3 : 11
(4) 3 : 12
= 2
~ Let Ramu earn x in each of the other 11 months; then he earns 3x in July. His annual earnings are 11x + 3x = 14x, so the ratio of July's earnings to the annual earnings is 3x : 14x = 3 : 14. Option (4), 3 : 12, wrongly takes the annual earnings as 12x, ignoring July's higher pay.

(ii) What percent of 1/3 is 1/2 ? | (B) 1200%
