Q 1 | eng | Grammar
Which of the following statements is incorrect regarding the usage of the article?
(1) Eight-year-old Roxy Getter is the youngest girl who climbed the Mount Kilimanjaro in 2017.
(2) Raj: Have you got a cold? Raghu: No, I've got the flu.
(3) What a lovely dress!
(4) Dr. Kalam was elected President in 2002.
= 1
~ The names of single mountains take no article: we say "Mount Everest" and "Mount Kilimanjaro", so "climbed the Mount Kilimanjaro" is wrong and should be "climbed Mount Kilimanjaro". The other sentences are correct: "a cold" but "the flu" is the usual idiom for these illnesses, "What a + singular noun" is the normal exclamation, and a unique post after "elected" (President) takes no article.

Q 2 | eng | Grammar
Fill in the blanks using the correct preposition:
Where did you come ______?
(1) for
(2) from
(3) above
(4) into
= 2
~ "Where did you come from?" asks about a person's place of origin or starting point, and "from" is the preposition that marks the starting point of movement. "For" asks about purpose ("What did you come for?"), not place, while "above" and "into" make no sense after "come" in this question.

Q 3 | eng | Grammar
Fill in the blank with the appropriate verb form given below:
Raghu ______ cricket when his friend went home yesterday.
(1) has played
(2) is playing
(3) is played
(4) was playing
= 4
~ An action that was in progress in the past when another, shorter past action happened takes the past continuous (was/were + -ing): Raghu was playing cricket when his friend went home. "Yesterday" fixes the time in the past, so the present perfect "has played" and the present continuous "is playing" are wrong, and "is played" is a passive form.

Q 4 | eng | Grammar
Fill in the blank with the correct verb form.
I ______ French too well.
(1) am not understanding
(2) do not understand
(3) am not understand
(4) do not understanding
= 2
~ "Understand" is a stative verb (a verb of mental state), so it is normally used in the simple tenses, not the continuous: "I do not understand French too well", not "am not understanding". The negative of the simple present is do/does not + base form, so "am not understand" and "do not understanding" are both ungrammatical.

Q 5 | eng | Grammar
Choose the grammatically correct sentence from the options given below:
(1) Neither the teacher nor the students was present in the class.
(2) Neither the teacher nor the students were present in the class.
(3) Neither the teacher nor the students has present in the class.
(4) Neither the teacher nor the students is present in the class.
= 2
~ When subjects are joined by "neither ... nor" (or "either ... or"), the verb agrees with the subject nearer to it (rule of proximity). Here the nearer subject is "the students", which is plural, so the verb must be "were". "Was" and "is" are singular, and "has present" is not a correct verb phrase.

Q 6 | eng | Grammar
Which of the following sentences has a subject that does not agree with the verb?
(1) Every shirt, tie and coat is marked for reduction sale.
(2) Neither our Accounts Department nor our Head Office have a record of the transaction.
(3) Excessive absences were the reason for his failure.
(4) The committee has agreed to submit its report on Friday.
= 2
~ Two singular subjects joined by "neither ... nor" take a singular verb, so (2) should read "Neither our Accounts Department nor our Head Office has a record". The others are correct: "every" before nouns joined by "and" takes a singular verb, the plural subject "absences" takes "were" (the verb agrees with the subject, not with "the reason"), and "committee" acting as one body takes "has" and "its".

Q 7 | eng | Vocabulary
Pick the one word that gives the meaning of the following:
A large system consisting of many similar parts that are connected together.
(1) NETWORK
(2) DISTRIBUTION
(3) COMPUTER
(4) COMMUNICATION
= 1
~ A "network" is a large system of many similar parts connected to one another, such as a road, railway, telephone or computer network. "Distribution" is the act of sharing or spreading something out, a "computer" is a single machine, and "communication" is the exchange of information, so none of them fits the definition.

Q 8 | eng | Grammar
Identify the correct passive form of the following sentence from the options given below:
'We would have painted the walls green.'
(1) The walls would have been painted green by us.
(2) The green walls would have been painted by us.
(3) The walls in green would be painted by us.
(4) The green walls will have been painted by us.
= 1
~ In the passive, the object "the walls" becomes the subject, "would have painted" becomes "would have been painted", the complement "green" stays after the verb and the agent becomes "by us". Options (2) and (4) turn "green" into an adjective before "walls", which changes the meaning; (3) has "would be" in place of "would have been", and (4) also changes "would" to "will".

Q 9 | eng | Grammar
Pick the option that gives the correct indirect form of the following sentence:
Direct: The politician said, "It gives me great pleasure to be here, this morning."
Indirect:
(1) The politician said that it gave him great pleasure to be there that morning.
(2) The politician said it gives me great pleasure to be there that morning.
(3) The politician said that it gave him great pleasure to be here this morning.
(4) The politician said it gives me great pleasure to be there this morning.
= 1
~ With a past reporting verb ("said"), the simple present "gives" becomes "gave", the first-person "me" becomes "him" (the politician), "here" becomes "there" and "this morning" becomes "that morning", joined by "that". Option (1) makes all these changes. Option (3) keeps "here" and "this morning", and (2) and (4) keep "gives me" from the direct speech.

Q 10 | eng | Grammar
The following sentence is in Direct Speech. Choose the correct option which expresses the same sentence in Indirect Speech:
The spectators said, "Bravo! Well played, Sachin".
(1) The spectators called Sachin, bravo because he had played well.
(2) The spectators said that Sachin has played well.
(3) The spectators encouraged Sachin saying that he played well.
(4) The spectators applauded Sachin saying that he had played well.
= 4
~ An exclamation such as "Bravo!" is reported with a verb that conveys the feeling, here "applauded", and the past "played" moves back to the past perfect "had played". Option (4) does both. Option (2) loses the exclamation and wrongly uses the present perfect "has played", (3) keeps the simple past and uses "encouraged", which changes the sense, and (1) is clumsy and puts "bravo" in as if it were a name.

Q 11 | eng | Comprehension
In the following question, there are six sentences of a paragraph marked S1, S6, P, Q, R, S. The positions of S1 and S6 are fixed. You are required to choose the most logical sequence of the sentences that form the paragraph:
S1: Proverbs contain homely but universal truths.
P: Some of them point out the incongruities of situations in life.
Q: Naturally, they are translatable from one language to another.
R: Therefore, their appeal is direct to anyone.
S: Many of them had their birth in folk literature.
S6: They are everyman's philosophy.
(1) QPRS
(2) QSPR
(3) PSRQ
(4) SPQR
= 4
~ After S1 introduces proverbs, S says where many of them came from (folk literature) and P adds what some of them do (point out life's incongruities); "many of them ... some of them" reads naturally in that order. Q, "Naturally, they are translatable", follows from their universal truths, R ("Therefore, their appeal is direct to anyone") draws the conclusion, and S6 sums up: they are everyman's philosophy. Hence S, P, Q, R.

Q 12 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct option which expresses the following sentence in passive voice:
Do you imitate others?
(1) Are others imitated by you?
(2) Are others being imitated by you?
(3) Were others imitated by you?
(4) Have others are imitated by you?
= 1
~ "Do you imitate others?" is a simple present question. Its passive is am/is/are + subject + past participle + by + agent; with the plural subject "others" this gives "Are others imitated by you?" Option (2) is present continuous passive, (3) is simple past, and (4) is ungrammatical.

Q 14 | eng | Comprehension
Read the following passage and answer the questions (from 14 to 16) that follow:
Insect behaviour generally appears to be explicable in terms of unconscious, inflexible stimulus-response mechanisms. For instance, a female sphex wasp leaves her egg sealed in a burrow alongside a paralyzed grasshopper, which her larvae can eat upon hatching. Before she deposits the grasshopper in the burrow, she inspects the burrow; if the inspection reveals no problems, she drags the grasshopper inside by its antennae. As thoughtful as this behaviour appears, it reveals its mechanistic character upon interference. Darwin discovered that prior removal of the grasshopper's antennae prevents the wasp from depositing the grasshopper, even though the legs or ovipositor could also serve as handles. Likewise, Fabre moved the grasshopper a few centimeters away from the burrow's mouth while the wasp was inside inspecting. The wasp returned the grasshopper to the edge of the burrow and then began a new inspection. Fabre performed this disruptive maneuver forty times; the wasp's response never changed.
The author mentions the work of Darwin and Fabre in order to
(1) provide experimental evidence of the inflexibility of one kind of insect behaviour.
(2) contradict the conventional wisdom about "typical" wasp behaviour.
(3) illustrate the strength of the wasp's maternal affection.
(4) explore the logical implications of the thesis articulated earlier.
= 1
~ The passage claims that the wasp's apparently thoughtful behaviour "reveals its mechanistic character upon interference". Darwin's removal of the antennae and Fabre's moving the grasshopper forty times are experiments showing that the wasp's routine never adapts, so they provide experimental evidence of the inflexibility of this kind of insect behaviour. They support, not contradict, the opening claim, say nothing about maternal affection, and are evidence rather than an exploration of logical implications.

Q 16 | eng | Comprehension
Read the following passage and answer the questions (from 14 to 16) that follow:

Q 21 | ari | Numbers & Simplification
Consider the following statements:
(A) The number of ordered pairs of positive integers (m, n) such that m + n = 648 and g.c.d. of (m, n) = 27 is 8.
(B) If the number x is of the form x = 48m + 39 for some positive integer m, then the remainder when x is divided by 8 is 1.
Select the correct option among the following:
(1) Both (A) and (B) are true.
(2) Only (A) is true.
(3) Only (B) is true.
(4) Both (A) and (B) are false.
= 2
~ (A): write m = 27a and n = 27b with gcd(a, b) = 1; then a + b = 648/27 = 24, and gcd(a, b) = 1 exactly when a is coprime to 24. The values a = 1, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23 give 8 ordered pairs, so (A) is true. (B): 48m is divisible by 8 and 39 = 4 × 8 + 7, so x leaves remainder 7, not 1; (B) is false.

Q 23 | ari | Ratio, Average & Ages
If 3x = 2y + 5z and 3y = 4x + z, then x : y : z =
(1) 17 : 13 : 11
(2) 23 : 17 : 1
(3) 11 : 13 : 17
(4) 17 : 23 : 1
= 4
~ From the second equation, z = 3y − 4x. Substituting in the first: 3x = 2y + 5(3y − 4x) = 17y − 20x, so 23x = 17y and x : y = 17 : 23. Taking x = 17 and y = 23 gives z = 69 − 68 = 1. Check: 3 × 17 = 51 = 2 × 23 + 5 × 1. Hence x : y : z = 17 : 23 : 1.

Q 24 | ari | Percentage, Profit & Loss
If 8% of 7% of 5% of x is 105 then the value of x in lakhs is
(1) 3.75
(2) 0.375
(3) 0.0375
(4) 0.00375
= 1
~ 8% of 7% of 5% of x = 0.08 × 0.07 × 0.05 × x = 0.00028x. Setting 0.00028x = 105 gives x = 105 ÷ 0.00028 = 3,75,000. Since 1 lakh = 1,00,000, x = 3.75 lakh. The order of taking the percentages does not matter, because the factors are simply multiplied.

Q 26 | ari | Ratio, Average & Ages

(1) 15

Q 27 | ari | Ratio, Average & Ages
The average height of 25 students out of 40 students in a class is 163 cm and the average height of the remaining students is 169 cm. Then the average height of the students in that class, in cm, is
(1) 164.75
(2) 165.25
(3) 165.75
(4) 166.25
= 2
~ The remaining students number 40 − 25 = 15. Total height = 25 × 163 + 15 × 169 = 4,075 + 2,535 = 6,610 cm, so the class average is 6,610 ÷ 40 = 165.25 cm. Shortcut: 163 + (169 − 163) × 15/40 = 163 + 2.25 = 165.25 cm.

Q 30 | ari | Time & Work
10 men and 15 women can complete a work in 6 days. 12 men and 27 women can complete the same work in 4 days. Then the number of days required for 2 men and 12 women to complete the same work is
(1) 12
(2) 10
(3) 9
(4) 8
= 1
~ Equating the total work, 6(10m + 15w) = 4(12m + 27w), so 60m + 90w = 48m + 108w, which gives 12m = 18w, i.e. 1 man = 1.5 women. The work is 6 × (15 + 15) = 180 woman-days. 2 men and 12 women equal 3 + 12 = 15 women, so they need 180 ÷ 15 = 12 days.

Q 31 | ari | Time & Work

(2) 16,900
(3) 8,625
(4) 16,225

Q 34 | ari | Numbers & Simplification
If the number 6653A321 is divisible by 11 then A =
(1) 1
(2) 0
(3) 2
(4) 4
= 2
~ A number is divisible by 11 when the sums of its digits in odd and even places (counted from the right) differ by 0 or a multiple of 11. For 6653A321, the odd places give 1 + 3 + 3 + 6 = 13 and the even places give 2 + A + 5 + 6 = 13 + A. The difference A must be 0 (11 is not a digit), so A = 0; indeed 66530321 = 11 × 6048211.

Q 35 | ari | Ratio, Average & Ages
P and Q started a business together with an initial investment in the ratio 3 : 2. P invested for 8 months but Q for less time. They shared their profits in the ratio 2 : 1. Then for how many months did Q invest ?
(1) 3
(2) 4
(3) 6
(4) 7
= 3
~ In a partnership the profit is shared in the ratio of capital × time. P's share is 3 × 8 = 24 units and Q's is 2 × t. So 24 : 2t = 2 : 1, giving 4t = 24 and t = 6 months, which is less than P's 8 months, as the question says.

Q 39 | ari | Percentage, Profit & Loss
If the cost price of 24 pens is equal to the selling price of 20 pens of the same kind, then the outcome of this transaction is
(1) 24% profit
(2) 24% loss
(3) 20% profit
(4) 20% loss
= 3
~ Let one pen cost ₹ 1. Then 24 pens cost ₹ 24, which is the selling price of 20 pens, so one pen sells for 24/20 = ₹ 1.20. The gain is ₹ 0.20 on a cost of ₹ 1, a 20% profit. Shortcut: profit% = (24 − 20)/20 × 100 = 20%, the difference being divided by the number of pens sold.

Q 40 | ari | Time & Work
A person A, can complete a piece of work in x days and another person B requires 2x days to finish the same work. If both work together they can finish the work in 14 days, then x =

(2) 20
(3) 21
(4) 24
= 3

Who is the presiding deity in the thousand pillar temple at Hanumakonda?
(1) Nameswara
(2) Someswara
(3) Rudreswara
(4) Bhimeswara
= 3
~ The Thousand Pillar Temple at Hanumakonda was built in 1163 by the Kakatiya king Rudradeva (Rudra I). It is a trikutalaya (three-shrine temple) whose main deity is Rudreswara, a form of Shiva named after the king, with the other two shrines for Vishnu and Surya; it is therefore also called the Rudreswara Swamy temple. Nameswara, Someswara and Bhimeswara are not its presiding deity.

Q 198 | tg | Telangana History & Culture
During the Kakatiya period, lands were of several divisions. What were the 'Acchu Kattu lands'?
(1) Dry lands
(2) Irrigated lands
(3) Garden lands
(4) Pasture lands
= 2
~ Kakatiya inscriptions divide cultivated land into velichenu (dry land growing rain-fed crops), nirunela or achchukattu (wet land irrigated from tanks and canals, used mainly for paddy) and totapadu (garden land). Achchukattu lands were therefore irrigated lands; the Kakatiyas built many tanks to extend them. Dry lands, garden lands and pasture (grazing) lands were separate categories.

Q 199 | tg | Telangana History & Culture
Which among the following places was famous for diamond polishing during the Qutub Shahi period?
(1) Karwan
(2) Kurnool
(3) Guntur
(4) Masulipatnam
= 1
~ Under the Qutb Shahis, diamonds from the Kollur and other Krishna valley mines were cut and polished at Karwan, near Golconda in Hyderabad, which became a famous centre of the diamond trade; the name "Golconda diamonds" comes from this trade. Masulipatnam was the kingdom's chief port, known for its textiles, while Kurnool and Guntur were not diamond-polishing centres.

Q 200 | tg | Telangana History & Culture
The inscriptions issued by Naganika speak of the Aswamedha and Rajasuya sacrifices conducted by a Sathavahana king. Who among the following was that king?
(1) Simukha
(2) Sathakarni I
(3) Apilaka
(4) Hala
= 2
~ Naganika (Nayanika), queen of the Satavahana king Satakarni I, issued the Naneghat inscription (near Pune). It records the Vedic sacrifices performed by her husband, including two Ashvamedhas and a Rajasuya, and the large gifts given to priests. Simukha founded the dynasty, Hala compiled the Prakrit "Gathasaptasati", and Apilaka is known mainly from his coins.
