Q 1 | alg | Sequences & Series
If pˣ = qʸ = rᶻ, where x, y and z are in GP, then consider the following statements :
I. p, q and r are in AP.
II. ln p, ln q and ln r are in GP.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) I only
(2) II only
(3) Both I and II
(4) Neither I nor II
= 2
~ Let pˣ = qʸ = rᶻ = k; then x = ln k/ln p, y = ln k/ln q, z = ln k/ln r. Since x, y, z are in GP, 1/ln p, 1/ln q, 1/ln r are in GP, and hence so are ln p, ln q, ln r (y² = xz gives (ln q)² = ln p·ln r). Nothing forces p, q, r into AP, so only II holds.

Q 2 | alg | Sets, Relations & Functions
If A and B are non-empty subsets of a set, and Aᶜ and Bᶜ represent their complements, then which of the following is/are correct?
I. A − B = Bᶜ − Aᶜ
II. A − Bᶜ = Aᶜ − B
Select the answer using the code given below.
(1) I only
(2) II only
(3) Both I and II
(4) Neither I nor II
= 1
~ Bᶜ − Aᶜ = Bᶜ ∩ A = A − B, so I is true. A − Bᶜ = A ∩ B while Aᶜ − B = Aᶜ ∩ Bᶜ, which are different in general, so II is false.

Q 3 | alg | Permutations & Combinations
Let y = x! and z = (2x)!. If (z/y) = 120, then what is the value of (3x)!?
(1) 362880
(2) 181440
(3) 90720
(4) 45360
= 1
~ (2x)!/x! = 120 is satisfied by x = 3, since 6!/3! = 720/6 = 120. Then (3x)! = 9! = 362880.

Q 4 | alg | Permutations & Combinations
Let n be a natural number. The number of consecutive zeros at the end of the expansion of n! is exactly 2. How many values of n are possible?
(1) 3
(2) 4
(3) 5
(4) More than 5
= 3
~ Trailing zeros of n! = [n/5] + [n/25] + … . This equals exactly 2 for n = 10, 11, 12, 13, 14. At n = 15 it becomes 3. So there are 5 values.

Q 5 | alg | Sequences & Series
If (10 + log₁₀ x), (10 + log₁₀ y) and (10 + log₁₀ z) are in AP, then consider the following statements :
I. The GM of x and z is y².
II. The AM of log₁₀ x and log₁₀ z is log₁₀ y.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(1) I only
(2) II only
(3) Both I and II
(4) Neither I nor II
= 2
~ Subtracting 10 from each term, log₁₀ x, log₁₀ y, log₁₀ z are in AP, so 2 log₁₀ y = log₁₀ x + log₁₀ z and II is true. This gives y² = xz, so the GM of x and z is y, not y². Statement I is false.

Q 7 | alg | Sequences & Series
How many terms are identical in the two APs 19, 21, 23, … up to 110 terms and 19, 22, 25, 28, … up to 75 terms?
(1) 35
(2) 36
(3) 37
(4) 38
= 3
~ The common terms form an AP 19, 25, 31, … with common difference LCM(2, 3) = 6. The first AP ends at 19 + 109×2 = 237 and the second at 19 + 74×3 = 241. So we need 19 + 6(k − 1) ≤ 237, which gives k ≤ 37 (the last common term is 19 + 6×36 = 235). There are 37 common terms.

Q 8 | alg | Complex Numbers
If
α = (−1 + √−3)/2
then what is the value of
(1 + α¹⁹ − α³⁵)¹⁰⁰ − (1 − 3α²⁵ + α³⁸)⁵⁰?
(1) −2
(2) −1
(3) 0
(4) 2
= 3
~ α = ω, so α¹⁹ = ω, α³⁵ = ω², α²⁵ = ω and α³⁸ = ω². Then 1 + ω − ω² = −2ω², and (−2ω²)¹⁰⁰ = 2¹⁰⁰ω²⁰⁰ = 2¹⁰⁰ω². Also 1 − 3ω + ω² = −4ω, and (−4ω)⁵⁰ = 2¹⁰⁰ω⁵⁰ = 2¹⁰⁰ω². The difference is 0.

Q 11 | alg | Sets, Relations & Functions
In a class of 45 students, 34 like to play cricket and 26 like to play football. Further, each student likes to play at least one of the two games. How many students like to play exactly one game?
(1) 45
(2) 30
(3) 25
(4) 15

Q 12 | mat | Determinants

(1) has a unique solution
(2) has infinitely many solutions
(3) is inconsistent
(4) is consistent and has exactly two solutions
= 3

Q 13 | alg | Complex Numbers
If
((1 − i)/(1 + i))^(2m) ((1 + i)/(1 − i))^(2n) = 1
where i = √−1, then what is the smallest positive value of (m − n)?
(1) 1
(2) 2
(3) 4
(4) 8
= 2
~ (1 − i)/(1 + i) = −i and (1 + i)/(1 − i) = i. So the product is (−i)^(2m)·i^(2n) = (−1)^m(−1)^n = (−1)^(m+n) = 1, which needs m + n to be even. Then m − n is also even, so its smallest positive value is 2.

Q 14 | mat | Determinants
In obtaining the solution of the system of equations x + y + z = 7, x + 2y + 3z = 16 and x + 3y + 4z = 22 by Cramer's rule, the value of y is obtained by dividing D by D₂, where
D = |[[1, 1, 1], [1, 2, 3], [1, 3, 4]]|
What is the value of the determinant D₂?
(1) −13
(2) −3
(3) 3
(4) 13
= 2
~ D₂ is D with its second column replaced by the constants (7, 16, 22): |[[1, 7, 1], [1, 16, 3], [1, 22, 4]]| = 1(64 − 66) − 7(4 − 3) + 1(22 − 16) = −2 − 7 + 6 = −3. With D = −1, Cramer's rule gives y = D₂/D = 3 (the booklet's "dividing D by D₂" is reversed).

Q 15 | mat | Matrices
Consider the following in respect of non-singular matrices A and B :
I. (AB)⁻¹ = A⁻¹B⁻¹
II. (BA)(AB)⁻¹ = I, where I is the identity matrix
III. (AB)ᵀ = AᵀBᵀ
How many of the above are correct?
(1) None
(2) One
(3) Two
(4) All three
= 1
~ The correct rules are (AB)⁻¹ = B⁻¹A⁻¹ and (AB)ᵀ = BᵀAᵀ, so I and III fail in general. (BA)(AB)⁻¹ = BAB⁻¹A⁻¹, which is not I unless A and B commute. None is correct in general.

Q 16 | mat | Determinants
The value of the determinant
|[[a, b, c], [l, m, n], [p, q, r]]|
is equal to
(1) |[[a, b, c], [p, q, r], [l, m, n]]|
(2) |[[l, m, n], [a, b, c], [p, q, r]]|
(3) |[[p, q, r], [a, b, c], [l, m, n]]|
(4) |[[a, p, l], [b, q, m], [c, r, n]]|
= 3
~ Option (3) is a cyclic rearrangement of the rows (two swaps), so its value is unchanged. Options (1) and (2) are single row swaps, so they change the sign. Option (4) is the transpose with two columns swapped, which also gives −D.

Q 17 | alg | Complex Numbers
Let 1, ω, ω² be three cube roots of unity. If x = a + b, y = aω + bω², z = aω² + bω, then what is x² + y² + z² equal to?
(1) 6ab
(2) 3ab
(3) a² + b²
(4) 1
= 1
~ x² + y² + z² = a²(1 + ω² + ω⁴) + b²(1 + ω⁴ + ω²) + 2ab(1 + ω³ + ω³). Since 1 + ω + ω² = 0 and ω³ = 1, this is 0 + 0 + 2ab×3 = 6ab.

Q 18 | alg | Permutations & Combinations
How many 4-digit numbers that are divisible by 4 can be formed using the digits 1, 2, 3 and 4 (repetition of digits is not allowed)?
(1) 3
(2) 6
(3) 9
(4) 12
= 2
~ A number is divisible by 4 when its last two digits are. From these digits the possible endings are 12, 24 and 32. Each leaves 2! = 2 arrangements for the first two places, so the total is 3 × 2 = 6.

Q 19 | mat | Determinants
If a, b, c are the sides of a triangle ABC and p is the perimeter of the triangle, then what is
|[[p + c, a, b], [c, p + a, b], [c, a, p + b]]|
equal to?

(2) 2p³
(3) 3p³
(4) 4p³
= 2

Q 31 | alg | Quadratic Equations & Inequalities
Passage: For the following two (02) items : Consider the equation abx² + bcx + ca = cax² + abx + bc

If the roots of the equation are equal, then which one of the following is correct?
(1) ac = b²
(2) a + c = 2b
(3) 1/a + 1/c = 1/(2b)
(4) 1/a + 1/c = 2/b
= 4
~ The equation is a(b − c)x² + b(c − a)x + c(a − b) = 0. Its coefficients add up to 0, so x = 1 is a root. For equal roots the product of roots must be 1, so c(a − b) = a(b − c), which gives 2ac = b(a + c), i.e. 1/a + 1/c = 2/b.

Q 44 | mat | Determinants
Consider the following statements :
Statement-I : If X is an n×n matrix, then det(mX) = mⁿ det(X), where m is a scalar.
Statement-II : If Y is a matrix obtained from X by multiplying any row or column by a scalar m, then det(Y) = m det(X).
Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?
(1) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II explains Statement-I
(2) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct but Statement-II does not explain Statement-I
(3) Statement-I is correct but Statement-II is not correct
(4) Statement-I is not correct but Statement-II is correct
= 1
~ Both properties are standard. Multiplying X by m multiplies each of its n rows by m, so applying Statement-II n times gives det(mX) = mⁿ det X. Statement-II therefore explains Statement-I. (Answer by Learn with Anil; UPSC has not published its key. Some may argue (2), but II is the direct reason for I.)

Q 45 | mat | Determinants

Q 94 | cal | Differential Equations
