Q 1 | eng | Spotting Errors
Find the part of the sentence that has an error; if there is no error, choose (d).
Over long periods of time, layers of sediments builds up to a height of a few kilometers.
(1) Over long periods of time,
(2) layers of sediments builds up
(3) to a height of a few kilometers.
(4) No error
= 2
~ The subject is the plural "layers", so the verb must be plural: "layers of sediments build up". "Builds" wrongly agrees with "sediments"/singular.

Q 2 | eng | Spotting Errors
Find the part of the sentence that has an error; if there is no error, choose (d).
When a gas is cooled down it turns into a liquid from a process called condensation.
(1) When a gas is
(2) cooled down it turns into a liquid
(3) from a process called condensation.
(4) No error
= 3
~ A change happens "by" or "through" a process, not "from" a process, so part (c) should read "by a process called condensation".

Q 3 | eng | Spotting Errors
Find the part of the sentence that has an error; if there is no error, choose (d).
If you want to know the news you can read a newspaper.
(1) If you want
(2) to know the news
(3) you can read a newspaper.
(4) No error
= 4
~ The conditional sentence is grammatically correct: "If you want ..." (present) with "you can read ..." in the main clause, so there is no error.

Q 4 | eng | Spotting Errors

Columbus made his first voyage from Europe to America on 1492.
(1) Columbus made his
(2) first voyage from Europe to America
(3) on 1492.

Q 5 | eng | Spotting Errors
Find the part of the sentence that has an error; if there is no error, choose (d).
Whenever the sky is clear, you can see the stars in the night.
(1) Whenever the sky is
(2) clear, you can see
(3) the stars in the night.
(4) No error
= 3
~ The idiomatic phrase is "at night" (we say "in the morning/evening" but "at night"), so part (c) should read "the stars at night".

Q 6 | eng | Spotting Errors
Find the part of the sentence that has an error; if there is no error, choose (d).
I'm not working tomorrow, so I don't had to get up early.
(1) I'm not working tomorrow,
(2) so I don't had to
(3) get up early.
(4) No error

Q 8 | eng | Spotting Errors

Q 52 | phy | Mechanics & Properties of Matter

Q 53 | phy | Mechanics & Properties of Matter
A sphere of volume V is made of a material with lower density than water. While on Earth, it floats on water with its volume f₁V (f₁ < 1) submerged. On the other hand, on a spaceship accelerating with acceleration a < g (g is the acceleration due to gravity on Earth) in outer space, its submerged volume in water is f₂V. Then :
(1) f₂ = f₁
(2) f₂ = (1 − a/g) f₁
(3) f₂ > f₁
(4) f₂ = (a/g) f₁
= 1
~ For floating, ρ_water × f × V × g_eff = ρ_body × V × g_eff, so f = ρ_body/ρ_water. The effective gravity cancels, so the submerged fraction is the same in the accelerating spaceship: f₂ = f₁.

Q 54 | phy | Mechanics & Properties of Matter
Two identical containers X and Y are connected at the bottom by a thin tube of negligible volume. The tube has a valve in it, as shown in the figure. Initially container X has a liquid filled up to height h in it and container Y is empty. When the valve is opened, both containers have equal amount of liquid in equilibrium. If the initial (before the valve is opened) potential energy of the liquid is P₁ and the final potential energy is P₂ then :
[Figure: Two identical vertical containers X (left, filled with liquid to height h) and Y (right, empty) joined at their bases by a thin horizontal tube fitted with a closed valve.]
(1) P₁ = P₂
(2) P₁ = 4P₂
(3) P₁ = 2P₂
(4) P₁ = 8P₂
= 3
~ Initially mass m has its centre of mass at h/2, so P₁ = mgh/2. Finally each container holds m/2 up to height h/2 (centre of mass at h/4), so P₂ = mg(h/4); hence P₁ = 2P₂.

Q 55 | phy | Mechanics & Properties of Matter
A particle is moving in a circle of radius R with a constant speed v. Its average acceleration over the time when it moves over half the circle is :
(1) v²/R
(2) πv²/2R
(3) 2v²/πR
(4) 0
= 3
~ Over half a circle the velocity reverses, so the change in velocity is 2v, and the time taken is πR/v. Average acceleration = 2v ÷ (πR/v) = 2v²/πR.

Q 57 | geo | Physical Geography

Q 58 | geo | Physical Geography
Which of the following statements is/are correct ?
1. Hypocenter is the point on the surface of the Earth, nearest to the focus.
2. Velocity of earthquake waves is higher in denser materials.
3. P waves move faster and are the first to arrive at the surface of the Earth.
Select the correct answer using the code given below :
(1) 1 and 2
(2) 2 and 3
(3) 1 and 3
(4) 3 only
= 2
~ The hypocentre is the focus itself, inside the Earth; the point on the surface directly above it is the epicentre, so statement 1 is wrong. Seismic waves travel faster through denser material, and P waves are the fastest and arrive first.
