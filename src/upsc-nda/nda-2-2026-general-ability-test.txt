Q 36 | eng | Sentence Improvement
The following item has four sentences, one of which is grammatically correct. Select the most appropriate correct sentence.
(1) She insisted that he apologises immediately.
(2) She insisted that he apologise immediately.
(3) She insisted that he may apologise immediately.
(4) She insisted that he apologised immediately.
= 2
~ After verbs of demand or insistence ("insist that", "demand that"), English uses the mandative subjunctive, i.e. the base form of the verb for every subject: "that he apologise". "Apologises" and "apologised" are ordinary indicative forms, and "may" weakens the demand.

Q 37 | eng | Sentence Improvement
The following item has four sentences, one of which is grammatically correct. Select the most appropriate correct sentence.
(1) The police has arrested the accused last night.
(2) The police have arrested the accused last night.
(3) The police arrested the accused last night.
(4) The police had arrested the accused last night.
= 3
~ A definite past time ("last night") needs the simple past, so "The police arrested the accused last night" is correct. The present perfect cannot go with "last night", "police" is plural (so "has" is wrong anyway), and the past perfect needs a second, later past event.

Q 38 | eng | Sentence Improvement
The following item has four sentences, one of which is grammatically correct. Select the most appropriate correct sentence.
(1) The teacher made the students to rewrite the essay.
(2) The teacher has the students to rewrite the essay.
(3) The teacher got the students rewriting the essay.
(4) The teacher had the students rewrite the essay.
= 4
~ Causative "have" and "make" take an object plus the bare infinitive: "had the students rewrite", so (4) is correct. "Made ... to rewrite" and "has ... to rewrite" wrongly add "to", and causative "get" normally takes "to" ("got the students to rewrite").

Q 43 | eng | Sentence Improvement
Read the following pairs:
Active Voice | Passive Voice
I. These grapes taste sour. | These grapes are sour when they are tasted.
II. Forget the past. | Let the past be forgotten.
Identify the pair(s) wherein the Passive Voice is correctly stated.
(1) I only
(2) II only
(3) Both I and II
(4) Neither I nor II
= 3
~ "Taste" is a linking verb with no object, so grammar books give the passive as "These grapes are sour when they are tasted" (like "Honey tastes sweet" → "Honey is sweet when it is tasted"). An imperative becomes passive with "Let + object + be + past participle": "Let the past be forgotten". Both pairs are correct.

Q 44 | eng | Sentence Improvement
Read the following pairs:
Active Voice | Passive Voice
I. They asked me my name. | My name was asked by them.
II. They found him guilty. | He found them guilty.
Identify the pair(s) wherein the Passive Voice is correctly stated.
(1) I only
(2) II only
(3) Both I and II
(4) Neither I nor II
= 1
~ "Ask" takes two objects, and school grammars accept either one as the passive subject: "I was asked my name by them" or "My name was asked by them". So I is accepted. Q 51 | phy | Mechanics & Properties of Matter
A bicycle racing track is a level circular road of radius 10 m. Three cyclists P, Q and R with identical cycles are cycling on the track with speeds 2 m/s, 3 m/s and 5 m/s respectively. If the coefficient of friction between the tyres and the road is 0·1, then which cyclist will slip ? (Take g = 10 m/s²)
(1) P only
(2) Q only
(3) R only
(4) All will slip
= 3
~ Friction can supply the centripetal force only up to μmg, so the maximum safe speed is v = √(μgr) = √(0·1 × 10 × 10) = √10 ≈ 3·16 m/s. Only R (5 m/s) exceeds this and slips; P and Q are below it.

Q 52 | phy | Mechanics & Properties of Matter
The bob of a simple pendulum is displaced from its mean position and then released from rest 0·20 m above its mean position. What is the speed of the bob as it passes through the mean position ? (Take g = 10 m/s²)
(1) 0 m/s
(2) 1 m/s
(3) 2 m/s
(4) √10 m/s
= 3
~ Potential energy lost becomes kinetic energy: v = √(2gh) = √(2 × 10 × 0·20) = √4 = 2 m/s.

Q 53 | phy | Mechanics & Properties of Matter
A tennis ball of mass 350 g is thrown vertically upwards at a speed of 4 m/s. If M is the maximum gravitational potential energy of the ball and H is the maximum height it reaches, then which one of the following is correct ? (Take g = 10 m/s²)
(1) M = 5·6 J, H = 1·6 m
(2) M = 2·8 J, H = 1·6 m
(3) M = 5·6 J, H = 0·8 m
(4) M = 2·8 J, H = 0·8 m
= 4
~ All the initial kinetic energy becomes potential energy at the top: M = ½ × 0·35 × 4² = 2·8 J, and H = v²/2g = 16/20 = 0·8 m.

Q 54 | phy | Light & Sound
Which of the following is correct regarding the total internal reflection at the interface between two different optical media ?
(1) The phenomenon of total internal reflection can occur only if light is passing at any angle from a medium of low refractive index to a medium of high refractive index.
(2) The phenomenon of total internal reflection can occur only if light is passing from a medium of high refractive index to a medium of low refractive index and at an angle greater than the critical angle.
(3) The phenomenon of total internal reflection can occur only if light is passing at any angle from a medium of high refractive index to a medium of low refractive index.
(4) The phenomenon of total internal reflection can occur only if light is passing from a medium of low refractive index to a medium of high refractive index and at an angle greater than the critical angle.
= 2
~ Total internal reflection needs two conditions: light must travel from the denser (higher refractive index) medium towards the rarer one, and the angle of incidence must exceed the critical angle. At smaller angles the light is partly refracted out.

Q 55 | phy | Mechanics & Properties of Matter
Consider the following statements :

II. The inertial mass and gravitational mass of an object are proportional and the proportionality constant is the same everywhere.
III. The inertial mass and weight of an object are the same at different places on Earth.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct ?
(1) I only
(2) II only
(3) I and II
(4) II and III
= 2
~ Mass does not change from place to place, but weight (mg) varies because g varies with latitude and altitude, so I and III are wrong. Inertial and gravitational mass are found to be proportional (in fact equal) everywhere, so II is correct.
