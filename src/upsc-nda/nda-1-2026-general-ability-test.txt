Q 1 | eng | Synonyms & Antonyms
Select the word that is nearest in meaning to the underlined word/words.

(2) adages

Q 51 | phy | Mechanics & Properties of Matter
A pendulum of length L oscillates with an angular amplitude of θ = 60° and time period T. Let T₀ = 2π√(L/g) be the time period for small angle of oscillations, where g is the acceleration due to gravity. If air resistance is negligibly small and the string remains straight, then which one of the following is correct?
(1) T will be slightly greater than T₀.
(2) T will be slightly smaller than T₀.
(3) T will be exactly equal to T₀.
(4) T will depend upon the mass of the bob.
= 1
~ T₀ = 2π√(L/g) holds only for small angles; for larger amplitudes the period grows, approximately T ≈ T₀(1 + θ₀²/16). For 60° the exact value is about 1.07 T₀, so T is slightly greater than T₀ and still independent of the mass of the bob.

Q 52 | phy | Mechanics & Properties of Matter
Two forces of equal magnitude simultaneously act at a point. It is observed that the resultant force is equal in magnitude to the individual forces. Which of the following statements is/are correct?
I. The angle between one of the individual forces and the resultant force is π/3.
II. The angle between the individual forces is 2π/3.
Select the answer using the code given below.
(1) I only
(2) II only
(3) Both I and II
(4) Neither I nor II
= 3
~ R² = F² + F² + 2F²cos θ = F² gives cos θ = −1/2, so the forces are at θ = 2π/3. The resultant of two equal forces bisects the angle between them, so it makes π/3 with each force; both statements are correct.

Q 53 | phy | Heat & Thermodynamics
A solid of mass m has temperature-dependent specific heat as C(T) = C₀ + αT, where C₀ and α are constants. The solid is heated from T₁ to T₂. Which one of the following is the correct expression for quantity of heat (Q) of the solid mass?
(1) Q = mC₀(T₂ − T₁)
(2) Q = m(T₂ − T₁)[C₀ + α(T₁ + T₂)]
(3) Q = m(T₂ − T₁)[C₀(T₁ + T₂) + α]
(4) Q = m(T₂ − T₁)[C₀ + (α/2)(T₁ + T₂)]
= 4
~ Q = m∫(C₀ + αT)dT from T₁ to T₂ = m[C₀(T₂ − T₁) + (α/2)(T₂² − T₁²)]. Taking out (T₂ − T₁) gives m(T₂ − T₁)[C₀ + (α/2)(T₁ + T₂)].

Q 54 | phy | Heat & Thermodynamics
In a certain process, the relation between pressure (P) and volume (V), for an ideal gas, is given by PV² = constant. If the initial temperature is T₁ and the final temperature is T₂; and the initial volume is V₁ and the final volume is V₂, then which one of the following is correct?
(1) T₁/T₂ = V₁/V₂
(2) T₁/T₂ = (V₁/V₂)²
(3) T₁/T₂ = V₂/V₁
(4) T₁/T₂ = (V₂/V₁)²
= 3
~ From PV = nRT, P ∝ T/V; putting this in PV² = constant gives TV = constant. So T₁V₁ = T₂V₂, i.e. T₁/T₂ = V₂/V₁.

Q 55 | phy | Heat & Thermodynamics
For an ideal gas, a process is described by P = kT, where k is a constant. If the molar heat capacity for this process is C, then which one of the following is correct (where the symbols have their usual meanings)?
(1) C = C_P
(2) C = C_V
(3) C = (C_P + C_V)/2
(4) C is never constant
= 2
~ For an ideal gas P/T = nR/V, so P = kT means the volume stays constant (an isochoric process). The molar heat capacity at constant volume is C_V.

Q 56 | phy | Mechanics & Properties of Matter
For an object in gravitational field, the gravitational potential is the same at two points A and B, but the gravitational field is not the same at these two points. Which one of the following statements is correct?
(1) Positive work is done by gravity in moving the object from point A to point B.
(2) Negative work is done by gravity in moving the object from point A to point B.
(3) Gravity must be non-conservative in such a region.
(4) No work is done by gravity in moving the object from point A to point B.
= 4
~ Gravity is conservative, so the work it does is W = −m(V_B − V_A), which depends only on the potentials at the end points. Equal potentials give zero work, whatever the field strength at the two points.
