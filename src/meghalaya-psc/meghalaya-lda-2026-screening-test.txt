# Meghalaya PSC: Lower Division Assistant under the Heads of Department [July 2025 advertisement], screening test held 22 August 2026, Series I (MPSC's own booklet, 150 questions x 2 marks); answers: MPSC's answer key of 10 September 2026

Q 1 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct option to fill in the gaps :
We ______ some terrifying news last night.
(1) hear
(2) heard
(3) get
(4) got
= 2
~ “Last night” is a finished past time, so the simple past is needed, which rules out “hear” and “get”. One hears news (learns it by being told), so the standard answer is “We heard some terrifying news last night.” “Got” is also grammatical in everyday speech, but it is informal, so the key takes “heard”.

Q 2 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct option to fill in the gaps :
My teacher ______ a book last year.
(1) write
(2) wrote
(3) written
(4) had wrote
= 2
~ “Last year” marks a finished past time, so the simple past “wrote” is used. “Written” is a past participle and needs “has/had”, and “had wrote” is ungrammatical (it would be “had written”).

Q 3 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct option to fill in the gaps :
The train ______ at 8:00 at the station yesterday.
(1) did not arrive
(2) did not arrived
(3) did not pass
(4) did not passed
= 1
~ After the auxiliary “did/did not”, the main verb stays in its base form: “did not arrive”, never “did not arrived” or “did not passed”. A train arrives at a station; “pass at the station” is not idiomatic (a train passes, or passes through, a station).

Q 4 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct option to fill in the gaps :
My cousin wears sandals but when I last saw him, he was ______ boots.
(1) wear
(2) wore
(3) wearing
(4) worn
= 3
~ “Was” + present participle (-ing) forms the past continuous, used for an action in progress at a past moment: “when I last saw him, he was wearing boots”.

Q 5 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct option to fill in the gaps :
As she was ______ the stairs, she slipped and fell.
(1) climbing
(2) climb
(3) climbed
(4) had climbed
= 1
~ A longer action in progress that is interrupted by a short past action takes the past continuous (was + -ing): “As she was climbing the stairs, she slipped and fell.”

Q 6 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct option to fill in the gaps :
The house ______ to him since his birth.
(1) belong
(2) belonged
(3) had belonged
(4) has belonged
= 4
~ “Since his birth” shows a state that began in the past and continues now, which needs the present perfect: “The house has belonged to him since his birth.” “Belong” is a stative verb, so it is not used in the continuous.

Q 7 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct option to fill in the gaps :
We ______ shopping before it started raining.
(1) finishing
(2) had finished
(3) finish
(4) would finish
= 2
~ Of two past actions, the one that happened first takes the past perfect: we had finished shopping (earlier) before it started raining (later, simple past).

Q 8 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct option to fill in the gaps :
She ______ me since 2004.
(1) knows
(2) know
(3) has known
(4) had known
= 3
~ “Since + a point of time” (since 2004) with a situation continuing to the present needs the present perfect: “She has known me since 2004.” The past perfect “had known” would need a later past reference point.

Q 9 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct option to fill in the gaps :
Last night, the dog ______ for a long time.
(1) barked
(2) is barking
(3) has barked
(4) barking
= 1
~ “Last night” is a definite, finished past time, so the simple past “barked” is used. The present perfect and present continuous cannot go with a definite past time, and “barking” alone has no auxiliary.

Q 10 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct option to fill in the gaps :
I ______ she will be late.
(1) won’t think
(2) thinking
(3) don’t think
(4) will think
= 3
~ An opinion held now takes the simple present of “think”, and English normally puts the negative on “think”: “I don’t think she will be late.” “Won’t think” and “will think” wrongly make the opinion future, and “thinking” has no auxiliary.

(3) over
(4) from

Q 12 | eng | Grammar

(2) to
(3) of
(4) from

Q 13 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct option to fill in the gaps :
He decided to enter ______ a new course of life.
(1) in
(2) into
(3) over
(4) upon
= 4
~ “Enter upon (or on)” means to begin something new – a career, a course of life, a term of office. “Enter into” is used with agreements, contracts or discussions.

Q 14 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct option to fill in the gaps :
She was overcome ______ fatigue and went to bed.
(1) by
(2) to
(3) of
(4) in
= 1
~ “Overcome by (or with)” an emotion or physical state means overpowered by it: overcome by fatigue, overcome by grief. Of the options, only “by” fits.

Q 15 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct option to fill in the gaps :
There is no limit ______ the wants of man.
(1) over
(2) to
(3) upon
(4) for
= 2
~ The noun “limit” takes the preposition “to”: there is no limit to something. So: “There is no limit to the wants of man.”

Q 16 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct option to fill in the gaps :
He is born ______ an intelligent mother.
(1) to
(2) with
(3) into
(4) by
= 1
~ A child is “born to” (or “born of”) a parent. “Born into” is used with a family, class or condition (born into wealth), and “born with” with a quality one has from birth.

Q 17 | eng | Grammar

Have you caught up ______ your pending work?

(4) with

Q 26 | eng | Vocabulary
Choose the correct meaning for the words given below.
Agnostic
(1) One who is sure about God’s existence
(2) One who is aware about God’s existence
(3) One who is not sure about God’s existence
(4) One who is not aware about God’s existence
= 2
~ The standard meaning is option (C): an agnostic is one who is not sure whether God exists, holding that it cannot be known. One who denies God’s existence is an atheist; one who believes in God is a theist. MPSC’s key gives (B), “aware about God’s existence”, which does not match the dictionary meaning. (Official MPSC key; some sources differ.)

Q 27 | eng | Vocabulary
Choose the correct meaning for the words given below.
Altruist
(1) A hater of mankind
(2) A lover of mankind
(3) One who is selfish
(4) One who is cruel
= 2
~ An altruist is selflessly concerned for the welfare of others; of the options, “a lover of mankind” comes closest (strictly, that phrase defines a philanthropist). A hater of mankind is a misanthrope, and a selfish person is an egoist.

Q 28 | eng | Vocabulary
Choose the correct meaning for the words given below.
Cacographist
(1) One who is good at spellings
(2) One who has good handwriting
(3) One who is bad at spellings
(4) One who has bad handwriting
= 3
~ Cacography (Greek kakos, “bad” + graphia, “writing”) covers both bad spelling and bad handwriting. In the one-word-substitution lists used in Indian exams, however, a cacographist (or cacographer) is “one who is bad at spelling”, so (C) is the expected answer. One with beautiful handwriting is a calligraphist.

Q 41 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct sentence from the following.
(1) It’s high time we play a match.
(2) It’s high time we played a match.
(3) It’s high time we will play a match.
(4) It’s high time we started playing.
= 2
~ After “It’s (high) time”, English uses the past simple with a present meaning: “It’s high time we played a match.” The present (A) and future (C) forms are wrong. (D) follows the same rule and is also correct, but (A)–(C) are all versions of “we … a match”, and (B) is the correct version of that sentence, so it is the expected answer.

Q 42 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct sentence from the following.
(1) He was surrounded over a crowd of well-wishers.
(2) He was surrounded by a crowd of well-wishers.
(3) He was surrounded in a crowd of well-wishers.
(4) He was surrounded under a crowd of well-wishers.
= 2
~ “Surround” in the passive takes “by” (the doer of the action): “He was surrounded by a crowd of well-wishers.” Over, in and under do not go with “surrounded”.

Q 43 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct sentence from the following.
(1) Anyone of the two girls can solve this puzzle.
(2) Neither can solve this puzzle of the two girls.
(3) Either of the two girls can solve this puzzle.
(4) None of the two girls can solve this puzzle.
= 3
~ “Either” (one or the other) is used for two persons or things; “anyone” and “none” are used for more than two. (B) misplaces “of the two girls”. So: “Either of the two girls can solve this puzzle.”

Q 44 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct sentence from the following.
(1) Would you mind to work with me?
(2) Would you mind working with me?
(3) Would you mind and work with me?
(4) Would you mind in working with me?
= 2
~ The verb “mind” is followed by a gerund (-ing form), not a to-infinitive, and needs no linking word: “Would you mind working with me?”

Q 45 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct sentence from the following.
(1) Are you hearing a strange noise?
(2) Do you hear a strange noise?
(3) Will you hear a strange noise?
(4) Do you hearing a strange noise?
= 2
~ “Hear” is a stative verb of perception and is normally not used in the continuous, so the simple present question “Do you hear a strange noise?” is correct. (D) wrongly mixes “do” with an -ing form.

Q 46 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct sentence from the following.
(1) She left yesterday, isn’t she?
(2) She left yesterday, did she?
(3) She left yesterday, didn’t she?
(4) She left yesterday, is she?
= 3
~ A positive statement takes a negative question tag, formed with the auxiliary of the verb’s tense. “Left” is past simple, so the auxiliary is “did”: “She left yesterday, didn’t she?”

Q 47 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct sentence from the following.
(1) Do you know him? No, I do.
(2) Do you know him? No, I did.
(3) Do you know him? No, I don’t.
(4) Do you know him? No, I didn’t.
= 3
~ A short answer repeats the auxiliary of the question in the same tense, and “No” takes the negative form: “Do you know him?” → “No, I don’t.” “Did/didn’t” changes the tense.

Q 48 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct sentence from the following.
(1) I am elder to you, aren’t I?
(2) I am elder to you, am I?
(3) I am elder to you, didn’t I?
(4) I am elder to you, amn’t I?
= 1
~ The negative question tag for “I am” is the special form “aren’t I?” (“amn’t I” is not standard). A positive statement needs a negative tag, so “am I?” is wrong, and “didn’t” does not match “am”.

Q 49 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct sentence from the following.
(1) The train arrives at the platform 7.
(2) The train arrives at platform 7.
(3) The train arrives on the platform 7.
(4) The train arrives on platform 7.
= 4
~ No article goes before a noun followed by a number (platform 7, room 12, gate 3), which rules out (A) and (C). A platform is treated as a surface, which takes “on” (on the platform, like on the floor), so the key takes (D). “At platform 7” is also common, e.g. in station announcements.

Q 50 | eng | Grammar
Choose the correct sentence from the following.
(1) What is his opinion about the work that you have completed?
(2) What is his opinion about the work which you completed have?
(3) What is his opinion about the work when you have completed?
(4) What is his opinion about the work you completed?
= 1
~ (A) is the full standard form: the relative pronoun “that” introduces the clause, with the present perfect “have completed” for work just finished. (B) has wrong word order (“completed have”) and (C) wrongly uses “when” for a thing. (D), which drops “that” and uses the simple past, is also acceptable, but (A) is the full form the options are built on.

Q 51 | geo | Indian & World Geography
What percentage of India’s land area is plain area?
(1) 40%
(2) 41%
(3) 42%
(4) 43%
= 4
~ NCERT (Class 10, ‘Resources and Development’) puts about 43% of India’s land area under plains, about 30% under mountains and about 27% under plateaus. The plains – mainly the Northern (Indo-Gangetic) Plains and the coastal plains – are the largest share and support most of India’s agriculture and industry.

Q 52 | geo | Indian & World Geography
Which type of soil is ideal for growing cotton?
(1) Regur soil
(2) Laterite soil
(3) Desert soil
(4) Mountainous soil
= 1
~ Regur is the black soil of the Deccan, formed from weathered basalt (Deccan Trap) and called ‘black cotton soil’. Its clayey texture holds moisture well and it is rich in lime, iron, magnesia and potash, which suits cotton grown in Maharashtra, Gujarat and Madhya Pradesh.

Q 54 | his | Indian History & Culture
What was the main objective of the Civil Disobedience Movement?

(2) To promote education

Q 56 | his | Indian History & Culture
Who gave the slogan ‘Inquilab Zindabad’?
(1) Bhagat Singh
(2) Maulana Hasrat Mohani
(3) Lala Lajpat Rai
(4) Subhash Chandra Bose

Q 57 | geo | Indian & World Geography
Which State in India is known for its laterite soil?
(1) Uttar Pradesh
(2) Bihar
(3) Rajasthan
(4) Meghalaya
= 4
~ Laterite soil develops under heavy rainfall with alternate wet and dry spells, where intense leaching removes lime and silica and leaves iron and aluminium oxides. Such conditions occur on the Meghalaya plateau (and in Kerala, Karnataka and the hills of Odisha and Assam), whereas Uttar Pradesh and Bihar have mainly alluvial soil and Rajasthan desert soil.

Q 58 | geo | Indian & World Geography
What is the total geographical area of India?

Q 59 | geo | Indian & World Geography
Which type of soil is most common in Northern India?
(1) Black soil
(2) Laterite soil
(3) Alluvial soil
(4) Red soil
= 3
~ The Northern Plains are built of alluvium deposited by the Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra river systems, so alluvial soil dominates Northern India. It is the most widespread soil in the country and, being very fertile, supports rice, wheat, sugarcane and pulses.

Q 60 | geo | Indian & World Geography
Which State has the largest area under permanent forests?
(1) Bihar
(2) Kerala
(3) Madhya Pradesh
(4) Uttar Pradesh
= 3
~ Permanent forests are the reserved and protected forests kept for timber, forest produce and protection. As NCERT notes, Madhya Pradesh has the largest area under permanent forests, which make up about 75% of its total forest area.

Q 61 | eco | Indian Economy
Which sector contributes the most to India’s GDP?
(1) Primary sector
(2) Secondary sector
(3) Tertiary sector
(4) All of the above
= 3
~ The tertiary (services) sector – trade, transport, communication, finance, real estate, public administration and other services – contributes over half of India’s Gross Value Added, more than the primary (agriculture) and secondary (industry) sectors put together.

Q 62 | eco | Indian Economy
What is the main objective of the National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development (NABARD)?
(1) To provide credit to industries
(2) To provide credit to agriculture and rural development
(3) To provide credit to services sector
(4) To provide credit to infrastructure development
= 2
~ NABARD, set up on 12 July 1982 by an Act of Parliament, is India’s apex development bank for agriculture and rural development. It refinances co-operative banks, Regional Rural Banks and commercial banks for farm and rural credit and funds rural infrastructure.

Q 63 | eco | Indian Economy
Which economic policy is associated with liberalization, privatization and globalization?
(1) LPG policy
(2) Fiscal policy
(3) Monetary policy
(4) Trade policy
= 1
~ LPG stands for Liberalisation, Privatisation and Globalisation – the New Economic Policy of 1991 under Prime Minister P. V. Narasimha Rao and Finance Minister Manmohan Singh. Fiscal policy concerns taxes and government spending, and monetary policy concerns money supply and interest rates.

Q 64 | eco | Indian Economy
Which sector is known for providing services such as healthcare, education and finance?
(1) Primary sector
(2) Secondary sector
(3) Tertiary sector
(4) Construction sector
= 3
~ The tertiary or service sector produces services rather than goods – healthcare, education, banking and finance, trade, transport and communication. The primary sector draws on natural resources (farming, mining) and the secondary sector makes goods (manufacturing, construction).

Q 65 | eco | Indian Economy
Which institution provides financial assistance to small industries in India?
(1) Small Industries Development Bank of India (SIDBI)
(2) Reserve Bank of India (RBI)
(3) Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)
(4) Ministry of Finance
= 1
~ SIDBI, set up on 2 April 1990 under the SIDBI Act, 1989, is the principal financial institution for promoting, financing and developing micro, small and medium enterprises (MSMEs). The RBI is the central bank and SEBI regulates the securities market.

Q 66 | pol | Indian Polity & Governance
What is the system of government in India?
