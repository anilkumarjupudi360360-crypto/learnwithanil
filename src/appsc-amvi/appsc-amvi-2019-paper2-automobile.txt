Q 1 | thm | Thermodynamics
Which of the following is true for open system?
= Mass can transfer, energy can transfer
~ An open system (control volume) is one whose boundary allows both mass and energy (heat and work) to cross, for example an engine cylinder during intake and exhaust, a compressor, a turbine or a nozzle. A closed system allows only energy transfer with a fixed mass (a piston-cylinder with the valves shut), and an isolated system allows neither mass nor energy to cross its boundary.

Q 2 | thm | Thermodynamics
A piston cylinder contains air at 600 kPa, 290 K and a volume of 0.01m^3. A constant pressure process gives 60 kJ of work out. Find the final volume of the air.
= 0.11 m^3
~ In a constant-pressure process the boundary work is W = p(V₂ − V₁). So 60 kJ = 600 kPa × (V₂ − 0.01) m³, giving V₂ − 0.01 = 60/600 = 0.1 m³ and V₂ = 0.11 m³. The temperature (290 K) is not needed for the volume; it would be used to find the mass of air, m = pV/RT = 600 × 0.01/(0.287 × 290) ≈ 0.072 kg.

Q 3 | thm | Thermodynamics
As per kinetic theory of gases, value of gamma for monoatomic gases, diatomic gases and polyatomic gases are
= 5/3, 7/5, 4/3
~ Kinetic theory gives γ = cp/cv = 1 + 2/f, where f is the number of degrees of freedom of the molecule. A monatomic gas (He, Ar) has 3 translational degrees, so γ = 5/3 ≈ 1.67; a diatomic gas (O₂, N₂, air) has 5 (3 translational + 2 rotational), so γ = 7/5 = 1.4; a non-linear polyatomic gas has 6, so γ = 8/6 = 4/3 ≈ 1.33 (vibrational modes neglected).

Q 4 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
__________ is the compression ratio for positive ignition (PI) engine.
= 6 to 10
~ Positive (spark) ignition engines are usually given compression ratios of about 6 to 10, because a higher ratio raises the temperature and pressure of the end gas and causes knock (detonation) with ordinary petrol. Compression ignition (diesel) engines use about 16 to 22, since they need hot compressed air for self-ignition of the fuel. Modern petrol engines with knock sensors and direct injection often run above 10:1.

Q 5 | thm | Thermodynamics
The ratio of specific heat at constant volume (cv) and specific heat at constant pressure (cp) is
= < 1
~ cp − cv = R, so cp is always larger than cv: at constant pressure part of the heat supplied does expansion work, while at constant volume all of it raises the internal energy. Hence cp/cv = γ > 1 (1.4 for air), and the ratio asked, cv/cp = 1/γ, is less than 1 (about 0.714 for air).

Q 6 | ice | Fuel Supply & Combustion
One kg of hydrogen requires __________ kg of oxygen to produce ---- kg water.
= 8,9
~ The combustion equation is 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. By mass, 4 kg of hydrogen combines with 32 kg of oxygen to form 36 kg of water. Dividing by 4, 1 kg of hydrogen needs 8 kg of oxygen and produces 9 kg of water. This 9 kg per kg figure is used to find the water in the products when working out the higher and lower calorific values of a fuel.

Q 7 | flu | Fluid Mechanics
The value of 1 cm of Hg is equal to
= 1333 N/m²
~ The pressure of a liquid column is p = ρgh. For mercury (ρ ≈ 13,600 kg/m³), 1 cm Hg = 13,600 × 9.81 × 0.01 ≈ 1334 N/m²; with the standard values (13,595 kg/m³ and 9.80665 m/s²) it is 1333.2 N/m², i.e. about 1333 Pa. Hence 76 cm Hg, the standard atmosphere, is 1.013 × 10⁵ N/m².

Q 8 | flu | Fluid Mechanics
Which of the following is correct pressure co-relation?
= Absolute pressure = Gauge pressure + Atmospheric pressure
~ A pressure gauge reads pressure relative to the local atmosphere, so absolute pressure = gauge pressure + atmospheric pressure. Below atmospheric pressure, absolute pressure = atmospheric pressure − vacuum pressure. Thermodynamic relations such as pV = mRT always use absolute pressure; for example, a tyre inflated to 2 bar gauge holds air at about 3.01 bar absolute.

Q 9 | thm | Thermodynamics
Availability of a system is depending on the
= State of a system and surrounding
~ Availability (exergy) is the maximum useful work obtainable as a system is brought into equilibrium with its surroundings (the dead state). It therefore depends both on the state of the system (p, T, composition) and on the state of the surroundings (p₀, T₀); for a closed system, availability = (U − U₀) + p₀(V − V₀) − T₀(S − S₀). A system already at the dead state has zero availability.

Q 10 | thm | Thermodynamics
Calculate the enthalpy of 25 kg of fluid that occupies a volume of 12000 litre, if the internal energy is 28000000 J /kg and the pressure is 0.3 MN/m²
= 703.6 MJ
~ Enthalpy H = U + pV. The internal energy U = 25 kg × 28,000,000 J/kg = 700 MJ. The volume is 12,000 litres = 12 m³, so pV = 0.3 × 10⁶ N/m² × 12 m³ = 3.6 MJ. Therefore H = 700 + 3.6 = 703.6 MJ, and the specific enthalpy is 703.6/25 = 28.144 MJ/kg.

Q 11 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
The process of cleaning the cylinder, after the expansion stroke, is called
= Scavenging
~ Scavenging is the clearing of burnt exhaust gases from the cylinder at the end of the expansion stroke and their replacement by fresh charge (air or air-fuel mixture). It is essential in two-stroke engines, where the incoming charge from the crankcase or a blower pushes the exhaust out through the ports (cross, loop or uniflow scavenging); in four-stroke engines the valve overlap gives a similar effect.

Q 12 | ice | Fuel Supply & Combustion
Which of the following is an advantage of a compression swirl?
= All of the given options
~ Compression swirl is produced in indirect-injection diesel engines (for example the Ricardo Comet swirl chamber) when air is forced through a tangential passage into the chamber during compression. Advantages given in textbooks include a very strong, organised swirl for rapid mixing and combustion, suitability for high speeds, a low injection pressure with a single-hole (pintle) nozzle, smoother running and no loss of volumetric efficiency, unlike intake-generated swirl; the answer means every option listed was such an advantage. Its drawbacks are higher heat loss, poor cold starting (glow plugs needed) and higher fuel consumption.

Q 13 | ice | Fuel Supply & Combustion
In a 4- stroke compression ignition (CI), the fuel is injected about
= 15° before top dead centre
~ In a four-stroke CI engine, fuel injection begins towards the end of the compression stroke, typically about 10–20° of crank angle (commonly quoted as 15°) before TDC, so that the ignition delay is over and combustion begins near TDC; injection continues a little past TDC. Injecting too early lengthens the delay and causes diesel knock, while injecting too late reduces power and increases smoke and fuel consumption.

Q 14 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions
A cetane number of C₁₀H₇CH₃is __________
= 0
~ Cetane number rates the ignition quality of diesel fuel against two reference fuels: n-cetane (n-hexadecane, C₁₆H₃₄), given 100, and alpha-methylnaphthalene (C₁₀H₇CH₃), an aromatic with very poor self-ignition, given 0. A fuel's cetane number is the percentage of cetane in the reference blend that matches it in a standard engine. In the current ASTM D613 test, heptamethylnonane (cetane number 15) has replaced alpha-methylnaphthalene as the low-reference fuel.

Q 15 | ice | Fuel Supply & Combustion
In S.I. engine, Raising the coolant temperature__________
= Decreases delay period
~ Ignition delay (lag) in an SI engine is the time from the spark to a noticeable pressure rise, and it is shortened by a higher charge temperature. Raising the coolant temperature keeps the cylinder walls hotter, so the charge is hotter at the time of the spark and the delay period decreases; the same effect increases the knock tendency. A higher compression ratio and a slightly rich mixture also shorten the delay.

Q 16 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
The ratio of the actual volume of gas taken into the cylinder during suction stroke to the piston displacement volume is called
= Volumetric efficiency
~ Volumetric efficiency is the actual volume of charge drawn in during the suction stroke (measured at ambient or reference conditions) divided by the swept (piston displacement) volume. It is below 1 in naturally aspirated engines, typically about 70–90%, because of intake restrictions, heating of the charge and residual exhaust gas, and can exceed 1 with supercharging or turbocharging. It largely decides the power an engine of a given size can produce.

Q 17 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
Morse test is used to calculate __________
= Brake Power
~ The Morse test is used on multi-cylinder engines. With the engine at constant speed, each cylinder is cut out in turn, the dynamometer load is reduced to restore the speed, and the brake power is measured each time. The indicated power of the cut cylinder = BP with all cylinders firing − BP with that cylinder cut; their sum gives the engine's IP, and hence friction power (IP − BP) and mechanical efficiency. Brake power is what the test measures, but most texts say it is used to find indicated power. (Official final key; some sources differ.)

Q 18 | ice | Fuel Supply & Combustion
__________ is not an integral part of a carburettor
= Fuel Pump
~ A carburettor meters petrol and mixes it with air. Its integral parts include the float chamber with float and needle valve, the venturi (choke tube), the main jet and discharge nozzle, the idling and accelerating systems, the choke valve and the throttle valve. The fuel pump (mechanical, driven from the camshaft, or electric) is a separate unit of the fuel supply system that lifts petrol from the tank to the float chamber.

Q 19 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
Usually crankcase is made of -------
= both Aluminium and Cast iron
~ The crankcase houses the crankshaft and forms the oil sump; in most engines its upper part is cast in one piece with the cylinder block. Grey cast iron, cheap, rigid and wear-resistant, is the traditional material and is still used in heavy commercial-vehicle engines, while aluminium alloy is used in most modern car and two-wheeler engines to save weight and dissipate heat better. The lower oil pan is often pressed steel.

Q 20 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
Inside a cylinder, dissociation is __________ temperature.
= Increases with
~ Dissociation is the breaking up of combustion products at high temperature, mainly CO₂ ⇌ CO + ½O₂ and H₂O ⇌ H₂ + ½O₂. These reactions absorb heat, so dissociation increases with temperature and becomes significant at the peak cylinder temperatures reached with near-stoichiometric mixtures. It lowers the maximum temperature and pressure and hence the work and efficiency of the cycle. Higher pressure suppresses it, because dissociation increases the number of moles.

Q 21 | mec | Theory of Machines
__________ is used to connect non-intersecting and non-parallel shafts
= Spiral gears
~ Shafts that neither intersect nor are parallel (skew shafts) are connected by spiral gears, that is, crossed helical gears, which have point contact and suit light loads such as an oil-pump or distributor drive taken off the camshaft. Worm and worm-wheel and hypoid gears also connect skew shafts. Parallel shafts use spur or helical gears, and intersecting shafts use bevel gears.

= All of the given options
~ Compared with belt, rope and chain drives, a gear drive transmits an exact (positive) velocity ratio without slip, can transmit large power, suits small centre distances with a compact layout, has high efficiency and gives reliable service; the key's "All of the given options" means the choices listed were such advantages. Q 23 | mec | Theory of Machines
The diametral pitch of a gear is given by,
Where, D=Pitch circle diameter T= Number of teeth,
= T/D
~ Diametral pitch is the number of teeth per unit length of pitch-circle diameter: Pd = T/D (teeth per inch in the British system). It is the reciprocal of the module m = D/T (in mm), and the circular pitch is pc = πD/T, so pc × Pd = π. Two gears can mesh only if they have the same diametral pitch (or module).

Q 24 | mec | Theory of Machines
Contact ratio is known as __________
= Length of arc of contact / the circular pitch of a gear
~ Contact ratio = length of the arc of contact ÷ circular pitch; it equals the average number of pairs of teeth in contact. It must be greater than 1 so that a new pair of teeth engages before the previous pair separates, giving smooth, continuous transmission, and designers normally keep it well above 1. A higher contact ratio shares the load between more teeth and reduces noise.

Q 25 | mec | Theory of Machines
Mitre gears are used for
= equal speed
~ Mitre gears are bevel gears of equal size (the same number of teeth) connecting two intersecting shafts at right angles. With equal teeth the speed ratio is 1:1, so they turn the drive through 90° without changing the speed. Bevel gears of unequal size are used when a change of speed is also wanted, as in the crown wheel and pinion of a vehicle's final drive.

Q 26 | mec | Theory of Machines
In worm and wheel, the shaft axes are
= Perpendicular to each other
~ In a worm and worm-wheel drive the shafts do not intersect, and their axes are normally at right angles (perpendicular) in different planes. A single-start worm advances the wheel by one tooth per revolution, so a high reduction ratio is obtained in one stage and the drive can be self-locking. Automobile uses include worm-type steering gears and worm-drive rear axles.

The number of teeth on each of the two equal spur gears in mesh are 35 and the module is 6 mm then, __________ is the circular pitch.
= 18.8

Q 28 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions

= C₈H₁₈
~ The question means iso-octane (printed "is o-octane"). Iso-octane is 2,2,4-trimethylpentane, an isomer of octane with the alkane formula CₙH₂ₙ₊₂, i.e. C₈H₁₈. Q 29 | mec | Engineering Mechanics
Fluid friction between two lubricated surfaces are due to
= Viscosity and Oiliness
~ Friction between lubricated surfaces depends on the film. With a thick film the surfaces are fully separated and the resistance comes from shearing the oil, so it depends on its viscosity (fluid or hydrodynamic friction). When the film becomes thin (boundary lubrication), friction depends on oiliness, the oil's ability to cling to the metal and form an adsorbed layer. The key takes both properties together.

Q 30 | mec | Engineering Mechanics
Coefficient of Friction is,__________
= limiting friction (F) / Normal reaction (R_N) between the two bodies
~ The coefficient of friction μ = F/R_N is the ratio of the limiting force of friction (the maximum friction, just before sliding begins) to the normal reaction between the surfaces. It also equals tan φ, where φ is the angle of friction between the resultant reaction and the normal. It depends on the materials and the condition of the surfaces, not on the area of contact.

Q 31 | mec | Machine Design
Nominal diameter and core diameter of the screw thread is denoted by d₀ and dc respectively, then the mean diameter of the screw is given by __________
= (d0 + dc)/2
~ For a screw thread with nominal (major) diameter d₀ and core (minor) diameter dc, the mean diameter is their average, d = (d₀ + dc)/2. For a square thread of pitch p, dc = d₀ − p, so d = d₀ − p/2. The mean diameter is used to find the helix angle (tan α = p/πd) and the effort and torque in screw-jack and power-screw calculations.

Q 32 | mec | Theory of Machines
In a screw jack, the effort required to lift the load S is __________
Where, A = Helix angle, and B = Angle of friction
= P = S tan (A+ B)
~ Lifting a load with a screw jack is like pushing it up an inclined plane whose angle is the helix angle α. With friction angle φ (tan φ = μ), the effort applied horizontally at the mean radius of the screw is P = W tan(α + φ), written here as P = S tan(A + B). For lowering, P = W tan(φ − α), and the efficiency is tan α/tan(α + φ), neglecting collar friction.

Q 34 | chs | Brakes
A vehicle’s brake efficiency is affected by
= Both Vehicle weight and braking effort
~ Brake efficiency is the retarding (braking) force produced, expressed as a percentage of the vehicle's weight: η = (F/W) × 100, which also equals (deceleration/g) × 100. It is therefore affected both by the vehicle weight, including its load, and by the braking effort developed at the wheels; a deceleration of 0.5 g, for instance, means 50% brake efficiency. Q 35 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
The Indicated power of an engine is __________ the brake power
= >
~ Indicated power is the power developed by the gases on the pistons, while brake power is the power delivered at the crankshaft. Part of the IP is used to overcome friction and drive the accessories (friction power), so IP = BP + FP and IP is greater than BP. Mechanical efficiency ηm = BP/IP, typically about 80–90% at full load.

= 42.5 MJ/kg

Q 38 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
4 cylinder in-line engine has a __________ firing order
= 1-3-4-2
~ In a four-cylinder in-line engine the crank throws of cylinders 1 and 4 are at 180° to those of cylinders 2 and 3, so one cylinder fires every 180° of crank rotation. The possible firing orders are 1-3-4-2 (the most common) and 1-2-4-3. Both avoid firing adjacent cylinders one after the other, giving better balance and spreading the load on the main bearings.

Q 39 | thm | Thermodynamics
__________is known as entropy principle
= Second law of thermodynamics
~ The second law of thermodynamics leads to the principle of increase of entropy: the entropy of an isolated system (system plus surroundings) can never decrease. It increases in every real (irreversible) process and stays constant only in a reversible one (ΔS_isolated ≥ 0). The principle follows from the Clausius inequality, ∮δQ/T ≤ 0, and fixes the direction in which natural processes occur.

Q 40 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions

Q 44 | ice | Fuel Supply & Combustion
__________ (TBFI) are a substitution of electromechanical replacement for the carburettor
= Throttle Body Fuel Injector
~ Throttle body fuel injection (TBFI or TBI, a single-point system) replaced the carburettor with one or two electronically controlled solenoid injectors mounted in a throttle body above the throttle valve. The ECU sets the injector opening time from sensor signals, giving more precise mixture control than a carburettor. Q 45 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions
Complete a catalytic reaction: NO + CO = __________ + CO₂
= ½ N₂
~ In a three-way catalytic converter, rhodium promotes the reduction of NO by CO: NO + CO → ½N₂ + CO₂ (or 2NO + 2CO → N₂ + 2CO₂). Q 46 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions

= Iron
~ A three-way catalytic converter uses precious (noble) metals as catalysts: platinum and palladium to oxidise CO and HC, and rhodium to reduce NOₓ. Q 47 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions
__________ is used to control the particulates from a diesel engine
= Diesel Particulate Filter
~ A diesel particulate filter (DPF), usually a wall-flow ceramic (cordierite or silicon carbide) honeycomb with alternately plugged channels, traps the soot particles in diesel exhaust and removes over 90% of the particulate mass. The trapped soot is burnt off periodically (regeneration) by raising the exhaust temperature or with the help of a catalyst. Since BS-VI emission norms came into force on 1 April 2020, DPFs have become standard on Indian diesel vehicles.

Q 48 | thm | Thermodynamics
An Isobaric process is a
= Constant pressure process
~ An isobaric process takes place at constant pressure (Greek isos, equal, and baros, weight). For an ideal gas V/T stays constant (Charles's law), the work done is W = p(V₂ − V₁), and the heat supplied Q = mcp(T₂ − T₁) equals the change in enthalpy. Isochoric means constant volume, isothermal constant temperature and isentropic constant entropy (reversible adiabatic).

Q 49 | mec | Strength of Materials
1 MPa (Mega Pascal): __________ N/mm²
= 1 ×10^0
~ 1 MPa = 10⁶ Pa = 10⁶ N/m². Since 1 m² = 10⁶ mm², 10⁶ N/m² = 10⁶ N ÷ 10⁶ mm² = 1 N/mm², i.e. 1 × 10⁰ N/mm². That is why stresses and pressures in machine design are quoted interchangeably in MPa or N/mm²; for example, a yield stress of 250 MPa is 250 N/mm².

Q 50 | mec | Strength of Materials
__________ is correct expression for Poisson’s ratio
= Lateral strain / Longitudinal strain
~ Poisson's ratio (μ or ν) is the ratio of lateral strain to longitudinal (linear) strain within the elastic limit, taken as a positive number because the two strains have opposite signs. It is about 0.25–0.33 for most metals (about 0.3 for steel) and cannot exceed 0.5 for an isotropic material. It links the elastic constants, for example E = 2G(1 + μ).

Q 51 | mec | Strength of Materials
Hook’s law holds good up to
= Elastic Limit
~ Hooke's law states that, within the elastic limit, stress is directly proportional to strain (σ = Eε, E being Young's modulus). Strictly, proportionality holds up to the limit of proportionality, which for most metals lies very close to (just below) the elastic limit, so textbooks usually say Hooke's law holds good up to the elastic limit. Beyond it the material no longer returns fully to its original shape.

Q 52 | mec | Strength of Materials

~ Normal strain is the change in length per unit original length (ε = δL/L) produced by a normal (direct) stress acting perpendicular to a section. It may be tensile strain (increase in length) or compressive strain (decrease in length), and it occurs both as longitudinal strain along the load and as lateral strain across it. Q 53 | thm | Thermodynamics
Which of the following property is dependent on a mass of a thermodynamic
system?
= Volume
~ Properties that depend on the mass (size) of a system are extensive properties, such as volume, total energy, internal energy, enthalpy and entropy. Properties independent of mass, such as pressure, temperature and density, are intensive. Dividing an extensive property by the mass gives a specific (intensive) property, e.g. specific volume v = V/m. Volume is therefore the mass-dependent property.

Q 54 | thm | Thermodynamics
Law: “The change of internal energy of a perfect gas is directly proportional to the
change of temperature” is known as
= Joule’s law
~ Joule's law states that the internal energy of a perfect gas is a function of temperature only, so its change is directly proportional to the change of temperature: dU = m·Cv·dT. Joule showed this with his free-expansion experiment, in which a gas expanding into a vacuum showed no change of temperature, so internal energy does not depend on pressure or volume.

Q 55 | thm | Thermodynamics
Which of the following is the correct expression to corelate gas constant (R) and
constant volume specific heat (CV)?
= γ = 1 + R/CV
~ For a perfect gas the gas constant is the difference of the specific heats, R = Cp − Cv, and the ratio of specific heats is γ = Cp/Cv. Dividing R = Cp − Cv by Cv gives R/Cv = γ − 1, i.e. γ = 1 + R/Cv, or Cv = R/(γ − 1). For air, R = 0.287 kJ/kg K and Cv = 0.718 kJ/kg K, giving γ ≈ 1.4.

Q 56 | thm | Thermodynamics
Efficiency of a cycle is considered as
= Workdone / Heat Supplied
~ The efficiency of a heat-engine cycle (thermal efficiency) is the ratio of the net work done to the heat supplied: η = W/Q₁ = (Q₁ − Q₂)/Q₁, where Q₂ is the heat rejected. Because some heat must always be rejected (second law), the efficiency of any cycle is less than 1; the Carnot cycle gives the highest possible value between two temperatures, 1 − T₂/T₁.

Q 57 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
The volumetric efficiency of the SI engine is comparatively
= Lower than CI engine
~ Volumetric efficiency is the ratio of the charge actually drawn in to the swept volume at intake conditions. In an SI engine the throttle valve restricts the intake, the carburettor venturi adds resistance and the mixture is often preheated, so less charge enters; an unthrottled CI engine draws in air only, through less restriction. Hence the volumetric efficiency of an SI engine is generally lower than that of a CI engine.

Q 132 | ael | Starting & Ignition
Cruising conditions require the ignition timing to be:
= advanced
~ At cruising (part throttle, light load) the manifold vacuum is high and the charge is lean and diluted with residual exhaust gas, so it burns slowly. The spark must therefore occur earlier, i.e. the ignition timing is advanced; this is done by the vacuum advance unit in conventional distributors and by the ECU map in electronic ignition. At full throttle the vacuum falls and the advance is reduced to avoid knocking.

Q 133 | ael | Electronics, Sensors & ECU
An injector pulse width, in milliseconds, is commonly:
= 2.0–3.50
~ Injector pulse width is the time for which the ECU energises a solenoid injector and holds it open, so at a constant fuel pressure the pulse width sets the quantity of fuel injected. It is only a few milliseconds, and the key takes 2.0–3.5 ms as the common value. The ECU lengthens the pulse for higher load, acceleration and cold starting, and shortens it or cuts it off on overrun.

Q 134 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions

= carbon dioxide and water
~ Complete combustion of a hydrocarbon fuel turns all its carbon into carbon dioxide and all its hydrogen into water vapour, for example C₈H₁₈ + 12.5 O₂ → 8 CO₂ + 9 H₂O. Q 135 | ice | Fuel Supply & Combustion

= multi-point system
~ In multi-point fuel injection (MPFI), each cylinder has its own injector in the intake manifold just upstream of the intake valve (port injection), controlled by the ECU. Q 136 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions

NOₓ
= 1000 ⁰C

Q 137 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions
Measurement of exhaust emissions, just after starting the engine from cold, gives

= the temperature of the catalyst is low
~ A catalytic converter works only after it reaches its light-off temperature (roughly 250–300 °C); below this, CO and HC pass through largely unconverted. Just after a cold start the catalyst is cold, and the engine also runs on a richer cold-start mixture, so tailpipe readings are above specification. Emission tests are therefore made with the engine and catalyst at normal operating temperature.

Q 139 | ael | Lighting, Instruments & Wiring
One characteristic of a thermal type fuel gauge is its:
= slow moving needle
~ A thermal (bimetal) fuel gauge has a heater winding on a bimetal strip linked to the needle; the float-operated rheostat in the tank unit varies the current and hence the heating and bending of the strip. Because the strip heats and cools slowly, the needle moves slowly and does not swing with fuel surge on bends or braking. Q 143 | ice | Fuel Supply & Combustion
Increase in jacket water temperature __________ the delay period
= Decreases
~ The delay period is the time between the start of fuel injection and the start of combustion in a CI engine. A higher jacket water temperature raises the temperature of the cylinder walls and of the compressed air, so the fuel droplets vaporise and reach self-ignition sooner; the delay period therefore decreases, giving smoother combustion and less diesel knock. A higher compression ratio, intake temperature and cetane number also shorten it.

Q 147 | chs | Clutch & Gearbox

= Road speed in highest gear / Road speed in lowest gear
~ The ratio span (spread) of a gearbox compares its extreme ratios. Q 148 | chs | Wheels & Tyres

= excessive camber
~ Camber is the outward or inward tilt of the wheel from the vertical, seen from the front. ~ An out-of-balance mass m on a rotating wheel produces a centrifugal force F = mv²/r = mω²r, where v is the linear speed of the mass and r its radius from the axis of rotation.
