Q 1 | thm | Thermodynamics
A system is in thermodynamic equilibrium when
(1) It exchanges mass but not energy with its surroundings
(2) It exchanges both mass and energy with its surroundings
(3) Macroscopic variables like pressure, volume and temperature do not change with time
(4) It exchanges energy but not mass with its surroundings
= 3
~ A system is in thermodynamic equilibrium when it is at the same time in thermal, mechanical and chemical equilibrium, so its macroscopic properties such as pressure, volume and temperature are uniform and do not change with time, with no net internal flows. Whether a system exchanges mass, energy or both with its surroundings only tells us whether it is open or closed; it does not decide whether the system is in equilibrium.

Q 2 | thm | Thermodynamics
The Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics states that if two systems are each in thermal equilibrium with a third system, then
(1) They are in mechanical equilibrium
(2) They are in chemical equilibrium
(3) They are in thermal equilibrium with each other
(4) They are in a state of total thermodynamic equilibrium
= 3
~ The Zeroth Law says that if bodies A and B are each in thermal equilibrium with a third body C, then A and B are in thermal equilibrium with each other: there is no net heat flow when they are brought into contact, because they are at the same temperature. It is the basis of temperature measurement, the third body being the thermometer. It says nothing about mechanical or chemical equilibrium.

Q 3 | thm | Thermodynamics
Which of the following is not an example of a pure substance ?
(1) Water (H₂O)
(2) Nitrogen (N₂)
(3) Air (a mixture of gases like O₂, N₂, Ar)
(4) Helium (He)
= 3
~ A pure substance has a fixed and uniform chemical composition throughout. Water, nitrogen and helium are single chemical species and qualify. Air is a mixture of nitrogen, oxygen, argon and other gases. It behaves like a pure substance only while it remains a gas of uniform composition; when it is partly liquefied, its components condense at different temperatures and the liquid and vapour have different compositions, so air is not a true pure substance.

Q 4 | thm | Thermodynamics
The Mollier diagram is a plot of
(1) Temperature and entropy
(2) Temperature and enthalpy
(3) Pressure and enthalpy
(4) Enthalpy and entropy
= 4
~ The Mollier diagram, devised by Richard Mollier, plots enthalpy (h) on the vertical axis against entropy (s) on the horizontal axis, usually for steam. On this h–s chart an isentropic expansion is a vertical line and the enthalpy drop can be read directly, which makes it convenient for nozzle and turbine calculations. The temperature–entropy plot is the T–s diagram, and the pressure–enthalpy (p–h) chart is mainly used for refrigerants.

Q 5 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
For a fixed amount of heat supplied and a fixed compression ratio, the thermal efficiency of the air-standard cycle follows the order
(1) Otto > Diesel > Dual
(2) Otto > Dual > Diesel
(3) Diesel > Otto > Dual
(4) Dual > Otto > Diesel
= 2
~ For the same compression ratio and the same heat input, the Otto cycle adds all its heat at constant volume at the end of compression, so the gas then expands through the full ratio, rejects the least heat and is the most efficient. The Diesel cycle adds heat at constant pressure while the gas expands, so it rejects the most heat. The dual cycle adds part at constant volume and part at constant pressure and lies between: η Otto > η Dual > η Diesel.

Q 6 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
The Stirling cycle consists of
(1) Two isothermal and two constant pressure processes
(2) Two isothermal and two constant volume processes
(3) Two isothermal and two adiabatic processes
(4) Two adiabatic processes and one constant volume processes
= 2
~ The Stirling cycle consists of isothermal compression, constant-volume heat addition, isothermal expansion and constant-volume heat rejection. With a perfect regenerator, which stores the heat rejected at constant volume and returns it later, its efficiency equals that of the Carnot cycle working between the same temperatures. Two isothermal and two constant-pressure processes make the Ericsson cycle, and two isothermal and two adiabatic processes make the Carnot cycle.

Q 7 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
The diagram factor is defined as the ratio of
(1) Brake power to indicated power
(2) Indicated work done to brake work done
(3) Area of the actual indicator diagram to the area of the theoretical indicator diagram
(4) Thermal efficiency to mechanical efficiency
= 3
~ Diagram factor = area of the actual indicator diagram ÷ area of the theoretical (hypothetical) indicator diagram, which equals the ratio of the actual to the theoretical mean effective pressure. It allows for losses such as valve throttling, heat transfer and the rounding of the diagram's corners, and it is always less than 1. The ratio of brake power to indicated power (option 1) is the mechanical efficiency.

Q 8 | mec | Theory of Machines
The radial distance from the pitch circle to the root of the tooth is known as the
(1) Addendum
(2) Clearance
(3) Dedendum
(4) Whole depth
= 3
~ In gear terminology the dedendum is the radial distance from the pitch circle to the root (dedendum) circle, while the addendum is the radial distance from the pitch circle to the tip (addendum) circle. The whole depth is addendum plus dedendum. Clearance is the radial gap between the tip of a tooth and the root of the mating gear, equal to the dedendum minus the addendum.

Q 9 | mec | Theory of Machines
What is the correct mathematical formula for the module of a gear ?
(Where D is the pitch circle diameter and N is the number of teeth)
(1) D/N
(2) D/(2N)
(3) 2D/N
(4) 3D/N
= 1
~ The module m is the pitch circle diameter divided by the number of teeth, m = D/N, usually expressed in millimetres. It is the reciprocal of the diametral pitch (N/D). The circular pitch is π times the module, p = πD/N = πm, and two gears can mesh only if they have the same module. For example, a gear with a pitch circle diameter of 100 mm and 50 teeth has a module of 2 mm.

Q 10 | mec | Theory of Machines
In a simple gear train, how is the driven gear's motion related to the driver gear if there is an odd number of gears ?
(1) Opposite direction
(2) Same direction
(3) Does not depends on gear ratio
(4) Depends on gear ratio
= 2
~ Each external mesh reverses the direction of rotation. With an odd number of gears in a simple train (for example driver, one idler and driven) there is an even number of meshes, so the driven gear turns in the same direction as the driver; with an even number of gears it turns the opposite way. Idlers change only the direction, not the speed ratio, which depends only on the teeth of the driver and the driven gear.

Q 11 | mec | Theory of Machines
In an epicyclic gear train, what is the purpose of the arm (or carrier) ?
(1) It is a fixed, stationary gear
(2) It carries the planet gears and rotates
(3) It is the primary output shaft
(4) It fixes the sun gear's position
= 2
~ In an epicyclic (planetary) gear train the arm, or planet carrier, holds the spindles of the planet gears and rotates about the common axis of the sun and ring (annulus) gears, so the planets spin on their own axes and also revolve round the sun gear. Holding one member and driving another gives different speed ratios, which is how automatic gearboxes and differentials use it. The carrier is not a gear, and it need not be fixed or be the output.

Q 12 | mec | Strength of Materials
What is the primary factor that distinguishes the torsional shear stress distribution in a solid shaft compared to a hollow shaft of the same outer diameter, when subjected to the same torque ?
(1) Shear stress is zero at the center of both shafts
(2) Shear stress is maximum at the center of the hollow shaft but zero at the center of the solid shaft
(3) Shear stress is zero at the inner surface of the hollow shaft but uniform across the solid shaft
(4) Shear stress is highest at the outer surface of the hollow shaft, while the solid shaft's stress increases linearly from the center to the outer surface
= 4
~ By the torsion equation τ/r = T/J, shear stress is proportional to the radius in both shafts. In a solid shaft it is zero at the axis and rises linearly to a maximum at the outer surface. A hollow shaft has no material near the axis, so its stress rises from a non-zero minimum at the inner surface to its maximum at the outer surface; for the same torque and outer diameter that maximum is higher, because the hollow shaft's J is smaller.

Q 13 | mec | Strength of Materials
For a solid shaft under pure torsion, how does the angle of twist (θ) change if the shaft's length (L) is doubled, while the polar moment of inertia (J) and modulus of rigidity (G) remain constant ?
(1) It remains the same
(2) It is halved
(3) It doubles
(4) It is quadrupled
= 3
~ From the torsion equation T/J = Gθ/L, the angle of twist is θ = TL/(GJ). With the same torque and with G and J unchanged, θ is directly proportional to the length, so doubling L doubles the angle of twist. In other words, the torsional stiffness of the shaft, GJ/L, is halved when its length is doubled.

Q 14 | mec | Machine Design
When a shaft experiences both a bending moment (M) and torque (T) simultaneously, what is the nature of the stresses induced ?
(1) Only shear stress due to torque is present
(2) Only normal stress due to bending is present
(3) Only shear stress due to bending is present
(4) Both normal stress (from bending) and shear stress (from torque) are developed in the shaft
= 4
~ The bending moment produces normal (tensile and compressive) bending stress, σ = 32M/(πd³) at the surface of a solid shaft, and the torque produces torsional shear stress, τ = 16T/(πd³). Shafts under this combined loading are designed using equivalent values: by the maximum shear stress theory the equivalent torque is Te = √(M² + T²), and by the maximum normal stress theory the equivalent bending moment is Me = ½[M + √(M² + T²)].

Q 15 | mec | Engineering Mechanics
A body slides down a 45° inclined plane in twice the time it would take on a frictionless surface.
What is the coefficient of kinetic friction, μₖ ?
(1) 0.33
(2) 0.75
(3) 0.5
(4) 1.0
= 2
~ For the same distance from rest, s = ½at², so taking twice the time means the acceleration is one quarter of the frictionless value: a = (g sin 45°)/4. With friction, a = g(sin θ − μₖ cos θ). Equating, sin θ − μₖ cos θ = ¼ sin θ, so μₖ = ¾ tan θ = 0.75 × tan 45° = 0.75.

Q 16 | mec | Machine Design
A screw is considered self-locking if
(1) The friction angle is less than the lead angle
(2) The friction angle is greater than the lead angle
(3) The friction angle is equal to the lead angle
(4) The friction angle is half the lead angle
= 2
~ For a square-threaded screw the effort needed to lower the load is P = W tan(φ − α), where φ is the friction angle (tan φ = μ) and α the lead (helix) angle. If φ > α this effort is positive, so the load cannot run down by itself when the effort is removed: the screw is self-locking. If φ < α the screw overhauls. A self-locking screw always has an efficiency below 50 %.

Q 17 | mec | Machine Design
What is the primary function of a screw jack ?
(1) To convert linear motion into rotary motion
(2) To convert rotary motion into linear motion to lift or lower heavy loads
(3) To measure the pitch of a screw
(4) To create a locking mechanism for a nut
= 2
~ A screw jack is a power screw: turning the handle (rotary motion) makes the screw or nut move axially, so a heavy load such as a vehicle can be lifted or lowered with a small effort. Square or trapezoidal threads are used, and the screw is made self-locking so that the load stays up when the effort is removed. Converting linear motion into rotary motion is the reverse of what it does.

Q 18 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions
Which statement correctly compares flash point and fire point ?
(1) The fire point is always lower than the flash point.
(2) The flash point and fire point are the same temperature.
(3) The fire point is typically higher than the flash point.
(4) The flash point is higher than fire point.
= 3
~ The flash point is the lowest temperature at which an oil or fuel gives off enough vapour to flash momentarily when a small flame is applied. The fire point is the lowest temperature at which the vapour, once ignited, keeps burning (for at least about five seconds). Sustained burning needs more vapour, and so a higher temperature, so the fire point is higher than the flash point.

Q 19 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions
Which of the following fuels has the highest calorific value ?
(1) Wood
(2) Coal
(3) LPG
(4) Petrol
= 3
~ Typical gross calorific values are roughly: wood 15–20 MJ/kg, coal 25–35 MJ/kg, petrol about 46 MJ/kg and LPG (propane–butane) about 50 MJ/kg. Hydrocarbon fuels with a higher hydrogen-to-carbon ratio release more heat per kilogram, and the light molecules of LPG (C₃H₈, C₄H₁₀) contain more hydrogen than petrol, so LPG has the highest calorific value per unit mass among these.

Q 20 | thm | Thermodynamics
A device used for measuring heat exchange is called a
(1) Thermometer
(2) Joule meter
(3) Calorimeter
(4) Pressure gauge
= 3
~ A calorimeter measures the quantity of heat exchanged in a process, such as a heat of combustion, specific heat or latent heat, from the temperature change of a known mass of water or another medium. The bomb calorimeter (solid and liquid fuels) and the Junkers gas calorimeter (gaseous fuels) are used to find the calorific value of fuels. A thermometer measures only temperature and a pressure gauge only pressure.

Q 21 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions
Which of the following is the chemical that is used as the reference standard for a Cetane number of 100 ?
(1) Heptane
(2) Octane
(3) Hexadecane (Cetane)
(4) Naphthalene
= 3
~ The cetane number of a diesel fuel is found by comparing its ignition delay in a test engine with blends of reference fuels. Normal hexadecane (C₁₆H₃₄, cetane), which ignites very readily, is rated 100; the low reference was originally alpha-methylnaphthalene (0) and is now heptamethylnonane (15). Normal heptane (0) and iso-octane (100) are the reference fuels for the octane number of petrol.

Q 22 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
Which of the following is an advantage of a multi-cylinder engine over a single-cylinder engine of the same total displacement ?
(1) Higher low-end torque
(2) Simpler construction and lower manufacturing costs
(3) Smoother operation with less vibration
(4) Better fuel efficiency at low power demands
= 3
~ Splitting the same displacement among several cylinders gives more frequent, overlapping power strokes, so the torque is more uniform and a lighter flywheel is enough, and the inertia forces of the cylinders can be arranged to cancel, giving better balance and less vibration. Smaller cylinders also permit higher speeds. The price is more parts, higher cost and more friction, so simplicity, low cost and economy at light loads favour the single-cylinder engine.

Q 23 | ice | Fuel Supply & Combustion
The primary function of a pre-whirl chamber is to
(1) Increase the engine's compression ratio
(2) Create a high-velocity, swirling motion to improve fuel-air mixing
(3) Cool the incoming air charge
(4) Allow for the use of a high-pressure common-rail fuel system
= 2
~ A pre-whirl (swirl) chamber, such as the Ricardo Comet design, is a roughly spherical chamber in the cylinder head of an indirect-injection diesel engine, joined to the cylinder by a tangential passage. During compression the air is forced into it and set into a strong, fast swirl; the fuel injected into this swirling air mixes quickly and starts to burn, and the burning mixture then rushes into the main chamber. This allows lower injection pressures and simpler pintle nozzles.

Q 24 | ice | Fuel Supply & Combustion
Which of the following factors increases the ignition delay period in a CI engine ?
(1) Increased compression ratio
(2) Increased air turbulence
(3) Supercharging
(4) Lower cetane number fuel
= 4
~ Ignition delay is the interval between the start of injection and the start of combustion. A fuel with a lower cetane number has poorer self-ignition quality, so the delay becomes longer. A higher compression ratio and supercharging raise the temperature and pressure of the compressed air, and greater air turbulence speeds up the evaporation and mixing of the fuel; all three shorten the ignition delay.

Q 25 | ice | Fuel Supply & Combustion
Which of the following factors can increase the tendency of knocking in a Compression Ignition (CI) engine ?
(1) A longer ignition delay period
(2) Using a fuel with a higher cetane number
(3) A lower compression ratio
(4) A lower temperature of the unburnt fuel-air mixture
= 1
~ Diesel knock is caused by a long ignition delay: fuel keeps accumulating in the chamber during the delay and then burns all at once, giving a very rapid pressure rise. A higher-cetane fuel shortens the delay and reduces knock. In a CI engine a lower compression ratio and a lower charge temperature also lengthen the delay (they reduce knock only in SI engines), but they act through the delay itself, so the key takes the longer ignition delay as the answer.

Q 26 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
What is the typical firing order for a four-cylinder inline engine ?
(1) 1-2-3-4
(2) 1-3-2-4
(3) 1-3-4-2
(4) 4-3-2-1
= 3
~ In an inline four-cylinder engine the crankpins of cylinders 1 and 4 are in line and 180° from those of cylinders 2 and 3, and one cylinder fires every 180° of crank rotation. After cylinder 1 fires, the next must be 2 or 3, and the third must be 4, so the only possible orders are 1-3-4-2 (the usual one) and 1-2-4-3. Orders such as 1-2-3-4 and 1-3-2-4 cannot occur with this crankshaft.

Q 27 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
Why is the firing order of an engine so important ?
(1) It determines the engine's horsepower rating
(2) It ensures the engine's balance, efficiency and performance by distributing power strokes evenly
(3) It is a purely aesthetic choice in engine design
(4) It affects the fuel type the engine can use
= 2
~ The firing order is chosen so that the power impulses are spaced evenly through the cycle and alternate between the ends of the crankshaft. This gives smoother torque and better balance, reduces vibration and the torsional stresses on the crankshaft and main bearings, avoids overloading neighbouring cylinders and improves the distribution of the mixture. It does not set the horsepower rating or the type of fuel the engine can use.

Q 28 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
Which of the following is not a function of piston rings ?
(1) To seal high-pressure gases
(2) To help in detonation
(3) To maintain sufficient lubrication
(4) To transfer heat to the cylinder wall
= 2
~ Piston rings seal the high-pressure combustion gases (compression rings), control the oil on the cylinder wall, scraping off the surplus while leaving a thin lubricating film (oil control ring), and conduct heat from the piston to the cylinder wall and on to the coolant. Detonation is abnormal, uncontrolled combustion of the end gas in an SI engine that causes knocking and damage; piston rings play no part in producing it.

Q 29 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
Piston rings are commonly made from which material ?
(1) Aluminum alloy
(2) Brass
(3) Cast iron or alloy cast iron
(4) Steel
= 3
~ Piston rings are usually made of fine-grained grey cast iron or alloy cast iron, because it keeps its elasticity at high temperature, wears well against the cylinder bore and its graphite helps lubrication. Top compression rings are often chrome-plated or molybdenum-coated, and some modern engines use steel rings, but cast iron remains the common material. Aluminium alloy is used for the piston itself, not its rings.

Q 30 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
During normal operation of an internal combustion engine, where is the highest temperature typically found on the piston ?
(1) Piston pin boss
(2) Skirt of the piston
(3) Top ring groove
(4) Center of the piston crown
= 4
~ The piston crown faces the burning gases directly, and heat from the centre of the crown has the longest path to flow outward through the rings to the cylinder wall, so the centre of the crown is the hottest part of the piston. The temperature falls toward the edge of the crown, the top ring groove, the pin bosses and the skirt, which is the coolest part. Heavily loaded pistons are therefore often cooled by oil jets under the crown.

Q 31 | ice | Fuel Supply & Combustion
Which type of injection is considered the most accurate ?
(1) Throttle body injection
(2) Manifold injection
(3) Direct injection
(4) Port injection
= 3
~ In direct injection the injector sprays fuel straight into the combustion chamber at high pressure, so the ECU controls exactly how much fuel enters each cylinder and when, with no fuel wetting the manifold or port walls. Throttle body (single-point) injection feeds all cylinders from one point, and manifold or port injection meters fuel for each cylinder but outside it, where some fuel lags in wall films. Direct injection is therefore the most precise.

Q 32 | ice | Fuel Supply & Combustion
What is the purpose of the venturi in a carburetor ?
(1) To increase the fuel flow
(2) To decrease the pressure and draw fuel into the airstream
(3) To regulate the amount of air entering the engine
(4) To provide a consistent air-fuel ratio
= 2
~ The venturi is a narrowed section of the carburettor barrel. As air passes through it, its velocity rises and its pressure falls (Bernoulli's principle). The main fuel nozzle opens at the throat, so the pressure difference between the float chamber, which is at atmospheric pressure, and the throat draws fuel into the airstream, where it is atomised and mixed with the air. The throttle valve, not the venturi, regulates the amount of air entering the engine.

Q 33 | ice | Fuel Supply & Combustion
What is the purpose of internal baffles inside a fuel tank ?
(1) To increase the tank's volume
(2) To reduce fuel sloshing during movement
(3) To allow for easier fuel delivery
(4) To help the fuel pump run cooler
= 2
~ Baffles are perforated partitions inside the fuel tank that divide it into connected compartments. They stop the fuel from surging or sloshing from end to end when the vehicle accelerates, brakes or corners, which keeps the fuel pickup covered so the supply is not interrupted, reduces noise and loads on the tank, and steadies the fuel-gauge reading. They also stiffen the tank, but they do not increase its volume.

Q 34 | mec | Theory of Machines
The unbalanced mass in a single-cylinder engine is balanced by counteracting the centrifugal force of the disturbing mass with the centrifugal force of a balancing mass. This is done for which of the following ?
(1) Dynamic balancing
(2) Neither static nor dynamic balancing
(3) Both static and dynamic balancing
(4) Static balancing
= 4
~ When a balancing mass is placed opposite the disturbing mass so that their centrifugal forces cancel (m₁r₁ = m₂r₂), the resultant force on the shaft is zero and the shaft stays at rest in any angular position; this force balance is static balancing. Dynamic balancing additionally requires the couples produced by masses in different planes to cancel. The question describes only the cancelling of centrifugal forces, so the key gives static balancing.

Q 35 | mec | Theory of Machines
The primary unbalanced force in a single-cylinder engine is maximum when the crank angle (θ) is
(1) 0° and 90°
(2) 0° and 180°
(3) 90° and 180°
(4) 180° and 360°
= 2
~ The primary unbalanced force of the reciprocating mass m is F = mω²r cos θ, where r is the crank radius and θ the crank angle from inner dead centre. cos θ has its largest magnitude, 1, at θ = 0° and 180° (the two dead centres), so the primary force is maximum there (mω²r) and zero at 90° and 270°. The secondary force, mω²r cos 2θ/n, is maximum at 0°, 90°, 180° and 270°. (A crank angle of 360° is the same position as 0°, so option 4 names the same dead centres; the key is option 2.)

Q 36 | mec | Theory of Machines
What is the purpose of a balancing mass in a single-cylinder engine ?
(1) To increase the speed of the engine
(2) To balance the rotating parts and reduce vibration
(3) To provide torque to the engine
(4) To reduce the engine's stroke
= 2
~ A single-cylinder engine has rotating parts (crankpin, crank webs, big end of the connecting rod) and reciprocating parts (piston, gudgeon pin, small end) whose inertia forces shake the engine. Counterweights on the crank webs, opposite the crankpin, balance the rotating mass and part of the reciprocating mass, reducing vibration and bearing loads. They do not raise the speed, add torque or change the stroke.

Q 37 | ice | Cooling & Lubrication
Which type of lubrication system is most commonly used in two-stroke engines ?
(1) Splash lubrication system
(2) Wet sump lubrication system
(3) Pressure lubrication system
(4) Mist lubrication system
= 4
~ Small crankcase-scavenged two-stroke petrol engines cannot keep a pool of oil in the crankcase, because the crankcase handles the incoming charge. They use mist (petroil) lubrication: oil is mixed with the petrol in a small proportion of a few per cent, or metered by a separate oil pump. When the fuel evaporates, the oil mist lubricates the cylinder walls, piston, bearings and crankpin. Splash, wet-sump and pressure systems are used in four-stroke engines.

Q 38 | ice | Cooling & Lubrication
Which of the following is a component of a pressure lubrication system ?
(1) Scoop on the connecting rod
(2) Oil mixed with fuel
(3) Oil pump that forces oil through channels
(4) Oil splashed onto components from a reservoir
= 3
~ In a pressure (forced-feed) lubrication system an oil pump, usually a gear pump, draws oil from the sump through a strainer and forces it through a filter into the main oil gallery. Drilled passages carry it to the main, connecting-rod and camshaft bearings, and a relief valve limits the pressure. A scoop on the connecting rod and oil splashed from a trough belong to the splash system, and oil mixed with fuel to petroil (mist) lubrication.

Q 39 | ice | Cooling & Lubrication
Which of the following is a key advantage of the liquid cooling system ?
(1) It is very cheap
(2) It provides even cooling throughout the engine
(3) It is dependent only on the supply of water
(4) It absorbs a considerable amount of power from the engine
= 2
~ In liquid cooling, coolant circulated by the water pump through jackets around the cylinders and head picks up heat from all the hot areas and carries it to the radiator, so the engine is cooled evenly and its temperature is held steady by the thermostat; the jackets also damp engine noise. Its drawbacks are higher cost and weight, dependence on the coolant and pump, and the power absorbed by the pump and fan.

Q 40 | ice | Cooling & Lubrication
In an air-cooled engine, what component helps to increase the surface area for heat dissipation ?
(1) Radiator fan
(2) Pressure cap
(3) Extended fins
(4) Circulating air
= 3
~ Air-cooled engines, as in most motorcycles and scooters, have thin extended fins cast on the cylinder barrel and head. The fins enlarge the area exposed to the air, so more heat is carried away by convection, either by the air rushing past the moving vehicle or by a fan and cowling. The radiator fan and pressure cap belong to liquid cooling, and circulating air is the cooling medium rather than a component.

Q 41 | chs | Clutch & Gearbox
What is the role of the dog clutch in a synchromesh gearbox after synchronization ?
(1) To initiate the speed matching process
(2) To provide the final positive engagement between the gear and the shaft
(3) To disengage the gears during a shift
(4) To absorb vibrations during operation
= 2
~ In a synchromesh gearbox the cone clutch (synchroniser ring) first uses friction to bring the gear and the main shaft to the same speed. Once the speeds match, the sliding sleeve moves further and its internal teeth engage the dog teeth on the gear, locking the gear positively to the shaft so that torque passes without slip. The cone only matches the speeds; the dog clutch provides the final positive engagement.

Q 42 | chs | Clutch & Gearbox
What is the primary function of a torque converter in an automatic transmission ?
(1) To engage and disengage the engine from the transmission
(2) To transmit power from the engine to the transmission
(3) To provide a fluid coupling to transmit torque from the engine to the transmission
(4) To control the gear selection and shift points
= 3
~ A torque converter takes the place of the friction clutch in an automatic transmission. Its impeller, driven by the engine, throws oil onto the turbine, which drives the gearbox input shaft, so torque is transmitted through the fluid and the vehicle can stand in gear with the engine idling. Unlike a simple fluid coupling, it also has a stator that multiplies torque at low speeds. Gear selection and shift points are controlled by the valve body or the transmission control unit.

Q 43 | chs | Clutch & Gearbox
Which of the following is a basic component of most automatic transmissions ?
(1) Flywheel
(2) Planetary gear set
(3) Friction clutch
(4) Clutch pedal
= 2
~ Conventional automatic transmissions use one or more planetary (epicyclic) gear sets, each with a sun gear, planet gears on a carrier and a ring gear. Holding or driving different members of the set with hydraulically applied multi-plate clutches and brake bands gives the various ratios and reverse without sliding gears in and out of mesh. The clutch pedal and driver-operated main friction clutch belong to manual gearboxes; the torque converter replaces them.

Q 44 | chs | Drive Line, Axles & Differential
What is the purpose of a slip joint in a propeller shaft ?
(1) To allow the shaft to bend sideways
(2) To transfer torque at an angle
(3) To transmit torque when the shafts are not parallel
(4) To allow the shaft to change length due to expansion or contraction
= 4
~ A slip joint is a splined sleeve and shaft at one end of the propeller shaft. As the rear axle moves up and down on its springs, the distance between the gearbox and the axle changes, and the slip joint lets the propeller shaft lengthen or shorten, the "expansion or contraction" of the option, while still transmitting torque. Transmitting torque at an angle, between shafts that are not in line, is the job of the universal joints.

Q 45 | chs | Drive Line, Axles & Differential
Which type of joint is used to allow the propeller shaft to transmit torque at an angle ?
(1) Universal joint
(2) Hinge joint
(3) Ball and socket joint
(4) Pivot joint
= 1
~ A universal joint (Hooke's joint), made of two yokes connected by a cross or spider with needle bearings, joins two shafts whose axes meet at an angle and lets the angle vary as the rear axle moves on its springs. Q 46 | chs | Drive Line, Axles & Differential
A differential allows the rear wheels to rotate at different speeds. This is essential for
(1) Starting the vehicle on a hill
(2) Increasing the vehicle's horsepower
(3) Proper steering during a turn
(4) Applying the brakes
= 3
~ On a turn the outer driving wheel travels a longer arc than the inner wheel in the same time, so it must turn faster. The differential divides the drive between the two half-shafts, letting the outer wheel speed up while the inner one slows down by the same amount, so the vehicle can turn smoothly without the wheels scrubbing or slipping and without straining the axle. Without it, cornering would cause tyre wear and make steering difficult.

Q 47 | chs | Clutch & Gearbox
Which type of clutch does not require a clutch pedal ?
(1) Single plate
(2) Multi-plate
(3) Centrifugal
(4) Cone
= 3
~ A centrifugal clutch engages automatically according to engine speed: weighted shoes or flyweights on the driving member are thrown outward by centrifugal force as the speed rises and press against the drum, while springs pull them back at idle so that the drive is disengaged. No pedal is needed, which is why it is used in mopeds, automatic scooters and other small vehicles. Single-plate, multi-plate and cone clutches are released by a pedal or lever.

Q 48 | chs | Clutch & Gearbox
In a single plate clutch, what is the function of the pressure plate ?

(2) To disengage the clutch disc from the flywheel when the pedal is depressed
(3) To hold the clutch disc between the flywheel and itself when the pedal is released
(4) To absorb heat generated during engagement
= 3
~ In a single-plate clutch the pressure plate is pushed towards the flywheel by the clutch springs (coil or diaphragm), clamping the friction-lined clutch disc between the flywheel and itself when the pedal is released, so engine torque passes to the gearbox input shaft. When the pedal is pressed, the release bearing and levers pull the pressure plate back against the springs and free the disc. Its main job is this clamping.

Q 49 | chs | Clutch & Gearbox
Why are multi-plate clutches used instead of single plate clutches ?
(1) To reduce torque transmission capacity
(2) To increase the torque transmission capacity
(3) To operate as a wet clutch only
(4) To increase the overall size of the clutch
= 2
~ The torque a plate clutch can transmit is T = nμWR, where n is the number of pairs of contact surfaces, μ the coefficient of friction, W the axial spring force and R the mean radius of the friction surfaces. Adding plates raises n, so a multi-plate clutch transmits more torque within a smaller diameter; that is why it is used in motorcycles, heavy vehicles and automatic transmissions. It can run dry or wet, and it keeps the size down, not up.

Q 50 | chs | Brakes

(2) Piston in the caliper
(3) Brake fluid
(4) Bleed screw
= 2
~ In a hydraulic disc brake the master cylinder sends fluid pressure to the caliper, where it acts on one or more pistons. Q 51 | chs | Brakes
What is the main advantage of disc brakes over drum brakes ?
(1) They are generally less expensive
(2) They are less prone to brake fade due to better heat dissipation
(3) They are simpler in design and require less maintenance
(4) They are more effective when the vehicle is in reverse gear
= 2
~ In a disc brake the rotor is exposed to the air (and is often ventilated), so the heat produced by braking is shed quickly. Friction therefore stays steady during long or repeated braking, and brake fade is much less than in an enclosed drum, which also expands away from the shoes when hot. Disc brakes also dry out quickly after getting wet. They usually cost more than drums and work equally well in forward and reverse.

Q 52 | chs | Brakes
Which of these is an advantage of hydraulic brakes over mechanical brakes ?
(1) They require more pedal effort for the same braking effect
(2) They require less pedal effort for the same braking effect
(3) They only provide uniform braking force to one wheel
(4) They are less effective at high speeds
= 2
~ In a hydraulic brake the pedal pushes a piston in the master cylinder, and the fluid pressure is transmitted equally to every wheel cylinder (Pascal's law). Because the wheel-cylinder pistons are larger than the master-cylinder piston, the force is multiplied, so less pedal effort gives the same braking effect. Hydraulic brakes also apply equal pressure at all wheels, have fewer rods and joints to wear or adjust, and work well at all speeds.

Q 53 | chs | Brakes
Which vehicle type typically uses air brakes due to their size and weight ?
(1) Passenger cars
(2) Motorcycles
(3) Trucks, buses and semi-trailers
(4) Electric scooters
= 3

Q 54 | chs | Steering & Suspension

(2) Turn at the same angle as the outer wheel
(3) Turn at a greater angle than the outer wheel
(4) Have a negative steering angle
= 3
~ When a vehicle turns, the inner front wheel runs on a smaller circle than the outer wheel about the same turning centre. To roll without side-slip it must therefore be steered through a larger angle than the outer wheel. Q 55 | chs | Steering & Suspension

(1) Turn at the same angle
(2) Turn about the same instantaneous center
(3) Have the same turning radius
(4) Be perfectly aligned at all times
= 2
~ Correct steering means that all wheels roll without lateral slip. This happens only when the axes of all four wheels, produced, meet at one point (the instantaneous centre) on the line of the rear axle, so that every wheel moves on a circle about the same centre. The wheels then turn at different angles and on different radii, which rules out options 1 and 3. The Ackermann condition, cot(outer angle) − cot(inner angle) = pivot distance/wheelbase, expresses this.

Q 56 | chs | Steering & Suspension
What is the primary function of the Ackermann steering mechanism ?

(2) To achieve true rolling by ensuring the wheels turn at different angles when cornering
(3) To provide power steering assist to the driver
(4) To improve fuel efficiency
= 2
~ The Ackermann steering mechanism is a four-bar linkage formed by the front axle, the two steering arms and the track rod. Its geometry turns the inner wheel through a larger angle than the outer wheel, so that the axes of the wheels meet near one point on the rear-axle line and the wheels roll truly, without side-slip, while cornering. It satisfies the correct-steering condition exactly in only three positions (straight ahead and one on each side) and gives no power assistance.

Q 57 | ael | Batteries & Charging

(2) To recharge the battery and power the electrical accessories

(4) To regulate the oil pressure
= 2
~ The alternator, driven by the engine through a belt, converts mechanical energy into electrical energy. Q 58 | ael | Batteries & Charging
Which component converts mechanical energy into electrical energy to charge the battery and power the electrical system ?
(1) Starter motor
(2) Alternator
(3) Regulator
(4) Ignition coil
= 2
~ The alternator converts the engine's mechanical energy into electrical energy. A belt from the crankshaft pulley spins its rotor (field winding) inside the stator windings, inducing alternating current, which built-in diodes rectify to DC for charging the battery and running the electrical system. Q 59 | ael | Lighting, Instruments & Wiring

Q 60 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions
What is the impact of high concentrations of pollutants like sulfur dioxide on the environment ?

(2) Is the primary cause of acid rain
(3) Do not cause the formation of smog
(4) Neutralizes acid in water bodies

~ Sulfur dioxide, released by burning sulfur-containing fuels such as coal and high-sulfur diesel, is oxidised in the air and dissolves in water droplets to form sulfuric acid. Q 61 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions

(2) Particulates
(3) Sulphur
(4) Unburnt Hydrocarbons (HC)

~ Diesel engines run with excess air, so their CO and unburnt HC emissions are lower than those of petrol engines. Their characteristic pollutants are particulates (soot or black smoke) and NOₓ. Q 62 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions
Which of the following factors increases the tendency for NOₓ formation in an SI engine ?
(1) Decreasing the air-fuel ratio
(2) Increasing the peak combustion temperature
(3) Increasing the ignition delay
(4) Lowering the engine’s compression ratio
= 2
~ Nitric oxide forms when the nitrogen and oxygen of the air combine at very high temperatures, roughly above 1800 K, and the rate rises steeply with temperature. Anything that raises the peak combustion temperature, such as a higher compression ratio, advanced spark timing or a mixture slightly lean of stoichiometric, increases NOₓ. Lowering the compression ratio reduces it, and exhaust gas recirculation (EGR) controls NOₓ by lowering the peak temperature.

Q 63 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions

(1) Oxides of nitrogen (NOₓ)
(2) Carbon monoxide (CO)
(3) Particulate matter (soot)
(4) Unburnt or partially burnt hydrocarbons, including aldehydes
= 4
~ The typical smell of diesel exhaust comes mainly from unburnt and partially oxidised hydrocarbons, especially aldehydes such as formaldehyde and acrolein. Q 64 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions

(1) Sulfur oxide compound
(2) Particulate matter
(3) Unburnt hydrocarbon (HC)
(4) Inert gas
= 3
~ Aldehydes, for example formaldehyde (HCHO) and acetaldehyde, are oxygenated organic compounds formed by partial oxidation of the fuel. Q 65 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions
Which of the following operating conditions is a primary cause for high NOₓ emissions in a diesel engine ?
(1) Low combustion temperature
(2) Insufficient oxygen supply
(3) High combustion pressure and temperature with sufficient reaction time
(4) Delayed injection timing
= 3
~ NOₓ (mainly NO) forms by the thermal (Zeldovich) mechanism, which needs high temperature, oxygen and time. In a diesel engine the high compression and combustion pressures give very high flame temperatures around the burning spray, where excess oxygen is present, and if the hot gases stay at that temperature long enough much NO forms. Low combustion temperature, a shortage of oxygen and retarded (delayed) injection timing all reduce NOₓ, which is why retarded timing and EGR are used to control it.

Q 66 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions
Diesel engine exhaust primarily contains which forms of nitrogen oxides ?

(2) N₂O₄ and N₂O₃
(3) Pure N₂ and O₂
(4) NO and NO₂
= 4
~ The nitrogen oxides in diesel exhaust are mainly nitric oxide (NO), which forms in the hot burning gases, and nitrogen dioxide (NO₂), formed by further oxidation of NO; NO₂ forms a larger share of the total in diesels than in petrol engines. Q 67 | thm | Refrigeration & Air Conditioning

(2) It compresses the refrigerant

Q 70 | chs | Steering & Suspension

Q 82 | thm | Thermodynamics

(2) First decreases and then increases
(3) Increases steadily
(4) Decreases steadily

Q 89 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions

(2) Sudden ignition of charge before the spark
(3) Autoignition of the charge after the spark is struck

= 3

Q 106 | ice | Fuel Supply & Combustion

~ The primary (low-tension) circuit of a battery coil ignition system consists of the battery, ignition switch, ballast resistor (if fitted), primary winding of the ignition coil, contact breaker points and the condenser connected across them. The secondary (high-tension) circuit consists of the coil's secondary winding, the distributor cap and rotor, the high-tension leads and the spark plugs. So the contact breaker, ignition switch and condenser all belong to the primary circuit.

Q 136 | ael | Starting & Ignition
The contact breaker points are opened by the cam and closed by the
(1) Same cam
(2) Centrifugal force
(3) Magnetic force
(4) Spring tension
= 4
~ The contact breaker has a fixed contact and a movable contact on a pivoted arm. As the distributor cam rotates, each lobe pushes the rubbing block and opens the points, breaking the primary current so that the coil induces a high voltage. When the lobe has passed, a leaf spring on the moving arm snaps the points closed again, and the primary current builds up for the next spark. The spring tension must be correct to prevent point bounce at high speed.

Q 137 | ael | Lighting, Instruments & Wiring
The two quantities used for specifying electrical wire are
(1) The colour and length
(2) The length and diameter
(3) The diameter and number of strands
(4) The colour and diameter
= 3
~ Automotive cables are made of stranded copper for flexibility and are specified by the number of strands and the diameter of each strand; for example, 14/0.30 means 14 strands each 0.30 mm in diameter. These two figures fix the conductor's cross-sectional area and hence its current rating. The length is decided by the routing of the cable, and the insulation colour only identifies the circuit under a colour code, so neither specifies the wire.

Q 138 | ael | Batteries & Charging
The frequency of a vibrating contact type for d.c. generator is
(1) 20 per second
(2) 200 per second
(3) 20 per minute
(4) 200 per minute
= 2
~ The vibrating-contact regulator of a d.c. generator (dynamo) controls its output by rapidly opening and closing contacts that insert a resistance into the field circuit and cut it out again, so that the average field current, and hence the voltage, stays nearly constant. The contacts vibrate very fast; the key gives about 200 times per second, and rates per minute would be far too slow. The question omits the word "regulator"; alternators with electronic regulators have since replaced this system.

Q 139 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
The carbon accumulation in an engine cylinder results in
(1) Increase of effective compression ratio
(2) Increase of volumetric efficiency
(3) Increase of clearance volume
(4) Increase of ignition time
= 1
~ Carbon deposits on the piston crown and in the combustion chamber occupy part of the clearance volume. Since the compression ratio is (swept volume + clearance volume)/clearance volume, a smaller clearance volume raises the effective compression ratio, which increases the tendency to detonation and, from glowing deposits, pre-ignition in SI engines. Carbon reduces rather than increases the clearance volume, lowers volumetric efficiency by heating the incoming charge, and does not change the ignition timing.

Q 140 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
Due to which of the following reasons the piston rings are plated with chromium, cadmium or phosphate ?
(1) To prevent clogging
(2) To improve heat transfer
(3) To reduce wear and eliminate scuffing
(4) To improve surface finish
= 3
~ Piston rings, especially the top ring, rub against the cylinder wall at high temperature with poor lubrication. Chromium plating gives a very hard, wear-resistant surface that greatly increases ring life, while cadmium and phosphate coatings form soft or porous layers that hold oil and help the rings bed in without scuffing, the local welding and tearing of ring and bore surfaces during running-in. The coatings are not meant to prevent clogging or to improve heat transfer.

Q 141 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions
Carbon Monoxide emission from S.I. engine are higher when the engine is
(1) Idling
(2) Cruising
(3) Accelerating
(4) Decelerating
= 1
~ Carbon monoxide comes from incomplete combustion of rich mixtures, so CO emission is highest when the SI engine runs on its richest mixture. At idling a rich mixture is supplied because the small charge is diluted by residual exhaust gas at low manifold pressure, so CO is highest; it is lowest when cruising, when a lean economy mixture is used. It is hydrocarbon (HC) emission, rather than CO, that rises sharply during deceleration.

Q 142 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions
Orsat flue gas analysis gives
(1) Mass analysis of wet products of combustion
(2) Volumetric analysis of dry products of combustion
(3) Mass analysis of dry products of combustion
(4) Volumetric analysis of wet products of combustion
= 2
~ The Orsat apparatus absorbs the gases of a measured sample (usually 100 cm³) one by one in chemical reagents, CO₂ in potassium hydroxide, O₂ in alkaline pyrogallol and CO in cuprous chloride solution, and measures the fall in volume each time; the remainder is taken as nitrogen. Because the sample is measured over water at room temperature, its water vapour condenses, so the result is a volumetric analysis of the dry products of combustion.

Q 143 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions
Flame Ionisation Detector is used for measuring
(1) CO
(2) HC
(3) NOₓ
(4) CO₂
= 2
~ The flame ionisation detector (FID) is the standard instrument for measuring unburnt hydrocarbons in exhaust gas. The sample is burnt in a small hydrogen–air flame; hydrocarbons produce ions roughly in proportion to the number of carbon atoms present, and the resulting current between two electrodes gives the HC concentration. CO and CO₂ are measured by non-dispersive infrared (NDIR) analysers and NOₓ by chemiluminescence analysers.

Q 144 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions
Chemiluminescence technique is used to measure
(1) NOₓ
(2) CO
(3) CO₂
(4) Smoke intensity
= 1
~ The chemiluminescence analyser measures oxides of nitrogen. Nitric oxide in the sample reacts with ozone to form excited NO₂, which emits light as it returns to its normal state; a photomultiplier measures this light, which is proportional to the NO concentration. NO₂ is first converted to NO in a converter, so total NOₓ is obtained. CO and CO₂ are measured by NDIR analysers, and smoke intensity by smoke (opacity) meters.

Q 145 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions
Non-dispersive infra-red analyser is widely accepted instrument for measuring
(1) NOₓ
(2) HC
(3) CO
(4) CO₂
= 3
~ The non-dispersive infrared (NDIR) analyser measures how much infrared radiation of a particular band the exhaust sample absorbs; each gas absorbs in its own band, so the absorption gives its concentration. It is the standard method for measuring CO in exhaust emission testing, while NOₓ is measured by chemiluminescence and HC normally by flame ionisation. CO₂ is, however, also measured by NDIR analysers, so option 4 is equally defensible. (Official final key; some sources differ.)

Q 146 | mfg | Engineering Materials
What is depth of hardened layer by flame hardening of steel wheel ?
(1) 0.15 mm
(2) 0.8 mm
(3) 0.35 mm
(4) 1.2 mm
= 2
~ Flame hardening heats the surface of a medium-carbon or alloy steel part, such as a wheel rim, gear or shaft, with an oxy-acetylene flame and quenches it at once with a water spray, so that only a surface layer turns into hard martensite while the core stays tough. The hardened depth depends on the heating time and the speed of the flame and is commonly quoted from about 0.8 mm upwards to several millimetres; the key takes 0.8 mm for this question.

Q 147 | mfg | Engineering Materials
By what process maximum hardness is obtained for a steel part ?
(1) Carburizing
(2) Nitriding
(3) Cyaniding
(4) Same hardness in all processes
= 2
~ Nitriding heats a finished alloy-steel part containing nitride-forming elements such as aluminium, chromium and molybdenum in ammonia gas at about 500–550 °C, forming very hard nitrides in a thin surface layer. It gives the highest surface hardness of the case-hardening processes, about 900–1100 HV, and needs no quenching, so distortion is small. Carburized and cyanided cases, hardened by quenching to martensite, reach only about 60–65 HRC (roughly 700–850 HV), so the hardness is not the same in all processes.

Q 148 | mfg | Engineering Materials
The ratio of E of a whisker of mild steel to E of a bulk material is
(1) 4
(2) 5
(3) 40
(4) 50
= 2
~ Whiskers are very thin, almost defect-free single-crystal filaments. Because they contain practically no dislocations, their strength approaches the theoretical strength of the metal and is many times that of ordinary bulk mild steel. Young's modulus, however, depends on the interatomic bonding and is structure-insensitive, so the elastic modulus of a whisker is essentially the same as that of the bulk metal; the ratio of 5 is credited to the key. Q 149 | thm | Refrigeration & Air Conditioning
Which of the following is a possible reason for flushing an A/C condenser ?
(1) To replace the old refrigerant with new refrigerant
(2) To remove sludge and debris
(3) To remove moisture
(4) To improve cooling efficiency
= 2
~ After the refrigerant has been recovered, the condenser of a vehicle A/C system is flushed with an approved flushing solvent to remove sludge, metal debris and degraded oil, typically after a compressor failure or contamination; otherwise the debris would block the condenser passages and the expansion valve and damage the new compressor.
