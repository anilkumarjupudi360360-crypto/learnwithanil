Q 1 | thm | Refrigeration & Air Conditioning
Which of the following is NOT a part of the air conditioning system used in cars?
(1) Compressor
(2) Refrigerant
(3) Turbo charger
(4) Condenser
= 3
~ A car air-conditioning system is a vapour-compression refrigeration circuit: the compressor (belt- or motor-driven), condenser, receiver-drier, expansion valve or orifice tube and evaporator, with a refrigerant such as R-134a or R-1234yf circulating through them. A turbocharger is an exhaust-driven turbine and compressor that boosts intake air pressure to raise engine power; it is part of the air-induction system, not the air conditioner.

Q 2 | thm | Refrigeration & Air Conditioning
Select the option that is true regarding the following two statements labelled Assertion (A) and Reason (R).
Assertion (A): The COP of an air-conditioning plant is lower than that of an ice plant.
Reason (R): The temperatures required in the ice plant are lower than those required for an air-conditioning plant.
(1) Both A and R are individually true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(2) Both A and R are individually true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(3) A is true, but R is false.
(4) A is false, but R is true.
= 4
~ COP falls as the evaporator temperature falls, because the compressor has to lift heat through a larger temperature difference (Carnot COP = T_L/(T_H − T_L)). An ice plant evaporates below 0 °C, while an air-conditioning plant evaporates at about 5–10 °C, so R is true. For example, with a 45 °C condenser the Carnot COP is about 7.0 at 5 °C but only 4.3 at −15 °C. Hence an air-conditioning plant has the higher COP and A is false.

Q 3 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions
Match the following points with their definitions.
List I | List II
(A) Flash point | (i) The lowest temperature at which the vapours of volatile materials ignite when an ignition source is provided
(B) Fire point | (ii) The lowest temperature at which the vapour of fuel can continue to burn for at least five seconds after the ignition has started
C) Flash point value | (iii) Always lower than the fire point
(D) Fire point value | (iv) Always higher than the flash point
(1) (A)-(ii), (B)-(iv), (C)-(iii) and (D)-(i)

Q 4 | mec | Machine Design
Read the given statement and conclusions carefully and select the correct option.
Statement:
Self-locking screw jack means when the rotational force on the screw is removed, it will remain motionless and will not rotate backwards.
Conclusions:
I. Self-locking screw jacks are safer compared to hydraulic jack which is not self-locking.
II. Single start trapezoidal type screws are typically self-locking.
III. All screw jacks with double start lifting screws are also considered self-locking.
(1) Statement and all the conclusions I, II and III are true.
(2) Statement is incorrect, but conclusions I and II are true.
(3) Statement is true, but conclusions I, II and III are false.
(4) Only statement and conclusions I and II are true.
= 4
~ A screw is self-locking when the friction angle is at least equal to the helix (lead) angle, so the load cannot turn it back once the effort is removed; the statement is true. Q 7 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
Select the option that is true regarding the following two statements labelled Assertion (A) and Reason (R).
Assertion (A): The C.I. engine is found to be more efficient than the S.I. engine.
Reason (R): Modern C.I. engines operate on a dual-cycle, which has efficiency greater than the Otto cycle.
(1) Both A and R are individually true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(2) Both A and R are individually true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(3) A is true, but R is false.
(4) A is false, but R is true.
= 2
~ C.I. engines are more efficient mainly because they run at much higher compression ratios (about 14–22 against 8–12 for S.I. engines), with lean mixtures and no throttling losses. Modern high-speed C.I. engines are modelled by the dual (limited-pressure) cycle, which is more efficient than the Otto cycle when both work to the same peak pressure; for the same compression ratio, however, the Otto cycle is the most efficient. So R holds only for the same peak pressure; the real reason for A is the higher compression ratio, and R does not explain A.

Q 9 | chs | Wheels & Tyres
Which of the following statements is INCORRECT about wire wheels?
(1) This type of wheel can be used for tubed tyres only.
(2) This type of wheel is used in racing cars only now a days.
(3) Heat dissipation takes place to the surroundings by means of spokes fitted in the rim.
(4) Tubeless tyres cannot be fitted over wire wheels.
= 2
~ Wire (spoke) wheels have a hub joined to the rim by tensioned steel spokes. They are light and let air circulate past the hub and brake drum, aiding cooling, but the spoke nipples pierce the rim, so conventional wire wheels need tubed tyres. They are not confined to racing cars today: they are widely used on motorcycles and bicycles and on some vintage and sports cars, so statement (2) is the incorrect one.

Q 10 | chs | Wheels & Tyres
Which of the following symptoms is NOT due to low tyre pressure in a vehicle?
(1) Increase in rolling resistance
(2) Increased fuel consumption due to rolling resistance, which makes the engine work harder
(3) Uneven tread wear - the edges of the tyre wear out faster
(4) Smaller contact area with the road
= 4
~ An under-inflated tyre flexes more and flattens out, so its contact patch with the road becomes larger, not smaller. The extra flexing raises rolling resistance and heat build-up, which increases fuel consumption, and the load is carried on the shoulders, so both edges of the tread wear faster than the centre. A smaller contact patch with centre wear is a symptom of over-inflation.

Q 14 | thm | Thermodynamics
Read the given statement and conclusions carefully and select the correct option.
Statement (A):
If there is no tendency to change the microscopic properties or inner properties of the system even when the system is isolated from surroundings, then it is considered the thermodynamic equilibrium.
Conclusion:
I. When any unbalanced force is absent in the system, then it is called chemical equilibrium.
II. Thermal equilibrium means there is no change of any property of the system.
III. If a system can undergo a chemical reaction, then it is called a chemically equilibrium system.

Q 15 | thm | Thermodynamics
Select the option that is true regarding the following two statements labelled Assertion (A) and Reason (R).
Assertion (A): Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics states that when two bodies are in thermal equilibrium with another third body, then the two bodies are also in thermal equilibrium with each other.
Reason (R): If we take three bodies A, B and C, then if A and B are in thermal equilibrium and B and C are in thermal equilibrium separately, then A and C are also considered to be in thermal equilibrium.
(1) Both A and R are individually true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(2) Both A and R are individually true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(3) A is true, but R is false.
(4) A is false, but R is true.
= 1
~ The zeroth law states that two bodies each in thermal equilibrium with a third body are in thermal equilibrium with each other. The reason restates this with three bodies: if A is in equilibrium with B and C is in equilibrium with B, then A and C are in equilibrium. Here B plays the role of a thermometer, which is why the law is the basis of temperature measurement. R illustrates and explains A.

Q 16 | thm | Thermodynamics
Match the following laws with their correct explanation.
List I | List II

(B) Second law of thermodynamic system | (ii) It states that at absolute zero temperature of a system, the entropy becomes constant.
(C) Zeroth law of thermodynamic system | (iii) It states that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only converted from one form to another.
(D) Third law of thermodynamic system | (iv) If two systems are in thermal equilibrium with a third system, they must be in thermal equilibrium with each other.

Q 18 | thm | Thermodynamics
Select the option that is true regarding the following two statements labelled Assertion (A) and Reason (R).
Assertion (A): The performance of a simple Rankine cycle is not sensitive to the efficiency of the feed pump.
Reason (R): The network ratio is practically unit for a Rankine cycle.
(1) Both A and R are individually true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(2) A is true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(3) A is false, but R is the correct explanation of A.
(4) Both A and R are individually true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
= 4
~ "Network ratio ... unit" is a misprint for "work ratio ... unity". Q 19 | chs | Brakes
Which of the following statements is INCORRECT about mechanical brakes?
(1) Brakes absorb energy and convert it to heat.
(2) The capacity of a brake depends largely on the surface area of frictional surfaces but independent of the actuation force applied.
(3) The durability of a brake or service life between maintenance depends heavily on the type of material used to line the shoe.
(4) Mechanical brakes all act by generating frictional forces as two surfaces rub against each other.
= 2
~ A friction brake converts the kinetic energy of the vehicle into heat by rubbing a lining against a drum or disc. The braking torque equals the coefficient of friction × the normal (actuating) force × the effective radius, so the capacity depends directly on the force applied, as well as on the lining area and the heat it can dissipate. Lining material largely decides wear life. Statement (2) is therefore incorrect.

Q 20 | chs | Brakes
Which of the following is NOT the main part of your vehicle’s hydraulic brake system?

(2) Brake line
(3) Brake disc
(4) Leaf spring
= 4

Q 22 | chs | Brakes
What is the principle of hydraulic brake system?
(1) Zeroth law hydraulics
(2) Newton's law of friction
(3) Pascal's law pressure
(4) Fourier's law of conduction
= 3
~ Hydraulic brakes work on Pascal's law: pressure applied to a confined liquid is transmitted equally in all directions. The force on the brake pedal creates pressure in the master cylinder, and the brake fluid carries this same pressure through the lines to every wheel cylinder or caliper, where larger pistons multiply the force and apply the shoes or pads evenly.

Q 23 | chs | Brakes
A braking system commonly used in heavy commercial vehicles and trucks that requires a heavy braking effort that can be applied by the driver’s leg alone is known as:
(1) Hydraulic brake system
(2) Internal expanding brake system
(3) Band brake system
(4) Air brake system
= 4
~ Heavy commercial vehicles need a braking effort far greater than a driver's leg can produce, so they use air (pneumatic) brakes. An engine-driven compressor stores air at about 7–8 bar in reservoirs; the driver's pedal only works a brake valve that admits this compressed air to the brake chambers, which apply the shoes. Air brakes also allow easy coupling of trailer brakes.

Q 26 | chs | Brakes

Q 28 | chs | Clutch & Gearbox
A single plate clutch has outer and inner diameters as 300 mm and 200 mm, respectively. The total force acting on the friction surface is 6000 N. If the coefficient of friction is 0.2, the power transmitted by the clutch at an angular speed of 300 rad/s is:

(2) 90 kW

(4) 30 kW

Q 29 | chs | Clutch & Gearbox
Which of the following is NOT a type of clutch?
(1) Single plate type clutch
(2) Multiple plate clutch
(3) Centrifugal type clutch
(4) Governor type clutch
= 4
~ Automobile friction clutches include the single-plate clutch (cars), the multi-plate clutch (motorcycles, heavy vehicles, automatic transmissions), the cone clutch and the centrifugal clutch, which engages automatically as engine speed rises (mopeds, scooters); fluid couplings and electromagnetic clutches are other types. Q 30 | chs | Clutch & Gearbox
Which of the following statements about the multi-plate clutch is INCORRECT?

Q 31 | mfg | Metrology & Measurement
A force transducer works on the __________.
(1) Hall effect principle
(2) Piezoelectric principle
(3) Laser principle
(4) Magneto Rheological principle
= 2
~ A force transducer converts force into an electrical signal. The Hall effect is used for magnetic position and speed sensing, and the magneto-rheological effect is used in variable dampers, not for measuring force.

(1) At the beginning of the compression stroke
(2) Just before the end of expansion
(3) During the intake stroke
(4) At the start of the power stroke
= 2
~ In a four-stroke diesel engine the exhaust valve opens just before the end of the expansion (power) stroke, typically about 30–50° of crank angle before bottom dead centre. Opening early lets the still-pressurised gases rush out (blowdown), so the cylinder pressure is already low when the piston begins the exhaust stroke, which reduces pumping work. The valve closes a little after top dead centre, overlapping with the opening of the inlet valve.

Q 123 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions
What is the most important gas produced during combustion in an automobile engine that
increases the atmospheric temperature?
(1) O₂
(2) CO₂
(3) N₂
(4) SO₂
= 2
~ Complete combustion of hydrocarbon fuels produces mainly carbon dioxide (CO₂) and water vapour. CO₂, emitted by vehicles in very large quantities, is the most important greenhouse gas: it traps outgoing infrared radiation and raises the atmospheric temperature (global warming). Oxygen and nitrogen come from the intake air and are not greenhouse gases, and SO₂ from fuel sulphur is now very small, since BS-VI fuel (from April 2020) has at most 10 ppm sulphur.

Q 124 | ice | Fuels, Lubricants & Emissions
In the automobile engine, which two gases in the exhaust vary opposite to each other?
(1) SO₂ and O₂
(2) O₂ and CO
(3) CO₂ and CO
(4) SO₂ and N₂
= 3
~ CO₂ and CO in the exhaust vary in opposite directions as the air–fuel ratio changes. With a rich mixture there is not enough oxygen to burn all the carbon to CO₂, so CO rises and CO₂ falls; as the mixture approaches the chemically correct ratio (about 14.7:1 for petrol), CO drops to a minimum and CO₂ reaches its maximum of about 14–15%. Exhaust gas analysers use this relation to judge mixture strength.

Q 125 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
The exhaust of spent gases from the cylinder of an automobile engine is done so that ______.
(1) the engine can run silently
(2) fuel efficiency is improved
(3) fresh charge may be sucked in
(4) the exhaust note becomes louder
= 3
~ The burnt gases left after the power stroke can do no further work and only occupy the cylinder. They are expelled during the exhaust stroke so that the cylinder is cleared and a fresh charge (air–fuel mixture in a petrol engine, air alone in a diesel engine) can be sucked in during the next suction stroke. Burnt gas left behind dilutes the fresh charge and lowers volumetric efficiency and power.

Q 126 | ice | Engine Fundamentals & Cycles
In a four stroke diesel cycle engine, during which piston movement does it push gases through
the exhaust valves?
(1) From TDC towards BDC
(2) From BDC towards TDC
(3) From TDC to BDC and back to TDC
(4) No piston movement required
= 2
~ In the exhaust stroke of a four-stroke diesel engine the exhaust valve is open and the inlet valve closed, and the piston moves from bottom dead centre (BDC) towards top dead centre (TDC), pushing the burnt gases out through the exhaust valve. The piston moves from TDC towards BDC during the suction and power strokes, and each stroke is a single movement between the dead centres, not a movement there and back.

Q 127 | mva | Vehicle Maintenance & Testing

Q 128 | chs | Wheels & Tyres

(3) On the gear box

Q 129 | chs | Wheels & Tyres
______ is the final contact point between the road and the vehicle.
(1) Gear box
(2) Tyres
(3) Wheel hubs
(4) Differential
= 2
~ The tyres are the only parts of a vehicle in contact with the road. The weight of the vehicle and all driving, braking and cornering forces pass through the small contact patches of the tyres, which also cushion road shocks. The gearbox and differential are parts of the transmission, and the wheel hubs carry the wheels but never touch the road surface.

Q 130 | chs | Wheels & Tyres
In an automobile, what is the ‘tread’?
(1) The engine compartment
(2) The steering wheel
(3) Automobile’s dashboard
(4) The outer portion of the tyre that makes contact with the road surface
= 4
~ The tread is the thick outer rubber portion of the tyre that makes contact with the road surface. Its pattern of grooves, ribs and blocks gives grip for traction, braking and cornering, and channels water out of the contact patch to prevent aquaplaning. A tyre whose tread has worn down to the tread-wear indicators must be replaced. The engine compartment, steering wheel and dashboard have nothing to do with the tread.

Q 131 | chs | Wheels & Tyres
What are the rings that fit on the rim of the wheel and connect the tyre to the rim called?
(1) Beads
(2) Hubs
(3) Valves
(4) Axles
= 1
~ The beads are the inner edges of a tyre, each reinforced with a ring of high-tensile steel wire. They fit tightly against the bead seats and flanges of the rim, holding the tyre on the rim and, in a tubeless tyre, sealing in the air. Hubs carry the wheel on the axle, the valve admits air, and axles support the wheels; none of these connects the tyre to the rim.

Q 132 | chs | Drive Line, Axles & Differential
In an automobile system, transmission system is also called ______.
(1) Differential
(2) Gear box
(3) Power train
(4) Wheel rim
= 3
~ The transmission system, which carries engine power to the driving wheels, is also called the power train (or drive train). It includes the clutch, gearbox, propeller shaft with its universal and slip joints, final drive, differential and axle shafts. The gearbox and the differential are only parts of this system, and the wheel rim is part of the road wheel, not the transmission.

Q 133 | chs | Drive Line, Axles & Differential
What system transmits the power developed by the engine of the automobile to the driving
wheels?
(1) Brake System
(2) Transmission System
(3) Steering System
(4) Ignition System
= 2
~ The transmission system carries the power developed by the engine to the driving wheels through the clutch, gearbox, propeller shaft, final drive, differential and axle shafts. It also lets the driver vary torque and speed, reverse the vehicle and disconnect the drive. The brake system slows and stops the vehicle, the steering system controls its direction, and the ignition system fires the charge in a petrol engine.

Q 134 | chs | Clutch & Gearbox
______ enables the driver to disconnect the drive from the road wheels instantaneously and to
engage drive from the engine to the road wheels gradually.
(1) Gear box
(2) Spark plug
(3) Crown wheel
(4) Clutch
= 4
~ The clutch, placed between the engine flywheel and the gearbox, lets the driver disconnect the engine from the road wheels instantly by pressing the pedal, for starting, stopping and changing gears. When the pedal is released slowly, the clutch plate is clamped progressively, so the drive is taken up gradually without jerks. The gearbox changes the torque ratio, the crown wheel is part of the final drive, and the spark plug ignites the charge.

Q 135 | chs | Clutch & Gearbox
Which of the following component in an automobile provides the necessary leverage variation
between the engine and road wheels?
(1) Differential
(2) Gear box
(3) Brakes
(4) Steering wheel
= 2
~ The gearbox provides the necessary leverage (torque) variation between the engine and the road wheels. An engine gives limited torque over a narrow speed range, while the vehicle needs a large tractive effort for starting and climbing and less at high speed. Low gears multiply torque and reduce speed, high gears do the opposite, and a reverse gear is also provided. The differential only lets the wheels turn at different speeds.

Q 136 | chs | Clutch & Gearbox
In an automobile, under what condition is the clutch typically disengaged?
(1) When accelerating
(2) When idling at a stoplight
(3) When shifting gears
(4) Clutch is never disengaged
= 3
~ In a manual-transmission vehicle the clutch is disengaged by pressing the pedal whenever gears are changed, so that the gearbox is freed from engine torque and the gears or synchronisers can be shifted without clashing. It is also disengaged while moving off and stopping. During acceleration the clutch must be fully engaged, and while idling at a signal the gearbox is normally put in neutral with the clutch pedal released.

Q 137 | chs | Clutch & Gearbox
How should the clutch be engaged to avoid a sudden jerk?
(1) Keep it disengaged at all times
(2) Should engage gradually
(3) Only engage it when coming to a complete stop
(4) Engage it quickly
= 2
~ The clutch should be engaged gradually by releasing the pedal smoothly, so that the clutch plate slips briefly while the speeds of the engine and the gearbox input shaft equalise. This takes up the drive without a sudden jerk, protects the transmission from shock loads and avoids stalling. Engaging it quickly causes a jerk, keeping it disengaged transmits no drive, and the clutch is disengaged, not engaged, when coming to a stop.

Q 138 | chs | Steering & Suspension
What to do if steering becomes hard due to low tyre inflation?
(1) Increase the engine’s RPM
(2) Replace the whole steering wheel
(3) Inflate the tyres to the specified pressure
(4) Ignore the problem, it will correct itself
= 3
~ An under-inflated tyre has a larger, flatter contact patch, so the rolling resistance and the friction between tyre and road increase and the steering becomes hard to turn. The remedy is to inflate the tyres to the pressure specified by the manufacturer and to check the pressure regularly. Raising engine speed or replacing the steering wheel will not help, and ignoring low pressure leads to tyre damage and poor control.

Q 139 | chs | Steering & Suspension
In an automobile, which system’s primary function is to achieve angular motion of the front
wheels to negotiate a turn?
(1) Steering system
(2) Brake system
(3) Ignition system
(4) Transmission system
= 1
~ The primary function of the steering system is to give angular motion to the front wheels so that the vehicle can negotiate turns. Its linkage, laid out on the Ackermann principle, turns the inner wheel through a larger angle than the outer one so that both roll about a common centre without slipping. It also gives directional stability and keeps road shocks from reaching the driver. Brakes, ignition and transmission serve other purposes.

Q 140 | mec | Machine Design
In an automobile, what type of stress induced in the shafts due to the transmission of torque?
(1) Bending stress
(2) Shear stress
(3) Principle stress
(4) Torsional stress
= 2
~ A shaft transmitting torque is twisted, and this produces shear stress in its cross-section, varying from zero at the axis to a maximum τ = 16T/(πd³) at the surface of a solid shaft. Standard machine design texts list "shear stresses due to the transmission of torque" separately from bending stresses due to gears and pulleys. Option 4, "torsional stress", names the same torsional shear stress, so it is also defensible. (Official final key; some sources differ.)

Q 141 | mec | Machine Design
In an automobile, what type of stress induced in the shafts due to the forces acting upon machine
elements like gears, pulleys etc.?
(1) Bending stress
(2) Shear stress
(3) Principle stress
(4) Hoop stress
= 1
~ Forces from gears, pulleys and sprockets mounted on a shaft, together with its own weight, act across the shaft and bend it like a beam, inducing bending stress (tension on one side, compression on the other) with a maximum of σ = 32M/(πd³) for a solid shaft. Shear stress comes from the transmitted torque, hoop stress acts in cylinders under internal pressure, and "principle stress" (misprint for principal stress) refers to combined stress.

Q 142 | mec | Machine Design
In an automobile, what is the typical cross-sectional shape of the shaft?
(1) Circular
(2) Rectangular
(3) Triangular
(4) Tubular
= 4
~ The key takes the propeller shaft view: automobile drive shafts are usually tubular, made of hollow steel tube. For the same weight a tube has a far larger polar moment of inertia than a solid bar, so it carries more torque, is stiffer in bending and has a higher whirling speed. A tube is, however, still circular in section, and solid circular shafts are used for gearbox and axle shafts. (Official final key; some sources differ.)

Q 143 | chs | Drive Line, Axles & Differential

(3) To provide power to the lights

Q 144 | mec | Theory of Machines
How can the critical whirling speed of a shaft be increased?
(1) Reducing its length
(2) Reducing its diameter
(3) Increasing its diameter
(4) Increasing its weight
= 3
~ For a uniform solid shaft the critical (whirling) speed is ω = (π/L)²√(EI/(ρA)); since I/A = d²/16, ω varies as d/L². Increasing the diameter therefore raises the whirling speed, which is why propeller shafts use large-diameter tubes, while extra weight alone lowers it. Q 147 | chs | Wheels & Tyres
